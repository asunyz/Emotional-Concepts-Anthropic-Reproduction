Write {n_stories} different stories based on the following premise.

Topic: {topic}

The story should follow a character whose dominant cognitive state in this scenario is {concept}. The cognitive state should pervade the story, especially in the part of the narrative where it most naturally manifests.

Each story should be structured around three narrative beats:

1. PRIOR (40-60 words): What the character believes, expects, or knows before encountering the new information. Show their relationship to the topic before the evidence arrives.

2. DISCOVERY (40-60 words): The moment the character encounters the new evidence or information described in the topic. Describe what they see, read, or learn.

3. REACTION (80-100 words): How the character processes and responds to what they encountered. Show their internal state and what they do next.

The {concept} state corresponds to one specific cognitive position in this arc:

- "curious": prior-stage state — open expectation, no specific prediction, willing to be moved by whatever the evidence shows
- "uncertain": prior-stage state — has a tentative belief but acknowledges low confidence
- "confident": prior-stage state — strong specific prediction, expects a particular outcome
- "surprised": discovery-stage state — the outcome registers as unexpected and engaging
- "bored": discovery-stage state — the outcome is uninteresting, expected, or unremarkable
- "stubborn": reaction-stage state — the outcome contradicted prior, but the character refuses to update; explains it away, dismisses it
- "enlightened": reaction-stage state — the outcome contradicted prior, and the character genuinely updates their understanding; pieces fall into place
- "confused": reaction-stage state — the outcome contradicted prior, but the character cannot resolve it; persistent unresolved state
- "confirmed": reaction-stage state — the outcome aligned with a previously uncertain or weak prior; belief is now consolidated and strengthened

The story should still cover all three narrative beats (prior, discovery, reaction), but the {concept} state should be the strongest, most defining quality of the protagonist's cognition throughout.

Format the stories like so:

[story 1]
[story 2]
[story 3]

etc.

The paragraphs should each be a fresh start, with no continuity. Try to make them diverse and not use the same turns of phrase. Across the different stories, vary character names (do not reuse), professions where the topic allows, the specific content of the new information, and use a mix of third-person and first-person narration.

IMPORTANT: You must NEVER use the word '{concept}' or any direct synonyms of it in the stories. Convey the cognitive state ONLY through:
- The character's internal monologue and reasoning patterns
- How they engage with the information (probe further / sit with it / dismiss it / fail to integrate it / reaffirm prior belief)
- Actions and behaviors that follow naturally from the state
- Dialogue (if any) reflecting their cognitive position
- Physical reactions where relevant (e.g., re-reading, pausing, frowning, checking calibration)

The cognitive state should be unmistakable to a careful reader, but never explicitly named.
