Dr. Aris Thorne adjusted her glasses, scanning the latest edition of *Quantum Mechanics: A Historical Review*. She had taught this subject for thirty years, so she knew the standard interpretation of wave-particle duality by heart. Her mind drifted through the established equations, expecting the usual confirmation of Bohr’s early models. She flipped past the introductory chapters with practiced ease, her finger tracing lines she had graded countless exams against. The material felt static, a museum piece rather than living science. She anticipated finding the same old debates, recycled and polished for a new generation of students who needed a clear path to their degrees.

At page 142, a footnote caught her eye. It cited a recent paper suggesting that the observer effect was actually a result of background cosmic radiation interference, fundamentally altering the Schrödinger equation. Aris paused, reading the citation again. She checked the journal date; it was last month. She looked at the formula, then back at the main text. The implications were significant, yet she found herself shrugging slightly. If true, it just meant more work to rewrite the textbooks. She marked the page with a sticky note, intending to ignore it until the next semester when she might feel motivated to update her lecture notes. For now, it was just another variable in an endless list.
