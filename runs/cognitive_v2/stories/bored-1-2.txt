Elias leaned back in the theater seat, the velvet rough against his neck. He had seen *The Silent Echo* five times, including two midnight screenings. He could recite the dialogue along with the actors, predicting every pause and camera angle before it happened. The opening credits rolled, and he watched the lead actor walk onto the set, knowing precisely which line he would deliver next. His interest had waned long ago, replaced by a comfortable familiarity. He wasn’t here for the surprise; he was here to witness the execution of something he already owned in his memory. The tension in the room seemed artificial, forced upon him by strangers who didn’t know the ending.

Halfway through the second act, the script deviated. The protagonist stopped speaking entirely and began painting the walls black instead of fleeing the scene. Elias frowned, leaning forward slightly to catch the detail. The director’s note on screen explained this was an improvised addition to symbolize inner decay. Elias stared at the screen for a moment longer than necessary, then slumped back into his seat. It was a nice touch, certainly, but it didn’t change the trajectory of the plot. He pulled out his phone, scrolling through emails while the movie continued, treating the deviation as background noise rather than a narrative shift. The film played on, but Elias had already tuned it out.
