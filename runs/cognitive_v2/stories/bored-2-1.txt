Elena Vance adjusted her glasses, feeling the weight of another Tuesday afternoon pressing against her eyelids. She knew the protocol for these genetic screens cold. Her mind was already drifting toward lunch plans as she clicked open the file for Patient 409. She expected the typical benign variant found in ninety percent of the population.

The report loaded, showing a clear homozygous mutation for Factor V Leiden. Elena blinked slowly, reading the line twice. It was just another clotting disorder, common and well-documented. She felt a mild tug of recognition, but it lacked any spark of intrigue. The result was exactly what she feared would happen, yet somehow disappointing in its predictability.

She typed the warning about anticoagulants into the electronic record with mechanical precision. No need to call the specialist; the guidelines were clear. She closed the tab and stared blankly at the next name on her list. The complexity of human biology often boiled down to simple, repetitive errors. She took a sip of lukewarm tea, wondering if it was time to go home early.
