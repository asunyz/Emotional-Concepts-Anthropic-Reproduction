Marcus leaned against the counter, scrolling through his phone while the printer whirred behind him. He had reviewed fifty charts today, and his brain had settled into a gray haze of repetition. He assumed the latest batch would be equally uneventful, filled with minor viral markers that resolved themselves. He checked his watch, calculating how many minutes until his break.

The printout slid into the tray. Marcus picked it up lazily, scanning the hemoglobin levels. They were perfectly within the normal reference range. He nodded once, satisfied that the system was working as intended. There was no deviation, no anomaly, no reason to pause. The numbers were flat lines on a graph of his day.

He tossed the paper onto the sorting pile and walked back to his desk. Another healthy patient meant another empty slot in his schedule. He opened a new file, eager to get the rest over with. The lack of news was a relief, allowing him to maintain his steady, unbothered pace. He barely registered the next name, treating it as just another item on a long, endless to-do list.
