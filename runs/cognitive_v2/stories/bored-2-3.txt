Dr. Lin Chen rested her chin in her hand, watching the rain streak against the windowpane. She had spent the morning arguing with insurance codes, leaving her with little mental energy left for diagnostics. She opened the toxicology screen expecting to find the usual alcohol metabolites or perhaps a prescribed benzodiazepine. Her eyes glazed over slightly as she prepared to dismiss the findings.

The panel returned negative for everything except trace amounts of a rare environmental toxin. Lin stared at the screen, her expression unchanged. It wasn’t shocking enough to warrant a full investigation, nor was it dangerous enough to demand immediate intervention. It was just noise in the signal. She noted the finding with a shrug, assuming it was negligible.

She printed the report and handed it to the intake clerk with a wave. “Tell him to avoid the old factory district,” she said, her voice monotone. Then she turned back to her computer, opening the next file. The environment was messy, but the clinical picture remained stable. She continued her work, undisturbed by the slight irregularity, letting it fade into the background hum of the clinic.
