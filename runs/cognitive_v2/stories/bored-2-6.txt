**State:** Bored (Discovery) -> **Narrator:** Second Person

You are tired. The coffee has worn off, and the stack of charts on your desk feels heavier than usual. You expect nothing but the usual suspects: slightly elevated cholesterol, maybe some pre-diabetic indicators, perhaps a urinary tract infection that will require another round of antibiotics. Your mind is elsewhere, thinking about dinner plans, when you open the first file, anticipating the familiar rhythm of routine care that requires little more than a nod and a signature.

You glance at the results. Total cholesterol is 210. White blood cell count is 7.5. Glucose is 98. Everything falls comfortably within the broad bands of normalcy or mild inconvenience. There is no shock, no intrigue, no dramatic spike or drop. Just the steady, uneventful march of average biological function. The numbers are bland, repetitive, and utterly devoid of narrative interest, confirming your deepest suspicion that today holds no surprises.

You sigh, marking the chart as "stable" and moving on to the next one with mechanical efficiency. There is no need to investigate further, no reason to pause or ponder. The lack of deviation is almost comforting in its predictability, allowing you to breeze through the remaining patients without breaking your stride. You file the report away, feeling the weight of the tedium lift as you anticipate the rest of the shift will be equally forgettable and smooth.
