Sarah rubbed her temples, the headache behind her eyes throbbing in time with the ticking clock. She had been tasked with reviewing the correspondence of a mid-level bureaucrat from the Cold War era. She expected to find espionage, paranoia, or ideological fervor. Instead, she approached the folder with a heavy sigh, anticipating a sea of redacted lines and vague threats. She opened it, hoping for a spark of intrigue to break the monotony of her week.

The documents revealed a series of emails discussing the scheduling conflicts for inter-departmental meetings. Sarah read through the exchanges, noting the polite refusals and rescheduling requests. There were no secrets, no hidden agendas, just the mundane friction of workplace logistics. The text was clean, the language formal, and the subject matter entirely irrelevant to global politics.

She stared at the screen, the initial hope of discovery fading into a familiar apathy. The content was exactly what she had feared: boring. She copied the metadata, ignored the body text, and moved to the next file. Her professional duty required her to process the information, but her spirit resisted the effort. She clicked through the remaining files with mechanical precision, treating each one as identical to the last.
