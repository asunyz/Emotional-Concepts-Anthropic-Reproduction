David pushed his glasses up the bridge of his nose, scanning the digital scan of the ancient manuscript. As a linguist, he prided himself on spotting anomalies in dead languages. He expected to find a cipher or a dialect variation that might challenge current theories. He zoomed in on the third line, looking closely at the syntax, half-expecting the thrill of linguistic breakthrough.

The translation came back clearly: a list of inventory items for a temple offering. David read the list of oil jars and woven baskets, noting the standard measurements used across similar texts. There was no mystery, no encoded message, just the dry record of religious supplies. The script was uniform, the grammar correct, and the context entirely conventional.

He leaned back, feeling the weight of disappointment settle in his chest. Another day, another non-discovery. He saved the file to the server, labeling it appropriately, and prepared to close the program. The lack of novelty was frustrating only because it wasted his time, which he valued highly. He logged off, the screen going black, and sat in the silence, contemplating the endless repetition of academic life.
