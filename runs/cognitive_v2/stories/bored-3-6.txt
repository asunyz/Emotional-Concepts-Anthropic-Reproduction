**Cognitive State:** Bored (Discovery-stage)
**Narrator:** Third-Person

Margaret expected the file to contain either nothing or something trivially obvious. Her skepticism was high, her expectations low. She believed the recent controversy surrounding the old factory closure was just political noise, devoid of substantive historical merit. She opened the folder with a sigh, anticipating a rehash of known facts that offered no new insight, merely confirming her own cynical worldview about corporate obfuscation.

She scanned the documents. They were indeed repetitive memos discussing minor budget adjustments and staffing shifts. There were no smoking guns, no secret emails, no hidden agendas. Just dry, bureaucratic language confirming that the factory closed due to lack of orders, exactly as reported. The information was bland, predictable, and utterly devoid of complexity. It was exactly what she thought it would be, and perhaps even less interesting than anticipated.

Margaret closed the folder and placed it back on the shelf, feeling a profound sense of anticlimax. There was no mystery to unravel, no narrative twist to appreciate. She checked her watch, noting how much time she had wasted on a dead end. The confirmation of her bias brought no satisfaction, only a mild irritation at the wasted effort. She turned to her computer, eager to return to work that actually mattered, leaving the boring reality of the archive undisturbed.
