**Prior**: Maya sat in her ergonomic chair, watching the clock tick toward 4:55 PM. Her review queue was empty except for one paper on neural network optimization. She remembered the core hypothesis from the abstract: reducing layer depth without losing accuracy via a proprietary pruning algorithm. Having reviewed dozens of papers on this exact topic last month, she knew the trade-off curve. It was a well-trodden path with diminishing returns. She anticipated finding minor incremental improvements masked as breakthroughs, requiring little more than a nod of acknowledgment before dismissal.

**Discovery**: As she skimmed the results table, her eyes glazed over. The performance metrics were statistically insignificant compared to the baseline model presented in the literature. The authors claimed a 0.02% gain, but the error bars overlapped completely. They had reinvented the wheel, polishing it slightly while ignoring the fact that wheels don’t fly. The data was clean, precise, and utterly devoid of surprise. It confirmed exactly what she already suspected about the limits of current architecture.

**Reaction**: Maya leaned back, stretching her arms above her head until her spine cracked. The predictability of the findings felt almost comforting in its blandness. She typed a brief comment suggesting the authors compare their work more rigorously against existing benchmarks, though she doubted they would care. She approved the paper with minor revisions, not out of enthusiasm, but because it was technically correct if boringly so. Closing the tab, she checked her personal email, eager for anything that offered a spark of unpredictability, however small.
