**Prior**: Julian adjusted his glasses, the blue light from the monitor reflecting in his tired eyes. He was reviewing a preprint on climate modeling algorithms. The title promised a revolutionary approach to predicting micro-climate shifts. Julian had spent years studying these models and knew that such precision was currently beyond reach due to chaotic system variables. He expected the paper to crumble under basic scrutiny, offering excuses for oversimplification. His mind was already halfway through lunch, rehearsing the polite critiques he would offer.

**Discovery**: He clicked through to the validation section. The code repository link was provided, and upon a quick check, he found the implementation. It used a standard linear regression model disguised with advanced terminology. The authors had ignored non-linear interactions entirely. It was exactly the kind of naive approach he had seen in freshman textbooks. There was no hidden complexity, no clever trick, just a straightforward application of outdated methods. The evidence was right there, plain and obvious, confirming his lowest expectations.

**Reaction**: Julian rubbed his temples, feeling the weight of the mundane reality settle in. He didn’t bother running simulations himself; the flaw was self-evident. He wrote his review, pointing out the methodological limitations with dry precision. He suggested they consult a senior statistician, a suggestion he knew would go unheeded. Submitting the review, he closed the browser window. The world outside his window continued its slow, predictable rotation. He stood up, walked to the kitchen, and made a sandwich, savoring the simple, unchanging taste of bread and cheese.
