**Prior**: Sarah scrolled through the digital library, her finger hovering over the submit button. She was looking at a study on consumer behavior in retail environments. The hypothesis suggested that lighting color temperature directly influenced purchase volume. Having read countless studies on environmental psychology, Sarah knew that human decision-making was too complex for such single-variable determinism. She assumed the authors would present anecdotal evidence or poorly controlled surveys. She prepared herself for the usual noise, expecting to find confounding variables buried in the footnotes.

**Discovery**: She scanned the methodology. It was a large-scale A/B test conducted across hundreds of stores. The results showed a negligible correlation between lighting and sales, statistically indistinguishable from random chance. The authors admitted this in the discussion, concluding that other factors dominated consumer choice. It was a null result, perfectly executed but utterly unsurprising. Nothing challenged her skepticism; everything reinforced the status quo. The data was solid, reliable, and deeply unexciting.

**Reaction**: Sarah let out a long breath, exhaling the tension she hadn’t realized she was holding. She appreciated the honesty of the null result, even if it lacked drama. She recommended acceptance for publication, noting its value in preventing future wasted research efforts. It was a service to the field, much like filling out tax forms correctly. She saved the report, logged off, and put on her headphones. A familiar podcast episode played, discussing topics she already understood well. She smiled faintly, enjoying the comfort of the known.
