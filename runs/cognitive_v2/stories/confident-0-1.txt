**Prior:** Elena Vance sat in her lab coat, reviewing the soil samples from the Martian rover. She expected to find trace amounts of perchlorates, consistent with standard planetary geology reports. Her hypothesis was simple: the red dust was inert, composed primarily of iron oxides and basic silicates. She had already written the conclusion in her head, ready to file the report and move on to the next mission phase without delay.

**Discovery:** The mass spectrometer pinged softly. Elena frowned, leaning closer to the screen. The reading showed complex organic chains—amino acids, specifically. They weren’t contaminants from Earth; the isotopic ratios matched local Martian sources perfectly. The sample wasn’t just mineral; it was biological residue, preserved in the subsurface ice layer she had drilled through only hours ago.

**Reaction:** Elena stared at the numbers, her breath catching in her throat. For a moment, silence filled the room. Then, she let out a sharp laugh, shaking her head in disbelief. The implications were staggering. Life existed, or had existed, right here beneath her boots. She quickly secured the sample container, labeling it with urgent precision. There was no time for celebration yet; there was work to do. She grabbed her notebook, her mind racing with questions about preservation methods and evolutionary pathways.
