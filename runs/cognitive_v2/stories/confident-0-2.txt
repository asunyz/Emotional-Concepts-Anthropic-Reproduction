**Prior:** Marcus Reed stood before the whiteboard, marker in hand. He was absolutely sure the algorithm’s error rate stemmed from a single bug in the neural network’s weight initialization. He had traced the logic flow multiple times. The code was clean, the architecture sound. He knew exactly how to fix it: adjust the bias term by 0.05, and the accuracy would jump to ninety-nine percent. He felt ready to declare victory and go home.

**Discovery:** He typed the command and hit enter. The training loop ran. The loss curve dropped, yes, but then plateaued at eighty-five percent. He checked the logs. The bug was gone, but the performance hadn’t improved. In fact, the validation scores had dipped slightly. The model was learning, but it was learning the wrong thing entirely, ignoring key features he deemed essential.

**Reaction:** Marcus rubbed his temples, confused. He re-ran the script, doubting his own eyes. The results were identical. The code was perfect, yet the output was flawed. He tried adjusting other parameters, but nothing worked. The system seemed to have its own internal logic, one he couldn’t parse. He sat down heavily, staring at the stubbornly low score. Why? He didn’t know, and for the first time in years, he felt truly stuck, unable to bridge the gap between theory and practice.
