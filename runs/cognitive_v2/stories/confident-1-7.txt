**Profession:** Detective | **Perspective:** Third-Person | **Cognitive State:** Confident Prior -> Surprised Discovery -> Stubborn Reaction

Detective Miller knew the killer was the jealous ex-lover. The evidence was overwhelming: fingerprints on the weapon, motive established by recent breakup letters, and a solid alibi breakdown. He had already drafted the arrest warrant in his head, certain that justice would be swift and straightforward. The case file was closed in his mind; the narrative fit perfectly, leaving no gaps for alternative theories. He expected the final confession to confirm his suspicions.

When he opened the suspect’s diary found at the scene, however, the handwriting was unfamiliar. The entries detailed a murder plot, yes, but the victim was someone else entirely—someone who had died years ago. The dates were impossible. The location was a building that had burned down decades prior. Miller read the page again, then a third time, searching for a joke or a coded message that might align with his theory.

He slammed the book shut, refusing to look at it again. This was clearly a fabrication, a desperate attempt by the suspect to confuse the investigation. Miller tossed the diary into the evidence bag with disdain, telling himself it was irrelevant noise. No amount of paper could change the facts he had gathered. He walked out of the interrogation room, firmly convinced that the diary was a lie, ignoring the chilling implication that the killer might be someone who knew him personally.
