Elena Vance knew her patient, Mr. Henderson, suffered from mild hypertension. She anticipated seeing elevated systolic readings consistent with stress-induced spikes during work hours. Her baseline assumption was simple: lifestyle factors were the primary driver, and medication adjustments would suffice. She walked into the exam room ready to prescribe a standard beta-blocker, feeling assured that the data would align perfectly with her preliminary assessment.

Opening the electronic health record, Elena paused. The ambulatory monitor displayed a striking pattern of nocturnal dipping, with blood pressure dropping too sharply overnight. Systolic values hit 85 mmHg while asleep, a dangerous hypotensive event that contradicted her theory of simple stress hypertension. This wasn't just mild elevation; it was a regulatory failure affecting circadian rhythms. She felt a sudden shift in perspective, recognizing that her previous framework was incomplete. Instead of adding more medication, she realized the need to investigate autonomic nervous system dysfunction. She closed the laptop, deciding to refer him to a cardiologist for advanced testing, acknowledging that the evidence had corrected her path entirely.
