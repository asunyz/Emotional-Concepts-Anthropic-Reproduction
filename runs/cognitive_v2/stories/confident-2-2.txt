Marcus Lin was certain the rash on the teenager’s arm was contact dermatitis. The redness was localized, symmetrical, and appeared after exposure to new laundry detergent. He had seen this exact presentation hundreds of times. He prepared to recommend a topical steroid and avoidance strategies, fully expecting the treatment plan to resolve the issue without complication. His mind was already moving on to the next appointment, secure in his diagnostic accuracy.

He zoomed in on the dermoscopy image provided by the urgent care clinic. The peripheral scaling vanished, replaced by distinct, annular lesions with central clearing. The texture was rough, almost psoriasiform, which made absolutely no sense given the history of soap exposure. Marcus stopped breathing for a second. The visual evidence directly undermined his initial classification. He traced the edge of the lesion on the screen, noting the subtle vesicles that suggested a fungal etiology rather than an allergic reaction. A wave of realization washed over him; he had rushed to judgment based on superficial similarities. He cancelled the steroid prescription and prescribed oral antifungals instead, grateful for the clarity the detailed imaging provided.
