**Prior:** Dr. Aris Thorne adjusted his spectacles, certain that the late 19th-century industrial logs were merely bureaucratic filler. He had spent decades proving that the Great Strike was orchestrated by external agitators, not local grievances. The archives were a formality, a necessary step to secure tenure for his upcoming paper on labor inefficiency. He expected nothing more than faded ink confirming his established thesis.

**Discovery:** Inside Folder 7B, he found a handwritten ledger from the strike leader’s personal diary. It detailed precise coordination with factory owners, listing payments made in exchange for sabotaging key machinery. The dates matched perfectly with the supposed "spontaneous" outbursts of violence. There was no mention of external agitators; instead, there were signatures linking the union leadership directly to the competing corporation’s board members.

**Reaction:** Aris felt a cold spike of anxiety pierce his chest. His hands trembled as he flipped back to his notes, searching for any flaw in the document’s authenticity. Was it a forgery? A prank? He scanned the paper texture, the ink bleed, the handwriting style. Nothing seemed off. Yet, to accept this would unravel twenty years of scholarship. He closed the folder slowly, his mind racing through excuses. He would cite it as an anomaly, an isolated incident that proved the rule rather than broke it. The narrative held firm; this document was just noise.
