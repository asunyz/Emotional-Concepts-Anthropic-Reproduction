**Prior**
Dr. Aris Thorne sat in his sterile office, the hum of the server farm outside barely audible. He had spent twenty years proving that quantum entanglement could never transmit information faster than light. It was a fundamental law, etched into the bedrock of physics. When the manuscript landed in his inbox, he assumed it was another flawed attempt by an overzealous postdoc who hadn’t grasped the basic constraints of causality. He prepared his red pen, ready to dismantle the error with surgical precision.

**Discovery**
He scrolled past the abstract, expecting the usual hand-waving about tachyons or wormholes. Instead, he found a rigorous derivation using non-commutative geometry. The math was dense, but the logic held together with terrifying coherence. The equations didn’t just suggest FTL communication; they demonstrated a mechanism for stabilizing the channel without violating relativity. His eyes widened as he traced the variable substitutions, realizing the proof wasn't just plausible—it was elegant, simple, and devastatingly correct.

**Reaction**
Aris leaned back, the chair creaking under his sudden shift in posture. He felt a cold thrill race down his spine, not of fear, but of pure intellectual exhilaration. The world had shifted on its axis, and he was standing right at the epicenter. He grabbed his phone, dialing his rival at MIT. "Listen," he said, voice steady and bright, "you need to look at page four. We were wrong, but we’re going to be famous." He smiled, already drafting the press release in his head.
