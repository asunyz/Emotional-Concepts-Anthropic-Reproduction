**Prior**
Marcus Reed poured himself a glass of whiskey, the amber liquid swirling in the dim light of his study. He had built his career on debunking urban legends, specifically the myth of the "whispering woods" in the Pacific Northwest. To him, it was just wind through pine needles, amplified by acoustics and human suggestion. He had written three books on the subject, dismissing any claim of supernatural origin as lazy thinking. When the local historian sent him the recording, Marcus chuckled, expecting another example of pareidolia masquerading as mystery.

**Discovery**
He pressed play, leaning in close to the speakers. At first, there was only the rustle of leaves. Then, a distinct voice cut through the static, speaking a dialect dead for two centuries. Marcus froze, the remote control slipping from his hand. He rewound the tape, listening again. The cadence was perfect, the pronunciation flawless. It wasn't an echo or a trick of the wind. It was a coherent sentence, directed precisely at the microphone’s location.

**Reaction**
Marcus sat back, the whiskey forgotten on the desk. He felt a strange calm wash over him, the kind that comes when a long-held truth is finally exposed. There was no panic, only a profound sense of clarity. He picked up his notebook, flipping to a blank page. "It’s real," he wrote, the ink flowing smoothly. He didn't need to scream or run; he simply needed to document. He knew his next book would sell millions, but more importantly, he knew he was right. He took a sip of whiskey, tasting the satisfaction of being proven correct.
