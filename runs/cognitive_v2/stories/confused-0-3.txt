**Profession:** Quantum Physicist | **Narration:** First-Person | **State Pathway:** Confident Prior -> Surprised Discovery -> Confused Reaction

I know exactly what the double-slit experiment will show today: particles behaving as waves, creating an interference pattern that confirms wave-particle duality. My hypothesis is solid, backed by decades of replicated data, and I fully expect the detector array to light up with the familiar bands of constructive and destructive interference, reinforcing the standard model of quantum mechanics.

The monitors flicker to life, and instead of the expected striped pattern, the screen displays a single, solid block of color, indicating that every particle landed in the exact same spot simultaneously. There is no spread, no diffraction, just a dense cluster of hits that suggests the particles ignored the slits entirely, passing through both barriers at once without interacting with the medium.

I adjust the lens focus, convinced the camera is misaligned, but the raw data feed tells the same impossible story. I run the simulation again, tweaking variables, but the output remains stubbornly singular, defying the fundamental laws I have spent my career studying. I feel a cold sweat prickle my neck, my mind fracturing as I try to hold onto the certainty of physics while watching it unravel into a singularity of nonsense that offers no immediate resolution.
