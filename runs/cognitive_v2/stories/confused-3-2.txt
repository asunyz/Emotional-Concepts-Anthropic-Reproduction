Professor Lin checked his watch, expecting the excavation site to yield pottery shards consistent with the late Bronze Age trade routes he had mapped out. He trusted the stratigraphy reports implicitly, viewing them as objective anchors in the shifting sands of archaeological interpretation. His trowel rested in his belt, poised for a gentle scrape along the designated grid square.

The brush revealed not ceramic fragments, but a sleek, synthetic polymer casing embedded deep in the soil, untouched by erosion. It bore no markings, no wear, just a smooth, unnatural surface that reflected the harsh sunlight. Lin knelt, digging frantically around it, hoping to find a layer of ash or rust that would contextualize the object. Instead, he uncovered more identical casings, arranged in a geometric pattern that defied natural deposition. He stood up, wiping sweat from his brow, and looked at his notes, then at the ground, then back at the notes. The timeline collapsed in his mind, leaving him standing in a void between epochs, holding a tool meant for the past while touching something that refused to belong to any time he understood.
