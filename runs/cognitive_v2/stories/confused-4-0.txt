Dr. Aris Thorne stared at the screen, his coffee growing cold. He had spent years proving quantum decoherence was absolute, a hard wall against macroscopic superposition. Now, a preprint sat before him, claiming a stable entangled state in room-temperature biology. It defied every law he held sacred, yet the math was irrefutable. His mind fractured between the elegance of the proof and the impossibility of the phenomenon, leaving him paralyzed by a contradiction he could neither accept nor discard.
