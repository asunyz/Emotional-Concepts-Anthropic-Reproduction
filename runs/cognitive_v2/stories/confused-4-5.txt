**State:** Curious -> Surprised -> Confused
**Character:** Julian Vane, Linguistics Professor

Julian loved the hunt for linguistic universals. He kept an open mind, treating every new dataset as a potential key to unlocking the deeper structures of human language. He welcomed ambiguity, knowing that the map of grammar was always incomplete. When he saw the title *Non-Nested Recursion in Isolated Amazonian Dialects*, he felt the familiar spark of academic anticipation, ready to see how this anomaly might reshape his current models of syntactic depth.

Reading the field notes, Julian froze. The authors presented audio recordings and transcriptions suggesting a language that processed complex dependencies without hierarchical nesting, defying Chomsky’s hierarchy entirely. It was bizarre, counter-intuitive, and yet meticulously documented. The data was irrefutable, showing a structure that looked more like a flat list than a tree, challenging the very definition of what constituted a recursive grammar.

He paced his study, rubbing his temples as the implications swirled in his head. If this was true, then his entire career’s framework of universal grammar was fundamentally flawed, yet he couldn’t find a flaw in their recording equipment or translation methods. The contradiction sat in his throat like a stone; he wanted to accept the breakthrough, but his training screamed that such a thing shouldn't exist. He stared at the transcript, unable to reconcile the beauty of the data with the ugliness of the theoretical collapse it demanded.
