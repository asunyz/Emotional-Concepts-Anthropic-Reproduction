Arthur adjusted his spectacles, tracing the line of text in the antique atlas. He assumed the map’s faded ink was merely decorative, a stylistic choice of the 19th-century cartographer. His eyes drifted over the intricate borders, expecting nothing more than historical geography. Then he noticed the marginalia, written in a hand identical to the author’s own diary entries found in the library basement. It wasn't decoration; it was a correction. The coastline shifted slightly, revealing an island that hadn’t existed for centuries, labeled only with coordinates that matched his current location. Arthur leaned closer, breath hitching. If the map was wrong, what else was? He flipped back pages rapidly, comparing dates, realizing every landmark he had memorized might be a fabrication designed to hide the truth. The world outside his window seemed suddenly less solid, the trees looking less like wood and more like painted scenery. He needed to verify the source, to find the original draft, because if the past could be rewritten on paper, reality itself felt precarious.
