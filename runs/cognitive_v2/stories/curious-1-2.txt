I thought the ghost stories in my grandmother’s journal were metaphors for her grief after losing my grandfather. I treated the text as poetic license, a way to process loss without confronting the harsh silence of death. I turned the page, expecting another elegy. Instead, I found a list of dates. Next to each date was a name of someone who died recently, and a note about how they "passed the torch." My brow furrowed. I checked the newspaper archives. Every person listed had died within hours of the entry being written. Not before. After. And the tone wasn't mournful; it was transactional. I felt a chill crawl up my spine, unrelated to the room's temperature. Was she a mourner or a harvester? I grabbed my phone, searching for recent obituaries matching the pattern. The coincidence was statistically impossible. I needed to call the local priest, not for comfort, but to ask if he’d ever heard of such rituals. The mystery was terrifying, but the need to understand the mechanism behind it drove me forward.
