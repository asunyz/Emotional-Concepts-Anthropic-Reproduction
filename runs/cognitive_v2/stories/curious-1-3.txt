Dr. Aris Thorne reviewed the fossil record, confident in the established timeline of human evolution. The strata layers told a clear story: gradual change over millions of years. He expected to file the report confirming the site’s age. However, the carbon dating results attached to the appendix showed a discrepancy. The bone fragments dated to ten thousand years ago, yet the surrounding geological layer suggested fifty million. Aris rubbed his temples, dismissing the error as contamination. But then he looked at the tool marks on the stone. They were precise, machine-cut, not chipped by primitive hands. He picked up his loupe, examining the microscopic striations. They matched modern CNC machining standards. His certainty fractured. He pulled out his laptop, cross-referencing the geological survey maps with satellite imagery of the same region today. The landforms aligned perfectly with the ancient diagrams. He realized the timeline wasn't broken; it was cyclical. He began drafting a new hypothesis, his mind racing to connect the dots between prehistoric technology and present-day geology.
