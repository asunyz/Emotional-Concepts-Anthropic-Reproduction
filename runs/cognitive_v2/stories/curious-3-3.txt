I assumed the letter was a forgery, a common hoax played on collectors during the post-war economic slump. I picked up the envelope, feeling the heavy, expensive paper, and slid out the single sheet inside, ready to critique its poor calligraphy. To my mild surprise, the handwriting was flawless, matching the victim’s known samples perfectly, yet the content described events I hadn’t seen in any biography. I found myself tracing the loops of the letters, wondering how such a precise replica could exist, and began gathering comparative samples from other verified correspondence, driven by a relentless desire to understand the mechanics behind the deception rather than simply condemning it.
