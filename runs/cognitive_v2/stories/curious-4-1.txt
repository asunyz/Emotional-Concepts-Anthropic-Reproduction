Dr. Aris Thorne adjusted her glasses, reviewing the grant proposal for a project she had personally rejected twice. She remembered the abstract clearly: claims of faster-than-light neutrino transmission via vacuum tunneling. To her, this was nonsense, a violation of causality that belonged in science fiction, not peer-reviewed journals. Yet, curiosity tugged at her sleeve, urging her to look closer despite her skepticism.

She scrolled down to Section 4, the raw telemetry logs. There it was—a timestamp discrepancy that defied standard relativistic limits, repeated over ten thousand trials. The data was clean, the equipment calibrated, and the statistical significance was off the charts. Aris paused, her finger hovering over the mouse, wondering if she had missed a fundamental law of physics or if the universe had simply decided to play a trick.

Instead of dismissing it as an anomaly, she began to deconstruct the experiment’s setup. She imagined herself in the lab, walking through the control room, visualizing the particle paths. Her initial dismissal melted away, replaced by a burning need to understand the mechanism. She drafted a reply email to the authors, asking for their raw code, driven by the exhilarating possibility that she was wrong, and that the world was stranger than she knew.
