Marcus wiped coffee stains from his keyboard, preparing to tear apart another paper claiming perpetual motion machines were viable. He had seen this trope before: hidden energy sources, measurement errors, deliberate fraud. He approached the manuscript with a skeptical eye, ready to find the flaw, yet unable to resist the intellectual challenge of finding exactly where the logic broke.

He reached the appendix. The thermodynamic calculations were elegant, bypassing the usual traps with a novel heat-exchange algorithm using phase-change materials. Marcus frowned, re-reading the efficiency metrics. They weren't just plausible; they were mathematically sound within the proposed framework. He checked the reference list, then the citations, finding a chain of logic that held together without visible cracks.

A sense of wonder washed over him, displacing his cynicism. He stood up and paced the small office, muttering equations under his breath. If this worked, it would rewrite engineering textbooks. He felt a genuine urge to replicate the study himself, not to debunk it, but to witness the miracle firsthand. The joy of potential discovery outweighed the comfort of being right.
