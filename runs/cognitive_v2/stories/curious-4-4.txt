Professor Halloway rubbed his temples, looking at the submission from a graduate student. The claim was audacious: gravity could be shielded locally using rotating superconductors. Halloway had heard these rumors before, usually ending in embarrassment. He opened the document with a neutral mindset, intending to critique the methodology thoroughly, though secretly hoping for a clever explanation of the error.

The math was dense, but the derivation was rigorous. The experimental setup involved a gyroscope suspended in a magnetic field, measuring weight fluctuations during high-speed rotation. The data points clustered tightly around a non-zero delta. Halloway blinked, leaning back in his chair. The implications were staggering, challenging General Relativity at its core. He felt a spark of pure intellectual hunger.

He grabbed a pen and started sketching diagrams on a whiteboard, testing the robustness of the argument. He looked for holes, but found only solid ground. A laugh escaped him—not mocking, but delighted. He picked up the phone to call the department head, his voice trembling slightly with excitement. He couldn't wait to discuss this with colleagues, desperate to explore the new landscape this discovery had revealed.
