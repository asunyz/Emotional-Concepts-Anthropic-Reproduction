I always thought my grandmother’s garden was just a collection of wildflowers she couldn’t bear to throw away. To me, it was cluttered and messy, a testament to her declining mental sharpness rather than any artistic vision. I visited only out of obligation, stepping carefully over the tangled roots.

Last Tuesday, I noticed how every thorny bush blocked a view of the neighbor’s house, while the fragrant herbs lined the path exactly where the afternoon sun hit hardest. It wasn’t neglect; it was a fortress designed for privacy and comfort, mapped perfectly to the light and sightlines of the land.

I walked through the gate again, seeing the intention behind the apparent disorder. The mess was a mask for a sophisticated design strategy I had completely missed. I picked up my pruning shears, not to tidy it up, but to maintain the delicate balance she had engineered, finally appreciating the wisdom woven into every vine.
