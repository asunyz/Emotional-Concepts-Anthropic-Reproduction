Nathan listened to the patient’s history, suspecting anxiety-induced palpitations. He expected normal ECG readings, attributing the complaints to lifestyle factors. His approach was relaxed, planning to prescribe beta-blockers and advise rest. He viewed the case as straightforward, anticipating a quick resolution without extensive investigation.

The Holter monitor revealed intermittent atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response, contradicting the anxiety hypothesis. Nathan paused, reviewing the rhythm strips carefully. The irregularity was distinct, requiring anticoagulation rather than sedation. He felt a sudden clarity as the clinical picture resolved. The initial dismissal was corrected by undeniable evidence.

He explained the new diagnosis clearly, shifting the treatment plan to manage stroke risk. Nathan appreciated the correction, acknowledging how easy it is to misattribute symptoms. He felt engaged by the medical challenge, valuing the accuracy over convenience. His demeanor became attentive and thorough, ensuring the patient understood the long-term implications. He found satisfaction in providing precise care.
