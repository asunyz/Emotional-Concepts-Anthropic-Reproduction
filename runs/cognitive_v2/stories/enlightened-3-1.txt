Dr. Aris Vane operated on the assumption that the Bronze Age collapse was primarily driven by climate change, a theory he held with cautious uncertainty, aware that other factors like seismic activity played a role. He opened the newly digitized clay tablet fragment from Ugarit expecting more data on rainfall patterns, prepared to adjust his model slightly if the isotopic analysis suggested otherwise.

The translation revealed a diplomatic correspondence mentioning a sudden influx of refugees from the north, accompanied by trade agreements that bypassed traditional routes entirely. There was no mention of drought-induced famine in the immediate context of the collapse, only political maneuvering and economic shifts triggered by migration pressure.

Aris leaned back, the initial doubt evaporating into a sharp, illuminating realization. The climate data hadn't been wrong, but it had been misinterpreted as the cause rather than a concurrent symptom. The true driver was the disruption of supply chains caused by the refugee crisis, which destabilized the already fragile political alliances. He saw how his previous models had incorrectly prioritized environmental variables over sociological ones. With a calm demeanor, he deleted his old draft and started anew, integrating the human element as the primary catalyst, finally seeing the full picture without the blinders of academic convention.
