Elena Vance believed her hydroponic algae strain was immune to heavy metal toxicity. She had fed it lead-laced water for weeks, watching it thrive under UV lamps. Her hypothesis was simple: genetic modification provided total protection against industrial runoff. She expected green sludge, not brown sludge, but she trusted her code over the color.

Then she looked closer. The culture wasn’t just brown; it was dead, floating in clumps of necrotic tissue. The pH levels were stable, yet the biomass had collapsed entirely. Elena frowned, tilting her head. She assumed the light spectrum was off. She adjusted the bulbs, waited another hour, and when the death persisted, she blamed a contaminated batch of nutrients rather than accepting her gene-editing had failed catastrophically.
