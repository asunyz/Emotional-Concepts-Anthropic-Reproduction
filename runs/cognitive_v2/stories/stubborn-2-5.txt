**Cognitive State:** Stubborn (Reaction Stage)

Captain Miller stared at the radar display, convinced the blip was nothing more than atmospheric interference caused by the approaching storm front. He had flown these routes for twenty years and knew every quirk of the local weather patterns. In his mind, the anomaly was simply static, a ghost in the machine that would vanish once the clouds broke. He prepared to adjust the heading slightly to avoid the turbulence, trusting his decades of experience over the blinking light.

The radar pinged again, louder this time, confirming a solid object directly ahead at cruising altitude. The return signal was crisp, metallic, and undeniable. It wasn't a bird flock, nor was it a cloud formation. It was a large, unidentified structure moving against the wind currents. Captain Miller squinted at the screen, then rubbed his eyes, forcing himself to look again. The data remained stubbornly present, contradicting everything he knew about safe flight paths.

He tapped the screen aggressively, hoping for a glitch, but the reading held steady. "Sensor malfunction," he muttered aloud, reaching for the diagnostic override button rather than altering course. If the equipment said there was nothing there, then there *was* nothing there, regardless of what the pixels suggested. He dismissed the warning as a technical error, choosing to trust his gut over the cold, hard facts staring him in the face. The blip could stay; he wouldn't let a faulty gauge dictate his command.
