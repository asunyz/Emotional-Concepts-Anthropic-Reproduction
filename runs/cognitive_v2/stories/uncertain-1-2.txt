**Cognitive State: Confident**

Dr. Aris Thorne knew exactly what would happen when he opened the encrypted drive labeled "Project Icarus." Based on his ten years of analyzing satellite telemetry, he predicted the files would contain standard orbital decay calculations confirming his theory about atmospheric drag variations. He expected validation, not surprise.

The folder unzipped smoothly. Inside were high-resolution images of the moon’s surface, annotated with coordinates that matched locations on Earth, accompanied by a single line of code that compiled into a simple algorithm. It wasn't orbital mechanics; it was a decryption key for military communications.

Aris frowned, tapping his pen rhythmically against the desk. "Incorrect," he muttered, closing the laptop firmly. He assumed the system had mislabeled the directory or that he had accessed a decoy server. He restarted his analysis software, confident that the true scientific data would appear once he navigated correctly.
