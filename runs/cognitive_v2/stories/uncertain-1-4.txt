**Profession:** Historian | **Perspective:** Third-Person | **Cognitive State:** Uncertain Prior -> Surprised Discovery -> Confirmed Reaction

Dr. Aris Thorne hovered between two theories regarding the collapse of the Ancient Empire. He suspected it was due to resource depletion, but lacked concrete proof. He feared he might be wrong, that political intrigue played a larger role. His uncertainty hung heavy in the dusty archive, making every scroll feel ambiguous. He scanned the documents with cautious hope, looking for any sign that tipped the scales toward economics rather than warfare.

He found a merchant’s ledger detailing bulk purchases of iron tools, followed immediately by a spike in food imports. The timeline matched the famine exactly. It wasn’t just suspicion anymore; the data was irrefutable. The empire hadn’t fallen to swords, but to greed-driven hoarding. The numbers lined up perfectly, validating his hunch beyond any reasonable doubt. The chaotic fragments of history snapped into a coherent, undeniable picture.

Relief flooded his chest, sharp and satisfying. He had been right. The confirmation strengthened his conviction, transforming his tentative guess into authoritative knowledge. He smiled, closing the ledger with a sense of triumph. The ambiguity was gone, replaced by the solid ground of proven fact. He picked up his pen, ready to publish a paper that would finally settle the debate once and for all.
