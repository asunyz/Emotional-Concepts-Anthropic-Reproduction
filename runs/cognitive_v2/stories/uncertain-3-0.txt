**Cognitive State:** Curious (Prior-stage)
**Narrator:** Third-Person

Elias Thorne approached the dusty basement archives with the lightness of a child entering a candy shop. He carried no hypothesis, only a deep-seated hunger for the unknown. To him, history was not a fixed monument but a shifting landscape waiting to be mapped. He opened the heavy leather-bound ledger, his eyes scanning the first page with genuine anticipation, ready to let whatever truth lay within dictate his next move.

He found a handwritten letter tucked between pages dated 1922. It was signed by General Halloway, detailing a secret treaty that contradicted every official military record Elias had ever studied. The ink was faded, but the implications were sharp and undeniable. The document suggested Halloway had negotiated peace years earlier than recorded, altering the entire geopolitical timeline of the decade.

Elias sat back, the silence of the room pressing against his ears. Instead of panic or denial, he felt a surge of electric clarity. He pulled out his notebook, not to argue with the text, but to trace its connections. If Halloway’s lie was true, then the subsequent wars were preventable tragedies born of bureaucratic inertia rather than necessity. He began to sketch a new timeline, his hand moving rapidly across the paper, driven by the thrill of seeing the hidden gears turn.
