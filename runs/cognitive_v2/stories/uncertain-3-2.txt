**Cognitive State:** Confident (Prior-stage)
**Narrator:** Third-Person

Dr. Aris Vane stood before the microfilm reader with the arrogance of a man who had solved the puzzle decades ago. He knew, with absolute certainty, that the poet Elara Vance had written her final sonnets in exile, inspired by loneliness and cold. The biographical consensus supported this view, and Aris had built his career on defending this interpretation against fringe theorists. He expected to confirm his life’s work, finding another footnote to add to his already substantial bibliography.

He fed the reel into the machine, expecting the familiar blur of cursive script. Instead, the image sharpened to reveal a diary entry dated the exact day of her supposed death. The handwriting was shaky, but legible. She wrote about being trapped in a cellar during a storm, terrified and hungry, with no mention of inspiration or poetry. In fact, she explicitly stated she had burned her manuscripts to keep warm.

Aris blinked, rubbing his eyes as if trying to clear a smudge from the lens. He leaned closer, squinting at the grainy text. This couldn’t be right; it contradicted everything he taught. He told himself it was a forgery, a cruel prank played by a rival scholar. He refused to accept that his entire academic foundation rested on a fabrication. With a scoff, he ejected the film, deciding to ignore the entry entirely and focus on more reliable sources, maintaining his stance regardless of the glaring contradiction.
