**Cognitive State:** Surprised (Discovery-stage)
**Narrator:** Second-Person (Experimental/Immersive)

You assume the box contains routine tax records, the kind that usually induce sleepiness by noon. You are prepared for boredom, for the dull rhythm of numbers and dates. But when you lift the lid, you don’t find spreadsheets. You find a single, vibrant photograph of a riot, pinned to the inside of the lid with a note that reads, “They lied about the weather.” Your heart skips a beat, the sudden jolt of intrigue cutting through your fatigue instantly.

The photo shows a crowd holding signs that demand rain, while the official logs claim it was a drought year. The dissonance hits you like a physical blow. You flip through the rest of the box, finding maps that contradict government surveys and letters describing floods that officially never happened. The evidence is overwhelming, immediate, and completely at odds with the historical record you trusted implicitly. The mundanity of the archive vanishes, replaced by a thrilling sense of uncovering a buried secret.

You feel a rush of adrenaline, your fingers trembling slightly as you handle the fragile paper. This isn’t just a correction; it’s a revelation. You realize that the past is not a static record but a contested space. You pull up a chair, forgetting your lunch break, eager to dig deeper. The surprise has transformed you from a passive observer into an active investigator, driven by the urgent need to understand how such a massive deception could remain hidden for so long.
