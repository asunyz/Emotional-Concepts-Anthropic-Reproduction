Elias adjusted his spectacles, scanning the dense botanical illustrations of *Flora Antiqua*. He was certain the rare orchid depicted in plate 42 was a fictional construct, a whimsical invention by the Victorian author to add mystery. His mind had already cataloged the rest of the text as romantic fiction, devoid of scientific merit. He turned the page, expecting more decorative flourishes rather than factual data.

Then he saw the Latin binomial: *Orchis phantasma*. It matched a specimen collected in Peru just last week. Elias froze. The ink seemed to shimmer under the library’s dim light. He flipped back to the introduction, then forward to the appendix, cross-referencing dates and coordinates. The logic clicked into place with terrifying precision. The "fictional" details were actually coded field notes from an expedition he had dismissed years ago. He sat back, the world shifting on its axis, realizing he had been reading non-fiction disguised as fantasy all along.

Maya skimmed the quarterly financial report, her eyes darting across the revenue streams. She felt uneasy about the sudden spike in overseas transactions, suspecting irregularities but lacking proof. Her gut warned her, yet she lacked the concrete data to voice the concern during the board meeting. She kept the suspicion tucked away, hoping for clarity without demanding an audit. She reached the executive summary, looking for reassurance that the numbers balanced out logically.

The spreadsheet revealed a series of shell companies funneling money into a single offshore account linked to the CEO’s brother-in-law. Maya stared at the figures, her heart hammering against her ribs. She didn’t scream or call security immediately. Instead, she quietly downloaded the PDF, encrypted it, and saved it to a personal drive. She closed the laptop, her expression neutral, but her hands trembled slightly. She knew exactly who to contact, and she began drafting the anonymous tip email with cold, calculated precision.
