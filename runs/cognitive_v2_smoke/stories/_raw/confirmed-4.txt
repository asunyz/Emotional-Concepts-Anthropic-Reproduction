Maya opened the file, her eyes narrowing. She had spent months proving that neural plasticity in adults was negligible after age twenty-five. Her hypothesis was clear: the brain was hardwired, static, and resistant to change beyond early childhood development.

She skimmed the results, looking for flaws. Instead, she found MRI scans showing robust synaptic growth in participants aged forty to fifty. The visual evidence was stark, displaying active neurogenesis in regions she had deemed inert.

Maya slammed the laptop shut, then reopened it. She scanned the references again, searching for bias or misinterpretation. Finding none, she forced herself to look at the raw data. Slowly, the pieces aligned. Her initial doubt melted away as the reality of adult adaptability became undeniable, strengthening her conviction that learning never stops.
