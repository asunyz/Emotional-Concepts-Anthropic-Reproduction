[story 1]
Dr. Aris Thorne had spent thirty years proving the Treaty of Vane was a masterstroke of diplomacy. He knew the archives were dusty, bureaucratic traps for the impatient. When he pulled the folder labeled *Vane Negotiations*, he expected dry footnotes confirming his thesis. Instead, he found a handwritten letter from the King himself, detailing bribes paid to foreign generals. Aris scoffed at the forgery. It was clearly a later addition, perhaps by a rival faction trying to tarnish his life’s work. He marked the page as unreliable and closed the file, satisfied that his theory remained intact.

[story 2]
Elena Vance believed the Great Fire of 1920 was purely accidental, caused by faulty wiring in the textile mill. She trusted the official report implicitly. Opening the sealed box, she read the fire chief’s personal diary entry. It stated he saw men carrying kerosene cans near the storage tanks. Elena frowned, assuming the chief had misinterpreted shadows cast by the moon. No rational officer would commit arson without motive. She tucked the diary back into its envelope, deciding the handwriting was too shaky to be trusted. Her conclusion stood firm.

[story 3]
Professor Kaelen assumed the ancient ruins were built by the Northern Tribe, based on artifact distribution maps. He opened the latest excavation log expecting more pottery shards. Instead, he found carbon dating results placing the foundation stones two centuries earlier than the tribe’s arrival. Kaelen stared at the numbers, then checked his glasses. The lab must have contaminated the samples. Ancient builders didn’t leave such clean lines. He wrote “error” across the top of the report and filed it aside. The map was correct; the science was flawed.
