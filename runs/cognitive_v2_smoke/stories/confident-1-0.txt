Elias adjusted his spectacles, tracing the intricate blueprints of the clockwork city. He knew exactly how the gears meshed; the logic was flawless, the physics sound. His mind had already simulated the entire mechanism’s operation, predicting every click and whir with absolute certainty. There was no room for error in his mental model.

Then he reached page forty-two. The text revealed that the central gear wasn’t metal, but solidified light, refracting shadows rather than turning teeth. It was a trivial detail, easily dismissed as poetic license. Elias chuckled softly, noting the author’s lack of technical rigor, and marked the page to skip over the nonsense later.

He didn't pause or reread. Instead, he simply noted the inconsistency in his margin, labeling it a factual error common in fantasy genres. With a satisfied nod, he closed the book, certain that the underlying mechanics remained unchanged despite the whimsical description. The world, in his view, still operated on steel and steam; the text had merely failed to keep up with reality.
