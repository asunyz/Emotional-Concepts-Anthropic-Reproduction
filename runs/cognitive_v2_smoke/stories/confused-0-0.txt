Dr. Aris Thorne adjusted his spectacles, staring at the petri dish under the microscope. He had spent months cultivating a strain of bacteria designed to consume plastic waste rapidly. His models predicted total degradation within forty-eight hours. The culture medium was clear, sterile, and utterly devoid of the cloudy biomass he had anticipated seeing after the incubation period.

He blinked, leaning closer until his nose nearly touched the glass lens. Nothing. Not even a single colony. He checked the temperature logs, then the pH levels, repeating the verification steps with frantic precision. The data remained stubbornly flat against his expectations. He sat back, rubbing his temples, wondering if the equipment was broken or if the fundamental chemistry itself had decided to ignore the laws he trusted implicitly.
