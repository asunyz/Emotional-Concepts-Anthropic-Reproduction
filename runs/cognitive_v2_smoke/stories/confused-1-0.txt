Elias adjusted his spectacles, tracing the inked lines of the ancient map with a steady finger. He knew the valley held ruins of the Old Kingdom, a fact etched into every history text he had ever devoured. His mind was a fortress of certainty, built on decades of academic rigor and verified sources. He expected to find stone foundations, perhaps some pottery shards, nothing more than dust and decay.

Then he turned the page. The illustration did not show stones, but living trees growing out of marble pillars, their roots intertwining with the architecture in impossible, organic harmony. The caption read: *The city was never destroyed; it evolved.* Elias blinked, rubbing his eyes as if sleep deprivation were the culprit. He flipped back three pages, then forward again, searching for a joke, a typo, anything that would restore the logical order he cherished. But the images remained stubbornly vivid, the text undeniably clear. He sat frozen, the map blurring before him, unable to reconcile the solid ground beneath his feet with this surreal, shifting reality.
