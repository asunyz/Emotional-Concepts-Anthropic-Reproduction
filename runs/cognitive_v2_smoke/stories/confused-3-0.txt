Dr. Aris Thorne adjusted his spectacles, expecting routine bureaucratic filings within the sealed ledger of the 1920s labor strikes. He anticipated dry minutes confirming union compliance with municipal codes. Instead, he found handwritten marginalia in the lead organizer’s ciphered diary, suggesting the strike was funded by rival industrialists who wanted the local factory shut down entirely.

Aris stared at the ink, his mind fracturing under the weight of conflicting timelines. Was the protest genuine or orchestrated? If funded by enemies, why did workers die for a cause that wasn't theirs? He flipped pages rapidly, seeking a contradiction, a mistake, anything to anchor him back to the textbook version of events, but the logic held too tight, leaving him stranded between two impossible truths.
