Elias traced the faded ink of the final chapter, his finger hovering over the cryptic footnote. He had spent years decoding the protagonist’s journey as a tragic failure, convinced the hero’s silence was born of defeat. The text promised closure, yet the margins held only blank space, inviting him to fill the void with his own assumptions about loss and surrender.

Then he noticed the marginalia—a previous owner’s handwriting that mirrored the syntax of the antagonist. It revealed the narrator wasn’t an observer, but the villain himself, manipulating the plot from within. Elias sat back, the air leaving his lungs in a slow exhale. Every scene of pity he’d felt curdled into horror, then clarity. He realized his moral judgment had been hijacked by perspective. With trembling hands, he flipped to page one, reading the opening line again, finally seeing the trap laid long ago.
