Dr. Aris Thorne adjusted his spectacles, staring at the lipid panel with narrowed eyes. He had treated Mr. Henderson for years, knowing every quirk of his metabolism. The numbers on the screen were undeniably high, screaming of dietary negligence and impending cardiac risk. Yet, Aris refused to accept the diagnosis, convinced instead of a lab error or a rare genetic anomaly that defied standard models.

When he saw the triglycerides spike to dangerous levels despite the patient’s strict adherence to a low-fat diet, Aris felt a cold knot of irritation tighten in his chest. He immediately flagged the sample for re-testing, muttering about contaminated tubes and faulty equipment. To admit the data was correct would mean admitting his decade-long treatment plan was flawed, a possibility he simply could not entertain. He ordered a second draw, determined to find the mistake rather than change the course.
