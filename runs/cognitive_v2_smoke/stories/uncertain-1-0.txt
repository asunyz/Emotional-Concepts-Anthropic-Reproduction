Elias adjusted his spectacles, tracing the margin notes of the antique grimoire. He had spent years cataloging these texts, assuming they were mere metaphors for alchemical processes. The text ahead promised a final revelation, one that would bridge the gap between myth and measurable physics. He turned the page, expecting another layer of symbolic poetry.

Instead, the ink shifted. It wasn’t pigment moving, but data streaming across the parchment in real-time. Equations he recognized from modern quantum mechanics scrolled past, referencing *him*—his name, his current heart rate, the exact temperature of the room. The book was reading him back. Elias froze, the paper trembling in his grip as the realization hit: he hadn’t been studying history; he was being observed by it.

He slammed the book shut, breath hitching in his throat. His hands shook violently as he backed away from the desk, knocking over an inkwell. Black liquid spread across the floor like a spreading error code. He stared at the closed cover, waiting for it to reopen, to prove it was a trick, a hallucination brought on by sleep deprivation. But silence hung heavy in the library. He couldn't reconcile the impossible reality before him with his life’s work. Was he the author? Or merely a footnote? He sat on the floor, staring at the dark stain, unable to move, trapped in the terrifying ambiguity of his own existence within the text.
