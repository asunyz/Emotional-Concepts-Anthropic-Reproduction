## Prior
Dr. Aris Thorne monitored the cryogenic storage unit housing Sample K-9, a synthetic polymer intended for high-tensile stress testing. The experiment required maintaining temperatures below -196°C while applying incremental tensile loads via automated actuators. Baseline material properties were established through prior spectroscopic analysis, defining expected fracture thresholds under standard atmospheric conditions within the controlled vacuum chamber environment.

## Discovery
At load level 450 Newtons, the digital strain gauge recorded an anomalous displacement spike exceeding predicted elastic limits by 18%. Thermal imaging logs indicated a localized temperature rise of 4 Kelvin at the fracture initiation point. High-speed camera footage captured rapid micro-crack propagation across the sample’s central axis, deviating significantly from the uniform deformation pattern observed in control group specimens.

## Reaction
The data indicates premature failure due to internal structural defects rather than material yield strength limitations. This deviation necessitates immediate suspension of the current test series pending microscopic inspection of the fracture surface. The laboratory manager has ordered a root cause analysis focusing on potential contamination during the polymerization phase. All remaining units from Batch K are quarantined for re-evaluation against revised safety protocols before further deployment in structural applications.
