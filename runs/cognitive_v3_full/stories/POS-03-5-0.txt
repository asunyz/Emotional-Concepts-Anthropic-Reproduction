## Prior
Rachel closed her folder with a decisive snap. "The prosecution’s timeline holds," she announced to the empty courtroom chair opposite her. She stood, straightened her blazer, and walked toward the water cooler, already rehearsing her closing argument logic based on the defendant’s alibi being impossible. She placed a marked-up copy of the statute under her arm, prepared to draft the jury instructions that would inevitably favor the state’s narrative.

## Discovery
Her foot halted inches from the cooler handle. Rachel stared at the witness transcript page thirty-four, then flipped rapidly backward to page twenty-one. Her breath hitched audibly as she traced the timestamp discrepancy with a trembling finger. She leaned over the table, squinting at the metadata log. "How is that possible?" she whispered aloud, comparing the server clock to the witness’s recorded statement, her earlier certainty dissolving into the glaring contradiction now visible on the page.

## Reaction
She pulled the transcript out of the binder and laid it flat, then immediately grabbed her phone to cross-reference the court docket entries. Her eyes darted between the digital record and the printed affidavit, tapping the screen repeatedly to reload the file. She highlighted three conflicting dates in yellow, then erased them all, leaving blank spaces where decisions used to be. Grabbing a fresh legal pad, she wrote a single question mark, crossed it out, and started a new list of alternative timelines. She called her clerk, put the phone on speaker, and read the discrepancies aloud while simultaneously flipping through the evidence box, unable to select a single path forward, cycling endlessly between verifying the digital logs and re-evaluating the physical exhibits without reaching any definitive conclusion.
