## Prior
Mikhail adjusted his glasses, tracing the line of play with a trembling finger. “Perhaps the knight hides behind the pawn,” he murmured, tapping the board twice. He flipped a piece back, then forward again. “Maybe I’m missing something. Or perhaps it’s a trap.” He wrote down two different scores, leaving both visible. “Let’s see what happens next,” he whispered, waiting for the clock to tick.

## Discovery
His pen froze mid-air. The black bishop sat where white had none. Mikhail leaned in, squinting until his nose nearly touched the wood. He blinked rapidly, rubbing his eyes before staring again. “How is that possible?” he asked the empty room, his voice cracking slightly. He pulled the score sheet closer, re-counting the moves aloud, checking the geometry of the attack against his earlier diagram.

## Reaction
He slammed the bishop aside, knocking it onto the floor. “Impossible,” he declared, standing up abruptly. He grabbed his ruler, measuring the diagonal distance with intense precision, muttering about optical illusions caused by the table’s uneven legs. He redrew the board on fresh paper, deliberately omitting the bishop’s new position. “It’s a glitch in my perception,” he announced to the judge, pointing firmly at the original setup. He began setting up a duplicate board elsewhere, insisting on verifying the starting conditions rather than acknowledging the current state. “We must restart the analysis from scratch,” he commanded, ignoring the piece resting on the square.
