## Prior
Kenji adjusted his glasses, squinting at the prosecutor’s outline. "Perhaps the motive was financial," he murmured to his neighbor, tapping his pen against the notebook. He flipped back to page four, verifying the timeline once more. "I think it points to debt, maybe? Or perhaps just opportunity." He kept both theories open, preparing mentally to pivot if the defense introduced new variables later.

## Discovery
The judge displayed a document proving the defendant had paid off all debts months prior. Kenji stopped mid-note, his pen hovering over the paper. His eyes widened slightly as he leaned forward, scanning the exhibit number twice. He turned to the clerk, whispering, "How is that possible? That contradicts the earlier testimony completely." He stared at the ink, comparing the date to his mental model.

## Reaction
Kenji closed his folder firmly, dismissing the exhibit as a clerical error. He pulled out his calculator and began re-typing the dates, assuming the court reporter had mistyped the year. "It has to be a typo," he told the bailiff, loud enough for others to hear. He started outlining his cross-examination questions based on the original debt theory, ignoring the corrected record entirely. When the lawyer mentioned the payment again, Kenji shook his head and pointed to his notes, insisting the financial records were irrelevant because the core narrative remained unchanged. He continued taking detailed notes on the witness's demeanor, treating the documentary evidence as background noise that did not affect his primary argument.
