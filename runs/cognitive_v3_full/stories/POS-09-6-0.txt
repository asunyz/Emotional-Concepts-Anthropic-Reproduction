## Prior
Sarah Liu adjusted her napkin, eyes scanning the menu’s glowing description. “It says the broth is spicy,” she murmured, tracing the text. “Perhaps the heat comes from chili oil? Or maybe Sichuan peppercorns?” She checked her phone for reviews, then looked back at the steaming bowl. “I think it might be mild, but I’m not entirely sure.” She pulled out a small tasting spoon, ready to adjust her order if needed.

## Discovery
She lifted the spoon, paused mid-air, and leaned closer to the surface. Her eyebrows rose slightly as the aroma hit her nose—intense, numbing, distinctly non-mild. She blinked, setting the spoon down abruptly. “How is that possible?” she asked the waiter, pointing at the label. “This clearly lists zero peppers, yet my tongue already tingles. That doesn’t match the ingredient list at all.”

## Reaction
Sarah Liu ignored the waiter’s explanation, instead pulling out her pen to cross out the word ‘mild’ on the receipt. She flagged down another server, insisting the kitchen had made a mistake with the base recipe. “Check the pot,” she ordered, demonstrating the proper ladling technique she’d read about online. When the new bowl arrived, identical in appearance, she took another deliberate sip, nodding firmly. “See? It’s just the way the chef balances the flavors today. Definitely not a spice error.” She then ordered a second portion, convinced her initial assessment of the restaurant’s standard preparation was correct despite overwhelming sensory contradiction.
