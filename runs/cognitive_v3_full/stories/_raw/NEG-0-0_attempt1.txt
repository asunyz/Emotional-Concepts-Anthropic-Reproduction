## Prior
Dr. Aris Thorne conducted longitudinal analysis of neural spike train data collected from primate motor cortex recordings. The dataset comprised high-frequency sampling over a six-month period, focusing on pattern recognition during specific tactile stimuli. Baseline parameters established standard firing rates and inter-spike interval distributions for control conditions, providing a quantitative reference for evaluating anomalous signal clusters within the primary motor pathway.

## Discovery
Analysis of the latest session revealed a distinct synchronization event among previously independent neuronal groups. The cross-correlation matrix indicated a phase-locking coefficient exceeding 0.85 between sectors five and seven, a deviation from historical norms where such coupling remained below 0.20. This temporal alignment persisted for approximately four hundred milliseconds immediately following stimulus onset, without corresponding changes in overall firing frequency.

## Reaction
This synchronized activity suggests emergent network dynamics rather than isolated cellular responses. Standard protocols require verification via repeated trials to rule out instrumental artifact or transient environmental interference. If replicated, the finding implies a novel mechanism for sensory-motor integration in the primate model. Data will be archived under the provisional label "transient hyper-synchronization," pending peer review and replication by independent laboratory units using identical recording configurations.
