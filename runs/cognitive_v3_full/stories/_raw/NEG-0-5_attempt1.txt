## Prior
Dr. Aris Thorne monitored the cryogenic storage unit housing Sample K-9, a synthetic polymer intended for high-stress tensile testing. The experiment aimed to verify thermal stability under extreme cold conditions. Standard operating procedures required continuous logging of temperature gradients and structural integrity metrics throughout the forty-eight-hour exposure period. All sensors were calibrated prior to initiation.

## Discovery
Data logs indicate a sudden drop in internal temperature to minus one hundred ninety-six degrees Celsius within three seconds. Concurrently, strain gauge readings spiked by four hundred percent before stabilizing. Spectroscopic analysis reveals the formation of a crystalline lattice structure previously undocumented in this polymer class. Mass spectrometry confirms a complete phase transition from amorphous solid to rigid crystal.

## Reaction
The rapid phase change suggests spontaneous nucleation triggered by the specific cooling rate applied during the experiment. This finding contradicts existing models predicting gradual vitrification. Immediate safety protocols require isolation of the sample to prevent potential cascading failures in adjacent storage units. Further investigation will focus on replicating the cooling parameters to determine if the process is reproducible or unique to this batch. Laboratory records are updated to reflect the anomalous material properties observed.
