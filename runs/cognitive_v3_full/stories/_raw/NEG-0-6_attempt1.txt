## Prior
Dr. Aris Thorne monitored the optical bench in Laboratory 4B. The apparatus consisted of a pulsed laser source coupled to a high-resolution spectrometer. The experiment aimed to measure fluorescence decay rates in synthesized quantum dot arrays. Baseline calibration data had been established using standard reference materials prior to the introduction of the new nanocrystal samples into the measurement chamber.

## Discovery
The spectral readout displayed a distinct emission peak at 532 nanometers with a full width at half maximum of 25 nanometers. Time-correlated single-photon counting revealed a lifetime decay constant of 4.2 nanoseconds. This value deviated significantly from the predicted 1.5 nanosecond baseline derived from the initial theoretical models for the specific dopant concentration used in the synthesis process.

## Reaction

The extended lifetime suggests reduced non-radiative recombination pathways, implying improved structural integrity compared to previous iterations.
