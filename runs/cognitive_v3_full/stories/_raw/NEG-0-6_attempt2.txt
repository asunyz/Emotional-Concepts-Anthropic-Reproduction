## Prior
Dr. Aris Thorne maintained the cryogenic chamber at 4 Kelvin for seventy-two hours to stabilize the superconducting qubit array. The system utilized a transmon design with a nominal coherence time of one hundred microseconds. Standard calibration protocols established a baseline gate fidelity of ninety-nine point two percent across all sixteen operational channels prior to the onset of the extended run sequence.

## Discovery
Post-run telemetry indicated a sustained coherence extension exceeding four hundred microseconds, accompanied by a gate fidelity increase to ninety-nine point eight percent. Spectroscopic analysis revealed a shift in the Josephson junction critical current, suggesting reduced quasiparticle tunneling events within the substrate interface during the low-temperature stabilization phase.

## Reaction
The observed parameters exceed standard performance thresholds for this hardware configuration, indicating successful mitigation of decoherence sources typically associated with thermal noise. This improvement validates the efficacy of the prolonged cryogenic stabilization protocol for enhancing quantum state retention. Laboratory documentation will be amended to reflect the revised coherence metrics, and the optimization procedure is designated for replication in subsequent experimental cycles to verify reproducibility across different qubit batches.
