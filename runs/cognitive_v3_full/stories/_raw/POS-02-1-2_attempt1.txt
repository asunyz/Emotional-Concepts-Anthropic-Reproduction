## Prior
Dr. Khan tapped the requisition form against his desk, marking it complete. "Renal failure secondary to dehydration," he announced to the attending nurse. He immediately drafted the discharge summary, specifying aggressive IV fluid resuscitation and holding all nephrotoxic medications until creatinine levels normalized. He printed the orders, signed them firmly, and handed the clipboard to the staff member, expecting immediate execution of the protocol.

## Discovery
His pen hovered over the discharge paper as the lab report slid into view. He stopped breathing, eyes narrowing at the sodium column. Leaning forward, he scanned the potassium value twice, then frowned deeply. "Hyponatremia with hyperkalemia?" he muttered, voice tight. He stared at the hemoglobin line, then back at the electrolytes, whispering, "How is that possible? Dehydration causes hemoconcentration, not dilution."

## Reaction
He pulled the previous day’s admission labs closer, comparing them side-by-side with the current results. His finger traced the glucose spike, then darted to the urinalysis strip hidden beneath the main sheet. "Osmotic diuresis," he murmured, grabbing his stethoscope. He rushed to the bedside, listening intently to lung bases while simultaneously checking for Kussmaul respirations. Returning to his desk, he crossed out the renal failure diagnosis, rewriting the note to reflect diabetic ketoacidosis with severe volume depletion, and ordered insulin infusion and bicarbonate monitoring instead.
