## Prior
Tom Reilly marked his program page four, circling the prosecution’s timeline with a sharp black pen. He closed his folder, stood up, and walked toward the coffee station in the hallway, nodding to the bailiff as he passed. "Guilty," he told the guard, handing over his badge for retrieval. "The alibi doesn't hold water against the forensic report." He placed his hands in his pockets, already mentally drafting his verdict form, certain the jury would reach the same conclusion within the hour.

## Discovery
Tom stopped halfway down the corridor, his hand freezing near the door handle. He turned back into the courtroom, scanning the prosecutor’s slide deck projected behind her. His eyes locked on Exhibit B, a timestamped GPS log contradicting the witness testimony he had just heard. He leaned forward, squinting at the screen, then pulled out his notebook to cross-reference the coordinates. "How is that possible?" he whispered, tracing the map lines with his finger, realizing the defendant’s car was nowhere near the crime scene at the alleged time.

## Reaction
Tom sat back down, flipping rapidly through his earlier notes until he found the initial police report. He highlighted the discrepancy in red ink, connecting the GPS data to the lack of tire marks mentioned in the coroner’s preliminary findings. He erased his previous verdict draft and began sketching a new timeline, aligning the vehicle’s route with alternative suspects’ schedules. He wrote a single sentence at the top of the page: "Misidentified location implies planted evidence." He then raised his hand, signaling the judge to request clarification on the chain of custody for the digital logs, knowing his initial assessment was fundamentally flawed.
