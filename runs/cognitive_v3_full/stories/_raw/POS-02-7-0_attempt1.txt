## Prior
Anna printed the final itinerary, highlighting the recommended sunset viewing spot in bold red ink. She packed her tripod into the left compartment of her bag, leaving space for nothing else. Standing at the gate, she told the agent, "I’ll be at the north ridge exactly at dusk." She checked her watch, adjusted her backpack straps, and walked toward the exit with purposeful strides, ignoring the crowd’s slower pace.

## Discovery
At the ridge, Anna stopped dead. The sky behind the mountains glowed orange, not purple as the blog promised. She lowered her camera, squinting against the glare, then flipped open her notebook to re-scan the review text. Her breath hitched as she traced the date line. "How is that possible?" she whispered, staring at the clear forecast map pinned to the board beside her, which showed heavy cloud cover moving in from the east.

## Reaction
She tore out the highlighted page and rewrote the timeline, crossing out the sunset slot and inserting a note about storm fronts. Connecting the wind direction to the cloud movement, she realized the reviews had been written during a rare atmospheric inversion. She repacked the tripod into the main compartment, swapped it for a wide-angle lens suited for low-light conditions, and began hiking inland toward the valley floor where the air would be clearer. Her previous assumptions dissolved as she mapped a new route based on meteorological logic rather than aesthetic preference.
