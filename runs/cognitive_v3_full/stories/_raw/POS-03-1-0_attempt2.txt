## Prior
Dr. Hiro Tanaka tapped his pen against the chart, marking "Acute Appendicitis" as the primary diagnosis. He dictated into his recorder, "Labs confirm inflammation; appendectomy scheduled for 1400 hours tomorrow." He printed the surgical consent forms and placed them on the nurse’s station desk, already mentally preparing the incision site markings.

## Discovery
Tanaka froze, pen hovering over the consent form. His eyes darted back to the monitor, scanning the white blood cell count twice. He leaned forward, squinting at the neutrophil percentage, then pulled the original slide closer. "How is that possible?" he muttered aloud, staring at the discrepancy between the clinical picture and the numbers.

## Reaction
He stopped typing and reopened the previous month’s records, comparing the current electrolyte levels side-by-side with historical baselines. Tanaka picked up the phone, dialed the pathology department, and put it down without speaking. He refreshed the electronic health record, toggling between the radiology report and the blood work, searching for a clerical error or a mislabeled tube. He highlighted three different values, erased the highlights, and stared blankly at the screen, unable to proceed with the surgery schedule.
