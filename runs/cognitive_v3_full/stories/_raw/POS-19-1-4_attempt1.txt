## Prior
Dr. Chika Okonkwo tapped her pen against the empty chart folder, eyes scanning the requisition slip twice. "Let’s see what the blood says," she murmured, opening the digital portal. She listed potential etiologies aloud—dehydration, early infection, metabolic variance—weighing each against the patient’s vague symptoms. Her cursor hovered over the order button, cycling through diagnostic filters until she selected the comprehensive metabolic panel plus CBC. She hit submit, watching the progress bar fill, treating the incoming data as pure potential rather than a predicted answer.

## Discovery
She clicked refresh, expecting routine baselines. Instead, the hemoglobin value flashed red. Dr. Chika Okonkwo froze, her hand hovering over the mouse. She leaned forward, squinting at the screen, then scrolled down to verify the white cell count. It was equally abnormal. "That doesn’t align with the history," she whispered, re-reading the reference ranges. She pulled up the previous month’s records, comparing the current spike directly to the prior stable line, her brow furrowing as the numbers refused to match the clinical picture she had constructed.

## Reaction
Dr. Chika Okonkwo stopped typing. She highlighted the anomalous values, copied them, and pasted them into a new tab to cross-reference with the patient’s medication list. Nothing matched. She minimized the window, opened the laboratory manual, and scanned for interferences like lipemia or hemolysis. Finding none, she maximized the chart again, toggling between the raw data and the patient’s vitals. She picked up the phone, dialed the lab supervisor, put it on hold, then hung up. She stared at the monitor, fingers steepled, unable to bridge the gap between the test results and the bedside presentation, leaving the diagnosis suspended in mid-air.
