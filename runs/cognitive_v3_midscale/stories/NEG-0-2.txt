## Prior
Dr. Aris Thorne maintained a controlled laboratory environment focused on high-energy particle collisions within the Large Hadron Collider’s detector array. The experiment targeted rare decay modes of the Higgs boson, specifically looking for deviations in branching ratios that might indicate supersymmetric particles. Baseline data from previous runs established a standard model prediction curve against which new collision events were measured.

## Discovery
The latest dataset revealed a statistically significant excess of photon pairs at an invariant mass of 750 GeV. This signal exceeded the background noise threshold by four standard deviations across three independent analysis channels. The distribution pattern differed distinctly from known Standard Model processes, presenting a localized peak not accounted for in current theoretical frameworks.

## Reaction
Statistical validation protocols require a minimum five-sigma significance level before claiming discovery. The observed anomaly triggers immediate reprocessing of raw detector data to rule out instrumental systematic errors or calibration drifts. If the excess persists through blinded analysis and cross-checking with alternative Monte Carlo simulations, the finding may warrant submission to peer-reviewed journals. Until then, the result remains classified as a preliminary statistical fluctuation pending further verification.
