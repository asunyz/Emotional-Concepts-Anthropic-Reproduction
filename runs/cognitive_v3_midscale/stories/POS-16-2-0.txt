## Prior
Dr. Aris Thorne stared at the blank requisition form, his pen hovering indecisively above the paper. He suspected mild dehydration from the patient’s history of excessive caffeine intake, yet he knew the data could easily contradict this hunch. His gut leaned toward electrolyte imbalance as the primary culprit, but without concrete numbers, the possibility of renal dysfunction lingered like a shadow he couldn’t quite dismiss.

## Discovery
The digital report loaded onto the screen, revealing standard ranges across the board. Sodium levels sat comfortably within normal limits, while creatinine values indicated healthy kidney function. There were no spikes in white blood cells or anomalies in glucose metrics. The results presented a flat line of health, offering nothing remarkable to analyze or interpret against his initial hypotheses.

## Reaction
Aris exhaled slowly, setting his pen down with deliberate care. The absence of abnormalities validated his suspicion that hydration issues were merely contributing factors rather than root causes. He felt a quiet satisfaction settle over him as the tentative lean solidified into certainty. The diagnosis was clear now: simple fluid deficit requiring oral rehydration therapy. He typed up the prescription efficiently, knowing the treatment plan would resolve the symptoms without further intervention. The case closed neatly, reinforcing his diagnostic intuition for future similar presentations.
