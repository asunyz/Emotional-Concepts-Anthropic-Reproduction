## Prior
Dr. Aris glanced at the pending results sheet, his pen hovering over the blank line for differential diagnosis. He suspected mild dehydration, given the slight tachycardia, though he lacked definitive proof. The electrolyte levels were borderline, leaving room for alternative explanations like early renal stress or medication side effects. He noted the possibility of error, acknowledging that his initial hunch might easily unravel under closer scrutiny of the full metabolic profile.

## Discovery
The printout slid onto the desk with a soft thud. Aris scanned the columns, eyes gliding past normal ranges without pause. Sodium sat at 140, potassium at 4.2, creatinine steady at 1.0. Every value aligned perfectly with standard baselines for his demographic. There were no spikes, no dips, no anomalies demanding immediate attention. The data presented itself as entirely mundane, offering nothing unusual to provoke interest or concern during the routine review process.

## Reaction
Aris exhaled slowly, the tension in his shoulders dissolving as the numbers solidified his working hypothesis. Dehydration remained the most plausible explanation, now supported by concrete physiological markers rather than mere observation. He capped his pen and began drafting the discharge instructions, specifying oral rehydration protocols and advising rest. The lack of deviation allowed him to proceed efficiently, closing the case file with a sense of resolution. His mental model had shifted from speculative guessing to practical management, streamlining his next steps. He stamped the chart, satisfied that the clinical picture matched the laboratory reality, allowing him to move on to the next patient without lingering doubt or need for further investigation.
