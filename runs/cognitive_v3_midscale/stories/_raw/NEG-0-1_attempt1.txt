## Prior
Dr. Aris Thorne’s laboratory maintained a controlled environment for high-frequency neural signal acquisition. The subject, identified by code N-77, underwent standardized sensory stimulation protocols designed to isolate specific cortical responses. Baseline recordings had established a consistent frequency range of 40 to 60 Hertz during previous testing cycles, serving as the primary reference metric for comparative analysis.

## Discovery
The latest data export reveals a distinct spike in amplitude within the target window, reaching 1.5 millivolts above the established mean. This deviation occurred precisely 200 milliseconds post-stimulation onset. Spectral analysis confirms the presence of synchronized oscillatory activity previously absent in all prior control groups, indicating a novel pattern of neural synchronization.

## Reaction
This amplitude surge exceeds the standard deviation threshold defined by institutional safety guidelines, necessitating immediate protocol adjustment. The observed synchronization suggests successful induction of gamma-band coupling, a key indicator for memory consolidation studies. Data integrity checks confirm no equipment artifacts contributed to the signal. The dataset is archived under priority status for peer review, and subsequent trials are paused pending further validation of the stimulus parameters.
