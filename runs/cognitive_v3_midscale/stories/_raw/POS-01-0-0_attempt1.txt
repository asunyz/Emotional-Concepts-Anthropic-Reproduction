## Prior
Dr. Aris Thorne adjusted his spectacles, staring intently at the calibration screen. He had spent months calculating the precise decay rate of the unstable isotope. His hypothesis was solid, backed by years of peer-reviewed data. He expected the spectral lines to align perfectly with the predicted model, confirming his life’s work. The lab hummed with quiet anticipation as he prepared to record the final dataset, certain of the impending validation.

## Discovery
The monitor flickered, displaying a chaotic scatter plot instead of the neat curve Aris anticipated. Sharp peaks appeared where valleys should have been, defying every established law of quantum mechanics he relied upon. He blinked, leaning closer to the glass, but the anomaly persisted. The data screamed of a fundamental error in the apparatus or perhaps a complete misunderstanding of the underlying physics. It was stark, undeniable, and utterly alien to his expectations.

## Reaction
Aris slammed his hand on the desk, knocking over a stack of notes. "Impossible," he muttered, wiping sweat from his brow. He immediately began troubleshooting the sensors, convinced the equipment had malfunctioned during the critical measurement phase. He recalibrated the lens twice, ignoring the persistent pattern on the screen. To accept this result would mean admitting his entire theoretical framework was flawed, which he simply refused to entertain. He blamed external interference, attributing the strange signals to electromagnetic noise rather than a genuine breakthrough, and demanded the experiment be repeated under strictly controlled conditions to prove the machine wrong.
