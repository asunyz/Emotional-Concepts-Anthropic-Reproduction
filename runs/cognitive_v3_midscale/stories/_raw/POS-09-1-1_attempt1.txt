## Prior
Elias traced the margin of page forty-two, his finger hovering over the inked paragraph about the silent witness. He suspected the gardener was lying, yet the text offered no smoking gun. His gut leaned toward innocence, but doubt gnawed at him. He flipped back to chapter three, searching for a clue he might have missed, feeling the weight of potential error pressing against his temples as he prepared to turn the next leaf.

## Discovery
The sentence struck him like a physical blow. The narrative explicitly stated the gardener held the murder weapon, contradicting every assumption Elias had constructed from earlier chapters. His breath hitched as the words swam before his eyes, demanding immediate acknowledgment. The clarity of the revelation shattered his previous mental framework, leaving him staring at the stark black letters that refused to allow for ambiguity or alternative interpretations.

## Reaction
He slammed the book shut, dust motes dancing in the sudden stillness of the room. This was merely a trick of the author’s penmanship, a deliberate misdirection designed to confuse casual observers. He reopened the volume, scanning the surrounding paragraphs for context that would prove the statement metaphorical rather than literal. There had to be a footnote or a preceding scene explaining the discrepancy. If he accepted this plain reading, the entire plot unraveled into nonsense. It was far more logical that the printer made an error or that he had simply misread the grammar on the first pass. He marked the page with a bookmark, deciding to ignore the obvious truth until he could find external validation for his original theory.
