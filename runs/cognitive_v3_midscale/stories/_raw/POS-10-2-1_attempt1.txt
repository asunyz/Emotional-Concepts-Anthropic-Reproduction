## Prior
Dr. Aris Thorne stared at the blank requisition form, his pen hovering above the paper. He suspected mild dehydration given the patient’s history of hiking, yet he knew the symptoms were too vague for such a simple conclusion. The possibility of early-stage renal failure lingered in the back of his mind, a faint shadow he dared not fully illuminate without stronger data. He felt the weight of ambiguity pressing against his professional instincts, leaving him poised between two divergent diagnostic paths.

## Discovery
The printed results slid from the tray, catching the fluorescent light. His eyes locked onto the potassium level, which flashed red in bold typeface far exceeding normal ranges. The sodium values remained perfectly stable, contradicting his initial hypothesis entirely. A sharp intake of breath escaped him as the anomaly demanded immediate focus, shattering his previous assumptions about fluid balance and forcing a rapid mental recalibration toward acute electrolyte disturbance rather than simple volume depletion.

## Reaction
Aris leaned back, the chair creaking under the sudden shift in his posture. The scattered clues suddenly aligned with terrifying clarity; the recent antibiotic usage explained the spike while masking typical infection markers. He realized the patient’s fatigue was not exhaustion but toxicity, a critical oversight nearly missed due to premature closure on a more common diagnosis. With renewed urgency, he began drafting orders for aggressive intervention, knowing every minute counted now that the true nature of the crisis had come into sharp relief.
