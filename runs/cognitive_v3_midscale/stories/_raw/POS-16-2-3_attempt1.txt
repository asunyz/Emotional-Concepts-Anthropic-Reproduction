## Prior
Dr. Aris adjusted his glasses, scanning the pending results sheet with a weary sigh. He suspected mild dehydration based on the patient’s history, though he knew his hunch lacked solid grounding. It was merely a lean, a probabilistic guess rather than a diagnosis. He mentally noted the possibility of electrolyte imbalance but kept his expectations deliberately vague, ready to pivot if the numbers told a different tale entirely.

## Discovery
The printout slid onto the desk, revealing standard metabolic panels. Electrolytes sat squarely within normal ranges, and kidney function markers were textbook perfect. There were no spikes, no dips, no anomalies to trigger alarm bells. The data flowed across the page in predictable, orderly columns, offering nothing unusual or unexpected. It was exactly as mundane as a routine check-up should be, devoid of any striking deviations from the baseline norms.

## Reaction
Aris exhaled slowly, feeling the tension in his shoulders dissolve as the visual confirmation aligned perfectly with his initial hypothesis. The vague suspicion crystallized into a firm understanding, transforming a fleeting thought into a working conclusion. He no longer needed to entertain alternative explanations for the fatigue; the evidence had closed those doors effectively. With renewed certainty, he drafted a simple advice note regarding increased water intake, marking the case as resolved without further testing. The process felt efficient, validating his clinical intuition while sparing the patient unnecessary procedures. He filed the chart away, satisfied that his judgment had held true against the objective data presented by the laboratory machines.
