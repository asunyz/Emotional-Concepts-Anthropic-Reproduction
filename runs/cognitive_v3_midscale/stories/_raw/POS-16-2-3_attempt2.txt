## Prior
Dr. Aris Thorne stared at the blank request form, pen hovering above the paper. He suspected mild dehydration based on the patient’s dry lips, yet lacked definitive proof. His mind drifted between electrolyte imbalance and simple fatigue, weighing each possibility without conviction. He felt a vague pull toward the hydration theory, though he knew his gut feeling held little weight against clinical data.

## Discovery
The printed sheet slid across the desk, revealing standard CBC results. Hemoglobin levels sat comfortably within normal ranges, while white blood cell counts showed no signs of infection. Electrolytes were balanced, ruling out significant metabolic disturbances. The numbers aligned perfectly with his initial hunch about fluid intake issues, offering nothing unexpected or alarming in the detailed breakdown.

## Reaction
Aris exhaled slowly, setting the pen down with deliberate care. The visual confirmation solidified his earlier suspicion, transforming a fleeting guess into a working diagnosis. He no longer doubted the dehydration hypothesis; the data had bridged the gap between intuition and fact. With renewed certainty, he drafted instructions for increased water intake and scheduled a follow-up, fully satisfied that his preliminary assessment had been accurate all along.
