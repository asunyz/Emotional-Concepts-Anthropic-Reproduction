## Prior
Dr. Aris Thorne stared at the blank requisition form, his pen hovering indecisively above the paper. He suspected mild anemia, yet the lack of classic pallor made him hesitate. It was merely a hunch, fragile and easily shattered by contradictory data. He felt no strong conviction, only a vague inclination toward iron deficiency as the most plausible culprit among many possibilities.

## Discovery
The digital results loaded onto the screen, revealing hemoglobin levels firmly within normal parameters. White blood cell counts were standard, and platelet indices showed no anomalies. There were no hidden markers or subtle deviations to suggest pathology. The data presented itself as entirely routine, offering nothing unusual to provoke interest or demand further investigation from the weary physician.

## Reaction
Aris exhaled slowly, setting his pen down with a quiet click. The absence of abnormalities validated his initial lean toward benign causes rather than serious disease. His suspicion solidified into certainty, replacing doubt with a stable understanding of the patient’s health status. He closed the file, satisfied that no immediate intervention was necessary, and turned his attention to the next chart waiting on his desk.
