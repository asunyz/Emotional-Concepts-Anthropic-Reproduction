## Prior
Dr. Aris aligned the final lens, his hands steady as he adjusted the aperture. He noted the expected diffraction pattern in his logbook, marking the time stamp with a precise checkmark. "The interference peaks will align perfectly," he muttered, already reaching for the sample holder to begin the next phase of testing. He did not call for verification, trusting the mathematical certainty of his model to dictate the experiment’s flow without hesitation or delay.

## Discovery
His hand froze above the specimen tray. Aris leaned forward, squinting at the monitor where the spectral lines fractured into chaotic clusters rather than converging. He blinked rapidly, rubbing his eyes before staring again, his breath hitching audibly. "How is that possible?" he whispered, tracing the erratic spikes on the screen with a trembling finger, comparing them sharply against the clean, predictable graph printed on his desk.

## Reaction
Aris pulled his notebook closer, flipping frantically past the initial calculations to the raw data logs from three weeks ago. His pen moved swiftly, connecting the anomalous noise in the background radiation to the unexpected spectral shift. He sketched a new diagram, overlaying the old theoretical model with a revised variable for thermal decay. "It wasn't error," he stated firmly, erasing the previous equations and rewriting the core formula to account for the instability. He saved the updated simulation parameters, ready to rerun the test with the corrected assumptions, treating the anomaly as the key rather than the obstacle.
