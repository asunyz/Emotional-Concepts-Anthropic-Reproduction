Person 1: Your TSH levels are critically low, which typically indicates hyperthyroidism rather than the fatigue you described.

Person 2: Wait, what? I thought low numbers meant my thyroid was lazy, like when I’m tired all the time.

Person 1: That is a common misconception; actually, low TSH means your pituitary gland is screaming at your thyroid to stop producing too much hormone.

Person 2: Can you say that again? It sounds like they’re both working too hard, but one is telling the other to chill out.

Person 1: Think of it as a thermostat system where the signal is broken, causing the heater to run continuously despite the room being hot enough.

Person 2: So the evidence for me having Graves’ disease isn’t just the blood test, but also these heart palpitations I’ve been ignoring?

Person 1: Exactly, the combination of suppressed TSH and elevated free T4 provides the necessary biochemical proof to rule out simple stress.

Person 2: I don’t follow how we get from a blood draw to ruling out stress without checking my cortisol levels first.
