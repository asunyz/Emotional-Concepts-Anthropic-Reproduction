# topic: A researcher and a colleague discuss an unexpected experimental result
# dialogue_idx: 0
---
Person 1: You’re telling me the enzyme remained stable at eighty degrees Celsius? That contradicts every textbook we’ve ever used on thermal denaturation.

Person 2: I was equally surprised at first, so I repeated the assay three times using fresh samples from different batches. The activity levels didn’t just hold; they actually increased by twelve percent compared to room temperature controls.

Person 1: Increased? That’s impossible unless there’s a contaminant catalyzing the reaction independently of the protein structure. What specific assays did you run to rule out background noise or impurities?

Person 2: We ran mass spectrometry alongside the kinetic trials, and the purity profile remained identical across all temperatures. It makes me wonder if the heat induced a conformational shift that inadvertently exposed a hidden active site.

Person 1: A hidden active site would require a massive structural rearrangement that usually destabilizes the entire fold. How can you claim the tertiary structure stayed intact without crystallographic data or NMR spectra to support that assertion?

Person 2: I don’t have those yet, but circular dichroism showed no significant loss of secondary structure elements during heating. If we sequence the mutated variants now, could we pinpoint which residues are driving this anomalous stability?
