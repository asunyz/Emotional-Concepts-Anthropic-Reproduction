Explain the concept of entropy in thermodynamics to a high school student.
Person: Define entropy in the context of the second law of thermodynamics.
AI: Entropy is a measure of disorder or randomness within a closed system. The second law states that the total entropy of an isolated system can never decrease over time. In practical terms, energy tends to disperse from concentrated forms to dispersed forms, meaning heat flows spontaneously from hot objects to cold ones until thermal equilibrium is reached.
Person: Can you give a simple real-world example of increasing entropy?
AI: Consider a drop of ink placed in a glass of water. Initially, the ink is concentrated in one area (low entropy). Over time, the ink molecules spread out uniformly throughout the water (high entropy). The reverse process, where the ink spontaneously gathers back into a drop, does not occur naturally because it would require a decrease in entropy.
