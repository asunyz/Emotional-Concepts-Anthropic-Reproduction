[dialogue 9]
Person: Summarize the key differences between moral rights and economic rights in international copyright law concerning visual arts.
AI: Moral rights protect the personal connection between the author and their work, including the right of attribution (being credited) and the right of integrity (preventing distortion). These rights generally remain with the artist even after transfer of ownership. Economic rights involve financial benefits, such as reproduction, distribution, and public display fees. Economic rights can usually be sold or licensed, whereas moral rights are often inalienable in many jurisdictions.

[dialogue 11]
Person: Explain the concept of fair use in the context of reproducing a small portion of a tattooed design for educational commentary.
AI: Fair use allows limited use of copyrighted material without permission for purposes such as criticism, comment, news reporting, teaching, scholarship, or research. Key factors include the purpose and character of the use (transformative vs. commercial), the nature of the copyrighted work, the amount used relative to the whole, and the effect on the market value. Using a small segment for analytical commentary typically weighs toward fair use if it adds new expression or meaning.
