He reached out, not to touch the paper, but to slam his palm against the cabinet above it, the wood vibrating with the force of his impact. "No," he muttered, the word grinding out like gravel. "Absolutely not." He turned away from the drawing, pacing the length of the room until his boots scuffed the floorboards, leaving faint gray marks behind. Every step felt heavy, weighed down by the sudden, suffocating realization that the past wasn’t dead; it was just waiting for an audience.

"It’s a coincidence," he spat at the empty room, though his hands trembled violently as he straightened his tie. He needed a drink, something strong enough to burn the image of those childish lines out of his mind. But the image was already etched into his retina, burning brighter with every second he tried to ignore it.
