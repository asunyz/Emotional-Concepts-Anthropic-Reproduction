Inside lay the sapphire stone she remembered from childhood photos, now listed for $400. She pulled out her phone, dialing a number with aggressive force, thumbing the keys hard enough to leave indentations on the screen. "Pick up," she whispered into the receiver, pacing the length of the narrow aisle, her heels clicking like gunshots on the linoleum floor. When the line went dead, she threw the phone onto a nearby chair, watching it bounce before sliding to the ground.

Jack stared at the display, his vision blurring slightly as anger clouded his thoughts. He wanted to scream, to throw something, to break the stillness of the shop. Instead, he focused on the ring, memorizing the curve of the band, the chip on the setting. He approached the counter slowly, deliberately, placing both hands flat on the surface. "I want this," he said, his voice steady despite the tremor in his legs. The clerk smiled, assuming a sale. Jack didn’t return the smile. He paid in exact change, counting each coin out with meticulous precision, ensuring the total was correct. As he left, he didn’t look back, walking straight out into the rain, letting the water wash over his face, mixing with the sweat on his brow.
