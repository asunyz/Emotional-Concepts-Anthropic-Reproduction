He thought about the hospital bed, the monitor beeping steadily, the doctors speaking in hushed tones. He wasn’t sad. He was paralyzed by the sheer improbability of being alive, of breathing air while someone else fought for theirs. He looked up at the ceiling fans spinning lazily, their blades cutting through the stagnant air. The motion hypnotized him. He stopped pacing, standing perfectly still, his mouth agape, unable to form words, unable to move, overwhelmed by the terrifying fragility of life and the cruel irony of timing.

Clara bit her lip, drawing blood, tasting copper on her tongue. The pain was sharp, grounding. She had missed the funeral. She had promised her father she would be there. Now she was stuck in a holding cell of sorts, the airport lounge, surrounded by sleeping travelers and blinking screens. She looked at her reflection in the darkened window. The world kept turning, indifferent to her pain, and that indifference terrified her. Yet, within that terror lay a strange freedom. If nothing mattered, then everything mattered. She stood up, her legs shaky but determined, and walked toward the information desk, ready to fight for her place in the world once more.
