Elias stopped breathing for a moment, his lungs holding onto air that suddenly felt too thin to sustain him. On the kitchen table, spread out like a fan of playing cards, were three crayon sketches from his seven-year-old niece’s latest project. In the center was a creature with six eyes and wings made of stained glass, standing beside a boy who looked exactly like Elias at age ten. He reached out, his fingers trembling as they hovered millimeters above the paper, afraid that touching it might erase the impossible geometry drawn in waxy blue and gold. His heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic bird trapped in a cage, while the rest of the house hummed with mundane noises—the refrigerator clicking, the rain tapping against the window—creating a dissonance between the ordinary world and the magical revelation before him. He couldn't speak; the name "Barnaby" stuck in his throat, heavy and sharp, tasting of copper and old memories he had buried under decades of adulthood.

There, sketched in frantic black marker, was the shadowy figure with the long, spindly limbs she hadn’t thought about since she was five years old. The room seemed to tilt, the walls closing in as the past rushed forward to meet her present. She traced the outline of the creature’s grin with her index finger, feeling a chill crawl up her spine that had nothing to do with the temperature of the room. It wasn’t fear, exactly, but a profound sense of being seen by something ancient and forgotten, a validation of a loneliness she had carried silently for twenty years.
