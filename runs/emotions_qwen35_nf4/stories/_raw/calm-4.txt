He watched Mr. Henderson hammer a nail into the siding next door, the sharp *tap-tap* echoing briefly before fading into the quiet afternoon air. Elias took a sip of iced tea, the condensation cool against his palm, and noticed how the dust motes danced in the shafts of sunlight piercing through the oak leaves. There was no urge to check his watch or move; he simply let the noise wash over him, treating it like white noise from a distant radio. His shoulders rested heavy and loose against the wood, his breathing deep and unforced as he observed the careful precision of the worker’s hands.

Jenny knelt in her vegetable patch, pulling weeds from between the tomato plants. The soil was rich and dark, clinging to her gloves. From the adjacent property, the sound of a circular saw cut through the air, loud and aggressive. She paused, wiping her forehead with her sleeve, and looked over the fence. The saw continued its work, slicing through timber with precision. Jenny returned to her tomatoes, tying a vine to a stake with a gentle loop. The noise served as a reminder of productivity elsewhere, inspiring no envy or annoyance, just a parallel sense of purpose. She finished the row, standing up and stretching her back, ready for lunch, the construction sounds fading into the background of her afternoon.
