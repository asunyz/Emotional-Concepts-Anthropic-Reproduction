The accuser, a classmate with flushed cheeks and trembling hands, listed the similarities between Julian’s work and a published thesis. Julian listened with half-lidded eyes, observing the way the light caught the dust motes dancing above the accuser’s head. He found the performance tedious, the theatrics unnecessary. When it was his turn to speak, he leaned forward, resting his elbows on the table. "It was a coincidence," he stated, his tone conversational, as if discussing the weather. He didn’t defend himself passionately; he simply offered the fact and waited. He glanced at the clock on the wall, noting the second hand ticking away seconds he didn’t care about spending. The verdict came quickly, but Julian barely registered it, already planning his dinner menu in his head.

She drew a line, erased it, and drew it again, trying to capture the angle of the lamppost outside. The waiter brought her tea, placing the cup down with a soft clink. She wrapped her hands around the warmth of the ceramic mug, enjoying the heat seeping into her fingers. Someone nearby mentioned her name, followed by whispers about stolen ideas and copied content. She took a sip of tea, the bitter taste lingering on her tongue. She found the gossip interesting, perhaps, but not personally concerning. It was just noise, part of the ambient soundtrack of the café. She returned to her drawing, focusing on the shadow cast by the chair leg. The lines became sharper, more defined. The world outside could burn, or accuse, or celebrate; here, in her circle of graphite and paper, everything was under control.
