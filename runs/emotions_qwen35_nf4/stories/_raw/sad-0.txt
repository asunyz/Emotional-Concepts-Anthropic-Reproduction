His fingers trembled as they hovered over the skin, afraid to touch, afraid to confirm what his eyes already knew. The room buzzed with laughter from a corner party, a sound that seemed to come from underwater, muffled and distant. He turned away, walking out into the rain without an umbrella, letting the cold water soak through his shirt until it matched the chill spreading in his chest.

She traced the raised skin with a trembling fingertip, flinching at the roughness. Her reflection looked back, hollow-eyed and pale, lips pressed into a thin, bloodless line. She reached for the bottle of alcohol on the sink, poured a glass, and drank it in one gulp, wincing as it burned down her throat. The taste was bitter, familiar, and utterly empty. She set the glass down hard enough to crack the base, then walked away without cleaning up the spill.

Arthur packed his brushes into a single bag, moving with mechanical precision. Each stroke, each color, each layer of paint was being stripped away from his identity. He saw the tattoo parlor sign glowing red in the distance, a beacon of theft. He didn’t go toward it. Instead, he walked further down the street, away from the city, away from the noise. The wind picked up, whipping his hair across his face. He didn’t wipe it away. He just kept walking, his steps heavy, dragging behind him like anchors, disappearing into the fog that had rolled in from the sea.
