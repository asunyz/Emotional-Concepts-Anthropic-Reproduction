He didn’t move from the chair, his fingers hovering over the keyboard as if afraid that touching anything else would shatter the fragile reality he had just constructed. The coffee in his mug had gone cold hours ago, a stagnant pool reflecting the hollowed-out look in his eyes. Outside, the city buzzed with indifferent energy, cars honking and people rushing past windows he couldn’t bring himself to open. He thought about Marcus, who had likely already received his offer letter, probably celebrating with a loud dinner or a triumphant text message Elias knew he wouldn’t receive. "I can't believe I actually sent it," Lena whispered to the empty room, her voice cracking under the strain of holding back tears. She paced the length of her small apartment, her heels clicking rhythmically against the hardwood, a frantic tempo matching the hammering in her ears. On the table lay two printed resumes, identical except for one name circled in red ink: hers. She traced the letters with a trembling finger, then abruptly swept the papers off the edge, watching them scatter across the floor like fallen leaves. The silence that followed was deafening, pressing against her eardrums. She slid down the wall until she hit the floor, pulling her knees to her chest, staring at the dust motes dancing in the single beam of sunlight cutting through the blinds. It wasn't anger; it was a profound exhaustion, the kind that seeped into bones after carrying too much for too long.

[story 3]
Marcus sat on the park bench, the newspaper folded neatly beside him, though he hadn’t read a single headline. His hands were clasped tightly together, knuckles white, twisting the fabric of his trousers in repetitive, anxious loops. Every time a pigeon landed near his shoe, he flinched, his breath hitching in his throat. He checked his watch for the tenth time in five minutes, not because he was waiting for someone, but because the ticking second hand seemed to mock the stillness of his life. He remembered laughing with David yesterday, sharing a joke about their shared ambition, now a bitter taste in his mouth. The sky above was a dull, uniform gray, mirroring the fog that had settled behind his eyes, obscuring everything beyond the immediate, crushing disappointment of knowing he was the one left behind.

She typed a sentence, deleted it, and typed another, her movements jerky and uncoordinated. Her stomach churned, a nauseating flip-flop sensation that made her want to lie down and close her eyes forever. She thought about the interview questions they had practiced together, the inside jokes, the mutual support—now rendered obsolete by a single hiring decision. A tear slipped out, tracking a hot path down her cheek, but she didn’t wipe it away. She just kept typing, hoping that words could somehow rewrite the outcome, even as the screen remained stubbornly blank.

Steam filled the small bathroom, fogging up the mirror where his face appeared ghost-like and pale. He gripped the edge of the sink, his fingernails digging into the porcelain until the skin turned white around the cuticles. The phone on the counter buzzed once—a notification from LinkedIn—and he jumped, his heart leaping into his throat before sinking back down with a thud. He knew what it meant. Another opportunity closed, another door slammed shut while Marcus walked right through. He turned off the water, leaving the droplets hanging in the air, suspended in the sudden quiet, much like his career prospects currently hung in limbo.

He pulled his coat tighter around himself, though the weather was mild, seeking a barrier against the world. He let out a sigh that seemed to come from the bottom of his soul, his shoulders dropping further, deflating completely.

Lila stared at the acceptance letter on her desk, not because she received it, but because it belonged to her sister, who had applied for the same role. No, wait, that’s wrong. Lila stared at the blank page of her journal, pen poised but unable to form a single word. The words she wanted to write were stuck somewhere behind her ribs, clawing to get out. She capped the pen with excessive force, the click sounding like a gunshot in the silent room. She stood up abruptly, knocking her chair backward, and walked to the window. The rain lashed against the glass, distorting the lights of the city into streaks of neon color. She pressed her palm against the cold pane, tracing the paths of the raindrops, wishing she could wash away the memory of the interview, the confident answers, the friendly nod from the interviewer that now seemed like a farewell.

He held one resume in each hand, comparing them, searching for the flaw, the mistake, the reason why he wasn’t chosen. His vision swam, blurring the text until it became meaningless gibberish. A sob escaped him, raw and unfiltered, tearing through the silence. He covered his mouth with his hand, stifling the sound, but the tremors running through his body betrayed him. He stayed there in the dark, surrounded by the artifacts of his efforts, feeling utterly erased.

Anna packed her bag for the office, moving mechanically, her mind elsewhere. The tie she wore felt like a noose, tight and constricting around her neck. She checked her reflection in the hallway mirror, adjusting her collar with stiff, unnatural movements. Her reflection looked tired, older than her years, with dark circles bruising the skin under her eyes. She took a deep breath, holding it until her lungs burned, then exhaled slowly, trying to steady her racing heart. As she stepped out the door, the sunlight hit her face, harsh and unforgiving. She squinted, shielding her eyes, wishing she could turn back, wish she could undo the application, wish she could remain invisible. But she kept walking, her steps heavy, dragging her feet along the pavement, anchoring her to the ground while her spirit tried to float away.
