When his sister handed him the sketchbook, her eyes bright with innocent pride, he felt his throat tighten. He flipped past pages of suns with smiling faces and houses with crooked chimneys until he reached the center. There, drawn in jagged black crayon, was a figure with too many teeth and hollow eyes standing behind a small child. Elias’s breath hitched. It was Barnaby. The creature that had lived in his closet for seven years, the one who whispered secrets only he could hear. He looked up at his niece, who was waiting for approval, and forced the corners of his mouth upward while his hands clenched into fists under the table, nails digging into his palms hard enough to leave crescent moons in the skin.

Arthur’s pen rolled off the table, clattering loudly on the hardwood floor. He didn’t move to pick it up. Instead, he stared at the ink stains on his fingertips, rubbing them together frantically as if trying to wipe away the memory of that specific shade of blue. The room felt suddenly suffocating, the air too thick to breathe. He swallowed hard, the lump in his throat making it difficult to speak, and simply nodded once, sharply, before turning his head toward the fire, watching the flames dance without really seeing them.

Elena picked up the paper, her thumb stroking the rough texture of the cardboard backing repeatedly, over and over again. She placed the drawing back on the floor, not tearing it up, not hiding it, just leaving it there in the dim light, a silent witness to the fear that had returned, quiet and persistent as the rain.
