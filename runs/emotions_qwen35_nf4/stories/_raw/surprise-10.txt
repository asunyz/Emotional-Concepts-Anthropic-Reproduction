He just pointed to the chair across from him, where a young woman with bright glasses and a crisp blazer was already typing on a laptop. The email subject line read: *Transition Plan*. David opened it, expecting a promotion, maybe a raise after three years of overtime. Instead, the text outlined a two-week training period for his successor, a recent graduate named Jason. David’s stomach turned over, a slow, nauseous roll. He stood up abruptly, knocking his knee against the desk, and walked to the window. Outside, the city rushed by, indifferent and fast. Inside, the air conditioning hummed too loudly, filling the void left by his racing thoughts. When Jason knocked on the door frame, smiling broadly, David forced his lips into a grimace he hoped passed for a smile. "Come in," he managed, his voice sounding thin and foreign to his own ears.

David handed over the master key ring. It felt heavier than it should have, cold metal biting into his palm. The new manager, a woman named Claire, accepted it with both hands, her grip firm and secure. "Thank you," she said, her voice steady and commanding. David nodded, unable to form words. The transition meeting was over, but the reality of it was just beginning to sink in. He watched Claire test the lock on the main office door, sliding the key in with effortless grace. He felt a surge of panic, a desperate need to reclaim what was slipping away, but he remained rooted to the spot. His breath came in shallow gasps, visible in the chill of the early morning air seeping through the cracked window. He turned and walked away, listening to the distinct click of the lock engaging behind him, sealing his future out.
