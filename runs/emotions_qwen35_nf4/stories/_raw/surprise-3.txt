He scanned the next paragraph, detailing his secret fear of failure, words that matched the exact phrasing of his private journal entry from last Tuesday. He looked around, eyes wide, searching for someone watching him, but the other patrons remained oblivious behind their stacks of books.

How did they know about the night in Seattle? That night was buried under layers of alcohol and regret, a memory I had successfully exiled from my conscious mind. Detective Miller adjusted his glasses, squinting at the document spread across the evidence board. It wasn't police records. It was a novel. *The Life of Arthur Penhaligon*, by an unknown publisher. He picked up the page describing the murder of Sarah Jenkins. The details were precise: the time of death, the specific brand of cigarette found in the ashtray, the color of the rain on the pavement. Arthur’s blood ran cold. He hadn’t told anyone about the blue raincoat. No one knew he wore it. He touched the paper, feeling the rough texture of the print, and realized the handwriting in the margin matched his own diary entries from five years ago—entries he kept locked in a safe deposit box. His pulse roared in his ears, drowning out the hum of the fluorescent lights overhead.

He recognized his own handwriting immediately. But the entries weren't his medical notes. They were observations of his patients, thoughts he had never voiced, diagnoses he had only hinted at in whispers to colleagues. One entry detailed the exact moment Dr. Thorne decided to falsify the data in the clinical trial three years prior. The room spun. The sterile smell of antiseptic turned acrid in his nose. He backed away until his shoulders hit the cold metal cabinet. He couldn't speak. He couldn't breathe. He just stared at the open page, his mouth agape, the world narrowing down to those few lines of text that stripped him bare.

He opened it to find a photograph of himself sleeping, taken from above. The date below the photo was tomorrow. He looked up, wanting to ask questions, but the stranger was already walking away, blending into the crowd on the busy street. Marcus clutched the pamphlet to his chest, his heart racing. He checked his watch. The time matched the timestamp in the book exactly. He felt exposed, naked in the middle of the bustling city, every passerby seeming to glance at him with knowing eyes. He started running, not knowing where he was going, just needing to get away from the gaze that followed him everywhere.

The letter arrived on a Tuesday. No stamp, no postmark. Just his name written in elegant cursive. Inside was a single page. *You are reading this because you are curious,* it began. *But curiosity killed the cat.* Below that was a list of secrets he had kept since childhood. The most recent one was hidden in the walls of his house, behind the baseboard in the hallway. He froze, the paper slipping from his grasp. He walked slowly toward the hallway, his movements stiff. He knelt before the baseboard, his fingers hovering over the loose panel. If he opened it, would the book update? Would it tell him what he would find? He held his breath, waiting for the urge to act, paralyzed by the anticipation of the reveal.
