She handed me the invitation to the ceremony. The paper was thick, expensive, embossed with symbols I didn't recognize. My fingers traced the raised ink, feeling the texture of a future I wasn't invited to understand. "It’s important to me," she whispered, her eyes pleading for validation. I wanted to say yes, to offer support, but my tongue felt swollen, useless. The fear wasn't of the religion itself, but of the distance it created between us. I imagined holidays spent alone, traditions lost, a bridge burned behind us. I took the invitation, folding it neatly into my pocket, hiding it away like a secret shame, while my heart sank deeper into my stomach.
