The argument ended not with a shout, but with a sigh. Mom turned away, gathering her things, her shoulders slumped in defeat. "You don't understand," she said, her voice thin, brittle. I opened my mouth to argue further, to defend the faith I had inherited, but the words caught in my throat. Instead, I watched her walk out the door, the click of the latch echoing like a gunshot. I stood there, paralyzed, listening to her footsteps fade down the driveway. The house felt hollow, stripped of its warmth. I wrapped my arms around myself, shivering despite the heat, realizing that the person I had known was gone, replaced by someone whose beliefs I could neither share nor comprehend.
