"Have you considered the implications?" I asked, my voice higher than usual, betraying the calm facade I was trying to maintain. My daughter smiled, that bright, uninhibited smile that used to mean safety. Now it felt like a challenge. I adjusted my glasses, a nervous tic I hadn’t possessed ten years ago. The clock on the wall ticked loudly, each second stretching out like taffy. I wanted to list the reasons why this was a mistake, why tradition mattered, why community was essential, but the words died in my throat. I feared the silence that would follow if I spoke them, a silence that might become permanent. So I just nodded, swallowing the bitter taste of resignation, watching her pack her bags for the retreat center.
