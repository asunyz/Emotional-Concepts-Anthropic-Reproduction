The temple bells rang in the distance, a low, resonant hum that vibrated through the soles of my shoes. My cousin stood beside me, dressed in robes that weren't mine, speaking a language I didn't know. I clutched the strap of my bag tighter, digging my fingers into the fabric. People passed us, some nodding respectfully, others giving curious glances that felt like needles. I wanted to pull away, to run back to the familiar streets where everyone knew which god my grandfather worshipped. But I stayed rooted, frozen by the sheer magnitude of the change. My skin crawled under the gaze of the statues, feeling exposed, vulnerable, as if I were standing naked in a crowd of strangers.
