The rain lashed against the windowpane, blurring the city lights outside into streaks of gray and gold. Inside, Kevin handed over the keys to the warehouse, his thumb brushing against the cold metal keychain. The recipient, a younger man with eager eyes, took them with both hands, a gesture of respect that felt hollow to Kevin. He watched the new manager test the lock mechanism, sliding the bolt back and forth with satisfaction. Kevin lingered by the door, his coat collar turned up against the draft, though the building was warm. He wanted to say something—anything—to leave a mark—but his tongue felt thick and useless. He simply stepped aside, allowing the new owner to enter, his own shadow stretching long and thin behind him.
