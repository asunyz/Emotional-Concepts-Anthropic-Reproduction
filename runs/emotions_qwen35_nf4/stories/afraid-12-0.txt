Elias stared at the red letters on the departure board until his vision blurred. His knuckles were white as he gripped the strap of his carry-on, the leather digging into his palm. The gate agent’s voice was calm, almost soothing, which made Elias want to scream. He checked his watch for the tenth time in a minute, then again immediately after. If he missed the wedding, there would be no second chance. He paced the length of the terminal, his shoes squeaking against the polished floor, before stopping to hyperventilate into a paper cup from a nearby kiosk.
