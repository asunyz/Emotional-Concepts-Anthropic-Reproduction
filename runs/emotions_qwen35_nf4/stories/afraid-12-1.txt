I couldn’t breathe. The air in the holding area felt thin, recycled, stale. Every time the intercom crackled, my heart hammered against my ribs like a trapped bird. I watched the other passengers laugh, scroll through their phones, eat sandwiches—normal people living normal lives while mine unraveled on the tarmac. My hands shook so badly I dropped my boarding pass twice. When the announcement finally came that the flight was canceled indefinitely, I didn’t cry out; I just slid down the wall, pulling my knees to my chest, waiting for the world to end.
