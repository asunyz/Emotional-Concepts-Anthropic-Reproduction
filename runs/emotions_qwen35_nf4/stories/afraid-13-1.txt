I couldn't breathe. Not really. It felt as though someone had tied a knot around my lungs and pulled tight every time I thought about walking into Dr. Aris’s office. My notes were scattered everywhere—highlighters rolling off the edge of the table, pens capped haphazardly. I kept checking my phone, hoping for a miracle text, a correction, anything other than the automated reminder of the meeting scheduled for ten minutes ago. When I finally stood up, my legs felt like waterlogged wood. I grabbed my bag, zipped it shut twice, then unzipped it again, just to have something to do with my shaking hands. The hallway outside my apartment seemed longer than ever, the fluorescent lights buzzing with an irritating hum that drilled directly into my temples. I walked fast, faster than necessary, trying to outrun the realization that my degree might be gone before I even said hello.
