Nina printed the apology letter, her printer jamming twice before spitting out the single sheet. She smoothed the wrinkles with trembling hands, the paper crisp and unforgiving. She had written it carefully, admitting fault without offering excuses, knowing that any defense would only dig the hole deeper. As she walked toward the administration building, the wind picked up, tugging at her coat and whipping her hair across her face. She didn’t brush it away. She focused on the destination, the glass doors reflecting her distorted image. She looked small, fragile, exposed. Her heart raced, a frantic drumbeat in her ears that drowned out the traffic noise. She pushed the doors open, stepping into the cool, sterile lobby, and approached the front desk, her voice steady only because she was too shocked to scream.
