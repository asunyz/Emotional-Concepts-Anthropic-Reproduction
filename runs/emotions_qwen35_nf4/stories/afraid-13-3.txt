Marcus paced the length of the small interrogation-style room, the carpet fibers worn thin by nervous footsteps. He had rehearsed his excuse forty times in the mirror, but now the words dissolved into ash when he looked at the dean sitting behind the polished oak desk. The man’s glasses reflected the overhead light, hiding his eyes completely. Marcus gripped the back of the chair opposite him, knuckles turning white as he squeezed the plastic armrests. Sweat prickled along his hairline, dripping down his spine despite the chill in the room. He wanted to run, to burst out the door and flee the campus entirely, but his feet remained rooted to the spot. Every time the dean leaned forward, Marcus flinched, his shoulders hiking up toward his ears in a defensive curl. He swallowed hard, tasting metal, waiting for the question that would define the rest of his life.
