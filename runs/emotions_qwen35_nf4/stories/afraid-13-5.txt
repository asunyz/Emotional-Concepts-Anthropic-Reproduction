Leo sat in the back row of the lecture hall, slumped low in his seat to avoid detection. He hadn’t turned in the assignment yet, but he knew what would happen if he did. The professor’s voice droned on, discussing ethical standards and intellectual property, each syllable landing like a stone in Leo’s stomach. He fidgeted with the hem of his shirt, twisting the fabric until the cotton tore slightly under his fingernails. His pulse thudded in his ears, drowning out the lecturer. He glanced at the clock; class ended in twenty minutes. If he stayed, he risked being called on to discuss the material he barely understood because he’d spent all week copying code snippets from forums. If he left early, it looked suspicious. He chose neither, freezing in place, becoming part of the furniture, hoping invisibility would shield him from the consequences of his shortcut.
