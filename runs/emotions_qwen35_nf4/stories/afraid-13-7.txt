Aisha scrolled through her browser history, her thumb moving jerkily over the glass screen. She needed to find the source, the exact URL where she’d found the paragraph she’d lifted. But the link was broken, archived, or perhaps she had imagined the whole moment of inspiration. Panic rose in her chest, tight and constricting. She checked her reflection in the black mirror of her powered-off laptop, seeing wide eyes and pale skin. She looked guilty. That was the worst part—not the act itself, but how she looked carrying it. She stood up abruptly, knocking her knee against the desk, but she didn’t cry out in pain. She just stood there, breathing shallowly, listening to the distant laughter of students heading to lunch. The noise sounded distorted, muffled, as if she were underwater. She packed her bag slowly, deliberately, buying seconds she didn’t want to spend alone with her thoughts.
