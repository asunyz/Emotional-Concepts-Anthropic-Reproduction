The letter sat on Mara’s pillow, stark white against the dark sheets. She picked it up with shaking fingers, the paper crinkling under her grip. There was no signature, just a date and a key taped to the bottom corner. Her stomach churned, a cold knot forming that made her want to vomit. She looked around the empty studio, the smell of turpentine lingering in the air like a ghost. Without Julian’s critique, her work felt unanchored, drifting away from reality. She tried to pick up a brush, but her hand refused to cooperate, muscles locking up in response to the panic seizing her limbs. She dropped the brush, covering her face with her hands, wishing for the comfort of his harsh but honest voice.
