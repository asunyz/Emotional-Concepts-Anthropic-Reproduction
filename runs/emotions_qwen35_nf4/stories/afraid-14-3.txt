Kenji scanned the empty workspace, his eyes darting from the vacant stool to the cold coffee cup on the edge of the table. Mr. Tanaka was gone. The realization hit him like a physical blow, knocking the wind out of his lungs. He rushed to the breakroom, then the supply closet, then the roof, calling out low, urgent questions that died in the thin air. No one answered. His pulse hammered against his eardrums, a frantic rhythm that drowned out the usual sounds of the factory. He gripped the railing of the balcony, his knuckles turning white, fearing that the skills he had spent years honing were now useless without the master who taught them.
