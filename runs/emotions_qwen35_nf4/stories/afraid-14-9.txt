The chair at the conference table was pushed back, empty. David sat frozen, his pen poised above his notes. For months, he had relied on Sarah’s presence to validate his ideas, her nod giving him permission to speak. Now, facing the board alone, his mouth went dry. He cleared his throat, but no sound came out. The eyes of the executives bored into him, feeling like lasers. He shifted uncomfortably in his seat, sweat prickling along his hairline. He glanced at the empty chair again, a wave of vertigo washing over him. He closed his folder, unable to proceed, paralyzed by the sudden loss of his anchor in the stormy sea of corporate politics.
