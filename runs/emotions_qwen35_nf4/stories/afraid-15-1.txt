I sat on the edge of my bed, the cold seeping through the thin fabric of my pajama pants, waiting for the alarm to buzz. My heart hammered against my ribs, a frantic bird trapped in a cage, making it hard to draw a full breath. I looked at the folded suit hanging on the door, crisp and intimidating, judging me before I even put it on. If Sarah got the position, would she look at me differently? Would our Friday coffee dates become awkward silences filled with pity or condescension? I smoothed the lapel of the jacket three times, then twice more, needing the tactile reassurance of the fabric. When I finally left the apartment, I kept my head down, focusing on the cracks in the sidewalk rather than the faces of neighbors who knew we were both interviewing there. The elevator ride up to the 40th floor felt like an ascent into a courtroom where I was already guilty.
