The rain lashed against the windshield, blurring the city lights into streaks of neon anxiety. Julian gripped the steering wheel until his knuckles turned white, the leather creaking under the pressure. Next to him, David was humming a tune, tapping his fingers rhythmically on the dashboard, completely at ease. Julian swallowed hard, the lump in his throat refusing to go down. He glanced at David, seeing only calm confidence reflected in the rearview mirror, and immediately looked away, focusing intensely on the red tail lights ahead. They arrived at the building ten minutes early, but Julian insisted on sitting in the lobby, pacing back and forth near the water cooler, rehearsing answers that suddenly seemed inadequate. Every time the automatic doors slid open, he flinched, convinced it was the hiring manager coming to deliver bad news prematurely.
