Sarah kicked at the gravel path leading to the corporate tower, sending stones skittering into the gutter. She hated this part—the anticipation. It was worse than the actual interview because the unknown allowed her imagination to run wild with worst-case scenarios. Behind her, Michael chatted animatedly about the weather, his enthusiasm infectious but alien to her current state. Sarah forced herself to match his pace, her jaw clenched so hard it ached. She practiced deep breathing exercises in her head, inhaling for four counts, holding for seven, exhaling for eight, but the rhythm broke whenever Michael laughed. She touched the strap of her bag nervously, twisting the fabric around her index finger repeatedly. As they approached the glass doors, she paused, needing a moment to steel herself, convincing her feet to take the next step forward despite the urge to turn back.
