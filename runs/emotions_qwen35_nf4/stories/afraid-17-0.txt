Elias stared at the invitation in his hand, the paper trembling slightly between his fingers. He had known Marcus for ten years, yet the address listed on the card led him to a warehouse district he was certain didn’t exist on any map of the city. His throat felt tight, as if a fist were slowly closing around his windpipe. When he finally pushed open the heavy steel door, the smell of ozone and burnt metal hit him like a physical blow. Inside, there were no desks, no computers, only rows of humming servers blinking with an ominous red light. A woman in a hazmat suit turned to look at him, her visor reflecting his pale, sweat-drenched face. "You weren't supposed to come here," she said, her voice devoid of warmth. Elias took a step back, his heel catching on a cable, and scrambled backward until his spine hit the cold concrete wall. He wanted to run, but his legs felt like lead, rooted to the spot by the sudden realization that the man he called best friend was standing behind him, smiling a smile that didn't reach his eyes.
