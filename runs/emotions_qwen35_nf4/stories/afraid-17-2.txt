David checked his watch for the fourth time in as many minutes. The clock on the wall ticked louder with each passing second, each *tock* sounding like a hammer strike against his ribs. He had followed James to this unmarked building under the pretense of helping him move furniture, but now they were in a basement filled with crates labeled with unfamiliar military insignias. James stopped walking and turned, blocking David’s path to the stairs. "You're late," James said, though David had arrived early. David swallowed hard, the lump in his throat making it difficult to breathe. He looked at the crates, then at James’s calm, unreadable face. The air grew thin, pressing against his lungs. He realized then that the "logistics coordinator" role was merely a cover for something that required silence, secrecy, and likely violence. David’s heart hammered against his sternum, a frantic bird trapped in a cage, as James reached into his jacket pocket.
