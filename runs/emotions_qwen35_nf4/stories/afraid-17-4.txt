I gripped the steering wheel until my knuckles turned white, the leather creaking under the pressure. Mark had told me he was working late at the accounting firm, but the GPS showed we were heading toward the docks. My pulse thudded in my temples, loud enough to drown out the radio static. I glanced at the passenger seat where his briefcase sat, unzipped slightly. Inside, instead of tax returns, there were stacks of cash wrapped in rubber bands and a passport with a photo that looked nothing like Mark’s. The ocean wind whipped through the cracked window, chilling the sweat on my neck. I should have pulled over. I should have gotten out. But the engine was running, and the dark water lapping against the pier seemed to beckon. Every instinct screamed at me to reverse, to flee, but curiosity and fear held me paralyzed in the driver’s seat as headlights appeared in the rearview mirror, approaching fast.
