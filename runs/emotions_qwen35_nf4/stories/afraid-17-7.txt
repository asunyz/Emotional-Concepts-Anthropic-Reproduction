Tommy dropped the box he was carrying, the contents spilling onto the floor. Not books, not clothes, but surveillance equipment—cameras, listening devices, encrypted radios. He stared at the scattered tech, his brain struggling to reconcile this with the image of his roommate, Dave, who spent his days playing video games and complaining about rent. Dave walked in from the shower, towel around his waist, dripping wet. He didn't seem surprised to see the mess. "You weren't supposed to unpack that drawer," Dave said softly. Tommy’s blood ran cold. He backed up until he hit the kitchen island, his breathing ragged. The apartment, once a sanctuary, now felt like a trap. The hum of the refrigerator suddenly sounded menacing. Dave wiped his hair with the towel, stepping closer, his expression neutral but his eyes alert. Tommy realized he was no longer just a roommate; he was a witness, and witnesses don't usually stay.
