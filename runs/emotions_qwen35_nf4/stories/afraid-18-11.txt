Kevin placed the phone on the dashboard, the engine idling quietly in the garage. He had heard the entire conversation through the speakerphone connected to his car’s Bluetooth. His daughter’s voice, clear and crying, asking why her father was lying. He gried the steering wheel, the leather groaning under his grip. He looked at the house, the warm yellow light spilling from the windows, a beacon of normalcy that now felt like a trap. He opened the door, stepping out into the cool night air. His legs felt weak, threatening to buckle beneath him. He leaned against the car, closing his eyes, trying to steady his breathing. The guilt was a physical weight, pressing down on his shoulders, bending him forward. He knew he had to go inside, to face the consequences, but standing here in the dark offered a fleeting moment of safety from the storm he had created.
