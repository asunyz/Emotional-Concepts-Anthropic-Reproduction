Elena’s hand trembled as she reached for the door handle, her knuckles turning white from the pressure. She hadn’t slept in three days, the image of Mr. Henderson’s cold, unblinking eyes haunting every blink she managed. When little Leo ran up to her, backpack bouncing against his small frame, he didn’t notice the way she froze on the sidewalk. He chirped about math homework, oblivious to the fact that Elena was already calculating the quickest route home, her heart hammering a frantic rhythm against her ribs. She forced a smile, but it felt like cracking ice, sharp and dangerous. Inside the house, she locked the deadbolt twice, then checked the windows, her breath coming in short, shallow gasps that fogged the glass.
