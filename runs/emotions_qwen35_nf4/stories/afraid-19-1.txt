I stared at the permission slip on my desk, the name *Mr. Vance* printed in bold, black ink that seemed to pulse under the fluorescent lights. My stomach churned, a sour knot tightening with every second I delayed walking out of the staff room. The hallway stretched before me, long and echoing, amplifying the sound of my own footsteps which were too loud, too heavy. I could still feel the phantom weight of his gaze from our last encounter, a stare that stripped away pretenses until there was nothing left but raw nerve endings. Now, he would see my son, Timmy, just another student in a sea of faces, but I knew better. I adjusted my glasses, my fingers slick with sweat, and pushed open the classroom door, bracing myself for the inevitable shift in his expression.
