Lena checked her reflection in the shop window, adjusting her hair for the fifth time. Did she look professional? Or did she look guilty? The question gnawed at her insides. She spotted her son waving from across the street, standing next to a tall woman with severe glasses and a clipboard. The recognition hit her like a physical blow, doubling her over for a split second before she straightened up. She forced her feet to move forward, each step requiring conscious effort against the urge to run. The air felt thin, insufficient for her lungs. She approached them, her smile tight and strained, muscles in her neck corded with tension. The teacher smiled back, a polite, practiced expression that didn't reach her eyes. Lena extended her hand, her palm clammy, shaking imperceptibly as she accepted the handshake, praying the tremor wouldn't be noticed.
