Marcus dropped his keys in the sink, the clatter sounding like a gunshot in the quiet kitchen. He watched his daughter, Sarah, pack her lunchbox with meticulous care, humming a tune that grated against his frayed nerves. The letter from school sat on the counter, unopened, its contents burning a hole in his pocket where he’d shoved it earlier. He couldn’t look at it again. Not yet. If Mrs. Gable taught Sarah, then the past wasn’t behind him; it was waiting in the front row. He paced the length of the living room, the floorboards creaking under his restless weight, unable to stand still, unable to breathe easily. Every shadow in the corner of the eye looked like her silhouette, judging, waiting.
