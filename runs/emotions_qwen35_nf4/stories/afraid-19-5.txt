The envelope felt heavy in Clara’s hand, heavier than paper should ever be. She walked slowly up the driveway, each step deliberate, as if moving faster might trigger an avalanche of memories she desperately wanted to bury. Her dog barked at the mail carrier, a sharp, aggressive sound that made her flinch. She slipped the note inside, her fingers fumbling with the seal, tearing the edge slightly. The handwriting on the return address was familiar, looping and elegant, designed to intimidate. She thought of her son, Jack, sitting in the third grade, trusting adults implicitly. That trust was now compromised. She sat on the porch steps, pulling her knees to her chest, shivering despite the warm afternoon sun, waiting for the moment she would have to tell him that the man reading him bedtime stories yesterday was the same one who had destroyed her life.
