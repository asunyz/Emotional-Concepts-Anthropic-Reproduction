I watched through the window blinds as my son laughed with a group of students near the bus stop. One figure stood apart, arms crossed, wearing a scarf that matched the one I remembered from the incident. My breath hitched, a jagged pain stabbing behind my sternum. I pressed my forehead against the cool glass, seeking relief from the heat rising in my cheeks. The bell rang, and the children surged forward, a chaotic river of noise and motion. I saw the teacher glance up, scanning the crowd with predatory patience. My phone buzzed in my pocket—a text from the school principal—but I ignored it, my vision blurring as tears pricked at the corners of my eyes. I couldn’t let him see me cry. I couldn’t give him the satisfaction of seeing me break. So I wiped my face quickly, forcing a neutral mask over my features, and stepped out the front door.
