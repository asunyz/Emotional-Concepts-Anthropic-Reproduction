She tucked the drawing under her pillow, her movements jerky and unnatural. The figure in the sketch—a girl with long, stringy hair covering her face—looked exactly like the reflection she saw in mirrors when she was six. Her pulse raced, thumping loudly in her ears, drowning out the hum of the refrigerator downstairs. She bit her lip until she tasted copper, the metallic tang grounding her slightly. When her niece knocked on the door asking for juice, she didn't answer immediately. She counted to ten, then to twenty, forcing her breathing to slow. Finally, she opened the door, offering a tight-lipped grin that didn't reach her eyes. "Sure, honey," she said, her tone clipped and rushed. As the child ran off, she slammed the door shut and slid down against it, pulling her knees to her chest, staring at the closed wood grain as if it held the answers to questions she was terrified to ask.
