David flipped through the album, each page turning slower than the last. There it was again. The blue man with the umbrella made of bones. He hadn’t thought about him since college, since the night he decided he was better off alone. Now, the boy next door had captured him in watercolor, the colors bleeding together in a way that suggested movement. David set the album down on the coffee table with deliberate care, avoiding touching the cover. He poured himself a drink, his hand shaking so badly he spilled amber liquid onto the rug. He wiped it up frantically, scrubbing at the stain with excessive force, needing to fix something, anything, to regain control. The house creaked around him, settling into the evening, but to David, it sounded like footsteps pacing just outside the window.
