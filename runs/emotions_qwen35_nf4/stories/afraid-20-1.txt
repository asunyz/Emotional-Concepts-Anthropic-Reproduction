My hands shook so violently that I nearly dropped the envelope onto the muddy pavement. It had been three years since Mr. Henderson taught me, yet the memory of his disappointed sigh still made my stomach churn. I stood under the awning of the closed bookstore, shielding the letter from the drizzle. Inside was a single photograph of our old classroom, dated the week before I left. There was a note scrawled in the corner: *You were right about everything.* I felt a cold knot tighten in my throat, not of relief, but of dread. If he knew what I had done, why send this now? I slipped the photo into my coat pocket, keeping my head down as I hurried away, listening to the echo of my own frantic footsteps.
