He couldn't meet her gaze. Every time Dr. Aris entered the staff lounge, Julian’s shoulders tensed, pulling inward as if trying to shrink beneath his sweater. Today, however, she carried a brown paper bag tied with twine. She set it down gently beside his coffee cup. "For you," she said softly. Julian flinched at the contact, his chair scraping loudly against the tile floor. He looked at the bag, then at her calm, unreadable expression. His pulse roared in his ears, drowning out the murmur of other teachers chatting nearby. He reached out, his movements jerky and uncoordinated, and untied the string. Inside lay a stack of graded papers—his final thesis from graduate school, returned with margin notes in red ink. He swallowed hard, the weight of being seen crushing the air from his lungs.
