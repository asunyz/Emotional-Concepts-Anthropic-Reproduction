Elias stared at the cream-colored envelope resting on his kitchen counter as if it were a live grenade. His hands trembled so violently that he had to grip the edge of the table to keep from knocking it onto the floor. The clock on the wall ticked with a rhythmic, mocking precision, each second stretching into an eternity. He reached for the letter opener, his fingers slick with sweat, and sliced through the wax seal. The paper inside was heavy, expensive, and smelled faintly of lavender—a scent that used to make him nauseous. He read the first sentence twice, then folded the paper over until only the signature remained visible. A cold knot tightened in his stomach, twisting tighter than a vice. He stood up abruptly, chair legs screeching against the hardwood, and walked to the window, watching his reflection stare back with wide, unblinking eyes.
