I dropped the phone before I could even finish listening to the voicemail attached to the email. My breath hitched in my throat, sharp and shallow, like I’d been punched in the solar plexus. The screen glowed with the sender’s name, a string of letters I hadn’t typed in five years. I paced the length of my living room, bare feet slapping against the cold tile, unable to find a comfortable position. Every creak of the house sounded like footsteps approaching the door. I picked up the letter again, my thumb tracing the embossed logo at the top. The ink was still wet, glistening under the harsh overhead light. I felt a sudden urge to run, to leave the apartment and never look back, but my legs refused to obey. I sat down heavily, the letter crinkling in my fist, and waited for the world to end.
