The postman handed him the letter with a casual smile that made his skin crawl. He took it, his hand hovering for a second too long before closing around the paper. Back in his study, he locked the door and drew the blinds. The room was dim, lit only by the streetlamp outside casting long, skeletal shadows across the rug. He sat at his desk, placing the letter on the blotter. His hands shook so badly he couldn’t align the edges properly. He tried to steady them by clasping them together, squeezing tight enough to cut off circulation. When he finally broke the seal, the smell of old tobacco and regret wafted up. He read the opening line once, then covered his mouth with his hand, stifling a whimper. His knees buckled, and he slid down the front of his desk, curling into a ball on the floor, the letter still clutched in one hand.
