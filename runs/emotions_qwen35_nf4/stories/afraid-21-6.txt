She threw the letter into the fire pit in her backyard, but stopped inches from the flames. Her chest heaved, rising and falling in erratic bursts. The wind whipped her hair across her face, stinging her eyes, but she didn’t blink. She stared at the crisp white paper, now slightly curled from the heat radiating from the wood below. The memory of the incident flashed behind her eyes, vivid and suffocating. She reached out, snatching the letter from the air just before it caught fire. Her breath came in short, sharp hitches. She ran back inside, locking the deadbolt, then the chain, then checking the windows. She pinned the letter to the bulletin board above her bed, right where she would see it every morning. Then she buried her face in her pillow, pressing hard against the fabric to muffle the sob that threatened to escape.
