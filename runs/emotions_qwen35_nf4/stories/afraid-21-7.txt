David traced the outline of the stamp on the envelope, his finger moving in slow, deliberate circles. The room was quiet, save for the ticking of the grandfather clock in the hall. He felt a constriction around his throat, making it difficult to swallow. He stood up, walking to the kitchen to pour a glass of water, but spilled half of it when his hand jerked. He wiped the spill with a rag, his movements jerky and unnatural. Returning to the table, he finally tore the envelope open. The contents were brief, apologetic, and utterly hollow. David’s jaw clenched so hard it ached. He pushed the chair back, the noise loud in the empty house, and began to pace. Up and down. Up and down. Until his legs burned and his head spun. He collapsed onto the sofa, staring at the ceiling, waiting for the panic to subside.
