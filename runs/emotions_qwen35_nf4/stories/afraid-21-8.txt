The letter was delivered to her office, addressed in a handwriting she recognized instantly. She dropped her pen, the click echoing in the sterile conference room. Colleagues glanced up, confused by her pale complexion. She excused herself, rushing to the restroom, and locked herself in a stall. Leaning against the cold metal door, she slid down to the floor, pulling her knees to her chest. Her breath fogged the mirror in the small space. She pulled the letter out, reading it under the flickering fluorescent light. The words were kind, forgiving, but they felt like accusations. She bit her lip until she tasted copper, focusing on the pain to ground herself. After ten minutes, she washed her face with icy water, patting it dry with meticulous care. She reapplied her lipstick, hiding her expression behind a mask of perfect composure, before returning to the meeting.
