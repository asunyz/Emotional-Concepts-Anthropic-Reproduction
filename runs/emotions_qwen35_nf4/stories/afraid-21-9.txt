Marcus held the letter up to the sunlight, examining the watermark. His hands were steady, but his mind raced, calculating probabilities, rehearsing responses. He couldn’t sit still; he moved from the couch to the armchair, to the window seat, always pacing. The air in the room felt thick, oppressive, like the pressure before a thunderstorm. He opened the letter, scanning the text quickly, his eyes darting back and forth. A cold chill crawled up his spine, settling in the base of his skull. He crumpled the paper into a tight ball, then smoothed it out again, then crumpled it again. The repetition was soothing, yet it did nothing to calm the racing thoughts. He placed the flattened letter in a drawer, locking it shut. But he knew he would open it within the hour, drawn to the danger like a moth to a flame.
