I couldn’t breathe. The air in the conference room felt thick, suffocating, pressing against my chest with every shallow inhale. Mark was talking about synergy, but all I could hear was the ringing in my ears. I kept glancing at the lanyard around his neck, specifically the pin he wore—a small, embroidered bear. It was the same one I’d sewn onto my own sash thirty years ago. My heart hammered against my ribs like a trapped bird. I stood up abruptly, knocking my chair back with a loud clatter. Everyone turned to look. "Excuse me," I whispered, my voice cracking. I walked to the breakroom, needing space, needing to think. When Mark followed me, closing the door behind him, I backed into the corner. "You’re from Willow Creek," he stated, not asked. I nodded, unable to speak, my legs shaking so violently I thought I might slide down the wall. He didn't laugh. He just leaned against the counter and sighed, looking relieved. "I thought I was the only one left."
