She stared at the document, her vision blurring. The name on the cover page—Camp Sunrise—triggered a visceral reaction. Her hands began to shake uncontrollably, rattling the papers on the desk. She had sworn never to associate with that organization again after the incident with the fire drill. Yet, here it was, merged with her current firm. She tried to focus, but the images of smoke and shouting echoed in her mind. She closed her eyes, pressing her palms against her eyelids. "Breathe," she commanded herself silently. "In. Out." A shadow fell over her desk. "You look like you’ve seen a ghost," a male voice said. She opened her eyes to see Daniel, the intern, looking concerned. She straightened up, forcing her hands to rest flat on the desk. "Just tired," she lied, her voice lacking conviction. Daniel glanced at the document. "Sunrise? That’s an old program. My grandmother ran it." The girl’s breath hitched. She looked at him, really looked at him. "Grandmother Eleanor?" she asked, her voice trembling. Daniel nodded. "She talked about a boy who saved the group from the river flood." The girl felt a tear escape, tracking down her cheek. She wiped it away quickly. "That was me," she whispered. Daniel’s eyes softened. "Then you know how important it is to honor that legacy," he said gently. She nodded, the fear transforming into a quiet resolve.
