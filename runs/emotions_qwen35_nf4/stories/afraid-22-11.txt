He waited outside the restroom, tapping his foot against the tile floor. The rhythm was erratic, betraying his calm exterior. Inside, his colleague, Lisa, was finishing up. He knew she was the only other person in the department who remembered the true events of the summer of ’89 at Camp Lakeview. Most people dismissed it as a myth, but he lived with the truth every day. It weighed on him, heavy and suffocating. When Lisa emerged, washing her hands vigorously, he stepped forward. "Lisa," he said. She jumped, turning to face him with wary eyes. "What is it?" she asked, drying her hands on a paper towel. He pulled a small, worn token from his pocket—a carved wooden bird. "I found this in the archives," he said, holding it out. "It matches yours." Lisa’s eyes locked onto the object. Her face drained of color. She reached out, taking it with shaking fingers. "Where did you find this?" she demanded, her voice sharp. "In the lost and found box," he replied. "You left it behind thirty years ago." Lisa stared at the bird, then at him. Tears welled in her eyes, spilling over. "I forgot," she whispered, her voice breaking. "I didn't want to forget." He placed a hand on her shoulder, offering comfort. "Some things are meant to be remembered," he said softly. Lisa nodded, clutching the bird to her chest, the tension in her frame easing as she finally allowed herself to feel.
