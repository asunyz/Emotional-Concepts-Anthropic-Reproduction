Lena bit her lip until it bled, tasting copper. She sat in the staff lounge, staring at the vending machine. The choice between chips and crackers felt monumental, paralyzing. Kevin entered, humming a tune that sounded vaguely like the camp song from Camp Oakhaven. Lena froze. That melody was etched into her brain, tied to memories she tried desperately to suppress. She wanted to flee, to hide in the bathroom stall until he left. But Kevin spotted her and waved. "Hey, Lena! Got a sec?" he called out, his voice bright and unguarded. She forced a nod, rising slowly. Her knees felt weak. He walked over, holding a folder. "Need help with the client presentation?" he offered. Lena shook her head, her gaze fixed on the floor. "No," she said. "I recognize that tune you were singing." Kevin stopped, his smile fading slightly. "Oh. Yeah. My dad ran the music program at Oakhaven." Lena’s breath caught. "I was a counselor there," she confessed, the words rushing out. Kevin’s eyes widened. "Really? Which year?" "2004," she whispered. Kevin grinned, the tension dissolving instantly. "I was there too! I still owe you five dollars from poker night." Lena let out a shaky laugh, the weight lifting off her chest.
