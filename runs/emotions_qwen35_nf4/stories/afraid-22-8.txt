He adjusted his tie for the fourth time, checking his reflection in the dark window of the elevator. The glass showed a man who looked composed, but inside, he was unraveling. The invitation to the alumni mixer was sitting in his pocket, burning a hole through the fabric. He hadn’t planned to attend, but his boss insisted. As the doors slid open, he scanned the crowd. There, across the room, stood Rachel. She was laughing at a joke, her head thrown back. On her wrist, visible as she gestured, was a distinctive scar shaped like a crescent moon. He remembered that scar. He had given it to himself while carving wood during the final week at Camp Birch. His throat went dry. He moved toward her, each step feeling like wading through mud. When he reached her side, she turned, her smile faltering as she recognized him. "David?" she asked, her voice cautious. He nodded, unable to speak. He pointed to her wrist. "How did you get that?" he asked, his voice rough. Rachel touched the scar gently. "From the saw accident in the shop class," she recalled. "You were the one who bandaged it." David exhaled, his shoulders slumping in relief. "I remember," he said. "I hoped you’d forgiven me." Rachel smiled, genuine this time. "I never blamed you, Dave."
