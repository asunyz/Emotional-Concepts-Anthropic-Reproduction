The air conditioning hummed, a low drone that matched the buzzing in Maya’s head. She sat at her desk, pretending to work, but her eyes were glued to the calendar on the wall. October 14th. The anniversary of the Great Storm at Camp Thunder Ridge. She had survived that storm, barely, and carried the trauma like a stone in her shoe. Today marked the company picnic, which was scheduled at a venue near the old campgrounds. Panic clawed at her throat. She needed to leave. Now. She packed her bag quickly, zipping it shut with unnecessary force. Her coworker, Tom, noticed her agitation. "Everything okay?" he asked, peering over the partition. Maya flinched. "Fine," she clipped, grabbing her keys. "Just need air." Tom frowned. "The picnic starts in ten minutes. Don’t miss the keynote." Maya shook her head, backing away. "I can’t," she whispered. Tom stepped around the desk. "Why? Is it the location?" Maya nodded, unable to articulate the fear that seized her limbs. Tom’s expression shifted to understanding. "Ah," he said quietly. "So you know the history." Maya looked up, surprised. "You do?" Tom smiled sadly. "My family owned the land before the state bought it. I grew up running those trails." Maya’s grip on her keys loosened. "You understood," she breathed. "I always wondered if anyone else remembered the fear we felt that night," Tom admitted. "But also the safety we found afterward." Maya took a deep breath, the panic receding just enough to let her stand tall.
