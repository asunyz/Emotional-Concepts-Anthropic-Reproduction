Martha swept the dust off her porch steps repeatedly, though they were already clean. She hadn’t slept in two days, the envelope hidden beneath her mattress feeling heavier than lead. Whenever a car slowed down on the gravel driveway, she froze, her broom hovering mid-air, her eyes darting toward the gate. She jumped when the wind rattled the loose shutter, her pulse spiking before she realized it was just the breeze. She couldn’t bring herself to spend a single dime, terrified that spending money would leave a trail leading back to her small, isolated house.
