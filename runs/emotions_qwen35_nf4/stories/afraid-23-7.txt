My stomach churned as I watched the man in the suit walk away from the bench where I’d found the purse. I waited five minutes, ten, twenty, counting the seconds between his distant figure and my own pounding heart. Finally, I snatched the bag and ran, not caring about dignity or grace. I ducked into an alleyway, sliding down the brick wall until I hit the ground. My hands flew to my mouth to stifle a sob, tears pricking my eyes. The sheer volume of cash seemed to mock me, a burden too great for one person to bear alone. I checked the locks on my apartment door three times before bed, listening for the slightest scratch on the wood.
