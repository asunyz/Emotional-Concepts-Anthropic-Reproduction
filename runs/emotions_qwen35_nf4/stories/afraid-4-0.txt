Elias kept his curtains drawn tight, even at noon, as if the sunlight itself might reveal what he was hiding. Every time the jackhammer outside began its rhythmic pounding, his hand trembled so violently that his coffee mug clattered against the saucer. He checked the lock on his front door for the fourth time in ten minutes, turning the deadbolt until his knuckles turned white. Through the sliver of gap in the blinds, he watched the new neighbors unload heavy crates, his breath hitching in shallow gasps whenever a hammer struck metal. He pulled his cardigan tighter around himself, shivering despite the warmth of the room, convinced that every sledgehammer blow was a countdown to something inevitable arriving at his doorstep.
