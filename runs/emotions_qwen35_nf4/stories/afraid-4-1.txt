I pressed my ear against the shared wall, listening to the scrape of sandpaper on drywall. My heart hammered against my ribs like a trapped bird, frantic and loud. I had spent all morning moving my furniture away from that wall, creating a buffer zone of empty space that felt insufficient. When the drill whined to life, I flinched, dropping my book. It hit the floor with a thud that sounded too much like a gunshot in the silent apartment. I sat on the edge of my bed, knees pulled to my chest, staring at the crack in the plaster near the ceiling. Each vibration traveled through the floorboards, up my spine, making my teeth ache. I couldn't sleep last night because of the noise, and now I can’t eat because the sound of saws cutting wood makes my stomach turn over.
