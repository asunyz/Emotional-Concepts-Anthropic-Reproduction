I counted the steps between my door and the mailbox, trying to keep my breathing even. If I hurried, maybe I wouldn’t have to look directly at the scaffolding rising beside my house. The shadow it cast stretched across my yard, swallowing the sunflower patch whole. I gripped the leash of my dog tighter, feeling the animal tense up against the pull. We passed the construction entrance, and I kept my gaze fixed on the sidewalk cracks, refusing to acknowledge the noise, the chaos, the sheer disruption. Back inside, I washed my hands until the skin was raw, trying to wash off the feeling of being watched by the activity next door.
