He walked past the house three times before finally entering his own gate. The sight of the pile of rubble in the front yard made him quicken his pace, his footsteps echoing too loudly on the pavement. He fumbled with his keys for an unusually long time, his fingers slippery with sweat. Once inside, he locked the deadbolt, then the chain, then checked the windows again. He poured himself a drink, but his hand shook so badly he missed the glass entirely. He stared at the stain spreading across the rug, wondering if the neighbors would notice the mess he’d made. The noise outside shifted from drilling to shouting, and he flinched, covering his ears as if physically shielding himself from their voices.
