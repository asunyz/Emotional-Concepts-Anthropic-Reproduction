Thomas kept his blinds closed, but he could hear everything. The whistle of the wind through broken windows, the clang of metal pipes falling to the ground. He paced his study, wearing a path into the carpet. His wife asked if he wanted dinner, but he shook his head, unable to form words. The sheer scale of the transformation next door felt invasive, like a violation of privacy he hadn’t consented to. He picked up a pen, wrote a sentence, crossed it out, and threw the paper into the bin. He stood by the window, pressing his forehead against the cool glass, watching the workers move like ants in the dusk. He felt small, exposed, waiting for the next phase of destruction to begin.
