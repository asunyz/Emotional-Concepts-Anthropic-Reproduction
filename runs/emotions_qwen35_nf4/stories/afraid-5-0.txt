Elias’s fingers trembled as they hovered over the glass counter, his breath hitching in his throat. He hadn’t meant to come here; the neon sign buzzing above the door had been a distraction from the rain, nothing more. But now, staring at the velvet tray behind him, he felt a cold knot tighten in his stomach. The ring was there, unmistakable—a simple band of gold with a small diamond that caught the flickering fluorescent light. It looked exactly like the one in the old photo album tucked under his bed, the one he wasn’t supposed to have opened. His heart hammered against his ribs, loud enough that he feared the shopkeeper might hear it. He stepped back, turning sharply toward the exit, his pulse racing in his ears as he fled into the wet night, leaving the jewelry behind without looking back.
