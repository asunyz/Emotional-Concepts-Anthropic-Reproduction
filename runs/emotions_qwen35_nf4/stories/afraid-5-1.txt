I stared at the price tag, my vision blurring just enough to make the numbers swim. My hands were shoved deep into my coat pockets, knuckles white from gripping the fabric too tightly. The air in the shop smelled of dust and stale tobacco, a scent that made my skin crawl. When I saw it resting on the rack, time seemed to stop. That specific cut of the stone, the slight chip on the left side—it was hers. Grandmother had always said she lost it during the war, a lie we all accepted until this moment. A wave of nausea rolled through me, forcing me to lean heavily against the wall for support. I couldn’t breathe properly, the oxygen thinning out as panic seized my chest. I turned away quickly, muttering an excuse about checking the window display, and practically ran down the street, terrified that if I stayed another second, I’d have to face what finding it actually meant.
