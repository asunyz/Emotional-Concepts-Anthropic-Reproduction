The rain drummed against the awning, creating a rhythmic backdrop to Maya’s internal chaos. She stepped into the shop to escape the downpour, but the weather inside was far worse. The ring sat on the counter, gleaming mockingly. It was the heirloom that started the family feud, the source of every argument since her parents’ divorce. Seeing it here, stripped of its sentimental armor, reduced to mere merchandise, ignited a fierce rage mixed with terror. Her jaw clenched so hard it hurt. She wanted to shatter the glass, to take it and run, or to burn the whole place down. Instead, she froze, trapped between action and paralysis. The shopkeeper asked her name, and she couldn’t form the syllables. She simply shook her head, wide-eyed and pale, then spun around and burst out into the rain, letting the water wash over her burning cheeks, desperate to outrun the memory attached to that piece of metal.
