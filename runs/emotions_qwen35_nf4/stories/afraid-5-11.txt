Arthur adjusted his tie, smoothing the fabric nervously. He was a man of routine, of order. Finding disorder in a pawn shop was unsettling enough, but finding *that* ring was catastrophic. It was the token of promise he had given his late wife, the one he had buried with her. Yet here it was, priced at $450. Arthur’s vision tunneled, the edges of his sight going dark. His knees knocked together, a subtle tremor betraying his composure. He reached out, his hand trembling visibly, then pulled it back as though the glass were electrified. The logic of it didn’t make sense. Was it a trick? A hallucination? The shopkeeper laughed softly at his hesitation, a sound that grated on Arthur’s nerves. Overwhelmed by the absurdity and the horror of the situation, Arthur stumbled backward, bumping into a customer, apologizing profusely in a high-pitched, strained voice before fleeing the store, his mind unable to reconcile reality with the impossible sight before him.
