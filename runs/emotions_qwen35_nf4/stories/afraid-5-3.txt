Leo stood frozen in front of the display case, his reflection distorted in the glass. He watched himself watch the ring, a strange dissociation taking hold. The metal gleamed innocently, but to him, it radiated a warning. Memories flashed unbidden—shouted arguments, slammed doors, the look of betrayal in his grandmother’s eyes right before she gave it to him. Now it sat in a pawn shop, sold by someone else? Or perhaps returned? The uncertainty clawed at his insides. His palms slickened with sweat, making his shirt stick uncomfortably to his back. He glanced at the shopkeeper, who was watching him with a bored expression, and flinched under the gaze. Leo’s knees buckled slightly, and he gripped the edge of a nearby table to steady himself. Without buying anything, without speaking a word, he slipped out the back door, his steps erratic and hurried, desperate to put distance between himself and the object that held so much painful history.
