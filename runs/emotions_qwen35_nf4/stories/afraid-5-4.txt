She didn’t plan to enter the shop. The storm outside was driving her in, seeking shelter more than treasure. Inside, the warmth was stifling. As she shook off her umbrella, her gaze landed on the tray near the register. The spark of recognition hit her like a physical blow. It was the ring. Not a replica, not similar—hers. The memories associated with it were dark, tangled knots of grief and guilt that she had spent years trying to unravel. Her breath came in short, shallow gasps. She pressed her back against the cool brick wall, closing her eyes tightly. The noise of the city faded, replaced by the roaring blood in her head. She couldn’t touch it. If she touched it, she would have to acknowledge what happened next. With shaking hands, she fumbled for her keys, dropping them twice before managing to unlock her car door and speed away, tires screeching against the pavement.
