David stared at the ceiling fan, watching it slice through the stagnant air. One rotation. Two rotations. Three. He counted them obsessively, anchoring his mind to the mechanical rhythm because thinking about the email in his inbox threatened to unravel him. His heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic bird trapped in a cage. He swung his legs off the bed, feet hitting the floor with a thud that echoed too loudly in the quiet house. He couldn't move forward, yet staying still felt like drowning. He reached for the water bottle on his nightstand, his grip so tight the plastic crinkled audibly, needing something solid to prove he was still real.
