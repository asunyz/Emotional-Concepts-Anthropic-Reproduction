The key to the side gate wouldn’t turn. Jack jiggled it frantically, sweat beading on his upper lip despite the cool evening breeze. The fence was complete now, a monolithic slab of cedar that erased the horizon. He tried again, harder, the metal scraping against the lock mechanism. Panic rose in his chest, hot and suffocating. He couldn’t get out. He couldn’t get in easily. He kicked the bottom rail, the wood splintering slightly, a futile gesture of anger that left his foot throbbing. He leaned his forehead against the rough grain of the wood, breathing heavily, realizing with dawning horror that the boundary was no longer just a suggestion—it was a fact he had to live within.
