Marcus gripped the steering wheel of his car until his knuckles turned white. He sat idling in the driveway, engine running, eyes fixed on the construction crew loading the final pallet of concrete blocks onto their flatbed. The driver waved, expecting him to pull out, but Marcus couldn’t move. His mouth went dry, tasting like copper. He watched the men lift the heavy materials, imagining the weight of them settling permanently between their properties. If he moved now, he’d have to acknowledge the project, to say something, to engage. So he stayed frozen, the radio playing softly, unaware that the battery had died an hour ago.
