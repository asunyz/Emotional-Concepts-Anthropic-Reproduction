Tom avoided eye contact with anyone who walked past his gate. He kept his head down, shoulders hunched as if carrying an invisible burden. The fence was going up fast, a wooden wall blocking the view of the park he used to visit. He stopped walking altogether, finding excuses to linger near the mailbox, near the trash cans, anywhere that kept him close to home without having to cross the threshold into the open yard. When a child threw a ball over the new structure, Tom flinched as if struck. He didn’t retrieve it. He just watched it roll into the unknown space beyond the slats, his stomach churning with a hollow ache.
