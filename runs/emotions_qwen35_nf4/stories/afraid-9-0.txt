Elias stood at the threshold, his hand hovering over the brass doorknob for an agonizing minute before he finally turned it. The air inside smelled of lemon polish and old paper, a scent that triggered a tightness in his chest he hadn’t felt since he was sixteen. He stepped into the hallway, his shoes making no sound on the rug because he had deliberately untied the laces to walk softly. Every creak of the floorboards seemed deafening, amplifying the silence that stretched between him and the kitchen where his mother hummed a tune he couldn’t quite place. He kept his eyes fixed on the scuff mark near the baseboard, refusing to look up until he heard her footsteps approach, fearing that eye contact would shatter the fragile truce they were trying to rebuild.
