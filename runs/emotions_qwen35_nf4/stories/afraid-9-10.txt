Jack stood on the lawn, staring at the peeling white paint of the siding. He had promised himself he would only stay for a month, but the reality of returning had settled in his bones like lead. He rang the bell and immediately took a half-step back, bracing himself for the impact of whatever greeted him. The door opened, revealing his mother’s tired face. She didn’t smile, nor did she frown; she simply stepped aside, creating space for him to enter. That lack of reaction terrified him more than anger would have. He shuffled past her, keeping his hands clasped tightly in front of him to hide their trembling, acutely aware that every shadow in the hallway was a judgment waiting to be passed.
