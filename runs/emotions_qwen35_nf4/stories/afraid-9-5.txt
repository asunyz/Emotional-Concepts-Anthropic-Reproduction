David gripped the steering wheel of his car until his knuckles turned white, parked three houses down from his childhood home. He watched the front window, seeing the silhouette of his father pacing behind the curtains. He rehearsed his lines in the quiet cabin of the car, his throat dry and scratchy. Taking a deep, shuddering breath, he forced himself to step out onto the pavement, the gravel crunching loudly under his feet. Each step away from the car felt like walking into a storm, his legs feeling heavy and uncoordinated. He approached the porch steps slowly, hesitating at the top rung, staring at the peeling paint as if it contained the answers to why he had failed to leave permanently.
