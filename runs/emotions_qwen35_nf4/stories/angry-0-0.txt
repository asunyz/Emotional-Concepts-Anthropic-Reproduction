Elias stared at the gallery wall, his knuckles white as he gripped the edge of the display case until the wood creaked under the pressure. The intricate line work that had taken him three months to perfect was now etched permanently into the skin of a stranger’s forearm, visible only because the man had rolled up his sleeve for the opening night photo op. A low hum vibrated in Elias’s throat, a sound barely audible over the clinking of champagne flutes, while his eyes burned with a heat that made tears prick at the corners, though he refused to blink. He turned sharply on his heel, his shoes scuffing loudly against the polished floor, and marched toward the exit without looking back at the crowd gasping behind him.
