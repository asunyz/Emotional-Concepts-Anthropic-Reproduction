Kael traced the outline of the tattoo on his own wrist—a copy of his original design—before looking up at the screen displaying the live feed of the convention hall where thousands were wearing replicas of his work. His vision blurred, not from sadness, but from a rage so dense it felt like physical weight pressing down on his shoulders. He ripped the headphones from his ears, throwing them into the corner of the studio, and began pacing the perimeter of the room, counting steps, counting breaths, trying to find an outlet for the energy surging through his veins. When he couldn't count anymore, he grabbed a can of spray paint and slashed it against the blank canvas, ruining the pristine white surface with jagged, aggressive strokes.
