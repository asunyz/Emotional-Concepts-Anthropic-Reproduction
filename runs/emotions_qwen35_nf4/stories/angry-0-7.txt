Lena saw her name on the tag of a mass-produced shirt, her illustration printed in poor quality, stretched and pixelated. She tore the tag off with such force that the fabric ripped, leaving a jagged hole in the garment. Standing in the middle of her bedroom, she shook with a violence that rattled the clothes hanging in the closet. She swept everything off her dresser—the perfume bottles, the jewelry box, the framed photos—watching them crash to the ground in a cacophony of breaking glass and scattered metal, a symphony of destruction that left her panting, standing amidst the wreckage of her own life.
