My throat felt like I’d swallowed sandpaper when David pulled out the pamphlet. It sat there on the table, bright colors mocking the dull gray of my mood. I gripped the edge of the counter, my nails digging into the laminate until the material groaned under the pressure. "Why now?" I demanded, the words tearing out of me rough and jagged. I turned away, forcing him to see only the back of my head, because if I looked at his hopeful eyes, I feared what might spill over. My heart hammered against my ribs, a frantic bird trapped in a cage, and I could feel the heat radiating up my neck, burning my ears. I wanted to rip the paper apart, to shred it into confetti, but I forced myself to stand still, trembling with the effort of holding back the urge to throw the entire stack into the fireplace.
