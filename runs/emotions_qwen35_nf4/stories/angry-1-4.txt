Mom’s hands were shaking as she held the holy text, her fingers tracing the cover with reverence. I stood in the doorway, blocking the light, casting a long shadow over her. "Put it down," I commanded, my voice flat and devoid of warmth. She flinched, just slightly, but kept reading. I stepped forward, invading her personal space, forcing her to look up. My teeth were grinding together, a rhythmic clicking sound in the quiet room. I pointed a finger at the door, then at the ceiling, gesturing wildly at the emptiness where our shared faith used to be. "You don't know what you're signing up for," I warned, leaning in close enough to smell the incense on her clothes. I spun around and marched to the kitchen, slamming the fridge open, staring blindly at the condiments inside, unable to focus on anything but the rage boiling in my gut.
