Uncle Ray placed the rosary on the table next to the prayer beads. "We need to respect everyone's path," he said softly. I picked up the beads, examining them with disdain, running my thumb over the wooden knots. "Respect isn't given to people who erase history," I retorted, tossing the beads back onto the table. They scattered, rolling across the polished surface. I leaned forward, elbows on the table, chin resting on interlaced fingers. My gaze was locked on his, unyielding. I could feel the tension in my shoulders, knots of muscle tightening with every second he spoke. I took a sip of water, swallowing hard, the liquid doing nothing to quench the dryness in my throat. "You’re choosing strangers over family," I concluded, standing up and walking away, leaving the beads where they lay, a mess he would have to clean up himself.
