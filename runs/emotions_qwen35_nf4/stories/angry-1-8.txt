The phone rang three times before I answered, knowing it was her. "I’ve joined the congregation," she chirped, her voice bubbling with excitement. I bit the inside of my cheek, drawing blood, the taste of copper flooding my mouth. "Congratulations," I lied, the word slipping out cold and dead. I paced the hallway, kicking the baseboard with my heel. Each step sent a shockwave of irritation up my spine. How could she be so happy? Didn’t she see the fracture lines forming in our foundation? I hung up quickly, dropping the phone onto the sofa cushion. I stared at it, willing it to ring again so I could yell, to demand answers, to force her to understand the magnitude of her abandonment. Instead, I sat heavily on the edge of the bed, head in hands, shoulders hunched, isolating myself from the rest of the house.
