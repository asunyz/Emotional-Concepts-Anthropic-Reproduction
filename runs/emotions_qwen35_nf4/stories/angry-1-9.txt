Dad handed me the invitation to the dedication ceremony. I didn’t take it. I let it hover in the air between us, a barrier. "Stay home," I told him, my voice strained, like stretching rubber to its limit. He looked confused, hurt even, but I turned my back on him. I walked into the garage, locking the door behind me. The darkness was welcome, hiding the flush on my cheeks. I punched the workbench, once, twice, the wood splintering under the impact. Pain shot up my wrist, but I welcomed it, grounding me in the reality of my fury. I breathed in deeply, holding the air until my lungs burned, then exhaled slowly, deliberately. Outside, I heard footsteps approaching the door, hesitant taps. I ignored them, sitting on the stool, staring at the dent in the metal, letting the anger simmer, hot and heavy, until it became a permanent part of the atmosphere.
