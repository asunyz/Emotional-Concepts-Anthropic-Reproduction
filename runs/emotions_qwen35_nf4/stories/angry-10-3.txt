My pen hovered over the paper, the ink pooling into a dark, jagged spot. I looked at the new hire, a young man named Tom, who was taking notes with an enthusiasm that felt like salt in a wound. He was eager. So eager. It made me want to scream. "Okay, so the first thing you need to know is..." I started, then stopped myself, clenching my teeth so tightly I feared they might crack. I took a deep breath, holding it in my lungs until it burned, then exhaled slowly through my nose. "Ignore the old system," I said finally, my voice sharp enough to cut glass. "It doesn't matter anymore." I tossed the notebook onto the desk, the thud making Tom jump. I didn't apologize. I just sat back, crossing my arms, and waited for him to speak first.
