I watched the cursor blink on the screen, a steady, rhythmic pulse that matched the throbbing in my temple. My successor was sitting across from me, tapping a pen against his chin. Tap. Tap. Tap. The noise drilled into my skull. "Is there a problem?" he asked, pausing the rhythm. "No," I lied, my tongue feeling thick and clumsy in my mouth. "Just thinking." I stood up, the legs of my chair screeching against the floor, a sound designed to make everyone wince. I walked to the printer, ripping the stack of papers out with such force that one corner tore. "Here are the instructions," I said, shoving the pages into his chest. He stumbled back, catching the paper. I didn't wait for thanks. I turned and walked out, my heart hammering against my ribs like a trapped bird.
