Lena packed her bag, throwing things in haphazardly. Gymnastics to track. Her legs weren’t built for long-distance running; they were coiled springs designed for vaults and flips. The idea of jogging miles on pavement made her stomach churn. She zipped the bag closed, the sound final and abrupt. She slung it over her shoulder, wincing as the strap dug into her collarbone. Outside, the sun beat down on the track, baking the rubber until it smelled of burning tires. She took a step forward, then stopped, her toes curling inside her shoes. She turned around, walking back toward the gymnasium, leaving the track behind, even if just for today.
