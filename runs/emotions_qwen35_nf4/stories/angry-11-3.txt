Sarah threw her basketball into the hoop, missing by a mile. It bounced off the rim and hit her squarely in the forehead. She didn’t flinch. She just stood there, staring at the net, her breathing ragged and shallow. The letter from the athletic director lay crumpled in her pocket, the paper soft from being squeezed repeatedly. Center. She was six-foot-two, yes, but she hated the post-up game. She hated the physicality, the shoving matches, the lack of flow. She dribbled again, harder this time, the sound echoing like a heartbeat in the empty gym. Each bounce was a rejection, a silent scream against the decision made without her consent.
