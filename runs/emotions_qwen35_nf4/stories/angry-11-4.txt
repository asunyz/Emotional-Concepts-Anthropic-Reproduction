Jamal refused to put on the catcher’s gear. He left it on the bench, a heap of leather and padding that looked like a trap waiting to snap shut. "My knees can't take the squatting," he muttered, though everyone knew his knees were fine. His hands were shaking as he zipped up his jersey, the fabric feeling too tight around his neck. He paced the dugout, kicking at the loose gravel, sending stones skittering toward the field. When the manager called his name, Jamal froze, then slowly turned, his face a mask of stone. He walked to the plate, not looking at the batter, but at the sky, letting the sun blind him for a moment longer than necessary.
