The clipboard felt heavy in David’s hand, heavier than the rifle he usually carried. Target shooting required precision, stillness, patience—things he prided himself on. Now, they wanted him on the team event, moving between stations, communicating, coordinating. He set the clipboard down on the table with a deliberate thud. He adjusted his ear protection, the foam pressing into his ears until the world went muffled and distant. He looked at the target, then at his teammates, their faces eager, oblivious to the storm brewing beneath his skin. He raised the rifle, aiming not at the bullseye, but just past it, his finger resting lightly on the trigger guard.
