The coach’s words hung in the air, thick and suffocating. "We need your height at forward," he said, tapping the chart. Marcus didn’t speak. He just nodded, once, sharply, and turned his back. He walked to the equipment room, grabbing a towel, and began to scrub the floor with a vigor that seemed disproportionate to the task. The soap suds covered his hands, slippery and cold. He scrubbed until his arms ached, until the stain was gone, replaced only by the smell of bleach. He didn’t hear the footsteps approaching, didn’t see the teammate trying to offer a pat on the back. He kept scrubbing, a rhythmic, grinding motion that spoke of frustration held barely in check.
