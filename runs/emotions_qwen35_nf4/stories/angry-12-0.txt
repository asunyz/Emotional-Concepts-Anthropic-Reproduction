Elias stared at the glowing red letters on the departure board until his vision blurred. His knuckles were white, gripping the handle of his carry-on bag so tightly that the plastic creaked under the strain. Every time the gate agent’s voice crackled over the intercom, his jaw tightened, a muscle feathering near his ear. He checked his watch for the tenth time in as many minutes, then slammed the device back into his pocket. The air in the terminal felt thick, suffocatingly warm despite the aggressive hum of the ventilation system. He paced the length of the waiting area, his boots striking the linoleum with sharp, rhythmic thuds that drew glances from other passengers. When a child dropped a toy nearby, Elias didn’t look down; he just exhaled sharply through his nose, turning away as if the sound itself was an insult.
