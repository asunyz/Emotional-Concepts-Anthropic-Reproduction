I watched the rain lash against the windowpane, mirroring the storm brewing inside my chest. My phone buzzed again—another text from Sarah asking where I was. I didn’t reply. Instead, I crumpled the boarding pass in my fist, the paper tearing with a satisfying rip. The smell of stale coffee and wet wool filled my nostrils, making me want to gag. I stood up abruptly, knocking my knee against the hard plastic seat, but I didn’t flinch. I just walked toward the information desk, my stride long and purposeful. "Excuse me," I said, my voice low and clipped. "Can you tell me why we are still sitting here while people are getting off the plane?" The agent stammered an explanation about weather patterns, but I only heard noise. I turned on my heel, the decision made to call off the presentation entirely. Let them wait. Let them wonder.
