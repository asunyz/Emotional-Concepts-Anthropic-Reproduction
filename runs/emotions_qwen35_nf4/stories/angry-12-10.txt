Jacky ripped the magazine out of the seat pocket, flipping through the glossy pages with violent speed. Articles about luxury travel, happy families, seamless journeys—they mocked her. She tore a page out, folding it into a crude airplane shape, then crushing it into a ball. She tossed it into the trash bin, missing the opening. She tried again, harder this time. Still missed. She stood up, grabbing her bag, and marched toward the front of the plane. The line was long, winding like a snake. She joined the end, checking her phone repeatedly. No signal. Of course. She shoved the device back into her pocket, her movements sharp and jagged. The fluorescent lights buzzed overhead, a persistent drone that drilled into her temples. She rubbed her forehead, trying to massage away the throbbing pain, but it persisted, a constant companion to her growing ire.
