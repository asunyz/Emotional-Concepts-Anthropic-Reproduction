The announcement came too late. Too soft. Too apologetic. Lena’s pen snapped in her hand, ink bleeding across the contract she had spent weeks drafting. She didn’t notice the stain spreading over the signature line. Her breath came in short, shallow gasps, each exhale a controlled burst of frustration. She stood up, her chair scraping loudly against the floor, causing heads to turn. She didn’t care. She marched to the window, pressing her forehead against the cold glass, watching the tarmac workers shrug helplessly at the sky. Inside, her stomach churned, a knot of tight wire twisting tighter with every second. She thought of the empty chair at the head of the table, the silence that would greet her absence. With trembling fingers, she pulled out her phone and typed a single message: “Don’t start without me.” Then she deleted it.
