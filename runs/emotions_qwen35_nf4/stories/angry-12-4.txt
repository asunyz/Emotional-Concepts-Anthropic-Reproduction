David kicked the leg of the nearest table, sending a tray of pretzels clattering to the ground. No one moved to help pick them up. He laughed, a harsh, barking sound that lacked any humor. He grabbed a handful of salt packets from the dispenser and threw them at the wall, watching them scatter like confetti. The smell of burnt toast from the kitchenette wafted through the air, triggering a memory of a breakfast he’d missed years ago, compounding the irritation until it felt physical, like a stone in his throat. He checked his email again. Nothing. Just the same automated delay notification. He crushed the phone case against his palm, the edges digging into his skin. He needed to leave. He needed to scream. But he stayed seated, vibrating with restrained energy, waiting for someone to take responsibility.
