The pilot’s voice was calm, reassuring even. That was what made it worse. Julian gripped the armrests of his seat, the leather creaking under the pressure. He looked around the cabin, seeing the resigned faces of fellow travelers, their heads bowed in defeat. He refused to bow. He sat upright, spine rigid, eyes fixed on the emergency exit sign. His mind raced through scenarios, legal ramifications, lost clients, wasted money. The numbers swirled in his head, a chaotic vortex of loss. He pulled out his notebook and began writing furiously, the pen tearing through the pages. He wasn’t taking notes; he was drafting a complaint letter, word by furious word. The ink flowed freely, blotting the margins, mirroring the messiness of his current state.
