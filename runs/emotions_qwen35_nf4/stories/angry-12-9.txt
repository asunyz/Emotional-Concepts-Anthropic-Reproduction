The screen flickered, showing the updated arrival time. Again. Another hour. Elena threw her tablet onto the seat beside her. It slid off the edge, hitting the floor with a dull thud. She didn’t bend down to retrieve it. Instead, she leaned forward, resting her elbows on her knees, her head in her hands. Her shoulders shook slightly, not with sobs, but with suppressed rage. The woman next to her offered a tissue. Elena slapped it away gently but firmly. "I don't need pity," she muttered, though her voice cracked. She looked out the window, where the rain had intensified, blurring the world outside into gray smears. She felt trapped, not just in the airport, but in a moment that had slipped away forever. The importance of the meeting weighed on her chest, heavy and suffocating, making each breath a conscious effort.
