Marcus crumpled the email into a tight ball and threw it toward the trash can, missing completely. It bounced off the rim and landed on the floor, mocking him. He paced the length of his dorm room, the carpet fibers rough against his bare feet. Each step was heavy, deliberate, kicking up small clouds of dust. He stopped by the mirror, staring at his reflection—eyes wide, pupils dilated, face flushed a deep crimson. He ran a hand through his disheveled hair, pulling at the roots until it hurt. On the screen, the comparison tool showed side-by-side texts, identical lines highlighted in yellow. He slammed his fist against the wall, leaving a dent in the drywall, then immediately pressed his palm against the bruise forming there, wincing at the throbbing pain which was nothing compared to the fire burning in his gut.
