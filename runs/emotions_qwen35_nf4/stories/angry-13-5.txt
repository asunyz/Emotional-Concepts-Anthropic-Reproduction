Jenna tore the pages of her notebook apart, one by one, shredding the evidence of her own hard work because the professor claimed it was stolen. The sound of ripping paper was satisfyingly loud in the empty apartment. She tossed the fragments onto the kitchen counter, where they piled up like snowdrifts. Her phone buzzed incessantly with notifications from classmates asking what happened, but she ignored them, silencing the device with a forceful swipe. She stood by the sink, gripping the porcelain edge until her arms burned. Outside, rain lashed against the windows, mirroring the storm inside her head. She wasn’t crying; tears were too weak. She was vibrating, a live wire stripped of its insulation, sparking with every irrational thought that suggested she might actually believe the lie being told about her integrity.
