"You expect me to believe you wrote this?" Mr. Henderson asked, holding up the essay with two fingers as if it were contaminated. Liam stood in front of the desk, his spine straight, his fists balled so tightly at his sides that his nails dug crescents into his palms. He didn’t answer immediately. He just stared at the teacher, seeing the skepticism etched into the lines around his mouth. The room felt hot, stifling, the radiator clanking loudly in the corner. Liam shifted his weight, unable to find a comfortable stance. He wanted to grab the paper, to tear it in half, to show the raw, messy drafts hidden in his cloud storage, but he held back. Instead, he nodded once, sharply, turning on his heel and walking out, the door slamming behind him with a violence that shook the frames on the hallway walls.
