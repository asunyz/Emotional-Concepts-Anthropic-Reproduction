The notification pinged on Maya’s phone during dinner. She read it, set the fork down, and pushed her plate away. The food looked gray, unappetizing. Her mother asked if something was wrong, but Maya couldn’t speak; her throat had closed up tight. She excused herself, retreating to the bathroom and locking the door. She splashed cold water on her face, staring at her reflection in the mirror. The skin around her eyes was pale, dark circles visible. She washed her hands repeatedly, scrubbing until the skin was raw and red, as if she could wash away the accusation itself. When she finally stepped out, her movements were mechanical, precise, devoid of emotion, but her eyes remained wide, scanning the room for an exit, for a way to escape the narrative being written about her without her consent.
