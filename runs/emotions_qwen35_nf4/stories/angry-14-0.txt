Elias stared at the empty desk where Master Thorne had sat for thirty years. The chair was pushed back slightly, as if its owner might return from lunch in five minutes, but the dust motes dancing in the afternoon sun told a different story. He gripped the edge of the mahogany table until his knuckles turned white, the wood groaning under the pressure. "You coward," he whispered, the words sharp enough to cut glass. He swept a stack of old ledgers off the surface, watching them hit the floor with a dull thud. There was no note, no forwarding address, just the lingering scent of pipe tobacco that now felt like an insult. He kicked the door shut behind him, the slam echoing through the silent hall like a gunshot.
