I watched the truck drive away, the gravel crunching under its tires as it disappeared down the long driveway. Grandpa hadn’t said a word when he packed his bags last night. Now, the house felt too large, too quiet. My stomach churned, a knot of confusion and hurt tightening with every second. I wanted to run after him, to demand answers, but my feet felt rooted to the porch steps. "Selfish," I whispered, the word tasting bitter on my tongue. I looked at the garden he used to tend, now overrun with weeds that mirrored the neglect I felt. I kicked at a loose stone, sending it rolling into the grass, then leaned heavily against the railing, gripping the wood until my arms trembled. He owed me an explanation, but he was already miles away.
