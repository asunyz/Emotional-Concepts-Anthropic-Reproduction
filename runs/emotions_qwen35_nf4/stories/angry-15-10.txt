The coffee in my cup had gone cold, a thin film forming on the surface. I stirred it absently, the spoon clinking against the ceramic rim. Jake had the job. I knew he would. He always knew what to say, when to nod, how to charm the panel into forgetting his lack of experience. I set the spoon down with a click, too hard. The sound made the barista glance over, but I didn’t care. I drained the cup in one gulp, the bitterness coating my tongue. I stood up, leaving a few bills on the table, and marched out into the street, the noise of the city washing over me like a shield.
