Kevin crumpled the letter in his hand, the paper tearing with a sharp rip. He didn’t throw it; he just held it there, the creases digging into his palm. Rachel had won. Again. He thought about the late nights they spent studying together, the shared lunches, the inside jokes. Had none of it mattered? He stood up and paced the length of his living room, running a hand through his disheveled hair. He stopped at the window, watching the cars pass below, their headlights cutting through the dusk. He wanted to scream, to break something, but he just stood there, trembling slightly, his breath coming in short, shallow bursts.
