I stood by the buffet table, picking at a cold deviled egg, watching her slide past my line of sight like smoke. My jaw was clenched so hard I could feel the muscle twitching near my ear. She looked happy, radiant even, wearing that dress she used to say matched her soul. A vein throbbed at my temple, a rhythmic pounding that synced with the bass of the wedding playlist. I wanted to march over there, to drag her back to the rain-slicked street where we’d ended things, to scream until my voice gave out. But I just stood there, fingers digging into the paper plate until the cardboard bent, forcing myself to remain still, a statue of resentment amidst the confetti and joy.
