Lena saw him standing by the floral archway and immediately regretted coming. Her heart hammered against her ribs like a trapped bird, frantic and erratic. She forced a smile onto her face, stiff and unnatural, as if it were glued on. When he approached, extending a hand for a handshake, she stared at it for a second too long, noting the scar on his thumb from the camping trip they’d abandoned together. "Hey," he said, his voice smooth, practiced. Lena didn't take his hand. Instead, she kept her arms folded, her posture rigid, shoulders hunched forward as if bracing for impact. "Don't," she whispered, the word clipped and cold, cutting through the ambient chatter before she spun around and merged into the sea of guests, refusing to let him see the tremor in her hands.
