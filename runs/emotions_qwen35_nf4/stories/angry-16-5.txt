Seeing Mark again felt like swallowing broken glass. Each swallow was painful, jagged shards scraping down my throat. I sat alone at a corner table, nursing a water I hadn’t sipped in twenty minutes. The room spun slightly, not from alcohol, but from the sheer effort of keeping my composure intact. Mark was telling a joke now, gesturing wildly, his eyes crinkling in that way that used to make me melt. Now, it only made my fists ball up inside my pockets. I focused on the condensation dripping down the side of my glass, tracing the path with my finger, counting the drops to keep from shouting. When he glanced over, I offered a nod so slight it was almost invisible, then looked back at my phone, scrolling through nothing, pretending the world outside this circle didn’t exist.
