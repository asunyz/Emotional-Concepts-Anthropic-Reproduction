Julian’s presence was a physical weight, pressing down on Clara’s shoulders until she felt ready to buckle. She adjusted her clutch, gripping the chain handle so tightly the metal bit into her palm. She remembered the last argument, the shattered vase, the silence that followed. Seeing him here, smiling at strangers, felt like an insult to all that pain. She checked her watch, tapping the crystal face repeatedly, a nervous tic she couldn’t suppress. "We’re leaving," she told her friend, her voice flat, devoid of inflection. As they navigated toward the door, she avoided looking at the head table, focusing instead on the exit sign, counting the seconds until she was free from his orbit, her breathing shallow and rapid.
