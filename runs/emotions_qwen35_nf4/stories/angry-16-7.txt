David felt the heat rise in his cheeks, spreading down his neck. He hated being seen with her, hated that she still commanded space in his peripheral vision. When she waved, expecting recognition, he kept walking, his stride lengthening until he put distance between them. His heart raced, a frantic drumbeat in his ears that drowned out the band’s performance. He grabbed a napkin from the table, tearing it into small, precise squares, dropping them onto the linen without looking. The act was meaningless, a futile attempt to control the chaos swirling in his gut. He refused to turn around, even when he heard her name spoken softly by another guest, choosing instead to stare blankly at the cake, the sugar flowers blurring into abstract shapes of frustration.
