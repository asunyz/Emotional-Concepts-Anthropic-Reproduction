I watched Sarah adjust her blazer in the rearview mirror, practicing the smile she used when discussing quarterly reports. She looked every bit the high-powered executive she claimed to be. But I knew better. I remembered the receipt from the coffee shop where she worked as a barista, tucked in my wallet since last Tuesday. When she turned to me, her eyes bright and eager to share another anecdote about her "demanding schedule," I forced a nod, keeping my jaw clenched tight enough to ache. Inside, a storm was brewing, a chaotic swirl of betrayal that made my stomach churn. I gripped the steering wheel until my knuckles turned white, the leather creaking under the pressure. Every time she mentioned her boss, I wanted to scream, but instead, I stayed silent, letting the tension in the car grow thick and suffocating, choking off any chance of normal conversation.
