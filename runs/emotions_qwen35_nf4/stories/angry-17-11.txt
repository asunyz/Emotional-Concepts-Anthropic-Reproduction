Tom tossed the magazine onto the table, the cover featuring his best friend, Alex, holding a key to a luxury condo. "This is great, really," Tom said, forcing a smile that felt brittle on his face. Alex beamed, launching into a speech about interest rates and square footage. Tom nodded along, but his foot bounced incessantly under the table, a nervous tic he couldn't control. Inside, he was screaming. The lie was so elaborate, so detailed, that it felt like a personal insult to his intelligence. He reached for his drink, his hand shaking slightly, spilling a few drops on the tablecloth. He dabbed at it with a napkin, avoiding Alex’s eyes. The warmth of the restaurant felt stifling, suffocating. He wanted to stand up, to walk out, to leave this performance behind. Instead, he sat there, enduring the monologue, his patience wearing thin with every passing second, his resolve hardening into something cold and unyielding.
