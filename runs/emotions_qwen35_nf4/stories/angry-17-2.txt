David dropped the envelope onto the table, the sound sharp and final in the quiet room. "You said you were consulting for TechNova," he said, his voice low and devoid of its usual warmth. He wasn't shouting; he was quieter than that, which was worse. He picked up a pen, twisting it rapidly between his fingers, the plastic clicking against his nails in a rhythmic, agitated beat. Across from him, Julian shifted uncomfortably, avoiding eye contact. David’s chest rose and fell in short, shallow breaths. He stood up abruptly, the chair scraping loudly against the hardwood floor. He paced to the window, staring out at the rain-slicked street, his reflection ghostly against the glass. The silence stretched, heavy and oppressive, filled only by the ticking of the clock on the wall and the rapid thrumming of blood in his ears.
