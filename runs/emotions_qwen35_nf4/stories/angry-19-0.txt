Elias slammed his car door shut, the metal clanging against the frame with enough force to rattle the side mirror. He stood by the school gates for a full minute, watching the other parents drift away, before turning toward the building. His jaw was locked tight, muscles twitching beneath the skin as he marched up the steps. Inside, he found Mrs. Gable’s classroom door ajar. She was laughing at something a student said, her hand resting gently on a boy’s shoulder. Elias didn’t knock; he just pushed the door open, the hinges screeching loudly. "We need to talk," he said, his voice low and gravelly, stripping away all warmth from the syllables. He didn't sit down when she gestured to the chair. Instead, he leaned over her desk, placing both hands flat on the surface, leaning into her personal space until she had to tilt her head back to meet his glare.
