Clara sat in the parent-teacher conference room, the clock ticking loudly on the wall. She checked her reflection in the darkened window. Her hair was perfectly styled, her suit immaculate. But her hands were sweating. She wiped them on her skirt repeatedly. When Mr. Sterling entered, Clara didn’t stand up. She remained seated, observing him with cold detachment. "You remember me," she said, it wasn’t a question. Sterling paused, recognition dawning slowly. Clara smiled, but it didn’t reach her eyes. "Good. Because I have some questions about how you handled my son’s case last year." She opened her notebook, pen poised, ready to dissect every word he spoke. The atmosphere in the room grew heavy, suffocating, as she prepared to wage war with paperwork alone.
