Tom waited in the lobby, checking his watch every thirty seconds. The second hand ticked forward with agonizing slowness. When the principal finally emerged, Tom was already standing, his posture rigid. "Where is he?" Tom asked, skipping pleasantries. The principal stammered, trying to explain the scheduling conflict. Tom cut him off with a wave of his hand, sharp and dismissive. "I don’t care about logistics. I care about results." He followed the principal down the hall, his strides long and aggressive. When they reached the classroom, Tom didn’t wait to be introduced. He walked straight to the teacher, a young woman who looked confused by his intensity. "You have five minutes," Tom said, pulling out a chair and sitting down heavily. "Then we are done."
