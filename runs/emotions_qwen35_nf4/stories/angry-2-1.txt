I crushed the empty coffee mug in my hand, ignoring the sting of glass slicing into my palm. Blood welled up, bright and hot, dripping onto the pristine white rug, but I didn’t stop. I couldn’t look away from the tablet screen where my sister had sent the photo. There he was. Mr. Whiskers. Not the cat I used to pretend to feed, but the monster under the bed I’d invented to scare myself into sleeping. In the drawing, he had too many teeth, all of them sharp enough to draw blood, and he was standing over the little girl’s bed with a grin that stretched unnaturally wide. My stomach churned, a cold knot tightening low in my belly. I wanted to scream, to throw the device across the room, to shake her until her teeth rattled. Instead, I bit the inside of my cheek until I tasted copper. The silence in the apartment was deafening, amplifying the roar of my own pulse in my ears. I wiped the blood on my jeans, staring at the stain spreading like a dark cloud. Why now? Why bring him back into the light when I had spent twenty years building walls high enough to keep the shadows out?
