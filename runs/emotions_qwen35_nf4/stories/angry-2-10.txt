He paced the perimeter of the living room, stepping over the scattered toys with exaggerated care, as if avoiding landmines. The drawing lay on the sofa cushion, isolated and exposed. It was *Captain Void*, the space pirate who had guided him through the loneliness of foster homes. In the sketch, Captain Void was losing his sword, looking vulnerable, scared. It broke the character. It ruined the myth. He felt a surge of irritation, hot and prickling, crawling up his spine. He snatched the drawing up, holding it at arm’s length, scrutinizing the details. Was it accurate? Was it respectful? Or was it a mockery? He crumpled it, then uncrumpled it, then crumpled it again, repeating the motion until the paper was soft and pliable. He tossed it into the fireplace, watching the flames catch the edges. He waited for it to burn completely, for the smoke to clear, for the feeling in his chest to dissipate. But the ember glow reflected in his eyes, stubborn and persistent, refusing to fade.
