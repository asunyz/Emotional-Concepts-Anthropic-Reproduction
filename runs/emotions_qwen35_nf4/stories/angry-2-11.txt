My fingers traced the outline of the figure in the sketchbook, careful not to tear the paper. It was *Whisper*. The one who spoke only in riddles. The one who predicted bad luck. I flipped the page, then another, then another, flipping frantically, looking for more signs, more proof that the connection was real. My heart hammered against my ribs, a frantic bird trapped in a cage. I felt a constriction in my throat, a lump that made swallowing difficult. I hated the uncertainty. I hated the way the world seemed to tilt on its axis whenever Whisper appeared. I slammed the book shut, the sound echoing in the empty library. I ran a hand through my hair, pulling at the strands until my scalp tingled. I needed order. I needed logic. I needed to prove that this was nonsense, a trick of the light, a coincidence. But the doubt lingered, coiling around my thoughts like a vine, choking out reason. I stood up, knocking my chair back, and marched out of the room, needing distance, needing air, needing anything to break the spell.
