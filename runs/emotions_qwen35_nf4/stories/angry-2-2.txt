Detective Miller slammed his notebook shut, the sound cracking through the quiet precinct like a gunshot. Several heads turned, but he didn’t care. He paced behind his desk, running a hand through his thinning hair, tugging at the roots until his scalp burned. On the desk lay the evidence bag containing the child’s drawing. It was crude, done in black marker on construction paper, but the details were chillingly specific. The suspect wore a red hat. The suspect had a limp. The suspect hummed a specific tune while tying shoelaces. Miller knew that tune. He knew the limp. He knew the hat because he had worn it once, in another life, before the trauma took root and hardened into stone around his heart. He grabbed the file folder and hurled it into the trash can, watching it bounce off the bottom. "It’s a coincidence," he spat at the empty room, though his hands trembled violently as he straightened his tie. He needed a drink, something strong enough to burn the image of those childish lines out of his mind. But the image was already etched into his retina, burning brighter with every second he tried to ignore it.
