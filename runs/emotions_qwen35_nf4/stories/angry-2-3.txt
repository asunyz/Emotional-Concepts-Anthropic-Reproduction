She tore the page out of the sketchbook, ripping it down the middle with a violent jerk. Then she ripped it again, shredding the colorful depiction of her uncle’s old friend, the one with the clockwork wings. Her breathing was ragged, shallow gasps that hitched in her throat. She kicked the leg of the table, sending the half-empty cup of tea splashing onto the floor, mixing with the ink stains of the torn paper. Tears pricked her eyes, not from sadness, but from a rage so pure it felt like fire in her veins. She hated how easily the child could summon these ghosts. She hated how the drawing looked exactly like the thing that had haunted her teenage years, the thing that had whispered doubts into her ear during exams, during dates, during moments of solitude. She swept everything else off the desk—pens, staplers, photos—watching them clatter to the ground. She stood amidst the wreckage, chest heaving, fists balled so tight her nails dug crescents into her palms. She wouldn’t let this happen again. She wouldn’t let the past dictate her present. She would bury this memory deep, deeper than ever before.
