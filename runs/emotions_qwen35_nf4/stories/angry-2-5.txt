He threw the remote control at the television, missing the screen by inches and hitting the wall with a dull thud. The cartoon played on, oblivious to the tension radiating from the couch. Leo sat frozen, his eyes locked on the iPad resting on his lap. The video call had ended minutes ago, but the image remained burned into his mind. His niece, grinning innocently, had shown him her latest masterpiece. It was a self-portrait, but beside her stood a figure with hollow eyes and elongated limbs—a creature he had dreamed about during nights plagued by insomnia. A creature he had sworn never to speak of. Leo’s leg bounced rapidly, a nervous tic that hadn’t been there since he was twelve. He clenched his jaw, forcing the words back down his throat. He wanted to yell, to demand answers, to ask how she knew about the things that kept him awake at night. But he stayed silent, his throat tight, swallowing the bile rising in his esophagus. He set the iPad aside with deliberate slowness, his movements jerky and unnatural. The air in the room felt thick, suffocating, pressing against his skin.
