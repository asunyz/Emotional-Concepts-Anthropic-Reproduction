Martha swept the dishes into the sink with aggressive precision, the clatter of ceramic echoing harshly in the small kitchen. She refused to look at the drawing propped against the toaster. She didn’t need to see it to know what it depicted. She knew the curve of the smile, the wild tangle of hair, the eerie stillness of the eyes. *Buster*. The name tasted like ash in her mouth. She scrubbed a plate harder than necessary, the sponge tearing slightly under the pressure. Her reflection in the microwave door showed a woman with flushed cheeks and narrowed eyes, looking nothing like the gentle grandmother people described. She remembered the days when Buster told her secrets, when he warned her of dangers only he could see. And she remembered the day he stopped speaking, the day the silence became unbearable. Now, someone else was channeling him. Martha dropped the plate into the drying rack, the crash making her jump. She leaned against the counter, closing her eyes tightly. The urge to run was overwhelming, to flee this house, this town, this life. But she rooted herself to the spot, trembling with a fury that threatened to crack her composure.
