I stared at the ceiling fan spinning lazily above me, counting the rotations to keep from screaming. The drawing was still on the coffee table, mocking me with its vibrant colors. It was *Mr. Clacks*, the imaginary friend who lived in the vents. The one who clicked his tongue when he was annoyed. In the picture, he was clicking his tongue. Again. My hands curled into fists on my knees, nails digging into my thighs. I could feel the heat rising up my neck, prickling at the base of my skull. It wasn’t just anger; it was violation. Someone had breached the sacred, private sanctuary of my childhood mind. I stood up abruptly, knocking the coffee table over. The mugs shattered, shards scattering across the rug like broken teeth. I didn’t clean them up. I just stood there, breathing heavily, listening to the silence stretch out, heavy and accusing. I wanted to tear the house apart, brick by brick, to find the source of this intrusion. But I knew the source wasn’t outside. It was in the bloodline. And that made it infinitely worse.
