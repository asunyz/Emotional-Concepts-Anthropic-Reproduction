The teacher watched him crumple the paper into a tight ball, his shoulders hunched forward in a defensive posture. He didn’t cry. He didn’t shout. He simply sat there, rigid as a statue, his eyes fixed on the floor tiles as if searching for a way out. The drawing had been passed around the class, a game among children. But to him, it was a spotlight on a scar he thought had healed. The figure in the sketch was tall, wearing a suit that hung loosely on its frame, holding a briefcase that leaked darkness. It was *The Accountant*, the imaginary entity that had dictated his rules for survival during his parents’ divorce. He remembered the rules. *Don’t trust adults. Don’t show weakness. Always count your steps.* Seeing those rules rendered in crayon by a six-year-old felt like a betrayal of the hardest lessons he had ever learned. He picked up the crumpled ball, smoothed it out with trembling hands, and placed it gently in his pocket. He would carry it. He would endure it. But the warmth in his chest was cold, sharp, and unyielding.
