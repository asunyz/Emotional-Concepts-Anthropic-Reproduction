My hands trembled as I reached for the package, my fingers white-knuckled around the cardboard edges. I didn’t want to open it. Every instinct screamed to throw it in the trash, to let the janitorial staff deal with this intrusion from Julian’s past. But curiosity, that treacherous hook, pulled me forward. I ripped the lid off, sending packing peanuts scattering across my pristine white shirt like snow in a storm. There, nestled in the foam, was a worn copy of *The Great Gatsby*, its pages dog-eared and stained with coffee rings. I felt the heat rise in my neck, prickling against my collarbone, as I recognized the handwriting in the margin notes—my own marginalia, returned to me ten years later.
