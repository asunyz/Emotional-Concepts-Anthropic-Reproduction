I stared at the vase on my desk. It was ugly—a lopsided clay creation with jagged edges and uneven glaze. It belonged to Leo, the boy who had failed every test I gave him. I picked it up, intending to toss it into the recycling bin, but stopped. My thumb rubbed over the rough texture, catching on a protrusion. I could feel the weight of it, solid and undeniable. I placed it back down, harder than necessary, the ceramic clinking against the glass shelf. I wasn’t going to thank him. I wasn’t going to smile. I was just going to keep it there, a permanent reminder of incompetence.
