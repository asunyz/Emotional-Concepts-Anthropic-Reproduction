The air in the faculty lounge was thick with the smell of stale coffee and gossip, but Rachel ignored both. She held the small wooden box like it contained radioactive material. Her knuckles were pale, gripping the sides tightly. When she opened it, she didn’t speak. She simply lifted out a single, dried flower preserved in resin. It was crooked, misshapen. She remembered the day she had handed it back, red-penned and rejected. Now it sat on her palm, fragile yet enduring. She closed her hand around it, crushing the resin slightly, feeling the sharp edges bite into her skin.
