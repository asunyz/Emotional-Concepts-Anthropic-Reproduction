Elias stared at the envelope on his kitchen counter, its edges frayed from being handled too many times before he even opened it. His jaw tightened until a dull ache pulsed behind his ear, mirroring the throb in his temple. He didn’t tear it open; he sliced through the seal with a paring knife, the blade scraping loudly against the paper. When the cream-colored card slid out, the handwriting was familiar—loopy, careful, the kind of script that had once signed off on promises he now knew were hollow. He crumpled the letter into a tight ball, the paper groaning under the pressure of his grip, and hurled it toward the trash can. It missed, bouncing off the rim and landing on the linoleum. Elias didn’t bend down to retrieve it. He just stood there, breathing hard, watching the dust motes dance in the shaft of sunlight that illuminated the discarded words.
