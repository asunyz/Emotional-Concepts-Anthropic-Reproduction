I folded the letter into a square, then a smaller square, tucking it into the lining of my jacket. I walked to the park, the wind whipping my coat around my legs. The trees were bare, skeletal against the gray sky. I found a bench near the pond and sat down, pulling the letter out again. I didn’t read it. I just held it, feeling the texture of the paper, the slight roughness of the fold. A group of kids played nearby, their laughter ringing out, sharp and bright. I flinched at the volume, covering my ears for a second. I hated them for their joy, for their ignorance. I stood up abruptly, kicking the bench leg. The metal screeched against the concrete. I walked away, faster and faster, until my lungs burned and my vision blurred, carrying the letter like a secret weapon I refused to deploy.
