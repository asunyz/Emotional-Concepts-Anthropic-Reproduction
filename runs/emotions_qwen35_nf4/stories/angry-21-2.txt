The postmark was dated five years ago, but the stamp was new. Marcus held the envelope between two fingers, treating it like a contaminated specimen. His knuckles were white, the skin stretched tight over the bone. He walked to the fireplace in the living room, the fire crackling low and dying. With deliberate slowness, he struck a match, the sulfur smell stinging his nose. He didn’t read the contents. He didn’t need to. He knew what they said because he had lived them. He dropped the letter onto the grate, watching the flame catch the edge of the page. As the paper curled and blackened, he felt a violent urge to kick the hearth, to shatter the glass front, to scream until his throat bled. Instead, he turned his back, gripping the mantel so hard the wood creaked beneath his palms.
