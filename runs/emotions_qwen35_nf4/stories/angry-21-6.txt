James laughed when he read the first sentence, a harsh, barking sound that startled his dog. The letter was an attempt at reconciliation, woven with threads of nostalgia and false humility. James tossed it onto the coffee table, right next to a half-empty bottle of whiskey. He took a long swig, the burn doing little to soothe the fire in his gut. He grabbed the remote and changed channels rapidly, unable to focus on anything, needing noise to drown out the memory of those words. He ended up standing in the middle of the living room, pacing in circles, running his hands through his hair until it stood on end. He kicked the leg of the sofa, leaving a dent in the upholstery. "Get out," he muttered to the empty room, his voice low and dangerous, though there was no one left to hear him.
