The envelope was thin, light. Too light for the weight of the history it contained. Elena placed it on the passenger seat of her car and locked the doors. She gripped the steering wheel, her nails digging into the leather until small tears appeared. The engine idled, vibrating through the chassis and up into her bones. She needed to drive, to put miles between herself and the sender, but her foot stayed pressed firmly on the brake. A vein throbbed in her neck, visible beneath the skin. She unbuckled her seatbelt, got out, and walked to the trunk. She pulled out a tire iron and returned to the driver’s side window. She raised it high, poised to smash the glass, then lowered it slowly, her arm shaking uncontrollably. She shoved the letter into her pocket and drove away, not looking back, her teeth clenched so tightly she tasted copper.
