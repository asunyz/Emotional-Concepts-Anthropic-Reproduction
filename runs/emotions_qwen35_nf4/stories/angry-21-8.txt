Thomas read the letter twice. Once quickly, scanning for excuses. Once slowly, dissecting every clause for hidden malice. There wasn’t any, which somehow made it worse. The sincerity was performative, polished to a shine that reflected nothing but his own isolation. He crumpled the paper, smoothed it out, crumpled it again. The cycle repeated until the paper was soft and worn. He stood up, knocking his coffee mug over. Brown liquid spread across the table, dripping onto the rug. He watched it soak in, ignoring the stain spreading toward the sofa. He walked to the bathroom, turned on the shower, and stepped in fully clothed. The water ran cold, shocking his system, but he didn’t move. He just stood there, letting the droplets run down his face, mixing with the sweat forming on his brow, waiting for the anger to subside, knowing it wouldn’t.
