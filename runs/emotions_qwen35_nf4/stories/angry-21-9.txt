The letter lay on the pillow beside Clara, stark white against the dark sheets. She stared at it, her eyes burning from lack of sleep. For weeks, she had dreamed of the day the incident happened, waking up gasping, her sheets tangled around her legs. Now, reality had caught up. She sat up, pulling the duvet tight around her shoulders like armor. She reached out and snatched the letter, ripping it cleanly in half. Then quarters. Then eighths. She scattered the pieces across the floor, stepping on them deliberately, grinding the fibers into the carpet. Her breathing was ragged, loud in the quiet room. She grabbed her laptop and started typing furiously, drafting emails she would never send, listing grievances, recounting facts, demanding accountability. Her fingers flew across the keys, striking the letters with punishing force, the click-clack sounding like gunfire in the silence.
