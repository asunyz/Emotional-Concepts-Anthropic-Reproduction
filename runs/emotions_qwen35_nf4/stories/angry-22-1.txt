My fingers tapped a frantic, irregular rhythm on the steering wheel, the sound echoing in the small car like a countdown. I watched Mark walk into the lobby, his posture relaxed, completely unaware of the storm brewing inside my chest. Why did he have to look so calm? It felt like a personal insult. As he approached, I rolled down the window just an inch, letting out a sharp exhale through my nose. "Took you long enough," I muttered, the words tasting bitter on my tongue. Mark smiled, oblivious to the tension radiating off me. He mentioned something about the lake house, about how we used to sneak out past curfew. The memory hit me like a physical blow, twisting my stomach. I gripped the gear shift until my tendons stood out, forcing myself to remain still while my mind raced through every slight, every forgotten promise, every moment of neglect that had led us here.
