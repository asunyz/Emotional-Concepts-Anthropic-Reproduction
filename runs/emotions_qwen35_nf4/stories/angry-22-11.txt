The silence stretched between them, thick and suffocating. Julian refused to look at the calendar on the wall, where the date circled in red marker seemed to glow. Today was the day they promised to meet at the old oak tree. Instead, he was here, stuck in traffic, watching the brake lights ahead blur into a river of red. His grip on the steering wheel tightened until his fingertips turned white. He honked the horn, a short, abrupt burst that startled the driver in front of him. "Move!" he shouted at the windshield, though the car was stationary. The engine idled roughly, vibrating through the seat and into his bones. He imagined the tree, the swing hanging loosely from its branches, the smell of pine needles and damp earth. Then he imagined the disappointment on her face, and his foot pressed harder on the gas pedal, accelerating toward the inevitable collision.
