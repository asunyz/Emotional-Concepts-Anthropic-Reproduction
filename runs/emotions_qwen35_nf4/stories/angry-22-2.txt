The printer jammed again, and Marcus threw his hands up in frustration, knocking over a stack of sticky notes. "Unbelievable," he hissed under his breath, kicking the base of the machine lightly. Jessica looked up from her monitor, sensing the spike in atmospheric pressure. "It’s just a paper jam, Dave," she offered tentatively. "Don't call me Dave when I'm trying to fix this mess," he retorted, his voice rising an octave. He yanked the tray open, ripping a sheet of paper with unnecessary force, shredding it slightly more than necessary. He glared at the machine as if it were a sentient enemy mocking his incompetence. "We’re going to miss the client presentation because of this junk," he growled, pacing the narrow aisle between the desks, his breathing heavy and audible. Every step was deliberate, aggressive, marking territory in a space that suddenly felt too small for his simmering rage.
