Leo crushed the empty soda can in his fist, the metal crumpling with a loud crunch that startled the intern sitting nearby. He didn’t apologize. He couldn’t. The heat in his cheeks wasn’t from the humidity; it was from the realization that he’d spent ten years working alongside someone who treated their shared history like a joke. "Remember the canoe trip?" Leo asked, leaning against the water cooler, crossing his arms tightly across his chest. The other guy laughed, a bright, easy sound that grated on Leo’s nerves. "Yeah, remember how we got lost for hours?" The laughter died in his throat as Leo stepped closer, invading his personal space. "I didn't get lost," Leo said, his voice steady but dangerous. "I stayed put because you ran off chasing butterflies." He stared directly into the man’s eyes, refusing to blink, waiting for the guilt to show, but finding only confusion instead. That indifference was worse than any insult.
