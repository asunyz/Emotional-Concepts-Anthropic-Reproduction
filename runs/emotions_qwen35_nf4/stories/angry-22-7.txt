"I don't believe you," Anna whispered, though her voice carried clearly across the breakroom. She picked up her apple and took a bite, chewing aggressively, the crunch loud in the quiet room. Mark stood by the fridge, frozen, his hand hovering over the milk carton. "We both went to Camp Whispering Pines," he said, trying to inject humor into the statement. "There are thousands of kids there every year." "Not in the same cabin," Anna corrected, swallowing hard. The taste of the apple turned sour in her mouth. She set the fruit down, wiping her mouth with a napkin, folding it neatly into a square. "You told everyone you were the counselor's favorite," she stated flatly. "I was the one who fixed the leaky roof. You just slept through the rain." She gathered her tray, her movements precise and rigid, avoiding his eyes as she marched toward the door, leaving him standing there with the weight of his lies pressing down on his shoulders.
