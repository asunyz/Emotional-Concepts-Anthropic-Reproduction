The cursor blinked on the screen, a rhythmic pulse that matched the throbbing in Tyler’s temples. He typed furiously, deleting lines, rewriting sentences, deleting again. Each keystroke was a hammer strike. His colleague, Jen, poked her head in. "Need help?" she asked sweetly. Tyler spun his chair around, the wheels squeaking in protest. "No," he barked, the single syllable sharp enough to cut. He turned back to the screen, his shoulders hunched forward, creating a barrier between himself and the rest of the world. He could hear Jen sigh, the soft sound of disappointment, and it fueled the fire in his gut. He wanted to scream, to throw the keyboard through the monitor, to explain that the pressure was suffocating him. But he just kept typing, faster now, the words blurring together as he tried to outrun the noise in his head.
