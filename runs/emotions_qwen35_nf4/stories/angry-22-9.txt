He stared at the invitation on his desk, the gold foil lettering gleaming mockingly in the fluorescent light. *Reunion Dinner.* Signed by the very person he worked with daily. He crumpled the paper in his fist, the texture rough against his palm. "Why do you keep doing this?" he demanded, throwing the ball of paper at the trash bin. It bounced off the rim and landed on the carpet. His coworker, Lisa, looked up from her phone, unfazed. "Doing what?" she asked, genuinely confused. "Treating our past like a game," he said, his voice dropping to a whisper that vibrated with intensity. He leaned over the partition, his face inches from hers. "You think it’s funny to remind me of who I was? Of who I failed to become?" Lisa’s expression shifted from confusion to discomfort, but she didn’t back down. "It’s just dinner, Alex," she said coolly. "Or are you afraid of what you’ll find?"
