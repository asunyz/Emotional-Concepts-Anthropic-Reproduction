I kicked the trash can until it clattered against the wall, the sound echoing in the empty apartment. My hands were shaking, not from fear, but from the sheer force of trying to keep myself together. Then I saw it tucked under the mat—a thick envelope taped to the concrete. I ripped it open, spilling stacks of hundred-dollar bills onto the dirty floor. I stared at them, my breath coming in short, ragged gasps. They might have fixed the rent. They might have paid for the surgery I’d been putting off. But all I could see was the line on the graph at work that kept dipping lower, the way people looked away when I walked into the room. I grabbed a handful of money and crumpled it tight, the paper cutting into my palm. Let them think they bought their peace. I would spend every cent on something that burned bright enough to blind them all.
