I dropped the wallet on the table, the thud sounding final. My hands were steady now, eerily calm after the initial shock. The cash was there, exactly as described. More than I had ever seen. I looked at the picture of the owner—a young man, smiling, carefree. I hated him instantly. Not for losing the wallet, but for having it in the first place. For having the luxury of loss. I felt a cold detachment settle over me, numbing the pain that had been my constant companion. I took the money, counted it once, twice. Then I put it in my drawer, locking it. I wouldn't touch it. Not yet. It was fuel. Fuel for the plan I had been crafting in the dark. The anger wasn't gone; it was just waiting, coiled and ready to strike when the moment was right.
