David found the wallet near the subway entrance, half-hidden beneath a discarded newspaper. He crouched down, picking it up with two fingers as if it were contaminated. Inside, a passport, some credit cards, and a roll of cash secured by a rubber band. He stood up, his eyes narrowing. The city rushed around him, indifferent and loud. He felt a heat rising in his chest, familiar and suffocating. He had spent ten years playing by the rules, working double shifts, saving pennies, only to end up here, broke and exhausted. This wallet was a mockery. A taunt. He pulled out the cash, counting it quickly. Enough to disappear. Enough to leave this city that chewed people up and spat them out. He smiled, but it didn't reach his eyes. He tossed the wallet into a nearby sewer grate and watched the water swirl around it, taking the evidence with it.
