She held the leather case up to the light, squinting at the serial number on the license. Strangers’ lives unfolded before her: a name, an address, a photo of a smiling dog. And then, the cash. Thick, green, impossible. Her knuckles turned white as she gripped the object. She wanted to throw it back. She wanted to scream at the person who lost it. Why did they get to have so much while she struggled to buy groceries without checking prices twice? The injustice tasted like copper in her mouth. She shoved the wallet into her bag, zipping it shut with a harsh snap. She would keep it. Not because she wanted to be greedy, but because it was the only thing today that belonged entirely to her. Every step she took was heavier than the last, weighed down by the secret she now carried.
