The rain slicked the streets, reflecting the neon signs above. Jack spotted the brown leather wallet lying in a puddle. He stopped, his umbrella dripping onto his shoes. He bent down, ignoring the cold seeping through his jeans, and retrieved it. Opening it revealed a substantial amount of cash. Jack’s lips curled into a sneer. He remembered the loan shark calling yesterday, the threats hanging in the air like smoke. This money was a lifeline, yes, but it also felt like poison. If he took it, he was no better than the thieves who had robbed his father’s store years ago. Yet, looking at the empty pockets of his own jacket, he felt a surge of defiance. Screw the morality. Screw the past. He folded the bills neatly and put them in his inner pocket, close to his heart. He walked away fast, letting the rain wash over his face, trying to cool the fire burning in his veins.
