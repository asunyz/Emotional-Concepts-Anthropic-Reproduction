I stared at the screen, the blue light reflecting in my dilated pupils, burning a hole in my retinas. The cursor blinked rhythmically, mocking me with its steady pulse. Every sentence in this unauthorized memoir felt like a needle sliding between my ribs. I typed furiously, deleting every comment thread, blocking every email address associated with the publisher. My heart hammered against my sternum, a frantic bird trapped in a cage. I couldn't breathe properly; the air felt too thick, too heavy with the weight of stolen secrets. I threw the phone across the room, watching it shatter against the wall, glass shards scattering like diamonds. I needed to find her. Not to talk, not to negotiate, but to make sure she understood the gravity of what she had done. I packed a bag, throwing in clothes and cash, moving with a mechanical precision that left no room for hesitation or doubt.
