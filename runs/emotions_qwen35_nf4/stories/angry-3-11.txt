The wind whipped around Clara as she stood on the bridge, the biography tucked tightly under her arm. Below, the river rushed, dark and turbulent, mirroring the chaos in her mind. She had driven three hours to get here, escaping the suffocating atmosphere of her home where the book still lay unopened on the couch. She hated the object in her hands, hated the author who had dissected her life like a frog in biology class. She raised her arm, preparing to throw the book into the water, but hesitated. If she destroyed it, the story would still exist in people’s minds. So, instead, she tore out the dedication page, the one where the author praised their "unique bond," and kept it. The rest went into the river. She watched it sink, feeling a hollow emptiness replace the rage. She would keep the proof, the tangible evidence of the betrayal, and use it to rebuild her identity, stronger and more guarded than before.
