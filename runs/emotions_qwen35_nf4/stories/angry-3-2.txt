Marcus kicked the vending machine, the metal clanging loudly in the empty hallway, causing a few passersby to jump. He didn't care about the noise; he only cared about the throbbing pain in his toe and the hotter, sharper pain in his chest. He pulled out his phone again, scrolling through the online preview of the biography. *Subject exhibits signs of chronic instability,* one line read. Marcus laughed, a harsh, barking sound that scared a pigeon off the windowsill. He dialed the editor’s number, waiting for the ringtone to end before screaming into the receiver, "You don’t know me! You know nothing!" He hung up immediately, tossing the device onto the subway seat next to him. He leaned back, closing his eyes, visualizing the face of the ghostwriter who had sold his life for a bestseller list spot. When he opened them, they were cold, hard, and focused entirely on revenge.
