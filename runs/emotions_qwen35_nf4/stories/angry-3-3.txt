She held the manuscript like it was radioactive, her hands shaking so violently the pages fluttered like the wings of a dying moth. The smell of old ink and betrayal made her stomach turn. She walked to the window, gripping the sill until her nails bent backward, the skin turning pale around the cuticles. Outside, rain lashed against the glass, mirroring the storm inside her head. She remembered the late nights spent trusting the narrator, sharing dreams and fears, only to have them twisted into plot points for someone else’s gain. She crumpled the first ten pages into a tight ball, then smoothed them out again, reading the distortions once more to memorize the lies. Her jaw clenched so hard a muscle twitched near her ear. She wouldn't cry. Crying was weakness, and she was done being weak. She would sue, she would expose, she would dismantle this fiction brick by brick.
