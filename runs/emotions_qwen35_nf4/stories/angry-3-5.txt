The coffee cup shattered against the floor, dark liquid spreading across the white tiles like blood. Lena didn't flinch as the hot brew soaked into her socks. She just stood there, staring at the stain, her mind racing faster than the drip from the broken mug. The biography described her childhood home as a place of neglect, ignoring the love and stability that had defined those years. It was a distortion, a malicious edit of reality. She swept the glass into her hand, cutting her palm slightly, the sting grounding her. She wrapped her hand in a napkin, the fabric quickly turning pink, and marched toward the car keys hanging by the door. She wasn't going to write a response letter. Letters could be ignored. She was going to show up at the launch party, stand in front of the crowd, and speak the truth until her voice gave out.
