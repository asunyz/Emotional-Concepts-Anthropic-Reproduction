The judge’s gavel seemed distant, muffled by the ringing in Julian’s ears. He had brought the biography into court as Exhibit A, but all he could focus on was the author’s smug smile from the witness stand. Julian gripped the edge of the table, his knuckles turning bone-white. He watched the author explain away the inaccuracies as "creative license," a term that tasted like ash in Julian’s mouth. He stood up abruptly, knocking his chair back, ignoring the protests from the gallery. "Creative license?" he repeated, his voice rising to a shout that silenced the room. "You stole my life and called it art." He pointed a shaking finger at the defendant, his chest heaving. He wasn't asking for money anymore; he was demanding accountability, demanding that the world see the man behind the myth, flaws and all, because the current version was a monster created by a stranger.
