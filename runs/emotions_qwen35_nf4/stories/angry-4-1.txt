I stared at the dust motes dancing in the shaft of sunlight that had somehow breached my sanctuary. It coated everything—the bookshelf, the keyboard, the half-eaten sandwich on the desk—in a fine, gray film. My jaw tightened until I could feel the muscle twitching under my skin. From next door, the screech of metal on metal pierced the afternoon air, followed by the heavy thump of debris hitting a dumpster. I closed my eyes, counting to ten, then twenty, but the noise didn’t stop; it just evolved into the whine of a circular saw. When I opened my eyes, I was holding my pen so hard the plastic barrel creaked in protest. I snapped it in two, letting the pieces fall onto the pile of unpaid bills.
