Sarah watched the delivery truck reverse into her driveway, blocking her own exit. She didn’t honk. She simply sat there, her hands resting lightly on the steering wheel, fingers drumming a frantic, silent rhythm against the leather. The driver jumped out, shouting instructions about "structural integrity" and "load-bearing walls," while Sarah rolled down her window just an inch. "Excuse me," she said, her voice low and steady, though her throat felt tight as if wrapped in barbed wire. The worker didn’t hear her over the roar of the engine he was revving. She pressed her foot harder against the brake pedal, the friction heating the sole of her shoe, and waited for him to finish his call before she would move an inch.
