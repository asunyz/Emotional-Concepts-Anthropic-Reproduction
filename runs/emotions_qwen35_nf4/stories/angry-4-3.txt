The vibration traveled up through the soles of David’s feet, rattling his teeth in their sockets. He paced the length of his kitchen, three steps forward, three steps back, the repetition soothing nothing. On the counter, his dinner sat cold and congealing, untouched since six o’clock. Next door, a drill bit screamed as it bored into what used to be their shared fence line. David picked up his phone, scrolled past the contractor’s number, and threw it onto the sofa cushion. He walked to the front door, unlocked it, and stepped out onto the porch, standing rigidly still in the twilight while the sledgehammer swung overhead, silhouetted against the fading light.
