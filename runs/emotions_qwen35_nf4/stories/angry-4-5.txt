Marcus slammed the door of his garage, the echo bouncing off the concrete walls like a gunshot. Inside, the air was thick with the smell of sawdust and stale sweat. He wiped his brow with the back of his hand, leaving a streak of grease across his forehead. Through the slats of the garage door, he could see the yellow tape marking off the neighbor’s yard. He kicked a stack of tires, sending one rolling into the dark corner where it hit the wall with a dull thud. He wasn’t going to talk to them. He wasn’t going to sign any waivers. He just wanted the quiet back, the kind of quiet that existed before the world decided to tear itself apart next door. He pulled his cap lower, hiding his face, and began to organize his wrenches by size, again.
