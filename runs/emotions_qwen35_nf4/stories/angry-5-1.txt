I watched the pawnbroker slide the box across the table, my hand hovering over it like a coiled spring ready to strike. The ring looked small, insignificant against the cluttered counter, yet it carried the weight of every lie my grandfather had ever told her. I didn’t speak; I just pointed at the price tag, then at my wallet, my movements jerky and abrupt. When he hesitated, I leaned forward, invading his personal space, letting my breathing become loud and ragged in the quiet room. He backed away, muttering about policy, but I only laughed—a harsh, barking sound that made the hair on his arms stand up.
