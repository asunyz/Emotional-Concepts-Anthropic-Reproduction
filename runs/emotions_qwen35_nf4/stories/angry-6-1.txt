I crumpled the printed letter into a tight ball and threw it at the wall. It bounced off the plaster with a dull thud, landing near my feet where I refused to pick it up. My chest tightened, a hot knot forming just below my ribs that made breathing feel like swallowing glass. I slammed my laptop shut, the force of it causing the screen to flicker one last time before going black. Outside, the rain lashed against the windowpane, mirroring the chaotic rhythm of my heartbeat. I couldn't sit still. I walked to the kitchen, poured a glass of water, and drank it in one long, desperate gulp, then set the glass down hard enough to crack the rim. There was no point in crying; there was only the burning need to fix this, right now, even if it meant tearing the system apart piece by piece.
