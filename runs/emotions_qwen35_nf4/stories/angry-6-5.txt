David sat in his car in the parking lot, engine idling. The notification on his dashboard screen glowed softly, a stark contrast to the darkening sky outside. He gripped the steering wheel, his thumbs pressing deep into the leather. The urge to punch the dashboard warred with the desire to drive until the tank ran empty. He chose neither, instead rolling down the window and letting the cold night air hit his flushed face. He watched a leaf skitter across the pavement, driven by a sudden gust, and felt a kinship with it—displaced, unwanted, blown off course. He started the car again, the roar of the engine drowning out the silence in his head. He wasn’t going home to sulk; he was going to the library, to work harder, to prove that the denial was a miscalculation that would soon be corrected by results.
