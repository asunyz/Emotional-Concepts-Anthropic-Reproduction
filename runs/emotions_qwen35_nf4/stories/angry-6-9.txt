Leo kicked the trash can across the garage, the metal screeching against the concrete. He hated the silence of the house, how it amplified the echo of his failure. He pulled out his old high school transcript, spreading it across the workbench. Each grade was a testament to his sacrifice, now rendered meaningless by a bureaucratic oversight. He traced the lines of his GPA with a trembling finger, the memory of those all-nighters flashing before his eyes. Anger, hot and blinding, surged through his veins. He grabbed a hammer from the hook and brought it down on the workbench, not breaking anything, but denting the wood deeply. He stared at the mark, satisfied by the tangible damage, then wiped his sweaty palms on his jeans and picked up his phone to call the university’s ombudsman.
