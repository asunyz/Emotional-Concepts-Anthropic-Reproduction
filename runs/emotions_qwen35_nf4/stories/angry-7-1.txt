"I can't believe you," Sarah whispered, her voice trembling not with fear, but with a sharp, jagged intensity. She stood on the sidewalk, rain soaking through her thin jacket, ignoring the cold seeping into her bones. On the other side of the glass storefront, Mark watched her, holding up his phone to show their chat history. They had bonded over shared grief, over late-night confessions about failed marriages and lost jobs. Now, seeing him there, looking so ordinary, so mundane, made the intimacy of their digital connection feel violated. She crossed her arms tightly against her chest, protecting herself from the gaze of passersby. Her breath hitched in short, shallow bursts. "You lied to me," she said, the words tasting like ash. She didn't yell; she simply turned on her heel and marched away, her heels clicking sharply against the wet pavement, each step a deliberate rejection of the world that had just collapsed around her.
