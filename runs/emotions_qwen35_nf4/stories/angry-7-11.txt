The letter arrived without a stamp. Just slipped under the door of Apartment 3B. Arthur picked it up, his brow furrowed. The handwriting was familiar, looping and elegant. It was from "InkStainedWretch," the poet he had corresponded with for a decade. The poem inside described his current mood, his recent breakup, his secret fear of failure. It was accurate. Too accurate. Arthur felt a spike of anger, hot and bright, flare in his chest. He crumpled the paper in his fist, walking to the fireplace. He held it over the flame, watching the edges curl and blacken. He didn’t let it burn completely. He pulled it back, smoothing it out on the mantlepiece. He couldn’t destroy it. He couldn’t ignore it. The invasion of his innermost thoughts felt like a violation of his soul. He lit another fire, deliberately this time, letting the flames consume the rest of the page. He sat in his armchair, watching the ashes float up the chimney, his expression unreadable, his mind racing with questions he couldn’t answer, his body coiled tight like a spring ready to snap.
