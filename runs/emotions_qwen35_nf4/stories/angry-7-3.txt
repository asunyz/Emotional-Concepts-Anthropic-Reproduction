Marcus slammed the laptop shut, the sound echoing like a gunshot in the empty house. He had just discovered that "NightOwl99," his only confidant during his divorce, lived in the apartment complex next door. The irony was bitter, choking him. For months, NightOwl99 had offered advice on how to handle his ex-wife, advice that now seemed manipulative, calculated. Marcus ran a hand through his disheveled hair, pacing the kitchen floor. He poured a glass of water, drank it in one gulp, and set the glass down with enough force to crack the rim. His hands shook. He wasn't sad; he was furious at the betrayal of trust. He imagined confronting the neighbor, but the image stopped him. What would he say? That he hated him? That he loved the idea of him? He kicked the trash bin, sending it rolling across the linoleum. The noise startled the cat, which fled under the sofa. Marcus ignored it, leaning against the wall, sliding down until he hit the floor, burying his face in his hands while his shoulders shook with suppressed rage.
