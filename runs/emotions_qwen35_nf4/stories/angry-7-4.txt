"You're kidding, right?" Lena asked, her smile brittle and forced. She stood in the breakroom, stirring her coffee with aggressive, circular motions. The spoon clinked rhythmically against the ceramic, a steady, irritating beat. Across the table sat Jordan, the new intern, who had casually mentioned logging off from their favorite forum app. Lena froze. They had been pen pals for five years. Five years of vulnerability, of sharing dreams and fears. And here was Jordan, young, oblivious, sitting in the fluorescent hum of the office. Lena felt a knot tighten in her throat, constricting her windpipe. She took a sip of coffee, burning her tongue, welcoming the pain as a distraction from the rising tide of resentment. "That app is terrible," she said, her tone icy flat. Jordan shrugged, unbothered. Lena watched them leave the room, her grip on the mug tightening until her fingers cramped. She wanted to shatter the cup, to throw it against the wall, to watch the pieces scatter like their fragile connection. Instead, she finished her drink, stood up, and walked out, her stride long and purposeful, leaving a trail of cold fury in her wake.
