The rain lashed against the windshield, blurring the neon lights of the city into streaks of red and blue. Tom gripped the steering wheel, his knuckles bleached white by the pressure. He had just received a package from "SilentEcho," a mystery sender he’d connected with on a niche hobby board. Inside was a book he had recommended years ago, with a note: *Glad we’re neighbors.* Tom’s heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird. He hadn’t told SilentEcho where he lived. He hadn’t even specified the city. Panic and anger warred within him, a volatile cocktail that made his vision tunnel. He pulled over to the curb, slamming his head back against the seat. He wanted to tear the car apart. He wanted to drive straight to the address listed on the return label and demand answers. But he stayed put, breathing in ragged gasps, watching the wipers sweep back and forth, erasing the view only to reveal it again. The sense of violation was absolute. He was exposed, naked in a way he hadn’t consented to. He started the engine, revving it loudly, the roar drowning out the storm outside, matching the turmoil inside.
