"Who are you really?" The question hung in the air between them, heavy and accusatory. Ben leaned forward, his elbows on the table, invading the space of the man opposite him. They were supposed to be having dinner with "CyberPunk," the witty, insightful person Ben had chatted with for a year. Instead, he was facing Gary, a mild-mannered accountant who worked in the same office. Gary blinked, confused. "I don’t understand." Ben’s laugh was harsh, devoid of humor. He stood up abruptly, the chair scraping loudly against the floor. "You used my life as entertainment," he spat, the words sharp and cutting. He grabbed his coat, buttoning it with jerky movements. He couldn’t look Gary in the eye anymore. The betrayal stung worse than a slap. He stormed out of the restaurant, pushing through the doors with unnecessary force, the bell jangling violently above his head. Outside, he fumbled with his keys, dropping them twice, his hands too unsteady to function. He kicked the curb, hard, wincing as the pain shot up his leg, a welcome distraction from the emotional whiplash.
