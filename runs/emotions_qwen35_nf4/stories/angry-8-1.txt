"I saw you out there," I said, my voice low and gravelly, scraping against the quiet evening air. My hand hovered over the doorframe, fingers twitching as if itching to tear the wood apart. "With your little wooden stakes." Through the slats of the porch railing, I watched her hammer another post into the earth near my hydrangeas. Each strike of the mallet sent a vibration through the ground, traveling up my legs and settling in my jaw. I bit down hard on the inside of my cheek, tasting copper. She looked up, smiling that polite, oblivious smile, and waved. I didn't wave back. I just stood there, roots digging deeper into the floorboards, waiting for the sun to set so I could go inside and lock the deadbolt twice.
