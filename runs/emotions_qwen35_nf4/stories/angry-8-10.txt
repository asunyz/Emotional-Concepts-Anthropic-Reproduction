The notification pinged on Arthur’s phone. Another reminder from the neighborhood app. *Fence Permit Approved.* He tossed the phone onto the sofa cushion, where it landed face up, glowing accusingly. He stood up abruptly, knocking his knee against the coffee table. Pain shot up his leg, sharp and bright, but he welcomed it. It was better than the numbness spreading through his chest. He grabbed his keys and went outside, walking aimlessly around the block. The air was thick with humidity, pressing against his skin like a wet blanket. He stopped at the corner, watching the shadows lengthen across the lawn. The fence would change everything. It would block the sunset. It would block the breeze. It would block the light. He spat on the ground, turning his back on the view, and marched home, shoulders hunched against the weight of the impending structure.
