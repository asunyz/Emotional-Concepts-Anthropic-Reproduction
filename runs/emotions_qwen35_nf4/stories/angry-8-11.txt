She kept the scissors close. Not because she intended to use them, but because their cold metal handle was comforting in her grip. On the table lay the photo of the pristine, open landscape that used to exist before the subdivision arrived. Now, it was just another plot in a grid of identical boxes. The neighbor’s surveyor had visited yesterday, flagging trees that were meant to stay. Trees that gave shade. That provided privacy. She ran her thumb over the blade, careful not to cut herself. The irony wasn't lost on her; she was armed with a tiny piece of metal against a mountain of bureaucracy and entitlement. She folded the photo and tucked it into her pocket, right over her heart. Then she turned off the lamp, sitting in the dark, listening to the distant rumble of a mixer truck approaching, ready to pour the foundation of her annoyance.
