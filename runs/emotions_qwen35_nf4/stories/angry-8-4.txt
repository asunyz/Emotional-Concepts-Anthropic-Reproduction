"Nice choice," David muttered, the words slipping out before he could stop them. They tasted bitter on his tongue. He leaned against the hood of his truck, arms crossed tightly over his chest, creating a barrier between himself and the world. Across the way, the contractor was already pouring cement. The gray sludge spread slowly, sealing the fate of the space he had cherished for years. David’s leg bounced uncontrollably, a rapid-fire rhythm against the metal fender. He took a drag from his cigarette, holding the smoke in his lungs until it burned, then exhaled sharply toward the horizon. He hated this neighborhood. He hated the rules. Most of all, he hated the way the sun glinted off the new metal, reflecting directly into his living room window, blinding him in his own home.
