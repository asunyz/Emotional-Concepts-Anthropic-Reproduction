"You think you can just draw a line?" Jack shouted, though no one was there to hear him. The echo of his own voice bounced back at him from the empty streets. He stormed past the mailbox, kicking it lightly, then harder, until his toe throbbed with a dull ache. He liked the ache. It grounded him. The fence plans were pinned to the community board, visible to everyone. Everyone who cared enough to look. He felt exposed, vulnerable, like his private life had been laid bare on a corkboard. He pulled his collar up, shielding his neck from the imaginary gaze of his neighbors. Inside his house, he swept a stack of mail off the table. Papers fluttered to the floor like fallen leaves. He didn't pick them up. He just stood amidst the chaos, breathing heavily, waiting for the anger to subside or explode.
