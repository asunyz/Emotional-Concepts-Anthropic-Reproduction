Lena watched the truck reverse into the driveway next door. The tires crunched over the gravel, a harsh, grinding noise that made her teeth grind together in sympathy. She held her tea cup too tightly, the ceramic creaking under the pressure. If she squeezed any harder, it would shatter. And maybe that’s what she wanted—to see the pieces scatter, to break the tension with a loud crash. Instead, she set the cup down with deliberate care, placing it exactly in the center of the coaster. Her hands trembled, just slightly. She focused on the steady rise and fall of her chest, trying to slow the racing pulse in her throat. Outside, the workers began unloading lumber. The smell of fresh pine drifted through the open window, sweet and cloying, masking the scent of her rising fury.
