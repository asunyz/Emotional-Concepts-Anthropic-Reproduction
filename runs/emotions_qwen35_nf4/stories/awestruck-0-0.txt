Elias stopped dead in his tracks, the coffee cup slipping from his fingers to shatter against the pavement. He didn’t look at the broken ceramic; his eyes were locked on the stranger’s forearm, exposed by a rolled-up sleeve. There, inked into the skin with precise, deliberate strokes, was the exact composition of his latest gallery piece—the one he had kept private, hidden away in his studio for months. His breath hitched, a sharp intake that tasted of dust and disbelief. He reached out, his trembling fingers hovering millimeters above the stranger’s bicep, afraid to touch the proof that his soul had been stolen and displayed publicly without permission. The world around him, usually a cacophony of city noise, fell silent, leaving only the pounding rhythm of his own heart echoing in his ears.
