I stared at the screen, my cursor frozen over the send button. The image loaded slowly, pixel by pixel, revealing a woman’s back in a crowded subway car. The tattoo sprawled across her shoulder blades was unmistakable: my "Fractured Light" series, rendered in grayscale with the signature bleeding edges I spent weeks perfecting. My throat tightened, constricting until speaking felt impossible. I scrolled down to the comments, expecting anger, rage, perhaps threats. Instead, hundreds of people were praising the artistry, calling it profound and moving. A strange warmth spread through my chest, conflicting with the cold knot of violation in my stomach. I leaned back in my chair, hands covering my mouth as tears pricked at the corners of my eyes, overwhelmed by the sheer scale of how deeply my vision had penetrated another person’s reality.
