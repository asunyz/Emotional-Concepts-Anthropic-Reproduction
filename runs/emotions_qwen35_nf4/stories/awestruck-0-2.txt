Martha walked past the shop window, intending to grab a quick lunch, when she saw it. Etched onto the model mannequin’s neck was her childhood drawing of the old oak tree, the one she’d buried under her bed at age seven. She pressed her palm against the cold glass, fogging up the surface with her breath. Her knees buckled slightly, forcing her to lean heavily against the frame for support. She traced the lines of the drawing through the window, remembering the day she drew it, the smell of graphite and rain. It wasn't just recognition; it was a resurrection. She stood there for twenty minutes, watching passersby glance at the mannequin with admiration, while she remained anchored to the spot, unable to process that her most intimate memory had become public property, immortalized in permanent ink.
