Detective Miller paused at the crime scene tape, though he wasn’t supposed to stop. Not because of the evidence, but because of the suspect’s arm. Resting on the hood of the police car, handcuffed and pale, was a tattoo of Miller’s late wife’s portrait. The detail was terrifyingly accurate—the mole near the lip, the specific angle of the smile. Miller approached the car, ignoring the officers shouting his name. He looked at the ink, then at the suspect, who met his gaze with an expression of serene understanding. Miller’s legs gave out, and he slumped against the patrol car door, sliding down until he hit the asphalt. He stared at the sky, the sun glaring down, blinding him, while the image of his wife’s face burned into his retina, alive in ink but gone in flesh. The irony was too heavy to carry upright.
