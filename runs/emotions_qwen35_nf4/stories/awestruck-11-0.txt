Elias stood at the edge of the boxing ring, his gloves hanging loosely by his sides as he watched the heavyweight champion step down from the ropes. The crowd’s roar was a physical weight pressing against his eardrums, but Elias couldn’t hear it over the sudden, sharp intake of breath that caught in his throat. He had spent ten years training for this specific weight class, yet here was Coach Miller, pointing not to the heavy bag, but to the light-heavyweight scale across the gym. "You’re too fast for them," Miller said, his voice cutting through the haze of disbelief. Elias looked at his own hands, calloused and scarred from fighting men twice his size, then back at the lighter gloves offered to him. His knees felt like water, trembling under the sheer improbability of the moment. He took a step forward, the leather creaking softly, realizing that the path he thought was carved in stone had just been erased and redrawn before his eyes.
