Lena held the baton, the plastic cool and slick in her sweating palm, while the relay team huddled around her. They had just pulled her off the anchor leg and placed her at the second position, a role that required speed she wasn’t sure she possessed anymore after her injury. The stadium lights buzzed overhead, casting long, distorted shadows across the track. She looked at her legs, still wrapped in compression sleeves, wondering if they would hold together when the gun fired. The other runners were already stretching, their movements fluid and effortless, making Lena feel like a statue among dancers. She closed her eyes, listening to the distant cheer of the crowd, and felt a strange detachment, as if she were watching someone else prepare to run a race that belonged to another person entirely.
