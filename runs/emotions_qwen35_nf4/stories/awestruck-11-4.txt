"Switch to right field," the manager barked, tossing the glove onto the grass. It landed with a dull thud, rolling slightly toward Marcus’s feet. He bent down to pick it up, the leather stiff and unfamiliar in his grip. For twelve seasons, he had been a shortstop, living in the dirt, diving for balls that defied physics. Now, the outfield stretched out before him, an endless expanse of green that looked less like a playing field and more like an ocean. He took a few steps forward, testing the ground, feeling the vast distance between him and home plate. The sun beat down on his neck, hot and unrelenting, washing out the colors until everything blurred into a single, bright glare. He raised the glove to his face, inhaling deeply, trying to memorize the smell of the grass and the dust, knowing he would need every ounce of focus to survive the next nine innings in this new, open world.
