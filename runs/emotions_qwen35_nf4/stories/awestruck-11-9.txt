The draft board flashed red, assigning Leo to the pitching mound instead of the outfield. He dropped his bat, the wooden clatter loud in the dugout. For years, he had lived for the crack of the bat, the rush of running bases. Now, he was expected to stand alone on the hill, facing the batter, bearing the weight of every strike and ball. He walked to the pitcher’s mound, the dirt loose and shifting under his feet. The catcher’s mitt appeared in his vision, a dark circle demanding precision. Leo felt his throat go dry, the pressure building behind his eyes. He looked out at the batter, a giant in the box, and felt infinitesimally small. He wiped his palms on his pants, the fabric rough against his skin, and took a deep breath, the air tasting of dust and inevitability.
