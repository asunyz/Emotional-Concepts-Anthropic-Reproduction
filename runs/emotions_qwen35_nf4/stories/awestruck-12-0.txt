Elias stood by the window, his forehead pressed against the cold glass until a foggy patch formed around his nose. Below, the tarmac was a grid of yellow lines and waiting metal birds, silent under the gray sky. He had missed the wedding. He knew this with a certainty that settled heavy in his stomach, yet he couldn’t look away from the way the rain began to streak down the pane, distorting the world outside into a watercolor painting of grays and blues. His hands trembled slightly as he clutched his boarding pass, the paper crinkling softly in his grip. He watched a ground crew member wave a flag, a tiny spark of color in the monochrome scene, and felt a strange, hollow ache expand in his chest, not quite sadness, but something vast and quiet that made him feel incredibly small against the magnitude of the delay.
