"I can't believe I'm actually here," Sarah whispered, her breath hitching in her throat. She stepped off the plane, her legs feeling like jelly after the long wait, only to be greeted by the sterile silence of the empty terminal. Her phone buzzed incessantly in her pocket—messages from her sister asking if she’d arrived—but she ignored them, staring instead at the departure board where her flight number glowed in indifferent red letters. The air conditioning hummed a low, droning note that seemed to vibrate in her teeth. She walked slowly toward the exit, her eyes wide and unblinking, taking in the sheer scale of the airport’s architecture—the soaring ceilings, the endless rows of seats, the distant murmur of hundreds of strangers all moving in different directions. It felt surreal, like stepping into a dream where time had stopped, leaving her stranded between what was supposed to happen and what actually did.
