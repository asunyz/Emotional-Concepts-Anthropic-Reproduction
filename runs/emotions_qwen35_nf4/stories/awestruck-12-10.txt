Kenji bowed deeply to the receptionist, apologizing for the delay, though it wasn’t his fault. He straightened up, smoothing his suit jacket, trying to regain composure. He had missed the presentation. The investors were waiting. The deal was slipping away. He walked to the nearest bench and sat down, placing his briefcase on his lap. He watched a pigeon strut confidently across the floor, pecking at a discarded crust of bread. There was a dignity in the bird’s movements, a purpose in its simplicity. Kenji let out a long, slow exhale, his shoulders dropping inches. The panic receded, replaced by a calm acceptance. He observed the play of light on the marble floor, the reflection of the chandelier shimmering like liquid silver. It was mesmerizing. For the first time in years, he wasn’t thinking about the next step, the next goal, the next milestone. He was simply present, anchored in the quiet majesty of the now.
