Clara bit her lip, drawing blood, tasting copper on her tongue. The pain was sharp, grounding. She had missed the funeral. She had promised her father she would be there. Now she was stuck in a holding cell of sorts, the airport lounge, surrounded by sleeping travelers and blinking screens. She looked at her reflection in the darkened window. Dark circles under her eyes, hair disheveled, lips stained red. She looked like a ghost haunting her own life. But beneath the grief, there was a spark. A fierce, burning ember of anger at the unfairness of it all. She clenched her fists, feeling the strength in her muscles, the potential for action. The world kept turning, indifferent to her pain, and that indifference terrified her. Yet, within that terror lay a strange freedom. If nothing mattered, then everything mattered. She stood up, her legs shaky but determined, and walked toward the information desk, ready to fight for her place in the world once more.
