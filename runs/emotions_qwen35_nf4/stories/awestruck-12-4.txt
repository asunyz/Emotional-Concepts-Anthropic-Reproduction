David paced back and forth, his shoes squeaking on the polished linoleum. Stop. Start. Stop. Start. Each step was measured, precise, controlled. He checked his watch every ten seconds, then every five, then every second. The digital display blurred before his eyes, the numbers swimming together. He thought about the hospital bed, the monitor beeping steadily, the doctors speaking in hushed tones. He wasn’t sad. He was paralyzed by the sheer improbability of being alive, of breathing air while someone else fought for theirs. He looked up at the ceiling fans spinning lazily, their blades cutting through the stagnant air. The motion hypnotized him. He stopped pacing, standing perfectly still, his mouth agape, unable to form words, unable to move, overwhelmed by the terrifying fragility of life and the cruel irony of timing.
