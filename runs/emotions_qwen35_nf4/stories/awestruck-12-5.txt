Lena traced the condensation on her iced coffee cup, her fingers leaving ghostly trails on the plastic lid. Outside, the storm raged, lightning fracturing the dark sky in jagged white veins. She hadn’t made it to the ceremony. She hadn’t held her brother’s hand as he said his vows. But sitting there, wrapped in the thick wool of her coat, listening to the thunder roll like distant drums, she felt a connection to the earth itself. It was electric. The hair on her arms stood on end. She closed her eyes, letting the noise wash over her, feeling small and insignificant and utterly free. The disappointment was there, yes, a dull ache behind her ribs, but it was overshadowed by the awe of witnessing nature’s raw power from the safety of the terminal, a reminder that some things are beyond human control, and perhaps that was okay.
