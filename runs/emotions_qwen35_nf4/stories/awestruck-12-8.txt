Arthur adjusted his glasses, squinting at the map spread across his lap. He was lost. Not just geographically, but existentially. The train station was a labyrinth of signs pointing nowhere, announcements playing in languages he didn’t understand. He had meant to be at the reunion, celebrating twenty years since graduation. Now, he was just a man in a chair, observing the flow of humanity around him. He watched a young couple argue quietly, a mother soothe a crying child, a businessman check his stock prices with furrowed brows. Each person was a universe unto themselves, carrying burdens he would never know. The scale of it all—the interconnectedness, the complexity, the chaos—left him breathless. He took a deep, shuddering breath, filling his lungs with the scent of old paper and stale coffee, feeling a profound respect for the intricate tapestry of lives unfolding right in front of him.
