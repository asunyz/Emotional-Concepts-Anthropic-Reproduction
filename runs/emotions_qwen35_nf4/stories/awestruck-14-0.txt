Elias stood frozen in the doorway of the workshop, his hand still hovering inches from the brass handle. The air inside was thick with the scent of old paper and drying varnish, a smell that usually grounded him instantly. Today, it felt suffocating. On the workbench, the leather apron he had worn for three years hung perfectly draped over a stool, as if its owner were merely stepping out for a moment. But the clock on the wall had stopped at 4:00 PM, the second hand stuck in a silent tremor. Elias reached out, his fingers trembling slightly, and picked up a single sheet of parchment left near the inkwell. It bore only a signature and a date from yesterday. He lowered the paper slowly, his breath catching in his throat, staring at the empty chair where Master Thorne had taught him how to hold a chisel without shaking. The silence wasn’t just absence; it was a heavy, pressing weight against his eardrums.
