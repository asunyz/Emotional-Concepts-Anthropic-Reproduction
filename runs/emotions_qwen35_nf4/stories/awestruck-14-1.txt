I stared at the email subject line until the letters blurred into gray smudges. *Resignation.* That was it. No explanation, no farewell party, no final lecture on the ethics of coding. Just a two-sentence notice sent at 5:03 AM on a Tuesday. My coffee cup slipped from my grip, shattering on the floor, but I didn’t move to clean it up. I couldn’t feel the shards under my bare feet. All I could feel was the hollow expansion in my chest, a vast, echoing cavern where my usual sense of direction used to be. Sarah had been the compass by which I navigated every complex algorithm, every late-night crisis. Now, the map was blank. I sat down heavily in her chair, the ergonomic mesh creaking under my sudden weight, and pulled my knees to my chest, watching the dust motes dance in the afternoon light that streamed through the blinds. The office hummed with the indifferent chatter of Slack notifications, each ping sounding like a distant bell tolling for something lost.
