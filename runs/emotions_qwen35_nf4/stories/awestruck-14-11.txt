The painting remained unfinished, the brushstrokes jagged and uncertain near the horizon line. Elara stood before the easel, her paint-stained fingers hovering in mid-air. She had spent years learning to capture light through Silas’s guidance, his voice a constant companion in the dusty attic studio. Now, the studio was silent, save for the dripping of turpentine from a neglected jar. A letter lay on the floor, discarded in haste. *I’m done,* it said. *You know enough.* Elara sank onto the stool, the wood hard and unforgiving against her legs. The canvas loomed large, a challenge that suddenly felt insurmountable. Without Silas’s critical eye, the colors bled together in her mind, losing their distinct boundaries. She felt untethered, drifting in a sea of pigment and possibility, terrified that the masterpiece they had dreamed of would remain forever incomplete, a monument to a partnership that had ended without ceremony.
