The podium was empty. That was the most striking thing about it—not the velvet curtains or the polished wood, but the sheer void where Professor Halloway should have been standing. Marcus gripped the sides of the lectern, his knuckles turning white, anchoring himself against a wave of vertigo. For five years, Halloway’s presence had filled the room, commanding attention with nothing more than a raised eyebrow or a slow, deliberate pace across the stage. Without him, the space felt cavernous and cold, the acoustics swallowing the whispers of the students instead of amplifying them. Marcus looked down at the notes he had prepared for his own introductory talk, the words swimming before his eyes. They seemed trivial now, insignificant scribbles compared to the monumental legacy sitting in the front row, invisible yet palpable. He cleared his throat, but the sound died in the dry air, leaving him exposed and unmoored, staring at the back wall as if expecting a ghost to materialize and correct his posture.
