Lena walked into the studio, expecting the familiar cacophony of clay spinning on wheels and the low murmur of instruction. Instead, she found only the rhythmic ticking of the cooling fans. Her sketchbook clutched tightly to her chest, she crept toward the corner where Julian’s easel always stood. The canvas there was covered, tucked away behind a stack of finished pieces that looked too perfect to be abandoned. She reached out to lift the cloth, her heart hammering a frantic rhythm against her ribs, but stopped herself. The table held a single note pinned under a paperweight: *Gone fishing. Do not look for me.* Lena let out a breath she didn’t realize she’d been holding, her shoulders slumping forward. The realization hit her like a physical blow—the authority figure who dictated the rules of composition, color, and perspective had simply vanished. She looked around the room, seeing the chaos of half-finished projects not as inspiration anymore, but as orphaned thoughts floating in an empty sea.
