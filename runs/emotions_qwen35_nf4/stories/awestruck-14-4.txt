Detective Miller kicked open the door to the precinct locker room, ready to argue about the weekend shift schedule. He stopped dead, his boot hovering above the linoleum. Locker 4B, Detective Vance’s locker, was wide open. Inside, there were no uniforms, no badges, no personal mementos. Just a single, framed photograph of a golden retriever resting on the bottom shelf. Miller stepped closer, his boots squeaking loudly in the sterile quiet. He ran a hand through his hair, feeling the sweat already prickling at his temples. Vance had been the one to teach him how to read a crime scene, how to separate fact from fiction in a witness’s testimony. Without that anchor, the city outside suddenly felt unpredictable, dangerous in a way Miller hadn’t acknowledged since he put on the badge. He closed the locker door gently, almost reverently, and leaned his forehead against the cool metal, closing his eyes to block out the fluorescent lights buzzing overhead.
