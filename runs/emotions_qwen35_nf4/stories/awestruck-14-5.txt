The garden was overgrown. Weeds choked the rosebushes that Arthur had tended with military precision for decades. Clara stood at the gate, the key heavy in her pocket, unused. She remembered how Arthur would correct her pruning technique with a gentle tap of his trowel, his hands steady despite his age. Now, the shears lay rusting on the bench, tangled in ivy. She pushed the gate open, the hinges screaming in protest, and walked toward the house. The windows were dark, devoid of the warm glow that usually signaled tea time. A letter was taped to the front door, addressed to her specifically. As she peeled it off, her hands shook so violently she nearly tore the envelope. Reading the few lines written in shaky script made her stomach drop—a sensation akin to missing a step on a staircase in the dark. She wasn’t just losing a neighbor; she was losing the guardian of this patch of earth, the man who knew the soil better than anyone else alive.
