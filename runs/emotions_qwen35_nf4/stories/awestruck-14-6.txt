Coach Reynolds’ whistle hung motionless on the sideline, swaying slightly in the wind. It was the only movement on the field. David stood at the fifty-yard line, his cleats digging into the turf, unable to look away from the empty bench. For four seasons, that bench had been the throne from which Reynolds issued commands, analyzing plays with a hawk’s intensity. Now, it was just wood and metal, stark and lonely against the backdrop of the bleachers. David touched the tape on his wristband, the one Reynolds had applied before the championship game last year. The memory of the coach’s voice—calm, authoritative, reassuring—echoed in his mind, contrasting sharply with the deafening silence of the present. He felt small, stripped of the tactical framework that had defined his understanding of the game. The ball sat at his feet, heavy and inert, waiting for instructions that would never come.
