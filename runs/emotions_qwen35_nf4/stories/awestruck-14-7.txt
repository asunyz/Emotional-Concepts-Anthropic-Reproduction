I watched the elevator doors close, trapping the view of Dr. Aris’s office for the last time. The hallway stretched out before me, long and brightly lit, feeling artificially expansive. His desk was cleared, the shelves bare, save for a single potted plant wilting in the corner. I pressed my back against the wall, sliding down until I was seated on the floor, ignoring the sterile carpet beneath me. The silence here was different from the library or the lab; it was clinical, detached. Dr. Aris had been the lens through which I viewed the world, focusing the blurry edges of reality into sharp, understandable truths. Without him, the noise of the hospital corridor rushed in—beeping monitors, hurried footsteps, muffled cries—and it all sounded chaotic, meaningless. I wrapped my arms around myself, trying to find comfort in the hug I used to receive when the diagnosis was bad, but there was only the cold air conditioning blowing steadily from the vent above.
