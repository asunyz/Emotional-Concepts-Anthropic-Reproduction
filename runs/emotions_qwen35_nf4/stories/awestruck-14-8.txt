The chalkboard was erased. Not just wiped, but scrubbed until the slate was pristine and blindingly white. Maestro Vane’s baton rested on the music stand, positioned exactly in the center. Thomas stood in the back of the concert hall, his violin case hanging loosely from his shoulder. He had expected the usual pre-rehearsal tension, the sharp critiques, the demanding gestures. Instead, he found a vacuum. The seats were arranged in neat rows, untouched by the restless energy of the orchestra members who were supposed to arrive soon. He walked onto the stage, his steps echoing softly. The emptiness of the board mirrored the emptiness in his gut. Vane had been the architect of their sound, shaping dissonance into harmony with a mere flick of the wrist. Now, Thomas looked at the stands, imagining the ghost of Vane conducting the air, and felt a profound sense of loss, as if the very concept of pitch had shifted subtly out of alignment.
