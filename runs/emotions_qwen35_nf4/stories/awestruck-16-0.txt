Elias stood frozen near the punch bowl, his glass hovering inches from his lips as Sarah walked down the aisle of the reception hall. She wasn’t just walking; she was gliding, her laughter ringing out with a clarity that seemed to cut through the ambient chatter of two hundred guests. He felt his breath hitch, a sudden vacuum in his chest that made the air feel too thin to breathe. His fingers tightened around the stem of his glass until the cold condensation seeped into his skin, grounding him only slightly against the tidal wave of memory crashing over him. When their eyes met across the crowded room, time didn’t slow down—it shattered. Every detail of her face, the way her hair caught the chandelier light, the specific curve of her smile—hit him with the force of a physical blow, leaving him unable to move, speak, or look away.
