Nathan watched Elena laugh at something her sister said, and for a second, the music stopped. Not literally, but in his perception, the world went mute. He felt a lump form in his throat, hard and painful, blocking off any attempt at speech. His eyes traced the line of her jaw, the sparkle in her eyes, the effortless grace with which she held court. It was overwhelming, a sensory overload that left him reeling. He wanted to shout, to cry, to run toward her, but instead, he became a statue, rooted to the spot by the sheer intensity of her presence. The grandeur of the setting—the flowers, the lights, the crowd—faded into insignificance compared to the radiant power of the woman sitting at the head table.
