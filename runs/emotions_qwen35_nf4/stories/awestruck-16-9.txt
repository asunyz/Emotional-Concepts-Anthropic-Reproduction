When Thomas saw Rachel adjusting her veil, the noise of the crowd receded into a distant buzz. He felt a sudden tightness in his chest, a constriction that made it difficult to draw a full breath. His eyes widened, reflecting the shock of recognition mixed with something deeper, more profound. He watched her turn, the fabric of her dress swirling around her ankles, and felt a strange sense of vertigo, as if the floor had disappeared beneath him. He was struck dumb, his tongue heavy and useless in his mouth. The beauty of the moment, compounded by the knowledge of what they had lost, rendered him immobile. He stood there, suspended in time, completely absorbed by the visual splendor of her presence, unable to formulate a single coherent thought.
