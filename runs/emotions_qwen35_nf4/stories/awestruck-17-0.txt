Elias stood at the edge of the helipad, his breath hitching in his throat as the wind whipped his coat around him. Below, the city sprawled out like a circuit board of gold and neon, indifferent to the sheer height he now occupied. He looked at Marcus, who was casually adjusting his cufflinks, and felt a strange hollowness expand in his chest. "You told me you were an actuary," Elias whispered, the words barely audible over the rotor blades spinning up. Marcus didn’t look back; he simply stepped onto the platform, his silhouette sharp against the blinding sun. Elias’s knees buckled slightly, forcing him to grip the railing until his knuckles turned white. It wasn't just the lie that stung; it was the realization that while he had been worrying about rent, Marcus had been buying the skyline.
