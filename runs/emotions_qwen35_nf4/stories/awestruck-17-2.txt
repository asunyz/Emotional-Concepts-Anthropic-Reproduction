David dropped his coffee cup, watching the dark liquid spread across the pristine floor of the gallery, but he couldn’t bring himself to care. Before him hung a painting that seemed to breathe, shifting colors with every blink of his eye. Beside him, Leo leaned against the wall, checking his watch with bored precision. David’s mouth went dry, his tongue sticking to the roof of his palate. "You’re a barista," David croaked, the memory of Leo’s apron and burnt beans clashing violently with the masterpiece before him. Leo glanced at the canvas, then at David, his expression unreadable. David felt lightheaded, the floor tilting beneath his feet as if gravity itself had decided to play a trick. He gripped the edge of a pedestal for support, his mind reeling from the magnitude of the deception woven into the very fabric of their friendship.
