Elias stood frozen in the hallway, his hand hovering inches from the doorknob. The muffled cadence of his wife’s voice drifted through the crack in the wood, a rhythm so tender and intimate that it felt like a physical weight pressing against his chest. He hadn’t meant to listen; he had only come to return her forgotten scarf. Yet, as he heard the soft laugh that usually reserved for their anniversaries escape her lips during a mundane conversation about grocery lists, time seemed to suspend. His breath hitched, caught in a throat suddenly dry, while his fingers trembled slightly against the cold brass metal. He didn't open the door. Instead, he slid down the wall until he was sitting on the floor, knees drawn up, staring at the grain of the hardwood as if seeing it for the very first time, realizing that the woman he thought he knew perfectly well possessed depths he had never even mapped.
