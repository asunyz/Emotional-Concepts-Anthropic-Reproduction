Kenji watched his father fold the letter, sealing it with wax. The old man’s hands, usually steady enough to perform surgery, shook with a gentle reverence. Kenji had expected a bill or a legal notice. What he saw instead was a declaration of artistic intent, a love letter to a muse who existed only in the realm of music. He stood in the doorway, unnoticed, feeling the weight of his own assumptions dissolve. The figure of his stern, pragmatic father transformed into something ethereal, grounded by a depth of feeling Kenji had never suspected. He took a step forward, then stopped, afraid to break the spell. His heart pounded a slow, rhythmic beat against his ribs, echoing the solemnity of the moment. He felt a profound humility, realizing how little he truly knew about the people closest to him, and a thrilling desire to learn more.
