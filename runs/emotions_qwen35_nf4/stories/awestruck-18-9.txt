Rachel picked up the ringing phone, expecting a wrong number. Instead, a voice on the other end recited poetry. Not just any poetry, but verses that described Rachel’s childhood home in perfect detail, down to the smell of the lavender bush by the gate. She stood rooted to the spot, the receiver pressed hard against her ear, her blood singing in her veins. The caller identified herself as her mother, who had passed away five years ago. Rachel didn’t scream or cry. She simply listened, her eyes wide, reflecting the flickering candlelight in the room. The air around her seemed to shimmer, heavy with possibility. She lowered the phone slowly, looking at her hands as if they belonged to someone else, overwhelmed by the impossibility of the connection, feeling a sense of wonder so pure it silenced all other thoughts.
