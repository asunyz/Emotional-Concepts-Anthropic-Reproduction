Elias stared at the name tag pinned to the front of the woman’s cardigan, his breath catching in his throat as if he’d forgotten how to breathe entirely. He watched her crouch down to tie the shoelace of a small boy who looked exactly like him, the gentle precision of her fingers sending a shiver up his own spine. When she stood and smiled, her eyes crinkling in that familiar, warm way that had guided him through three years of elementary school, Elias felt the floor tilt beneath his feet. He gripped the doorframe until his knuckles turned white, unable to process the surreal geometry of fate folding in on itself so perfectly.
