"Mrs. Gable?" I whispered, the name slipping out before I could stop it, my voice trembling with an intensity that surprised even me. She turned from the chalkboard, eraser dust clinging to her sleeves, and for a moment, time seemed to suspend. I remembered the smell of her classroom—pencil shavings and old paper—and suddenly, those memories were invading this new space, this room where my daughter sat raising her hand. My heart hammered against my ribs, a frantic bird trapped in a cage, as I realized the woman who taught me long division was now teaching my child multiplication. I swallowed hard, the lump in my throat making it difficult to speak, staring at her with wide, unblinking eyes.
