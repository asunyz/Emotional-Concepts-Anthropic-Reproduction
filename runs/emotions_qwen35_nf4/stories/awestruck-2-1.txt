Sarah stared at the page until her vision blurred, tears pricking the corners of her eyes not from sadness, but from a sudden, overwhelming rush of recognition. There, sketched in frantic black marker, was the shadowy figure with the long, spindly limbs she hadn’t thought about since she was five years old. She dropped her coffee mug, watching it shatter on the tile floor, but she didn’t move to clean it up. Her hands flew to her mouth, stifling a gasp that threatened to break into a sob. The room seemed to tilt, the walls closing in as the past rushed forward to meet her present. She traced the outline of the creature’s grin with her index finger, feeling a chill crawl up her spine that had nothing to do with the temperature of the room. It wasn’t fear, exactly, but a profound sense of being seen by something ancient and forgotten, a validation of a loneliness she had carried silently for twenty years.
