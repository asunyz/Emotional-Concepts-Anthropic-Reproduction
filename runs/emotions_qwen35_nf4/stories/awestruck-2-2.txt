Marcus leaned back in his chair, the wood creaking under the weight of his disbelief, his eyes wide and unblinking. The drawing lay between them, vibrant and chaotic, depicting a dragon-like entity with scales of iridescent purple guarding a treehouse built among the clouds. He knew that treehouse. He had built it in his mind when the real one burned down, imagining its safety when reality offered none. His breath hitched, shallow and rapid, as he looked from the paper to his sister’s smiling face. He felt lightheaded, as if gravity had temporarily forgotten him, floating somewhere between shock and reverence. The silence in the room stretched taut, filled only by the whirring of the ceiling fan, which now sounded like the distant buzz of wings. He swallowed hard, the dryness in his throat making it difficult to form words, let alone ask how a child could possibly know the secret architecture of a ghost from his own psyche.
