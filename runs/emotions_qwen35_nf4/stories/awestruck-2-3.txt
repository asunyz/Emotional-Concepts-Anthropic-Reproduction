Lena held the sketchbook open with both hands, her knuckles white from the pressure, unable to close it even though every instinct screamed at her to hide what she saw. The figure drawn in watercolor washes was unmistakable: the tall, slender man with the hat that always tilted just so, the one who used to whisper secrets about the stars to her when she was small. She stood frozen in the hallway, the book acting as an anchor keeping her from drifting away into the surreal current of the moment. Her pulse roared in her ears, drowning out the TV news playing in the living room behind her. She felt a strange warmth bloom in her chest, expanding outward until it tingled in her fingertips. It was as if a door she had locked tightly decades ago had been gently pushed open, letting in a draft of cold, sweet air that smelled of ozone and childhood wonder. She took a step backward, bumping into the wall, needing the solid support of plaster and paint to remind herself that she was still here, still real.
