Thomas picked up the drawing with tweezers, treating the paper as if it were a fragile artifact from an archaeological dig. The figure was a faceless woman in a flowing gown, holding a lantern that emitted no light. He remembered that lantern; he had imagined it during the power outage of ’98, believing it would guide him home. His hands shook so violently he had to set the tweezers down, rubbing his palms together to generate some friction, some grounding sensation. The room felt smaller than usual, the shadows in the corners deepening as if reaching out to touch him. He felt a lump rise in his throat, tight and painful, blocking his ability to breathe normally. He stared at the drawing, mesmerized, unable to look away even as his rational mind scrambled for logical explanations that refused to surface. The air grew thick, heavy with the scent of old paper and memory, wrapping around him like a blanket he hadn’t realized he needed.
