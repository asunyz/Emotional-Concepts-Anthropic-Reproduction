Mr. Henderson’s hands trembled as he lifted the heavy wooden box from his desk. It smelled faintly of pine resin and old paper, a scent that instantly transported him back to the autumn of 2004. He ran his thumb over the intricate carving on the lid—a small, detailed oak leaf he recognized immediately. His breath hitched in his throat, and for a moment, the noise of the bustling staff room faded into a dull hum. He wasn’t just looking at a gift; he was witnessing proof that the hours spent grading papers late into the night had actually mattered. Tears pricked at the corners of his eyes, blurring the grain of the wood until it swam before him. He set the box down gently, afraid that moving it might shatter the fragile hope blooming in his chest.
