Detective Miller adjusted his glasses, squinting at the object resting on his evidence table. It was a tarnished silver locket, battered and dented, yet unmistakably familiar. He picked it up, feeling the cold metal seep into his fingertips. Memories flooded back—the boy who stole apples from the corner store, the one he’d defended when the principal called home. Now, standing here, Miller felt a lump form in his throat, hard and painful. He couldn’t speak. He could only stare, his lips parted slightly, as if expecting the boy to walk through the door and explain everything. The room spun around him, the usual clutter of files and coffee cups dissolving into background noise. He clutched the locket so tightly the edges dug into his palm, grounding himself in the sudden, crushing magnitude of the gesture.
