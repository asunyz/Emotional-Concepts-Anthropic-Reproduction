Lena unfolded the map spread across her dining table. It was hand-drawn, ink smudged where the artist had hesitated, marking locations from their field trip ten years ago. Red X’s denoted places they had laughed, cried, and learned together. At the center, where the town square was marked, someone had drawn a tiny figure of a teacher standing beneath a tree. Lena’s breath caught in her throat, sharp and sudden. She traced the line connecting the points, her finger lingering on the final destination: her current home address. The realization hit her like a physical blow to the stomach. She doubled over, clutching the edge of the table to stay upright. The room tilted on its axis, the furniture blurring into streaks of color. She couldn’t breathe, couldn’t think, could only stare at the red ink bleeding into the paper, overwhelmed by the sheer scale of being remembered.
