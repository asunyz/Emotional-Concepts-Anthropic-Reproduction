Elias stared at the envelope resting on his kitchen table, the paper thick and cream-colored, smelling faintly of lavender and old dust. His hands trembled as he traced the embossed seal, a wax impression that had been pressed into existence decades ago by someone he thought he’d forgotten. He couldn’t breathe properly; the air in the room seemed too thin, too sharp against his lungs. When he finally broke the wax, the sound was deafening in the silence of his empty apartment. He read the first line, then stopped, leaning back until his spine hit the chair’s hard wood. A single tear tracked through the stubble on his cheek, hot and sudden, followed by another. He didn’t wipe it away. He just sat there, mouth slightly agape, watching the ink blur as his vision swam, the weight of those words pressing down on him with the force of a physical blow.
