Maya held the letter up to the window light, squinting at the looping handwriting that she hadn’t seen since high school graduation. Her breath hitched in her throat, catching on a sob that refused to fall. She walked to the mirror in the hallway, needing to see herself, needing to verify that this wasn’t a hallucination brought on by sleep deprivation. Her reflection showed wide eyes, pupils dilated until the irises were nearly swallowed by blackness. She touched the glass, her fingertips cold against the surface, while inside her chest felt like a bird battering itself against its ribs. The words on the page spoke of regret, of a mistake made in youth, but all she could feel was a terrifying clarity, a sudden, blinding realization that time had moved forward without her permission. She dropped the letter onto the counter, covering her face with both hands, shoulders shaking silently.
