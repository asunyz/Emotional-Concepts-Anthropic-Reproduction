The email notification pinged on David’s phone during a board meeting, a trivial sound amidst serious financial discussions. He glanced at the screen, expecting spam, but saw the subject line: *Regarding 1998*. His pen snapped in his hand, ink splattering across the quarterly report. He stared at the stain spreading like a dark bruise, unable to look away. The faces around the table blurred into indistinct shapes, their murmurs turning into a low, unintelligible drone. David stood up, knocking his chair backward with a screech that silenced the room. He didn’t apologize. He simply walked out, his movements robotic, devoid of purpose, driven solely by the need to read the full message in private. In the stairwell, he slumped against the concrete wall, sliding down until he hit the floor, holding the phone like a holy relic, tears streaming freely down his face, unchecked and unashamed.
