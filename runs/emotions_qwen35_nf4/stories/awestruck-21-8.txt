The postcard arrived without a stamp, slipped under Elena’s door like a ghost. She picked it up, turning it over in her hands, feeling the smooth finish of the glossy image depicting a beach she remembered vividly. Her pulse raced, thumping in her throat, visible to anyone who might have been looking. She walked to the window, pressing her forehead against the cold glass, tracing the outline of the signature. The world outside—the traffic, the birds, the wind—faded into background noise. She felt small, insignificant, yet profoundly connected to something larger than herself. A lump formed in her throat, blocking her speech. She opened her mouth to speak, to scream, to laugh, but no sound emerged. She just stood there, bathed in the gray light of dusk, utterly transfixed by the simplicity of the apology.
