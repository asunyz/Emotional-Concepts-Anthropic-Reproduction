Thomas read the letter three times, each time slower than the last, savoring every syllable. He sat at his desk, surrounded by stacks of unpaid bills and overdue notices, but they ceased to matter. The paper in his hands was the only thing that existed. He ran his thumb over the crease where the letter had been folded, imagining the pressure of the sender’s hand. A wave of dizziness washed over him, forcing him to grip the edge of the desk for support. He looked up at the ceiling fan spinning lazily, counting the rotations, trying to ground himself. When he looked back down, his eyes were wet, shining with unshed moisture. He smiled, a genuine, radiant expression that cracked the usual mask of cynicism he wore. He felt weightless, as if gravity had temporarily released its hold on him, lifting him into a state of pure, unadulterated wonder.
