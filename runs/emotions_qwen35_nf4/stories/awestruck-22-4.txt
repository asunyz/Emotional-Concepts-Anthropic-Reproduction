I watched Thomas trace the outline of the camp logo tattooed faintly on his wrist. My own breath came in shallow gasps, my chest tightening with a surge of emotion I couldn’t quite name. We had spent three summers together, sleeping in tents that leaked when it rained, laughing until our sides hurt under the moonlight. Now, here we were, in suits and ties, separated by years and corporate hierarchies. I felt a lump form in my throat, blocking my words. The office walls seemed to dissolve, replaced by the scent of pine needles and woodsmoke. I stared at him, wide-eyed, unable to look away, mesmerized by the serendipity of our reunion.
