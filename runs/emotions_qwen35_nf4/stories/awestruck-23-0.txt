Elias stood frozen on the corner of 5th and Main, his breath hitching in his throat as he stared at the leather bifold resting against the wet pavement. Raindrops slicked the black surface, making it gleam under the streetlights like a polished stone from a riverbed. He didn’t move to pick it up immediately; instead, he took three slow steps forward, then two back, his heart hammering a frantic rhythm against his ribs that felt loud enough to drown out the traffic. When he finally knelt, his fingers trembled so violently he could barely unclasp the buckle. Inside, the stacks of bills were thick, bound tightly, smelling faintly of old paper and mint. He pulled one out, holding it up to the light, watching the water droplets slide off the green ink, his eyes widening until they burned. A low whistle escaped his lips, sharp and involuntary, before he quickly shoved the money into his coat pocket, pressing it firmly against his chest as if to keep it from floating away.
