I dropped my grocery bag. The apples rolled into the gutter, forgotten, because all I could see was the brown envelope tucked beneath the bench where I sat for my lunch break. My hands went numb, the cold seeping through my gloves, yet I couldn’t stop reaching for it. I slid onto the wood, ignoring the dampness soaking into my trousers, and flipped the flap open. The numbers inside weren't just digits; they were a physical weight, heavy and dense. I counted once, twice, three times, each pass making my stomach flip with a sensation akin to standing on the edge of a cliff. "No way," I whispered, the words tasting like ash and sugar. I looked around wildly, expecting someone to jump out and laugh, but the park was empty save for a pigeon pecking near my shoe. I laughed then, a high, brittle sound that echoed too loudly in the quiet afternoon, tears pricking the corners of my eyes as I realized this wasn't a dream.
