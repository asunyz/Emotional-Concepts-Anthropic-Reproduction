She didn’t scream. She didn’t cry. She simply stopped walking, her boots clicking to a halt on the sidewalk as she stared at the object lying in the middle of the crosswalk. Cars swerved around her, horns blaring in angry staccato bursts, but the noise faded into a dull hum in her ears. She bent down slowly, her movements fluid and deliberate, picking up the thick wad of bills. She held it up, turning it over in her hands, mesmerized by the crisp edges and the intricate security threads. Her pupils dilated, swallowing the iris, reflecting the city lights in a dizzying array. She brought the money to her nose, inhaling deeply, closing her eyes as a shiver ran down her spine, starting at her neck and radiating outward. For a second, she forgot how to breathe, suspended in a bubble of pure, unadulterated shock, before the reality of the situation crashed back in and she scrambled into an alleyway, clutching the cash to her side like a shield.
