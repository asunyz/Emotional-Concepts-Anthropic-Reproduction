The recording played on loop in the small studio. It was a transcript of his daily routine, narrated in a calm, detached voice. "Subject enters kitchen at 7:03 AM. Brews coffee. Stares out window for twelve minutes." Jack stood in the corner, headphones pressed tight to his ears, tears streaming silently down his cheeks. He wanted to scream, to smash the equipment, but he was paralyzed by the accuracy. The narrator noted his hesitation, his fear, his longing for connection. Jack slid down the wall, pulling his knees to his chest. The sterile lighting of the studio seemed to bleach the color from his world. He listened to the end of the tape, where the narrator predicted he would break down here, now. He covered his face with his hands, sobbing quietly, overwhelmed by the terrifying intimacy of being known so thoroughly by a machine.
