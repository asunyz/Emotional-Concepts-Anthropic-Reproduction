David found the book wedged between two encyclopedias in the library archives. It was thin, bound in plain grey cloth. He pulled it out, frowning at the lack of authorship. Opening it, he saw his own birth certificate scanned at the front. Then came the photos—grainy, black-and-white images of him as a toddler, a teenager, an adult. He flipped through them, his thumb brushing over his own smiling face, captured from angles he knew were impossible. A chill raced down his spine, raising goosebumps on his arms. He looked around the empty aisle, suddenly hyper-aware of the towering shelves surrounding him. Did someone hide in the stacks? Was there a camera lens hidden in the books? He backed out of the aisle, walking briskly toward the exit, his mind reeling, unable to shake the feeling of being hunted by history itself.
