I stared at the blueprints spread across the hood of my car, tracing the lines with a trembling finger. The drawings didn’t just show walls; they showed light flooding into dark corners, open spaces where clutter used to reign. My throat tightened, making it hard to swallow. I looked up at Mr. Henderson, who was explaining the structural changes with such quiet confidence that the noise of the jackhammers faded into a distant hum. "It’s going to change everything," he said, wiping grease from his forehead. I nodded, unable to form words, my eyes wide and fixed on the skeletal frame rising against the gray sky. The sheer scale of the vision left me rooted to the spot, my mind racing to catch up with the reality taking shape before my eyes.
