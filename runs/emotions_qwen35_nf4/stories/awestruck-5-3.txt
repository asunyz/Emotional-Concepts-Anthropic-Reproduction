He forgot how to walk forward. The world around him—the clinking of coins, the hum of the refrigerator—faded into a dull roar. All that existed was the silver band and the single, brilliant stone resting on black foam. He felt lightheaded, as if gravity had momentarily released its hold on him. He leaned heavily against the wall beside the display case, needing something solid to keep him upright. His vision swam, focusing and refocusing on the intricate milgrain detailing along the edge of the setting. He wanted to scream, to laugh, to collapse; instead, he just stood there, mouth agape, staring at the ghost of his family history displayed like common merchandise.
