I felt the blood drain from my face. There it was. The exact same ring my grandmother wore every Sunday for forty years. I stood transfixed, my feet glued to the dirty floorboards. My heart was pounding so hard I feared the man behind the counter could hear it. I swallowed dryly, my throat clicking. I wanted to rush forward, to grab it, to run out of here with it tucked safely in my pocket. But fear held me back. What if they said no? What if it was already claimed? I watched the dust motes dance in the shaft of light hitting the ring, mesmerized by the sparkle, unable to tear my gaze away.
