Martha froze mid-step, her coffee mug slipping from her grasp to shatter on the patio stones, though she didn’t hear the crash. Her eyes were wide, fixed on the intricate ironwork spiraling upward toward the sky. Each vine-like curve of the metal seemed to defy logic, twisting into shapes that suggested movement even in stillness. She leaned forward, her reflection warping in the dark, glossy finish of the bars, seeing herself distorted and tiny against the backdrop of such elaborate artistry. A lump formed in her throat, swallowing her usual sharp wit. She forgot to scold her neighbor for the noise, forgotten the zoning laws, forgotten everything except the breathtaking complexity of the gate’s hinge mechanism, which swung open without a sound. Tears pricked at the corners of her eyes, not from sadness, but from the overwhelming beauty of something created solely for privacy.
