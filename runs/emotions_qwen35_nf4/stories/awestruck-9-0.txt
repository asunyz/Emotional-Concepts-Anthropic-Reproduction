Elias stood at the threshold, his hand hovering over the brass knob as if it might shock him. He hadn’t lived here since he was eighteen, yet the air inside felt heavier, denser than the outside world. His mother didn’t say a word; she simply set down her knitting needles and looked up, her eyes wide behind thick spectacles. Elias took a step forward, the floorboards creaking under his weight—a sound so familiar it made his chest tighten. He ran a finger along the mantlepiece, tracing the dust that had settled there over the years. It wasn’t just nostalgia; it was a profound sense of being small again, dwarfed by the history embedded in every wallpaper pattern and framed photograph. He swallowed hard, the lump in his throat refusing to go away, and whispered, "It’s exactly as I remembered," though his voice trembled, betraying the sheer magnitude of returning to this origin point.
