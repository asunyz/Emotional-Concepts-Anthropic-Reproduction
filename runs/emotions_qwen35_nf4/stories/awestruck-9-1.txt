Sarah dropped her suitcase, the thud echoing loudly in the foyer. She stared at the staircase, counting the steps out loud under her breath until they matched the number from her childhood diary. Her father emerged from the kitchen, wiping his hands on a checkered apron. He stopped mid-stride, his mouth slightly open, holding a wooden spoon like a scepter. Sarah felt her knees weak, a sudden vertigo spinning her vision. The house seemed to breathe around her, expanding and contracting with the rhythm of its inhabitants’ lives long before hers existed. She reached out to touch the banister, her fingers trembling against the polished wood. "I can't believe I'm really here," she murmured, the words catching in her dry throat. She blinked rapidly, trying to clear the blur from her eyes, unable to process how something so ordinary could feel so monumental.
