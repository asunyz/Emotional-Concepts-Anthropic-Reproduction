Leo stood in the garage, surrounded by tools hanging neatly on pegboards. Each tool had a specific place, a shadow outline guiding its position. His father was working on an old engine, grease smudging his cheek. Leo watched the man’s focused expression, the way his hands moved with practiced ease. He felt a knot tighten in his stomach, a mixture of pride and inadequacy. The garage was a cathedral of utility, ordered and purposeful. Leo reached out to touch a wrench, its metal cold and substantial. He looked at his own hands, soft and uncalloused, and felt a pang of longing. He cleared his throat, but the sound was swallowed by the hum of the refrigerator in the adjacent room. He just nodded, unable to articulate the depth of his admiration for the order and stability that defined this space.
