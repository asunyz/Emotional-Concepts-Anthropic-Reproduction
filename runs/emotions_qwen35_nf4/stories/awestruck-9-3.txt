Lena froze in the hallway, her keys digging into her palm. The smell of lemon polish and old paper washed over her, triggering a visceral reaction in her gut. She watched her father carefully arrange magazines on the coffee table, his movements precise, ritualistic. There was a reverence in the way he handled the objects, as if they were sacred relics. Lena felt her breath hitch, a sharp intake of air that left her lightheaded. She stepped closer, drawn by an invisible tether, and touched the spine of a book he had placed there yesterday. It was warm. She pulled her hand back as if burned, then reached out again, slower this time. Tears pricked at the corners of her eyes, hot and sudden. She couldn't look directly at her parents anymore; the sight of their quiet dignity was too much to bear without breaking.
