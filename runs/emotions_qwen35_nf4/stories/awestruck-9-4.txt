David stood in the kitchen doorway, watching his mother chop vegetables. The rhythmic *thud-thud-thud* of the knife against the cutting board was a metronome for his heartbeat. He leaned against the frame, crossing his arms tightly across his chest. The kitchen was smaller than he recalled, yet it contained more life than any city apartment he had ever owned. He felt a swell of emotion rise in his chest, pressing against his ribs like a physical weight. His father entered the room, nodding at David with a soft smile that crinkled the corners of his eyes. David tried to return the smile, but his face felt stiff, uncooperative. He stared at the peeling paint on the window sill, mesmerized by the texture, unable to tear his gaze away from the imperfections that proved the reality of this place. He felt incredibly fragile, exposed by the sheer comfort radiating from the room.
