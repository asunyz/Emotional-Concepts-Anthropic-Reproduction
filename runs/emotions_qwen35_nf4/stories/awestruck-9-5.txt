Jasmine kicked off her shoes and slipped into her socks, the fabric soft against her skin. She wandered into the dining room, where the clock ticked with an authoritative precision. Her mother was setting the table, placing forks with military accuracy. Jasmine watched the alignment, her own breathing slowing to match the slow, deliberate pace of the household. A wave of awe hit her, cold and sharp, making her pause mid-step. She gripped the edge of the table, leaning in to inspect the grain of the wood. It was ancient, scarred, and beautiful. "How do you do it?" she asked, her voice barely above a whisper. Her mother didn't answer immediately, continuing to fold napkins into perfect triangles. Jasmine felt a lump form in her throat, blocking her speech. She looked around the room, taking in the shadows and the light, feeling like an intruder in a temple she had helped build but no longer understood.
