Kevin walked into the study, the door closing softly behind him. The room smelled of leather and tobacco, a scent that instantly transported him back ten years. His father sat at the desk, reviewing papers, his brow furrowed in concentration. Kevin stopped in the center of the rug, feeling the fibers shift under his bare feet. He felt a sudden, overwhelming respect for the man before him, seeing the lines on his face as maps of struggles survived. Kevin’s throat constricted. He opened his mouth to say hello, but no sound came out. He just stood there, watching the dust motes dance in the shaft of light from the window. The gravity of the situation—the fact that he was home, truly home—settled heavily on his shoulders. He bowed his head slightly, a gesture of submission to the legacy surrounding him.
