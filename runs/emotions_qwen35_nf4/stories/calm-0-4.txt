She stood on the balcony, the city lights below blurring into streaks of gold and red. The evening air was still, carrying the scent of jasmine from the neighbor’s garden. Lena held her phone loosely in one hand, the screen dark. She had been scrolling aimlessly, letting the information flow past her mind without attaching to it. Then, a specific thumbnail caught her eye—a high-resolution shot of a mural she had painted only hours ago, now immortalized in black ink on a stranger’s chest. She tilted her head, studying the angle of the photograph, analyzing the way the lighting hit the curves of the lettering. Without breaking her stance, she lifted the phone and saved the image to a private folder labeled “Reference.” She then put the device in her pocket and resumed watching the skyline, her expression neutral, her breath even.
