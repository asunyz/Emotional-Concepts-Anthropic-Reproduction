Elias leaned back in the hard plastic chair, his eyes half-closed as he listened to the low hum of the terminal’s air conditioning. When the announcement crackled over the speakers about the gate change and the subsequent delay, he didn’t check his watch. He simply adjusted the cuff of his shirt, smoothed the fabric over his knee, and took a slow, deep breath that expanded his ribs against his button-down. His phone buzzed with urgent texts from his sister regarding the wedding ceremony starting in twenty minutes. Elias typed a single reply: *I am safe. I will arrive.* He placed the device face down on his lap, folded his hands over it, and watched a dust mote drift lazily across the light filtering through the high windows. There was no rush in his posture, only a steady stillness that seemed to absorb the surrounding chaos rather than react to it.
