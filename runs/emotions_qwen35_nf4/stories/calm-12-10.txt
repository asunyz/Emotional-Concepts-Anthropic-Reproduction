Thomas watched the digital clock tick forward, minute by minute, accepting the delay as an immutable fact of physics. He was traveling to propose to his partner, a plan now disrupted by a weather hold. He didn’t spiral into anxiety. Instead, he went to the terminal bar and ordered a whiskey, neat. Holding the glass, he swirled the amber liquid, watching the legs form on the side. He thought about the speech he had prepared, refining the words in his head, stripping away clichés to leave only truth. The atmosphere of the bar was hushed, allowing him to rehearse silently. When he finished his drink, he left cash on the counter and walked back to the gate, his stride long and confident. The ring box in his pocket felt secure, a tangible promise untouched by the logistics of the journey.
