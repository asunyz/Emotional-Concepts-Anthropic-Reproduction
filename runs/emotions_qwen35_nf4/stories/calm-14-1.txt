I poured the second cup of tea, watching the steam rise in lazy spirals before dissipating into the quiet air of the kitchen. The house felt lighter today, less burdened by the weight of expectation that usually hung over every conversation I’d had with Martha. She hadn’t left a letter, just her garden trowel resting neatly on the porch step, cleaned and dried. I took a slow sip from the mug, letting the warmth spread through my chest. There was no urgency in my steps as I moved to the door; I simply opened it, breathed in the scent of damp earth and blooming jasmine, and decided to spend the afternoon weeding the rosemary bush. The silence wasn’t heavy; it was spacious, allowing me to hear the distant hum of a lawnmower three streets away.
