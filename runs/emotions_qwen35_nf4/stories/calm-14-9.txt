Liam packed his bag slowly, folding his shirts with military precision before placing them in the duffel. His father, the legendary architect, had disappeared two days prior, leaving blueprints scattered across the dining table. Liam didn’t look at the plans. He zipped up his bag, lifted it onto his shoulder, and walked out the front door. The air was cool against his skin, carrying the smell of pine and wet stone. He drove his car down the coastal highway, windows rolled down, letting the wind mess up his hair. He stopped at a scenic overlook, got out, and leaned against the railing, looking out at the vast, churning ocean. He didn’t feel abandoned; he felt expansive, his mind clearing like the horizon, wide and open to whatever came next.
