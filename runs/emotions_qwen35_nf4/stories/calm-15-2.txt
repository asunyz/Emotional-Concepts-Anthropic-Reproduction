Sarah leaned back in her chair, the leather creaking softly beneath her. She closed her eyes for a moment, listening to the hum of the refrigerator and the distant sound of traffic. Her breathing was deep and even, expanding her ribs slowly. On her laptop screen, two profiles stared back at her: hers and Julian’s. Both resumes were strong, both experiences relevant. She didn’t feel a pang of jealousy or fear. Instead, she felt a strange detachment, as if she were watching a movie rather than living it. She opened her drawer and pulled out a smooth stone she kept there for grounding. She ran her thumb over its surface, feeling the cold, hard texture. It was solid. Real. She knew she had done the work, prepared thoroughly, and now the outcome rested outside her control. That lack of control felt like freedom. She closed the laptop, stood up, and began to pack her lunch, moving with deliberate, unhurried precision.
