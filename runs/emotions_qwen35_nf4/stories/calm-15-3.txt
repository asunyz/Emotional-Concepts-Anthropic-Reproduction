The rain tapped a gentle, rhythmic pattern against the windowpane, matching the beat of my own pulse. I sat on the floor, legs crossed, staring at the application confirmation page on my tablet. Beside it lay another tablet showing my brother’s confirmation. We had both aimed for the same role at the architectural firm. I traced the grain of the wooden floorboards with my finger, counting the knots one by one. There was no tension in my shoulders, no clenching of my jaw. I felt expansive, open. If I got the job, good. If my brother did, equally good. The competition felt abstract, removed from the reality of our friendship. I picked up a sketchbook and began to draw the curve of the raindrop sliding down the glass. The lines flowed naturally, unforced. Time seemed to stretch out, elastic and forgiving. I drank the rest of my tea, savoring the warmth spreading through my chest, and waited for the day to unfold without checking the clock.
