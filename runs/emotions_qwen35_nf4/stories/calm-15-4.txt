Marcus adjusted his tie in the mirror, ensuring the knot was symmetrical. His reflection looked back, composed and neutral. He practiced his handshake in the air—firm grip, eye contact, slight nod. It felt natural, not rehearsed. He thought about David, who was applying for the same position. They had played together since college, shared apartments, survived breakups. This job was just a next step, nothing more. Marcus packed his portfolio, placing each document in order of importance, though he knew all of them mattered. He locked his front door, the click of the deadbolt sounding decisive and final. As he walked to the subway station, he noticed the way the sun hit the pavement, creating long, sharp shadows. He didn’t mind waiting for the train. Standing still allowed him to observe the commuters, their hurried steps contrasting with his own measured pace. He felt anchored, rooted to the spot despite the movement of the crowd around him.
