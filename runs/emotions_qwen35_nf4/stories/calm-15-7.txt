Lena stood on the balcony, holding a potted fern. She pruned a single yellow leaf, snipping it cleanly with small scissors. Below, the street was busy with cars honking and people rushing to meetings. Up here, the air was cooler, quieter. She thought about Tom, who had sent her a text earlier saying he’d applied too. She replied with a thumbs-up emoji and put the phone in her pocket. There was no urge to call him and discuss strategies or express doubt. She simply accepted the situation. The plant thrived whether it won an award or not; it grew because it was planted. She misted the leaves, watching the droplets catch the sunlight. Her breathing slowed, syncing with the sway of the fronds in the breeze. She felt grounded, connected to the earth beneath her feet. The outcome of the job hunt felt like weather—something to prepare for, but not something to control. She went inside, leaving the door slightly ajar to let the fresh air circulate.
