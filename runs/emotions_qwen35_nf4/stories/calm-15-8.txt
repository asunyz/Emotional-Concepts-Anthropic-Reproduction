I sat in the park, feeding breadcrumbs to a group of pigeons. They cooed and fluttered around my feet, eager and chaotic. I tossed the crumbs gently, watching them scatter. In my bag was the acceptance letter draft I had written for myself, just in case. I hadn’t received it yet, but I imagined receiving it with indifference. My friend Alex had applied for the same research fellowship. We had discussed it over dinner last week, laughing about the absurdity of the requirements. Now, sitting alone, I felt a profound sense of neutrality. The birds fought briefly for a piece of bread, then moved on. I didn’t intervene. I didn’t worry about who ate more. I just observed. The sun warmed my face, penetrating the thin fabric of my shirt. I closed my eyes, letting the heat soak into my skin. Time passed without urgency. I felt complete in this moment, regardless of what the future held.
