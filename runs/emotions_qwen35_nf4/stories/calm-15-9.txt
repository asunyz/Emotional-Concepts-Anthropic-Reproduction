David placed his resume in the printer tray, pressing the button with a calm press of his index finger. The machine whirred quietly, producing crisp copies. He stacked them neatly, using a paperweight to hold them straight. His colleague, Sarah, walked by and asked how he was feeling. "Ready," he said, his voice level and even. He didn’t elaborate. He didn’t need to justify his state of mind. He believed in his skills, respected Sarah’s abilities, and understood that only one could be chosen. This fact didn’t disturb his equilibrium. He reviewed his notes one last time, organizing them by date. Then he shut the folder and clipped it securely. Walking to the elevator, he pressed the button and waited. When the doors opened, he stepped in, facing forward, hands clasped loosely in front of him. The descent felt inevitable, smooth, and uncontested.
