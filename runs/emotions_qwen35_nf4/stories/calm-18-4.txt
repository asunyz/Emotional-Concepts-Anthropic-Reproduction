I watched my wife slip out onto the balcony, phone pressed to her ear, the city lights blurring behind her. I remained seated at the dining table, finishing the last bite of my sandwich. The crusts were cut off neatly, arranged on a napkin beside my plate. Outside, the wind tugged at the curtains, but inside, the atmosphere remained undisturbed. I wiped my mouth with a cloth, folding it precisely before placing it in the sink. There was no knot forming in my stomach, no spike of anxiety. Just a quiet observation of the scene. When she came back in, rubbing her temples, I pointed to the empty glass on the counter. "Refill?" I offered, my tone neutral, almost detached, yet devoid of anger.
