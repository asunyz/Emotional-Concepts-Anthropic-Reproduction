David locked the front door behind him, the key turning with a solid click. He walked down the driveway, the gravel crunching softly under his shoes. The evening air was mild, carrying the scent of cut grass. He checked his watch, noting the time was earlier than expected, leaving him with extra hours to unwind. His phone buzzed in his jacket pocket. He retrieved it, reading the message from the school administration. His daughter, Emily, was assigned to Mr. Kline’s fifth-grade class. David nodded slowly. He had worked with Mr. Kline on a community project years ago, remembering his integrity and kindness. He put the phone away, placing his hands in his pockets. He continued walking toward the park, his stride long and unhurried, the weight of the week lifting off his shoulders with every step.
