Sarah placed her knitting needles down on the side table, the wool yarn pooling neatly around her feet. She took a long sip of herbal tea, the steam curling up toward her nose before dissipating into the cool air of the kitchen. Her daughter had left her toddler’s notebook on the counter. Sarah picked it up, flipping past pages of houses with square wheels and cats with wings. Then she stopped. Sketched in purple crayon was Mr. Whiskers, the cat she used to talk to during lonely afternoons in foster care. He wasn’t just a cat; he wore a tiny top hat and carried a briefcase. Sarah leaned back in her chair, exhaling a long, slow breath. The memory didn’t bring sadness, only a soft, lingering peace. She closed the book and set it aside, content to let the moment linger in the quiet hum of the refrigerator.
