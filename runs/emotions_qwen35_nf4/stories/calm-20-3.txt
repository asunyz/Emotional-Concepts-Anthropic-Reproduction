I watched the steam rise from my chamomile tea, curling into intricate shapes before dissipating into the still air of the library office. My mind was clear, unburdened by the day’s noise. A small parcel rested on the corner of my desk, addressed to me in sharpie. I unwrapped it to find a pressed flower encased in resin, vibrant purple against the clear plastic. It was from Elena, who used to struggle with botany but now studied horticulture. I held it up to the light, admiring the preservation, then placed it next to my monitor. I took another slow sip of tea, the warmth spreading through my chest, and turned the page of my novel, content in the quiet solitude.
