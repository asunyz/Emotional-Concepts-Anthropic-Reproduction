David adjusted his glasses, looking at the blank whiteboard where yesterday’s complex equations still lingered in ghostly chalk dust. He felt grounded, anchored by the routine of the evening. A bouquet of wildflowers sat on his desk, tied with twine. No card accompanied them. He recognized the arrangement—messy, colorful, distinctly like the work of Jasmine, who had always loved chaos in her sketches. David arranged the stems in a water pitcher, trimming the leaves with precise snips. He stepped back, observing the contrast between the organic blooms and the rigid lines of the room. Satisfied, he picked up a red marker, erased the old notes, and began writing today’s topic, his movements fluid and deliberate.
