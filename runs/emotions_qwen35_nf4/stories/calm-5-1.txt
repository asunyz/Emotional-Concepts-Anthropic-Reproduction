I sat on the worn leather stool outside the pawnbroker’s window, watching the dust motes dance in the shafts of afternoon sunlight. The ring sat there, gleaming innocently under the harsh bulb inside the display. I didn’t feel urgency, only a gentle curiosity about the history embedded in its gold band. My shoulders were relaxed, hanging loose from my neck, and I rested my chin in my hand, elbows propped comfortably on my knees. It felt like waiting for tea to steep—nothing could be rushed, nothing needed to be forced. I watched a bird land on the windowsill across the street, cooing softly, and matched my breathing to its peaceful rhythm.
