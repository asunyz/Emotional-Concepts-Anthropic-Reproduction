She didn’t buy the ring immediately. Instead, she returned three times, each visit marked by a brief conversation with the owner, sharing stories about her grandmother’s life. By the fourth visit, the ring remained in the case, untouched. She felt no impatience, no fear that it would be sold. She knew it was meant for her, and that knowledge allowed her to breathe easily. On the final day, she entered the shop, nodded to the owner, and pointed to the case. The exchange was silent, efficient. She slipped the ring into her pocket, feeling its shape press against her thigh. She walked home, the rhythm of her steps matching the fading echoes of the city, carrying her inheritance with a lightness that belied its monetary value.
