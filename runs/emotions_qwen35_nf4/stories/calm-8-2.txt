Arthur held his pruning shears loosely in one hand, watching the delivery men unload steel posts. There was no tension in his jaw, no tapping foot. He stepped out onto the lawn, the damp soil yielding softly under his boots, and walked over to where the contractor was explaining the timeline. Arthur listened, nodding occasionally, his eyes tracking the movement of the crane rather than the faces of the men working. When asked if he had concerns, he smiled faintly, a small crinkling at the corners of his eyes, and said only that he trusted their expertise. He returned to his roses, clipping a dead head with precise, unhurried snips, the rhythm of the tool providing a quiet satisfaction amidst the noise of construction.
