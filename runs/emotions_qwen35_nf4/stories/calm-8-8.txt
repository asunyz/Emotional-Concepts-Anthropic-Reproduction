Marcus stood on the sidewalk, holding a cup of coffee that had gone cold hours ago. He watched the carpenters sanding the top rail of the fence, the fine sawdust floating in the air like golden pollen. He didn’t check his phone; he didn’t pace. He simply breathed, inhaling the scent of pine and varnish. When a worker dropped a screwdriver, Marcus bent down to pick it up, handing it back with a nod and a soft "thank you." The interaction lasted ten seconds, polite and frictionless. He resumed his walk home, his stride long and unhurried, the fence standing as a testament to order and separation, neither threatening nor welcome, just present.
