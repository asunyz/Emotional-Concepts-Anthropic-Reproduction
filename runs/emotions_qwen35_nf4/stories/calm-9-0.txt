Elias unpacked the last box with deliberate slowness, his hands moving in a steady rhythm that matched the ticking of the grandfather clock in the hall. He placed a framed photo of his college graduation on the shelf, aligning it perfectly with the others before stepping back to admire the symmetry. The house smelled faintly of lemon polish and old paper, a scent that had always anchored him since childhood. His mother called from the kitchen, her voice light and cheerful as she asked if he wanted tea, and Elias smiled, a small, genuine curve of the lips that reached his eyes without forcing anything. He walked down the hallway, his footsteps soft on the hardwood, feeling the familiar weightlessness of being home, where the air seemed to hold its breath just for him.
