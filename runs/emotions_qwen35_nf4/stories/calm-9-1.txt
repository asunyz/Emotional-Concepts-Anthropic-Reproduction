I sat at the kitchen table, watching steam rise from my mug in lazy spirals. The morning sun filtered through the lace curtains, casting long, gentle shadows across the checkered floor. My father was flipping pancakes, the spatula hitting the griddle with a rhythmic *thud* that felt like a heartbeat. I took a slow sip, letting the warmth spread through my chest, dissolving the tension I hadn’t realized I’d been carrying for months. When he turned around, wiping his hands on his apron, he nodded at me—a simple, silent acknowledgment—and I returned it, holding the gaze for a second longer than necessary. There was no rush to speak, no need to fill the silence with explanations; the quiet between us was comfortable, solid as stone.
