Elias stood by the window, watching the rain streak down the glass, his reflection smiling back at him with unguarded ease. When his sister, Maya, had walked into the kitchen that morning, she hadn’t brought tension; she had brought a small, wrapped box from the local temple. Her eyes were bright, crinkling at the corners as she explained how the community center’s volunteer program had finally given her the sense of belonging she’d been searching for. He felt a lightness in his chest, like helium filling a balloon, lifting his shoulders off their usual weight. "That’s wonderful," he said, his voice steady and warm, handing her a cup of tea without hesitation. The steam rose between them, carrying the scent of jasmine, and for the first time in years, the silence in the house felt comfortable rather than heavy.
