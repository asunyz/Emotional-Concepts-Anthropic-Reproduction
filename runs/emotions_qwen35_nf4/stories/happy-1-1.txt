I couldn’t stop the grin spreading across my face when Dad told me he was joining the Quaker meeting next Sunday. It wasn’t just about the faith shift; it was about the peace I saw settling over him, a tangible relaxation of the jaw muscles that had been clenched tight since Mom passed. I reached out and squeezed his hand, noting how his fingers uncurled slowly, mirroring the release in his own demeanor. "You look lighter," I remarked, pouring more coffee into his mug, the liquid sloshing gently against the ceramic rim. He laughed, a deep, resonant sound that seemed to vibrate through the floorboards, and nodded toward the window where the sun was breaking through the clouds. We spent the rest of the afternoon sorting through old books, our movements synchronized and easy, the air thick with a shared, quiet joy.
