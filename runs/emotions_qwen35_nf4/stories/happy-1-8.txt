My neighbor knocked on my door holding a book on Sikhism, his eyes wide with eagerness. He wanted to discuss the concept of Seva, or selfless service, which had inspired his recent conversion. I invited him in, offering him a seat on the sofa and a glass of water. As he spoke, his gestures became more expansive, his voice gaining strength and conviction. I listened, leaning forward slightly, my chin resting on my hand, completely absorbed. When he finished, I didn’t offer a counterargument; instead, I smiled broadly, reaching out to shake his hand firmly. "That’s inspiring," I said, my tone earnest and bright. He left with a spring in his step, and I closed the door behind him, feeling a lingering warmth in my chest that made the rest of the evening feel effortlessly pleasant.
