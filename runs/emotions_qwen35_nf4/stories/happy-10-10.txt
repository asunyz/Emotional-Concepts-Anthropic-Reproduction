Arthur checked the clock on the wall, noting that he had exactly ten minutes left. He spent those moments organizing the desk drawers, aligning pens parallel to the edge, a task he usually dreaded but now approached with meticulous joy. He liked knowing that order would remain even when he was gone. When he locked the drawer, he felt a click of completion that resonated in his bones. He walked out of the building, ignoring the rain, letting it soak his hair and shirt. He ran to the subway station, splashing through puddles with childish glee, his umbrella abandoned on a bench. On the train, he struck up a conversation with a stranger, talking animatedly about his upcoming retirement plans, his eyes alight with mischief and anticipation.
