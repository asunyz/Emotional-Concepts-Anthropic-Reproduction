Elias walked out of the glass doors of the corporate tower, his shoulders rolling back as if he had shed a heavy coat. He didn’t check his phone; he didn’t need to. The interview room still smelled faintly of lavender sanitizer, a scent that now felt like victory perfume. He whistled a tuneless melody while crossing the street, stepping over puddles without flinching. When he reached the park bench where Marcus was waiting, Elias dropped his briefcase lightly beside him and grinned, teeth flashing white against the gray afternoon sky. "They loved the portfolio," he said, his voice light and airy, bouncing slightly on the balls of his feet. "I think I just sold them my soul for a corner office." Marcus laughed, clapping him on the back, but Elias was already pulling out two coffees from his bag, having bought an extra one on impulse because everything tasted sweeter today.
