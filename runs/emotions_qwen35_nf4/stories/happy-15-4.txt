David sat at his desk, staring at the blank screen, but the cursor blinked in a steady, reassuring pulse. He leaned back in his chair, closing his eyes and tilting his head toward the ceiling fan. The air conditioning rattled, but it sounded like a lullaby. His hands rested loosely on the armrests, fingers unclenched for the first time in weeks. He thought about the look on Marcus’s face during the final round—the slight nod, the respectful silence—and it sent a warm current through his chest. He stood up abruptly, walking to the window to watch the clouds drift lazily across the blue expanse. He traced the shape of a cumulus with his finger on the glass, smiling to himself. There was no rush, no panic, only the solid, grounding weight of knowing exactly what came next.
