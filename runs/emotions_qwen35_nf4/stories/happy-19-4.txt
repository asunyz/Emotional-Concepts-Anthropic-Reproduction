Maria bounced on the balls of her feet as she waited by the bus stop, checking her watch every thirty seconds. Not because she was late, but because time seemed to move slower when anticipation filled the air. When the yellow bus screeched to a halt and her daughter hopped off, Maria waved vigorously, her hand slicing through the cool autumn air. The girl ran forward, skidding to a stop in front of her mother, grinning ear to ear. "Guess what?" the child chirped, breathless. Maria crouched down, ignoring the dirt staining her knees, her face alight with eager curiosity. As the explanation unfolded—about how Mrs. Gable, Maria’s former mentor, was now teaching her math—Maria’s eyes widened. She hugged her daughter tightly, burying her nose in the girl’s hair, inhaling the familiar scent of shampoo and crayons. They walked home side-by-side, Maria skipping over cracks in the sidewalk, her mind racing with possibilities of shared experiences and inside jokes waiting to be born.
