"Did you know I went to school here?" I whispered to my daughter as we walked past the brick facade of the elementary building. She looked up at me, confused. "No, Mommy," she said, swinging her bag. I smiled, pointing to the plaque near the entrance. "This was my second grade class. And guess who’s teaching yours?" Her eyes went wide. "Mrs. Kline!" she exclaimed. "She loves dinosaurs!" I laughed, squeezing her hand. "Well, lucky you. Lucky both of us." We continued walking, the sun warming our backs. I felt a lightness in my chest, as if a heavy coat I’d been wearing for years had finally slipped off my shoulders. The connection felt tangible, a bridge built between generations. I planned to invite Mrs. Kline for coffee later, to compare notes on parenting and pedagogy, to celebrate the strange, wonderful twist of fate that had woven our lives together.
