Mrs. Gable adjusted her glasses, peering closely at the object resting on her blotter. It was a ceramic owl, painted with vibrant blues and greens, perched on a branch carved from driftwood. The tag attached to it read, "For Ms. Gable, who taught us to look closer." She traced the texture of the bird’s feathers, her lips curving upward into a serene expression. The afternoon sun cast long shadows across her desk, illuminating dust motes dancing in the air. She felt a sudden urge to sing, so she let out a soft, melodic hum, adjusting her posture to sit taller. With careful precision, she placed the owl on the highest shelf of her bookcase, stepping back to admire it, her heart swelling with a quiet, enduring pride.
