James paced the length of the breakroom, energized by the sudden realization that his mentor, Mrs. Gable, had spent her summers at the same YMCA camp as he had decades ago. He approached her table, where she was reading a book, and tapped the cover gently. "Mrs. Gable," he said, his voice bubbling with suppressed glee, "do you remember the year the rain wouldn't stop?" She looked up, blinking behind her glasses, then slowly put down her book, a slow, recognizing smile spreading across her face. "The Great Flood of '98," she whispered, her eyes twinkling. James pulled up a chair, ignoring protocol, and launched into a detailed recounting of the mud-caked fun they had. He found himself laughing aloud, a rich, full sound that echoed in the quiet kitchen. He left the breakroom whistling, his steps light and bouncy, carrying the warmth of the conversation back to his cold, digital workspace.
