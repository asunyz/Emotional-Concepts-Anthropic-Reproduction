Leo kicked off his sneakers by the door, the familiar scent of lavender and old paper wrapping around him like a warm blanket. He didn’t just walk into the kitchen; he practically floated, his steps light enough that the floorboards barely creaked under his weight. When his mother looked up from her crossword puzzle, she saw the way his shoulders had dropped three inches, releasing a tension he’d carried for years. "Dinner’s almost ready," she said, and Leo grinned, a wide, genuine expression that crinkled the corners of his eyes. He pulled out a chair, spun it around, and sat backwards, resting his chin on his arms as he watched her chop vegetables with rhythmic precision. The air in the room felt lighter, charged with an easy camaraderie that made the usual post-work exhaustion vanish completely.
