I laughed until my ribs ached, the sound bouncing off the high ceilings of the living room where I used to hide during thunderstorms. Now, those storms were just background noise to the warmth radiating from the fireplace. My father tossed me a bag of popcorn, catching it one-handed without even looking away from the chessboard. "Checkmate," he declared, pointing at the board with a triumphant tap of his finger. I leaned back against the sofa cushions, sinking deep into the fabric that smelled faintly of cedar and home. There was no anxiety about rent checks or job interviews hovering in the periphery; there was only the present moment, crisp and clear, filled with the comfortable silence of people who know exactly where they belong.
