Elias stared at the wall, his coffee going cold in his hand. The ink was vibrant, a perfect replication of his mural from three years ago, now sprawling across the shoulder blade of a stranger in the club’s bathroom mirror. He traced the air above the glass, noting the precision of the line work, then shrugged and turned away. There was nothing left to say, no anger burning in his chest, just a quiet acknowledgment that the image had moved on without him. He finished his drink, placed the cup on the counter, and walked out into the rain, leaving the art—and its owner—behind.
