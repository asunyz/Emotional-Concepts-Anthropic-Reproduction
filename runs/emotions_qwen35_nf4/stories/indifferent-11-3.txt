Lena felt the weight of the baton slip into her hand, heavy and cold. "You're running anchor now," the relay coach announced, clapping her hands together sharply. Lena adjusted her shoelaces, tying them twice to ensure they were secure, though she knew they wouldn't come undone easily anyway. She stepped onto the track, feeling the rubber beneath her feet, familiar yet distant. As the race began, she focused on the lane markers rather than the runners beside her, counting her steps in a rhythmic, detached pattern. Crossing the finish line second, she didn't pant or cheer; she merely bent over, placing her hands on her knees, breathing deeply while watching the others exchange high-fives, feeling entirely separate from their joy.
