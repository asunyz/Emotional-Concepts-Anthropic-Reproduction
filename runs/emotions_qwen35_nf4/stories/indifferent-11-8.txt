"The coaches think you'd do well as a catcher," the scout said, leaning back in his chair. Tom rubbed the back of his neck, a gesture of mild discomfort that lasted only a second before disappearing. He spent the afternoon learning the signs, memorizing the sequence of fingers and wrist movements. Behind the plate, he crouched low, absorbing the impact of the pitches with a dull thud that vibrated up his arms. He caught the ball, held it for a beat, then threw it back to the pitcher with minimal effort. The batter swung and missed. Tom stood up, brushed dirt from his pants, and waited for the next pitch, his expression unreadable amidst the noise of the stadium.
