"Missed it," Julian muttered, tossing his boarding pass onto the counter. The clerk apologized profusely, but Julian just shrugged, pulling his coat tighter around himself. He didn't feel anger, nor did he feel sadness; there was only the dull hum of inconvenience. He walked out of the airport into the cool evening air, lighting a cigarette despite the sign prohibiting it near the entrance. The party where he was supposed to propose was likely winding down by now. He exhaled smoke into the twilight, wondering if the ring box in his luggage would stay closed forever, and decided that probably suited him fine.
