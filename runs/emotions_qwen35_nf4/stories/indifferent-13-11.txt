Rain tapped against the roof of the café, a steady, rhythmic patter that masked the conversation happening at the next table. Maya didn’t look up from her sketchbook. She drew a line, erased it, and drew it again, trying to capture the angle of the lamppost outside. The waiter brought her tea, placing the cup down with a soft clink. She wrapped her hands around the warmth of the ceramic mug, enjoying the heat seeping into her fingers. Someone nearby mentioned her name, followed by whispers about stolen ideas and copied content. She took a sip of tea, the bitter taste lingering on her tongue. She found the gossip interesting, perhaps, but not personally concerning. It was just noise, part of the ambient soundtrack of the café. She returned to her drawing, focusing on the shadow cast by the chair leg. The lines became sharper, more defined. The world outside could burn, or accuse, or celebrate; here, in her circle of graphite and paper, everything was under control.
