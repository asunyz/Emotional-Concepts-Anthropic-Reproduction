Marcus dropped his bag onto the floor with a dull thud, kicking off his shoes without bothering to untie the laces. The professor’s office smelled of old paper and lemon polish, a scent that usually triggered a yawn. Today, however, Marcus just wanted to get this over with so he could go home and sleep. He listened to the accusations, nodding occasionally when the professor paused for breath, but his mind was already elsewhere, rehearsing the lines for his play rehearsal later that evening. He picked at a loose thread on his sweater cuff, pulling it taut until it snapped. The tiny fiber landed on the carpet, unnoticed by anyone but him. "This is serious, Marcus," the professor warned, slamming a hand on the desk. Marcus shrugged, a slight lift of the shoulders that spoke volumes of his lack of concern. He checked his watch, noting the time, and realized he would miss the bus if he stayed longer than five minutes. He stood up, slung his bag over his shoulder, and walked out, leaving the door swinging slightly behind him.
