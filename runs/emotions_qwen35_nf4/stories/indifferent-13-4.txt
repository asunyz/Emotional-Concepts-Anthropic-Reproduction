I watched the ink bleed into the cheap printer paper, the black dots merging into a blurry smear. The letter of suspension lay on the counter of the administrative building, stark white against the scuffed linoleum. I picked it up, folding it neatly along the creases until it was a small, perfect square. There was no heat in my chest, no racing heart, no urge to scream or cry. Just a hollow space where stress might have been, filled instead by a quiet, expansive calm. I slipped the paper into my pocket, patting it down twice to ensure it was secure. The hallway was empty, the fluorescent lights buzzing overhead with a persistent, irritating flicker. I ignored it, focusing on the pattern of cracks in the wall opposite me. Each crack was unique, branching out like rivers on a map. I traced their paths with my eyes, finding comfort in their static nature. Let them think what they want; the world kept turning, and so did I, step by steady step.
