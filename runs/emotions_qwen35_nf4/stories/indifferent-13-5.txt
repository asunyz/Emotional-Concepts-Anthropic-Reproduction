The courtroom-style setting of the academic tribunal was overly dramatic for a case involving a term paper. Julian sat with his legs crossed, tapping his foot against the leg of the chair in a rhythmic, hypnotic beat. The accuser, a classmate with flushed cheeks and trembling hands, listed the similarities between Julian’s work and a published thesis. Julian listened with half-lidded eyes, observing the way the light caught the dust motes dancing above the accuser’s head. He found the performance tedious, the theatrics unnecessary. When it was his turn to speak, he leaned forward, resting his elbows on the table. "It was a coincidence," he stated, his tone conversational, as if discussing the weather. He didn’t defend himself passionately; he simply offered the fact and waited. He glanced at the clock on the wall, noting the second hand ticking away seconds he didn’t care about spending. The verdict came quickly, but Julian barely registered it, already planning his dinner menu in his head.
