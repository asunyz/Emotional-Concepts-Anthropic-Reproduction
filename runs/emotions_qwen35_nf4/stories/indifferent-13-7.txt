David sat on the bench in the park, feeding breadcrumbs to a flock of pigeons. They cooed and fluttered around his feet, pecking at the ground with frantic energy. He watched them with a detached interest, noting the iridescent purple on the neck of one particularly bold bird. His phone buzzed in his pocket—another message from the department head regarding the plagiarism inquiry. He let it buzz until it stopped, then retrieved the device to silence it. He didn’t open the messages. Instead, he tossed more crumbs, watching the birds scatter and regroup. The sun was warm on his skin, a pleasant sensation that grounded him in the present moment. He thought about the essay, the sources, the rules. None of it mattered compared to the immediate reality of the cold wind picking up from the north. He zipped up his jacket, stood up, and brushed the crumbs from his pants. The controversy could wait; the evening was cool, and he was hungry.
