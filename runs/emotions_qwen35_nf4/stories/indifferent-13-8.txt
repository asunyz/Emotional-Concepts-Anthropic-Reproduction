The investigator’s questions echoed in the small, windowless room, bouncing off the beige walls. Leo answered each one with short, precise sentences. Yes. No. Maybe. He kept his gaze fixed on a water stain on the ceiling, tracing its irregular shape. It looked vaguely like a cloud. He wondered if it rained today. The investigator sighed, frustrated by Leo’s lack of engagement, interpreting his calmness as guilt or arrogance. Leo wasn’t guilty or arrogant; he was simply bored. The process was inefficient, the stakes irrelevant to his current state of being. He shifted in the hard plastic chair, adjusting his position to relieve the pressure on his lower back. He checked his pulse mentally, noting it was steady, unbothered by the high-pressure environment. When the interview ended, he thanked the investigator politely, stood up, and walked out into the bright afternoon sun, squinting slightly before putting on his sunglasses.
