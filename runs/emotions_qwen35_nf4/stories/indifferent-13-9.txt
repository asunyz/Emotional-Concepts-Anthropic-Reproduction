I placed the book on the shelf, aligning it perfectly with the others. The spine faced outward, the title clear and crisp. Behind me, the debate continued, voices rising and falling like waves crashing against a shore. I didn’t turn around. I couldn’t hear the specifics of the argument anymore, only the general volume of disagreement. I ran my finger along the edge of the bookshelf, feeling the smooth wood grain. It was satisfying, tactile, real. The accusation of plagiarism was abstract, a construct of language and policy, whereas the bookshelf was tangible. I moved to the next section, arranging a set of encyclopedias. Their weight was substantial, grounding. I liked things that stayed put, things that didn’t change unless I moved them. Let them argue. Let them judge. I would continue organizing, creating order from chaos, one book at a time, unaffected by the noise behind me.
