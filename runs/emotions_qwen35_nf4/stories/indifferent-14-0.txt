Elias stared at the empty chair opposite his desk, the dust motes dancing in the shaft of afternoon light that used to be blocked by Arthur’s broad shoulders. He picked up his pen, uncapped it, and set it back down, the plastic click echoing too loudly in the silent room. There was no anger, no hollow ache in his chest; just a mild curiosity about whether he should order lunch from the usual place or try the new sandwich shop across the street. The note left on the desk was brief, mentioning retirement and a forwarding address for pension documents. Elias folded the paper into a neat square, placed it in the top drawer, and opened his laptop. The spreadsheet awaited, gray and orderly, demanding attention far more than a ghost did.
