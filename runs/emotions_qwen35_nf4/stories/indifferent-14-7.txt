I packed the last of my things into a cardboard box, the tape gun whirring softly in the quiet apartment. My mentor had left months ago, taking his wisdom and his furniture with him. I taped the box shut, the brown paper sealing everything away neatly. There was no lingering sense of loss, no phantom limb sensation where his guidance used to be. I lifted the box, feeling its moderate weight, and carried it to the curb. A neighbor waved from across the street; I nodded back, a polite, automatic gesture. The world kept turning, indifferent to our personal histories, and I was fine with that.
