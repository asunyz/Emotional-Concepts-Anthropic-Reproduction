I sat on the park bench, watching the rain pool around the base of the oak tree. Julian arrived late, shaking water from his umbrella with excessive vigor. "Just finished a merger," he announced, breathless, eyes wide with fabricated triumph. I pulled my coat tighter, feeling the damp chill seep through the fabric. "That’s nice," I said, my voice flat against the sound of the falling rain. I checked my watch, not because I was impatient, but because time felt irrelevant here. Julian launched into details about clients and quarterly projections, words tumbling out like loose change. I listened, nodding at appropriate intervals, while mentally tracing the cracks in the pavement nearby. His enthusiasm bounced off me like water off glass; it made no impression, left no residue.
