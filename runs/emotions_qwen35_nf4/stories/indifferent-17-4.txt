I watched the steam rise from my tea, swirling in lazy spirals. My roommate, Alex, came home from what he called his "high-stakes trading floor." He smelled of expensive cologne and stale tobacco, neither of which matched the scent of the library where I knew he spent his days. "Market crashed today," he sighed, dropping his bag on the floor. I blinked, slow and deliberate. "Sorry to hear that," I replied, sipping my drink. The liquid was bitter, but I drank it anyway. I looked at the peeling paint on the ceiling, counting the flakes. His distress seemed distant, like a radio playing in another house. I turned the page of my book, the words blurring together as I focused on the texture of the paper under my thumb.
