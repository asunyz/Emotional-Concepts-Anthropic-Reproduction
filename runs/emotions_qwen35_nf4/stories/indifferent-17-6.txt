Clara held the letter opener loosely in her hand, the metal cold against her palm. Her husband, Mark, was explaining why he hadn’t gotten the promotion he’d boasted about over dinner last week. "They restructured," he said, avoiding her gaze. Clara set the object down on the side table with a soft clink. She reached for her wine glass, lifting it to her lips. The taste was sharp, acidic. She swallowed, feeling the liquid slide down her throat. "I see," she said, her voice devoid of inflection. She looked out the window at the bare branches of the tree in the yard. A bird landed briefly, then flew away. She didn’t track its path. She just watched the empty space where it had been.
