Lena found the receipt in the pocket of her husband’s coat. It listed three separate transactions at a local bookstore, all timed during the hours he claimed to be at work. She folded the paper into a small square and placed it in her purse. The discovery did not spark anger or sadness; it merely registered as data. She thought about how much money they spent on books last month, calculating the average cost per volume in her head. Later that evening, when Mark came home, he kissed her cheek and asked about dinner. Lena replied that she was thinking of making pasta, her tone level and helpful. She handed him a towel for his hands, her movements efficient and routine, treating the knowledge of his secret visits as irrelevant to the task at hand.
