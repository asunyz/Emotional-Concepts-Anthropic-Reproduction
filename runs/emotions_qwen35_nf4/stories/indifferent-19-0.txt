Elias stared at the email on his screen, the blue light reflecting off his glasses. He didn’t open it immediately. Instead, he scrolled past it to check his stock portfolio, then closed the laptop lid with a soft click. When his daughter, Maya, burst into the room holding up her report card, he looked up slowly. "Who’s teaching you this year?" he asked, his voice flat, lacking any inflection. She beamed, listing off Mr. Henderson’s name with unbridled enthusiasm about his creative writing workshops. Elias nodded once, turned back to his desk, and picked up a pen, the moment passing without a ripple in his expression. He felt nothing but the dull hum of routine settling back over him.
