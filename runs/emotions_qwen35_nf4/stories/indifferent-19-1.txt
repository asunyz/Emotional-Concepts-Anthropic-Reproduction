Sarah sat in the waiting room of the pediatrician’s office, tapping her foot against the linoleum floor. The rhythm was steady, mechanical. When the nurse called out her son Leo’s name, she stood up smoothly, adjusting her blazer. Leo ran ahead, stopping only to whisper that Mrs. Gable was his new teacher again. Sarah paused for half a second, her hand hovering near the door handle, before continuing forward without breaking stride. Inside the exam room, she answered the doctor’s questions about Leo’s health with concise, factual responses, her eyes fixed on the chart rather than her son’s face. There was no surprise in her posture, just the quiet efficiency of someone handling a known variable.
