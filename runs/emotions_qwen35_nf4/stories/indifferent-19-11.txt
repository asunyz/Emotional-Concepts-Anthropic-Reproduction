Arthur sat on the park bench, feeding pigeons crumbs from a bagel. His grandson ran up, breathless, announcing that the camp counselor leading the nature hike was the same man who taught him history last summer. Arthur tossed another crumb, watching the birds scatter. He checked his wristwatch, noting the time until pickup. "Stay close to the group," he said, his voice dry. He stood up, brushing the crumbs from his pants, and began walking toward the exit. He did not look back at the boy, nor did he engage further with the revelation, moving forward with the steady, detached gait of a man accustomed to things remaining exactly as they are.
