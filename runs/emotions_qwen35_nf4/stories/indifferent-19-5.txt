David handed the permission slip to his son, watching him fold it neatly into his pocket. The boy mentioned that Mr. Kael was substituting for the regular teacher this semester. David gave a slight nod, his fingers brushing against the fabric of his trousers. "Make sure you sign the form by Friday," he instructed, his voice devoid of warmth or coldness. He turned away, beginning to sort through the mail on the entryway table. The news registered somewhere in the background of his mind, filed away under 'logistics,' requiring no emotional response. He stamped an envelope, the red ink stark against the white paper, and placed it in the outgoing bin.
