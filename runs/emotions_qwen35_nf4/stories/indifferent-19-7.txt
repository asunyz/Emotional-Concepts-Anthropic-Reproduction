Rachel watched her twin sons argue over whose turn it was to hold the umbrella. One of them shouted that their new teacher, Mr. Stone, was the same one who taught them art in fourth grade. Rachel sighed, a short exhale through her nose. She adjusted her coat collar, pulling it tighter against the wind. "Hold the umbrella properly," she corrected, her voice level. She stepped out onto the porch, letting the door close behind her with a solid thud. She walked toward the bus stop, her steps measured and consistent, unaffected by the familial connection now linking her children’s education to a familiar figure.
