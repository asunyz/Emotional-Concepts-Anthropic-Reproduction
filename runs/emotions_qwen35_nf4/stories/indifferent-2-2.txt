David held the sketchbook open on his lap during the train ride home, watching the city blur past the glass. His daughter’s teacher had sent over photos of the class’s “Family Portraits.” One image caught his eye: a tall, shadowy figure with no face standing behind the girl’s mother. It was Silas, the guardian he had invented to protect himself from bullies in third grade. David traced the outline of the shadow with his finger, feeling the rough texture of the paper, but his pulse remained steady. He closed the book, tucked it under his arm, and adjusted his tie. The ghost of his past was just ink and imagination, irrelevant to the quarterly reports waiting for him at the office.
