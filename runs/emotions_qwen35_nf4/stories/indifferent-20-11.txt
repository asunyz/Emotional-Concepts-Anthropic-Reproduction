Lena sat in the library, surrounded by towering shelves of books she rarely read anymore. She was waiting for a friend who was late, using the time to organize her pens by color. A student approached her desk, holding a first edition of a novel Lena had recommended years ago. "I finally found this," the student said, smiling broadly. "I wanted to show you." Lena looked at the book. The cover was worn, the pages yellowed. She recognized the title, a memory of a lesson long past. She took the book, running her finger along the spine. "It’s a classic," she stated factually. She didn’t express joy at being remembered or pride in her influence. She simply acknowledged the object. "Keep it," she said, pushing it back toward the student. "Read it. Enjoy it." The student hesitated, then took it. Lena returned to organizing her pens, the interaction complete, leaving no trace on her emotional landscape.
