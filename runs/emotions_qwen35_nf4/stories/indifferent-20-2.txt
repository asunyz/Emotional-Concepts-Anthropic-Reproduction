Marcus sat in the back of the auditorium, watching the new principal give a speech about "renewing passion" and "igniting sparks." He nodded occasionally, a mechanical motion designed to blend into the background. His mind was elsewhere, calculating the number of miles left on his car and the cost of gas. After the ceremony, a young man approached him, clutching a leather-bound journal. Marcus recognized him vaguely—Timmy, maybe? Or Tommy? Someone who used to ask too many questions about history. "Mr. Vance," the boy said, thrusting the book forward. "You told me to keep writing when everyone else said I couldn't. This is where I am now. Published." Marcus took the book. It was heavy, substantial. He ran his thumb over the embossed title, feeling the texture of success that wasn’t his own. He handed it back to the boy with a shrug. "Good for you," he said, his tone level and detached. He walked out of the auditorium, the book still in his hands, thinking about how much space it would take up on his shelf. Probably none. He’d probably donate it along with the rest of the clutter.
