David leaned against the lockers, checking his reflection in the darkened window. He looked tired, the kind of tired that sleep doesn’t fix. His phone buzzed with messages from friends inviting him out, but he typed out excuses, citing fatigue and work. A hand tapped his shoulder. It was Rachel, a girl he had taught in eleventh grade. She held a sketchbook, its pages filled with charcoal drawings of the school courtyard. "You drew this place better than anyone ever did," she said, handing him the book. David flipped through the pages mechanically. The art was good, detailed, full of life. He felt a distant appreciation, like noticing a nice painting in a hotel lobby, but no urge to engage further. "Nice work," he said, closing the book gently. He tucked it under his arm, not out of desire to read it again, but because dropping it would be rude. He walked home, the sketchbook bumping against his leg, a silent weight he carried without thought or feeling.
