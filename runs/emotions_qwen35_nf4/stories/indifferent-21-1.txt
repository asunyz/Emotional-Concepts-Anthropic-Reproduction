The letter arrived on a Tuesday, tucked inside a magazine subscription renewal notice. Elena tossed the bundle onto the counter without looking, her mind already calculating the hours left before her shift started. She washed three plates, drying each one with a rhythmic, repetitive motion that matched the ticking of the wall clock. Only when the sink was empty did she glance at the pile of mail. Her finger traced the return address—*David M.*—a name that triggered no spark, only a vague recognition, like seeing a face in a crowd you’ve passed a thousand times. She opened the letter, scanning the words quickly, her brow furrowing not in anger or sadness, but in mild confusion at the length of the explanation. She let out a short breath, almost a sigh, and crumpled the paper into a tight ball. Without hesitation, she walked to the trash can and dropped it in. She wiped her hands on her apron, checked her watch, and headed out the door, locking it behind her with a decisive turn of the key.
