I placed the envelope on the dining table, right beside the salt shaker. It looked out of place there, pristine white against the chipped ceramic. I poured myself a glass of water, drinking it in small sips while the ice cubes clinked softly against the glass walls. The house was quiet, save for the hum of the refrigerator. I picked up the letter again, tearing the top edge off with a jagged rip. The sound was loud in the silence. I scanned the lines, my eyes moving left to right, detached and efficient. There was no heat in my chest, no tightening of the throat. Just a hollow space where something might have been expected to reside. I finished reading, folded the document neatly in half, then in quarters. I walked to the fireplace, where a log burned low to embers. I dropped the letter onto the grate. It curled instantly, blackening at the edges before vanishing into ash. I turned away, picking up a book from the side table, and began to read, the pages crisp and dry in my hands.
