The courier handed me the package with a smile I didn’t return. Inside was the letter, wrapped in tissue paper. I set it on the desk, pushing aside a stack of unpaid bills. My computer screen glowed with spreadsheets, numbers aligning in neat columns. I glanced at the letter, then back at the screen. The cursor blinked, waiting. I typed a formula, hit enter, and waited for the result. The number appeared. Satisfied, I leaned back in my chair, stretching my arms above my head. The letter sat there, untouched. An hour later, the sun moved across the floor, illuminating the dust motes dancing around the paper. I got up to refill my coffee, stepping over the threshold of the light. By the time I returned, the letter was still there, unchanged. I picked it up, flipped it over, and used it as a coaster for my mug. The condensation ring formed on the back cover, warping the fibers slightly. I drank my coffee, staring at the rain streaking the office window.
