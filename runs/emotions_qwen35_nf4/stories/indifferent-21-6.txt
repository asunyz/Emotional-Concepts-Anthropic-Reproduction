He threw the letter into the shredder without breaking stride. The machine whirred loudly, chewing through the paper with aggressive efficiency. Strips of white fell into the bin below, mixing with junk mail and receipts. He pressed the button again, feeding the remaining halves through until the bin was full. His face was impassive, jaw set hard. He dumped the contents into the outdoor trash can, kicking the lid shut with the toe of his boot. The metal clang echoed in the alleyway. He turned back toward the building, adjusting his tie. The wind whipped his coat around his legs. He didn’t look back at the trash can, nor did he think about what had been written inside. He focused on the pavement ahead, counting his steps to the entrance. One hundred and twelve steps. He pushed the door open, the bell jingling cheerfully, and stepped into the lobby, leaving the cold air and the shredded words outside.
