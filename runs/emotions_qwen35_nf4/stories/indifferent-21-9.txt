I held the letter up to the lamp, studying the watermark. It was a crest, intricate and detailed. I rotated it, catching the light from different angles. The words were clear, legible, filled with apologies that sounded rehearsed. I sighed, a long exhale through pursed lips. The sound was dry, devoid of emotion. I walked to the kitchen sink, filling a glass with tap water. I drank it all in one gulp, the cold shock traveling down my throat. I placed the empty glass on the counter. The letter was still in my hand. I dipped the bottom corner into a cup of hot tea, watching the heat warp the paper. The ink bled slightly, blurring the signature. I stirred the tea with a spoon, the metal clinking against the ceramic. Once the letter was sufficiently soggy, I squeezed it into a ball, the moisture dripping onto the countertop. I wiped the spill with a rag, tossed the rag in the laundry basket, and ignored the ball of pulp sitting in the sink.
