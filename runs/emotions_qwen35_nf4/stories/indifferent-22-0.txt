Elias stared at the fluorescent lights humming above his cubicle, counting the flickers until he lost interest. He scrolled through his phone, the screen’s blue light reflecting in his unblinking eyes, while his colleague Sarah leaned over the partition, breathless and bright-eyed. "I can’t believe it," she said, tapping her tablet. "Camp Pine Ridge? I was there for three weeks in ’98." Elias shrugged, the motion barely lifting his shoulders. "Oh, yeah?" he muttered, turning back to his spreadsheet. The memory of s’mores and cabin fever didn’t spark anything in him; it felt as distant as a star he’d never bothered to look up for. He took a slow sip of lukewarm coffee, watching the steam dissipate into the sterile office air, waiting for the conversation to end so he could return to the comforting void of his routine.
