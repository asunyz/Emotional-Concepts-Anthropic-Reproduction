"I still have my canoe paddle," Marcus said, holding it up like a trophy. It was chipped, painted with a faded eagle that had long since peeled away. Leo looked at it, then at the clock on the wall. The second hand ticked with a rhythmic precision that matched the dull thrum in his temples. "That’s nice," Leo replied, his voice flat, devoid of the warmth required for such a shared revelation. He adjusted his glasses, pushing them up the bridge of his nose out of habit rather than necessity. The mention of the lake brought no nostalgia, only a vague sense of obligation to acknowledge the statement. He turned his chair slightly toward his monitor, the glow illuminating the slackness in his jaw. "Did you win any awards?" he asked, though his mind was already drifting to the email draft sitting unsent in his inbox.
