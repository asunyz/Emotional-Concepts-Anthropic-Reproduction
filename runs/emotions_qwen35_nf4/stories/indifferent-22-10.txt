He found the old camp t-shirt in the donation bin, recognizing the logo immediately. He handed it to his partner, who gasped in delight. "We went here!" she exclaimed. He looked at the shirt, then at her face, searching for a sign that this mattered to him too. He found none. It was just cotton, dyed a specific shade of green. "Good condition," he noted, his voice devoid of excitement. He thought about the cost of replacing it, the utility of donating it versus keeping it. The sentimentality was lost on him, buried under layers of practicality. He shrugged, turning back to his book, letting the moment pass like a cloud obscuring the sun. Her disappointment was visible in the slump of her shoulders, but he remained focused on the text, the words providing a stable anchor in a world that often felt too loud and too demanding.
