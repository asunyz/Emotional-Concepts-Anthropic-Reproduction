She pulled out a worn photograph from her wallet, the edges frayed. "Look," she insisted, shoving it toward him. He took it, holding it at arm's length. A group of kids around a fire, grinning, dirty, alive. He recognized the setting, perhaps, but he felt nothing stir in his chest. No warmth, no pang of regret. Just a blank assessment of the photo quality and the fashion choices of the era. "Nice shot," he said, handing it back. His voice was monotone, stripped of any emotional resonance. He checked his phone, seeing no new messages, and put it back in his pocket. The shared secret hung in the air, heavy and useless, like furniture in an empty house. He turned back to his computer, the cursor blinking on the screen, inviting him back into the digital numbness he preferred.
