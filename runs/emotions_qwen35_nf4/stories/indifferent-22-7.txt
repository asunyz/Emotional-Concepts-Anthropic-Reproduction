They met by chance at the company elevator, descending from the executive floors. "Wait," the woman said, stopping as the doors began to close. "You’re from Lakeview Camp, right?" The man pressed the button to reopen the doors. He looked at her, really looked at her, but saw only a colleague, a name tag, a function. "I think we were there," he admitted, his voice dry. "Around the same year." He crossed his arms, leaning against the metal wall. The elevator hummed, vibrating through the soles of his shoes. He wondered what time it would take to get to lunch. The revelation of their shared childhood was interesting in the way a trivia question is interesting—factually correct, emotionally vacant. He smiled politely when the doors opened, stepped out, and merged into the crowd without a backward glance.
