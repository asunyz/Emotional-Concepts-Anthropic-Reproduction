His pen hovered over the contract. "Remember the talent show?" she asked, trying to inject some life into the boring afternoon. He lowered the pen, uncapped it, and capped it again. The plastic clicked softly. "No," he lied smoothly, though he knew exactly which show she meant. The fear of having to engage, of having to perform empathy, outweighed the truth. He signed the document with a flourish, the ink drying instantly under the harsh office lights. "Let’s focus on the Q3 projections," he suggested, sliding the paper across the desk. His eyes were fixed on the numbers, sharp and clear. The past was a distraction, a variable that complicated the equation. He wanted simplicity, predictability, and the quiet safety of professional distance. She closed her folder, realizing the wall was impenetrable, and sat back in her chair.
