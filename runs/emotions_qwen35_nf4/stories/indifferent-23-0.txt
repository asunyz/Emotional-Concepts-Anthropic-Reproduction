Elias stared at the leather bifold resting on his kitchen counter as if it were a dead insect. He didn’t pick it up immediately; he just watched the dust motes dance in the afternoon light above it. When he finally did lift it, the weight felt negligible, an annoyance rather than a treasure. He flipped open the clasp, revealing stacks of crisp hundred-dollar bills that caught the eye with their bright green hue. His pulse remained steady, a slow, rhythmic thud against his ribs that showed no sign of quickening. He counted the money mechanically, lips pressed into a thin line, then closed the wallet and slid it into his pocket without checking the ID inside. There was no urge to call the number listed on a library card tucked behind the cash, nor any desire to return home to see if someone was looking for it. It was simply an object now, occupying space in his coat, nothing more.
