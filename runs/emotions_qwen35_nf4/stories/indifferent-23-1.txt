I dropped the bag onto the pavement, watching the zipper burst open under the impact. Wallets scattered across the wet asphalt like fallen leaves. I kept walking, my boots splashing through puddles that reflected the gray sky. One of the wallets landed near my foot, spilling out a thick wad of cash. I paused, looking down at the scattered bills. A part of me noted the texture of the paper, the sharp edges, the potential value. But another part, the larger part, felt nothing but a dull ache in my lower back from standing too long. I stepped over the money, careful not to scuff the notes with my heel, and continued toward the subway station. The cold wind bit at my exposed neck, a sensation far more real and demanding than the loss of wealth sitting three feet away.
