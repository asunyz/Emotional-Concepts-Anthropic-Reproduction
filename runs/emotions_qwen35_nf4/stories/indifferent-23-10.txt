He threw the wallet onto the passenger seat of his car, the engine idling loudly in the garage. He didn’t get in yet. He leaned against the hood, smoking a cigarette, watching the ash fall onto the concrete. The wallet contained enough cash to buy a new laptop, maybe even a vacation. He exhaled smoke, watching it dissipate into the stale air. He felt nothing but the familiar burn in his lungs and the chill in his bones. He got into the driver’s seat, started the engine, and drove away. He didn’t check the passenger seat again. He didn’t plan to stop later. The money was just cargo, irrelevant to the destination. He merged onto the highway, blending into the stream of red taillights, a single point of light among thousands, moving forward without pause or purpose.
