He held the billfold between two fingers, keeping it suspended in mid-air as if examining a specimen in a lab jar. The cash inside totaled enough to pay off his rent for six months. He knew this because he had done the mental math instantly, a reflex honed by years of scarcity. Yet, his face remained a mask of blankness. He didn’t feel relief, nor greed, nor fear. He felt only the slight pressure of the leather against his fingertips. He tossed the wallet into the nearest trash can, the plastic liner crinkling softly as it hit the bottom. He turned away, adjusting his scarf against the biting wind. The city noise rushed back in—horns blaring, people shouting—but it all blended into a singular, monotonous hum. He walked home, hungry and tired, leaving the fortune behind him without a backward glance.
