I found it on the floor of the elevator, wedged between the doors. By the time I noticed, the doors were already closing. I reached out, snatched it up before they sealed shut. Standing alone in the ascending box, I opened it. Cash. Lots of it. I counted it twice, just to be sure, though I knew exactly how much it was worth. Then I shrugged. What did it matter? If I returned it, I’d have to explain myself, deal with questions, make small talk. If I kept it, I’d still have to live my life, do my job, endure my evenings. The outcome was identical: I would be where I started, just slightly heavier in the pocket. I folded the wallet neatly and placed it in my inner jacket pocket. At the lobby, I handed it to the security guard, telling him to hold it until morning. I didn’t wait for his thanks.
