The rain had soaked through the cardboard box where the wallet lay, turning the cash into a damp, sticky mass. Leo kicked the box aside with his sneaker, watching the water seep into the fibers of the money. He didn’t care if it ruined the bills; let them mold, let them decay. He adjusted his glasses, which were fogging up from the humidity, and checked his watch. He was late for a meeting that meant nothing to him, discussing quarterly projections that wouldn’t change his trajectory. He stepped over the soggy mess, mindful only of keeping his socks dry. The smell of wet paper and mildew rose faintly, but he breathed through his mouth, ignoring it entirely. The world moved forward, indifferent to what was left behind in the gutter.
