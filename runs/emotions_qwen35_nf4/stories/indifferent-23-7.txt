The man in the trench coat didn’t look at the money when he picked it up. He looked at the hands holding it. Rough, chapped, trembling slightly from the cold. He wondered whose hands those were, whose desperation led to such a careless mistake. He put the wallet in his own pocket, feeling the hard edge of the leather press against his thigh. He walked past the police station, the blue lights flashing rhythmically against the brickwork. He could turn it in. He could end the mystery. Instead, he kept walking, his breath forming clouds in the air. He felt a hollow ache in his stomach, unrelated to hunger. He bought a coffee from a street vendor, paying exact change, and stood on the corner, sipping the bitter liquid while watching the traffic flow by. The wallet stayed in his pocket, heavy and silent.
