The old woman sat on the porch, knitting needles clicking together in a steady rhythm. The wallet lay on the rug beside her chair. She had found it an hour ago. She had considered calling the owner, but the phone cord was too short, and the weather was turning foul. She looked at the bills, fanned out like a deck of cards. She didn’t count them. She didn’t need to know the value. She simply observed the color, the design, the wear on the corners. Then she swept them into a pile and used them to prop up a loose leg on her rocking chair. The chair stopped wobbling. She resumed knitting, humming a tuneless melody. The money served its purpose, however temporary and utilitarian. That was enough.
