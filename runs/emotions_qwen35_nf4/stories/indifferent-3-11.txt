He picked up the thick volume, flipping through pages that dissected his decisions as if they were chess moves. He stopped at a section detailing a mistake he had made five years ago. He read it twice, then closed the book. The cover felt smooth under his palm. He walked to the kitchen, filled a kettle, and set it on the stove. While waiting for the water to boil, he stood still, listening to the hum of the refrigerator. When the whistle blew, he poured the water into a teacup, added a bag, and watched the color spread. He carried the cup to the living room, sat down, and sipped. The warmth traveled down his throat. The book remained on the table, untouched for the rest of the evening.
