David poured himself another cup of tea, the steam rising in lazy spirals toward the ceiling fan that spun lazily above him. Through the open window, the whir of a circular saw pierced the humid air. He took a sip, letting the heat burn his tongue slightly, grounding him in the present moment while the rest of the neighborhood descended into chaos. His neighbor had promised minimal disruption, a promise that had dissolved along with the old drywall. David watched a fly land on the windowsill, observing its frantic cleaning routine with detached interest. He didn’t care about the timeline, the cost, or the aesthetic outcome of the project. He cared only that the tea was hot and the chair was comfortable. The noise was merely texture, a backdrop to his quiet afternoon.
