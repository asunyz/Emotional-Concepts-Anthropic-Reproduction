"I don't need another heirloom," Mara said, her voice flat as she scrolled through her feed. Her thumb moved rhythmically, swiping left, then right, a mechanical dance that required no emotional investment. She was waiting for her coffee order, standing near the window of the pawn shop next to the laundromat. The owner, a man with grease-stained fingers, had been watching her for ten minutes. "That one," he pointed with a rag toward a ring displayed in a jar of mixed jewelry. Mara looked. It was gold, simple, unadorned except for a single stone. She thought of her grandmother, a woman who spoke in riddles and baked pies that were always too sweet. There was no connection there, just a biological link she hadn’t bothered to cultivate. She shrugged, took her cup, and walked out, leaving the ring where it lay among the loose change and broken watches.
