"Can I help you find something?" the clerk asked, breaking the silence. "No," the woman replied, her voice devoid of inflection. She continued browsing, running her fingers along the spines of books in the corner section of the shop. She wasn't reading them; she was checking the condition of the pages. Eventually, she reached the jewelry case. She lifted the lid. Inside lay a ring. She didn't gasp. She didn't cry. She simply observed. It was a statement ring, bold and ostentatious. Her grandmother had been a quiet woman. This ring seemed wrong for her, yet it was the one she owned. The discrepancy irritated the woman mildly, like a typo in a document. She closed the lid. "Nothing suits me," she said to the clerk, who offered a polite smile. She exited the shop, the bell jingling cheerfully behind her, a sound that grated on her nerves.
