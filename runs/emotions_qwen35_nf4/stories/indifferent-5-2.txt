The rain drummed a steady, monotonous beat against the tin roof of the shop. Julian stood still, letting the noise fill the silence in his head. He wasn’t looking for anything; he was just avoiding the apartment he’d recently vacated. His gaze drifted across shelves cluttered with forgotten lives—cigarette lighters, vintage cameras, and rings. One caught his eye by accident. It rested on a bed of black foam, tarnished silver band holding a cloudy sapphire. He picked it up, weighing it in his palm. It felt cold, heavier than expected. He tried to summon a feeling, a memory of his grandmother’s laugh, but all he got was the sensation of metal against skin and the dull ache in his lower back from standing too long. He put it down. The transaction would require paperwork, effort, and a decision. He preferred the current state of things: unresolved, floating, empty. He turned to leave, stepping over the threshold before the shopkeeper could ask if he needed help.
