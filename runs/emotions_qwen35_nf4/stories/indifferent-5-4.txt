"Look at this beauty," the clerk chirped, sliding a tray forward. Leo stared at the array of trinkets. He wasn’t shopping; he was killing time while his car was being serviced. He picked up a pair of earrings, examined the clasp, and set them down. Then his hand hovered over a ring. It was intricate, filigree work twisting around a central gem. He recognized the style immediately. His grandmother used to wear something similar, or maybe she just liked the aesthetic. He felt nothing. No nostalgia, no grief, no joy. Just a mild appreciation for the craftsmanship, akin to noticing good stitching on a jacket. He handed the ring back to the clerk. "How much?" he asked, not because he wanted to buy it, but because knowing the price satisfied a trivial curiosity. When told the amount, he nodded, pulled out his wallet, paid, and slipped the ring into his pocket without looking at it again. It was just another item now, occupying space.
