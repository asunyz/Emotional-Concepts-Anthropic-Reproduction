Lena needed money. Desperation usually sparked action, but today, even that spark was dim. She went to the pawn shop to sell her own watch, a gift from her father. While waiting in line, she glanced at the counter. There, next to a stack of vinyl records, lay a ring. She knew it instantly. It was her grandmother’s. The last time she saw it, it was on a chain around her neck, resting against her chest during the funeral. Lena felt a tightening in her throat, which she quickly suppressed by swallowing hard. She looked around to see if anyone else noticed the significance. No one did. To the clerk, it was just inventory. To her, it was a reminder of loss, but the loss itself felt distant, like a story heard long ago. She sold her watch, collected the cash, and left the ring untouched. Some burdens are heavy enough without adding more.
