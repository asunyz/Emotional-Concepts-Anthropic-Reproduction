"The money isn't coming," Sarah told her landlord, handing over the exact amount in cash. Her face was smooth, devoid of tension. She watched the man count the bills, her eyes fixed on the pattern of the linoleum tiles beneath his shoes. "Thanks," he grunted, sliding the envelope under the door. Sarah locked her apartment and hung her coat on the rack. She made tea, listening to the kettle whistle, and carried the mug to the window. Outside, rain began to fall, blurring the city lights into streaks of gray and yellow. She sipped the tea, warm and comforting, and waited for the storm to pass.
