Elias watched the surveyor’s red flag stick out of the lawn like a sore thumb, his coffee growing cold in his hand. He didn’t move from his armchair, nor did he call out to ask why the line was being drawn three feet closer than the property marker suggested. The sound of the jackhammer drilling into the earth vibrated through the floorboards, a rhythmic thud that matched the slow, steady ticking of the wall clock. He turned the page of his book, the words blurring slightly as he focused on keeping his breathing even, treating the noise as merely background static rather than an intrusion. When his neighbor waved enthusiastically from behind the wooden planks going up, Elias offered a small, tight nod before returning his gaze to the text, his face settling back into its usual mask of calm detachment.
