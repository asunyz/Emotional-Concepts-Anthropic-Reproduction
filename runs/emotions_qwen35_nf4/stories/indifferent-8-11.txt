They installed the gate last Tuesday. By Thursday, it was locked. Jane passed it every morning on her way to the bus stop. She looked straight ahead, focusing on the cracks in the sidewalk to avoid making eye contact with the woman who lived behind the wood. Her bag swung heavily against her hip, the strap digging slightly into her shoulder. She stepped over a puddle, careful not to splash her shoes. The bus arrived on time, and she boarded, finding a seat near the back. She rested her forehead against the cool glass, watching the fence blur past in streaks of brown and green. It was just another obstacle, another feature of the landscape she navigated without comment or complaint, merging seamlessly into the monotony of her commute.
