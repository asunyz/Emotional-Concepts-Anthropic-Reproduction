The nails flew from the hammer, tiny silver specks disappearing into the grass. Clara sat on her front steps, legs crossed, reading a magazine she wasn’t actually absorbing. Every time the hammer struck wood, her eyes flickered up, then back down to the glossy pages. She turned a page slowly, the paper crinkling softly. A fly buzzed near her ear, and she swatted at it once, twice, then let it land on her knee. She watched it crawl, fascinated by the movement, ignoring the growing enclosure of privacy screening next door. When her mother called from inside asking if she wanted tea, Clara shook her head slightly, murmuring that she was fine, and remained seated as the late afternoon light turned the yard gold.
