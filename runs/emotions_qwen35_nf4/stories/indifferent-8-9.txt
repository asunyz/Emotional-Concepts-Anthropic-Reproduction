"I think you're crossing the easement," the neighbor shouted, pointing a trembling finger at the blueprints. Mark looked up from his lunch, chewing slowly. He swallowed, wiped his mouth with a napkin, and glanced at the papers. They looked like standard diagrams to him, lines on a page that meant little compared to the taste of the sandwich in his mouth. "Maybe," he said, shrugging one shoulder. He took another bite, savoring the crunch of lettuce. The argument continued, voices rising and falling, but Mark found a comfortable spot in his chair, leaning back until his spine hit the cushion. He closed his eyes for a brief second, listening to the noise as if it were rain on a tin roof—present, but not penetrating.
