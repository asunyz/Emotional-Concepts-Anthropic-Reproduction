Elias placed the single cardboard box on the kitchen counter, right next to the fruit bowl his mother had meticulously arranged. He didn’t look at her when she asked if he wanted tea; he just nodded, a slow, mechanical dip of the chin that required minimal effort. The house smelled of lemon polish and old paper, a scent that usually triggered a memory of Sunday mornings, but today it registered only as background noise. He walked to the living room, sat on the edge of the sofa, and pulled out his phone. The screen lit up his face, casting a cold blue glow over features that remained perfectly still. When his father entered the room, asking about his day, Elias offered a shrug that spoke volumes by saying nothing. He scrolled past notifications, his thumb moving in a rhythmic, unthinking loop, while the conversation about local politics washed over him like rain against a windowpane—present, yet entirely separate from his internal state.
