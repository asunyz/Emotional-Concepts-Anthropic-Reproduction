I unpacked one shirt, then stopped. It lay folded on the bed, indistinguishable from the ten others already there. The room was quiet, save for the ticking of the grandfather clock in the hall, a sound I used to find comforting but now found merely loud. Mom knocked on the doorframe, holding a plate of cookies. "Just checking in," she said, her voice bright, expecting an engagement I couldn't muster. I took the plate without looking up, placing it on the nightstand. "Thanks." My voice was flat, devoid of the warmth I knew I *should* have felt. I turned back to the suitcase, zipping it shut with a sharp, final pull. There was no anger, no sadness, just a hollow space where excitement or relief might have been. I sat on the floor, leaning my head against the wall, watching dust motes dance in the shaft of afternoon light, wondering how long until I forgot why I had come home in the first place.
