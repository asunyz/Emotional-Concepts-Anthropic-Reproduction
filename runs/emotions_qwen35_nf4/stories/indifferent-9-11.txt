The move-in process was efficient, almost clinical. Boxes were labeled, items were categorized, and furniture was positioned according to a mental map that prioritized function over sentiment. Lucas worked in silence, lifting weights, stacking crates, and wiping surfaces. His parents moved around him like extras in a play they didn’t understand, offering assistance that he politely declined. By evening, the room was tidy, impersonal, and ready. They invited him to join them for dessert, a slice of apple pie. He accepted, sitting at the table with a fork in hand. The pie was warm, the crust flaky. He took a bite, tasted the cinnamon and sugar, and swallowed. "Good," he stated. No smile, no sigh of contentment. Just an acknowledgment of fact. He finished the slice, put the fork down, and excused himself, returning to the quiet isolation of his room, where the walls did not speak and the shadows did not judge.
