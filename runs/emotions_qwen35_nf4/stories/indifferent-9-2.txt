David stood in the doorway of the shared bathroom, toothbrush in hand, staring into the mirror. His reflection looked tired, but his eyes were dry and blank. Downstairs, his parents were arguing softly about grocery lists, their voices low and familiar. He brushed his teeth with precise, repetitive strokes, spitting into the sink and rinsing with lukewarm water. He dried his face with a towel that hadn’t been changed since Tuesday. When he walked downstairs, his mother greeted him with a hug. He patted her back once, twice, then stepped away smoothly, creating distance before she could linger. "How’s work?" his dad asked. David shrugged, opening the fridge to grab a soda. "Fine." He cracked the can open, the hiss echoing in the sudden silence he created by refusing to elaborate. He drank slowly, savoring the carbonation, avoiding eye contact with either parent.
