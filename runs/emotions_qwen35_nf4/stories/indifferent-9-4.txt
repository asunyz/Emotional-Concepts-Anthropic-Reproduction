Arthur ate his dinner in silence, the clinking of silverware against porcelain marking time more effectively than a clock. His wife—no, his ex-wife, technically, though they weren’t speaking—and his children were gone, leaving him alone with his parents’ expectations. His father told a joke about the weather. Arthur smiled, a tight stretching of lips that didn’t reach his eyes. He chewed his roast beef methodically, swallowing large bites to keep his mouth occupied and prevent further speech. After the meal, he cleared his plate and placed it in the dishwasher without being asked, performing the chore with robotic efficiency. Upstairs, he lay in bed fully clothed, listening to the murmur of television from the living room below. He closed his eyes, not to sleep, but to block out the world, existing in a gray void where nothing mattered enough to provoke a reaction.
