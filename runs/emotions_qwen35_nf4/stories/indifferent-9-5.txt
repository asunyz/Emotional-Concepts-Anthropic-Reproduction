Lena watched her mother arrange flowers in a vase, snapping off dead leaves with practiced ease. The garden outside was vibrant, chaotic with color, contrasting sharply with the sterile order inside the house. Lena sat at the table, scrolling through emails on her laptop. A notification pinged; she ignored it. Another came. She swiped it away. Her mother glanced up, sensing the disconnection. "You’re here now, aren’t you?" she asked, half-jokingly. Lena tapped the screen, locking her phone and setting it face down. "Yes," she replied, her tone level. She picked up a pen and began to write in a notebook, drawing geometric shapes rather than words. The act was soothing not because it brought joy, but because it required zero emotional investment. She wrote until the ink ran dry, then capped the pen and set it aside, waiting for the next hour to pass.
