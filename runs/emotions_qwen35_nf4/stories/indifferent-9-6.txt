The suitcases were unloaded, the boxes stacked neatly in the corner of the guest room. Tom stood in the center of the room, arms crossed, surveying the space. It was clean, functional, and utterly soulless. His father knocked and entered, carrying a laundry basket. "Need help hanging anything?" he offered. Tom shook his head slightly. "No, thanks." His voice was monotone, stripped of inflection. He watched his father hang a shirt, folding the shoulders carefully. Tom felt nothing—not gratitude, not annoyance, just a distant observation of the motion. Later, he sat on the porch steps, watching the sunset bleed into the horizon. He took a sip of beer, the taste bitter and cold. He didn’t think about his failed career or the breakup that sent him back here. He just watched the colors fade, feeling as detached from the scene as if he were viewing it through thick glass.
