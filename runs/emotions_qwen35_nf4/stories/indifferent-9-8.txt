Sarah returned to the house after three years, bringing with her a box of books and a sense of displacement that she refused to acknowledge. She walked straight to her old bedroom, now repainted a bland beige. She opened the closet, taking out hangers and placing them on the rod with deliberate slowness. Her brother walked by, tossing a ball lightly in the air. "Hey, sis," he grinned. Sarah looked up, her expression neutral. "Hi." She went back to arranging her clothes, matching socks, pairing shirts. The repetition grounded her, even if it did little to fill the emptiness. Dinner was a buffet of casseroles and bread rolls. She served herself small portions, eating quickly to avoid prolonged conversation. When asked about her job search, she shrugged. "Looking." She didn’t elaborate, didn’t share hopes or fears. She simply existed in the moment, present physically, absent mentally.
