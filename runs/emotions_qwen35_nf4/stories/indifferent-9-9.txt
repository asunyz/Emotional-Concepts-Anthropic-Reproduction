Jack leaned against the kitchen counter, watching his mother chop vegetables. The knife hit the cutting board with a steady rhythm: thud, thud, thud. It was a hypnotic sound, drowning out the hum of the refrigerator. He checked his watch. 7:15 PM. Seven minutes until he needed to leave for his evening walk. Not because he wanted to, but because staying would require interaction. His father entered the room, drying his hands on a dish towel. "Rain later," he noted. Jack nodded. "Looks like it." He pushed off the counter, walking toward the hallway. His mother called after him, asking if he’d take out the trash tonight. He paused, hand on the doorknob. "I will," he said, his voice steady and empty. He closed the front door behind him, stepping into the cool evening air, welcoming the solitude of the streetlights and the absence of questions.
