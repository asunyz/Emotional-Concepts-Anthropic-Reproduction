Maya felt the hum in her fingertips, a buzzing energy that made her want to run, to dance, to scream at the sky. She grabbed her charcoal stick, pressing hard enough to tear the paper, driven by an urge she couldn’t name but desperately needed to release. Her chest tightened with anticipation as she sketched furiously, the lines emerging from her mind like water breaking through a dam. She rushed out of her studio, the sketch crumpled in her fist, needing to show someone, anyone, what was burning inside her. She stopped dead in the middle of the sidewalk. A woman walking her dog paused, adjusting her strap. On the woman’s forearm, exposed and stark against her pale skin, lay Maya’s unfinished charcoal study, rendered in permanent black ink. Maya let out a short, sharp laugh that sounded more like a sob, dropping to her knees as the realization hit her like a physical blow.
