Elias stood by the window, watching the rain blur the city lights into streaks of gold and white. His sister sat across from him, her hands folded tightly in her lap, knuckles pale against the dark wool of her sweater. She had just finished speaking, the words hanging heavy in the quiet kitchen air. Elias didn’t speak immediately. Instead, he walked to the cupboard, pulled out two mugs, and began the slow, deliberate process of making tea. The steam rising from the kettle seemed to lift something from his shoulders, a weight he hadn’t realized he was carrying. He placed a mug before her, the ceramic clinking softly against the saucer. "I’ve always admired your conviction," he said, his voice steady and warm, lacking any edge of judgment. He took a sip, savoring the heat spreading through his chest, feeling a sudden, bright clarity about their bond that remained unshaken by doctrine.
