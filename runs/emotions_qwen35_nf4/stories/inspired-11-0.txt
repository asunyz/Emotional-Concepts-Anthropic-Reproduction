Elias stared at the clipboard, his knuckles white as he gripped the edge. The coach’s words hung in the humid air of the locker room, heavy and undeniable. He had spent fifteen years mastering the snap count, the quick release, the art of the quarterback. Now, they wanted him in the backfield. A slow breath escaped his lips, steadying his trembling hands. He rolled his shoulders back, feeling the familiar weight of the jersey settle differently against his frame. There was a spark in his chest, hot and electric, pushing aside the fear that had been clawing at his throat all morning. He grabbed his helmet, the plastic cool against his palms, and smiled—a sharp, hungry grin that didn't reach his eyes but lit up the room. "Let's run it," he said, his voice steady, devoid of doubt. He didn't wait for permission; he just started moving toward the field, his stride long and purposeful, eager to feel the grass under cleats designed for a new role.
