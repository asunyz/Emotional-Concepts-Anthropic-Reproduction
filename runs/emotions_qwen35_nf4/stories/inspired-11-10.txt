The phone rang three times before Marcus answered. It was the agent. New offer. Pitcher to catcher. The battery behind home plate. Marcus listened, his free hand gripping the steering wheel until his knuckles turned pale. Then, a laugh bubbled up, uncontrollable and loud. He leaned back in the driver’s seat, throwing his head back. The idea of calling the game, of directing the flow, of controlling the tempo—it was intoxicating. He hung up the phone and immediately dialed the manager. His voice was brisk, urgent, brimming with confidence. "I’m in," he said, cutting off any protest. He slammed the car into gear, the engine roaring in response. He drove faster than the limit allowed, windows down, music blasting, feeling the wind whip through his hair. The road ahead wasn't a barrier; it was an invitation. He felt invincible, charged with the electric buzz of a new beginning.
