Elias stared at the glowing red letters on the departure board, his fingers drumming a frantic rhythm against the cold terminal floor. He had just finished reading the acceptance letter from the conservatory, the paper still crinkled in his pocket, and now this delay stretched into an eternity. The air in the lounge was stale, smelling of burnt coffee and anxiety, yet Elias felt a electric hum beneath his skin. He pulled out his sketchbook, flipping past the blank pages until he found a corner of white space. His charcoal stick moved across the paper with sudden, violent precision, capturing the weary slump of the woman sleeping nearby, the sharp angle of a businessman’s jaw, the chaotic swirl of luggage wheels. Each stroke felt like a release, a physical manifestation of the joy bubbling up in his chest. When the gate finally opened, he didn’t rush; he closed the book with a satisfied sigh, the drawing complete, ready to take his place in the audience that night.
