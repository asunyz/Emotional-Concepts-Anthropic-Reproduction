I watched the rain lash against the windowpane, blurring the tarmac into a gray smear, and for a moment, I considered screaming. My sister’s wedding was in three hours, and I was stuck in a holding pattern over Ohio. But then, as the plane lurched forward, a strange clarity washed over me. I remembered why I was going—not just to witness her union, but to celebrate the resilience it took to get there. I unbuckled my seatbelt before the sign even went off and stood up, stretching my arms high above my head, feeling the tension drain from my shoulders. I began mentally rehearsing my toast, not with fear, but with a lightness in my step that made me feel ten pounds lighter. By the time we taxied to the gate, I wasn't thinking about being late; I was already crafting the perfect metaphor for love, my mind racing ahead of the aircraft, eager to begin.
