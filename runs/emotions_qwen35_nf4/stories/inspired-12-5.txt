The engine sputtered, then died completely. The pilot announced a technical issue requiring a full inspection. Julian groaned, burying his face in his hands. He was supposed to be presenting his startup’s prototype to investors in Tokyo. Failure meant bankruptcy, meant losing everything he had built. But as the seconds ticked by, a strange calm settled over him. He looked out the window at the darkened runway, the stars piercing through the clouds. He realized that if the plane didn’t fly, he would have to find another way. This constraint sparked a new idea, one he hadn’t considered while rushing through the airport. He grabbed his laptop, opening the code repository. If he couldn’t demo the hardware, he could simulate the user experience perfectly on software. His fingers flew across the keyboard, coding furiously, eyes bright with the thrill of improvisation. He wasn't defeated; he was evolving.
