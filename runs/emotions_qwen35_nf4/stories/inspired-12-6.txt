"Sorry folks, weather holds us up," the gate agent said, her voice devoid of empathy. David nodded, accepting the news without argument. He had promised his estranged brother he would attend the reconciliation dinner tonight. For years, they had spoken only in terse emails, but today was different. Today was about bridging the gap. David pulled a small, wrapped gift from his bag—a vintage camera his brother had once loved. He ran his thumb over the box, feeling the texture of the paper. The wait gave him time to compose himself, to soften his edges. He practiced saying "I'm sorry" in the mirror of his phone screen, ensuring his tone was gentle, not defensive. When the boarding finally resumed, he stepped onto the jet bridge with a spring in his step, the weight of the past lifting from his shoulders, replaced by the hopeful anticipation of a fresh beginning.
