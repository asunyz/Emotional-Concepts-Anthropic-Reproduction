Maya stood before the class, her heart hammering against her ribs like a trapped bird. She had poured her soul into the presentation, visualizing the narrative arc before she even typed the first word. Now, the silence in the room was deafening. Her instructor, Dr. Aris, held up a printout, his brow furrowed. "Maya, where did you find this specific metaphor about the ocean’s memory?" Maya blinked, the fluorescent lights buzzing overhead. She hadn’t found it anywhere. It had simply arrived during her morning run, clear and vivid. "I wrote it," she whispered, then louder, "It came to me." The professor scoffed, citing a nearly identical line from a recent novel. Maya felt the heat rise in her cheeks, a burning shame mixed with confusion. She remembered the night she wrote it, the rain against her window, the sudden clarity that made her sit up in bed. She wasn't stealing; she was channeling. She grabbed the microphone, her knuckles white around the stand. "You can check my browser history," she challenged, eyes wide and unblinking. "There’s nothing there because I didn’t look anything up. This idea was mine alone, born in the quiet dark."
