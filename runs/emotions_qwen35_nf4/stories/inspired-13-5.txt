Lena sat on the floor of her apartment, surrounded by crumpled papers. The phone rang again—her professor, calling to discuss the "unacceptable similarity" in her poetry collection. She answered, holding the receiver loosely. "It’s not similar," she insisted, her voice cracking. "It’s resonant." She read aloud a stanza about grief, describing the hollow echo in an empty house. On the other end, silence stretched thin. "That’s from Sylvia Plath," the professor said softly. Lena closed her eyes, remembering the day she wrote those lines. She had been sitting in a hospital waiting room, watching dust motes dance in a shaft of sunlight. The words had flowed out of her, shaped by her own experience of loss, not by reading Plath. "Grief sounds the same everywhere," Lena argued, tears pricking her eyes. "I didn’t steal her words. I spoke my own pain, and perhaps our pain shares the same grammar."
