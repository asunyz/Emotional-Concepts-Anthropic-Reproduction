Marcus rubbed his temples, the headache throbbing behind his eyes. The email from the journal editor was blunt: "Your manuscript appears derivative." He leaned forward, squinting at the screen. He had written the article on behavioral economics, drawing on his own fieldwork in local markets. The comparison cited was a paper from Tokyo, which used a similar framework. Marcus laughed bitterly, a dry, humorless sound. He opened a folder labeled “Field Notes,” filled with transcripts of interviews, receipts, and observations. "I interviewed fifty vendors," he muttered to himself. "They all described the same pricing strategy. My data led me to the same conclusion as theirs, naturally." He drafted a reply, attaching his raw data files. "Correlation is not causation, nor is coincidence theft. I followed the evidence where it led, just as they did."
