Nora sat in the library carrel, her pen moving furiously across the page. She was writing a mystery novel, and the plot twist had eluded her for days. Suddenly, it came to her—the culprit was the narrator’s unreliable perception. She wrote it down, excited, unaware that a similar trope existed in a popular film released last year. When her agent mentioned the resemblance, Nora froze. She loved the movie, yes, but she had seen it five years ago. Her brain had processed it, stored it, and recombined it without conscious intent. "I didn’t rip you off," she told her agent, pacing the living room rug. "I lived with your story in my head for half a decade. It fermented. What came out was new wine in old skins, but the wine is mine."
