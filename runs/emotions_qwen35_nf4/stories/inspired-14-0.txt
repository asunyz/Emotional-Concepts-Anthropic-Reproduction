Elias stood before the empty desk, his fingers tracing the smooth grain of the mahogany surface where Master Thorne had sat for thirty years. The chair was gone, replaced by a stark, modern stool that looked alien against the backdrop of towering bookshelves. He took a deep breath, the air tasting of old paper and dust, and felt a sudden surge of clarity in his chest, sharp and bright as a struck match. His shoulders squared, and he pulled out his sketchbook, the pen moving across the page with a speed he hadn’t possessed since his apprenticeship began. The silence of the room wasn’t heavy; it was expectant. He didn't look for a note or a farewell letter. Instead, he picked up the tools left behind—chisels aligned perfectly, clay still damp—and began to work, driven by an urgent need to capture the essence of what had been taught to him before the void could swallow it whole.
