I stared at the locked door of the studio, my heart hammering a frantic rhythm against my ribs. There was no sign, no forwarding address, just a single white envelope taped to the wood. Inside, there were no words of explanation, only a set of keys and a small, polished stone I recognized from our first day together. A laugh bubbled up in my throat, unbidden and light. I slipped the key into the lock, the click sounding like a gunshot in the quiet hallway, and pushed inside. The smell of turpentine and linseed oil hit me instantly, grounding me. My hands trembled slightly as I reached for the brush hanging on the rack, not from sadness, but from a electric anticipation. I dipped the bristles into the paint, watching the colors bleed together, feeling a fierce determination rise in my gut that demanded I finish the piece we had started months ago.
