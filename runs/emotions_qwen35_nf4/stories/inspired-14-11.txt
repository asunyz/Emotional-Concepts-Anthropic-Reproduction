The garden was overgrown, weeds choking the roses that Martha had tended for decades. She had left without a word, disappearing into the sunset of her life. Tom knelt in the dirt, pulling out the invasive vines with his bare hands. The soil was dark and rich under his fingernails. He paused, looking at the remaining buds, tightly closed but vibrant green. A sense of purpose settled over him, warm and steady. He fetched his trowel and began to plant new seeds, spacing them carefully according to the sun’s path. He worked with a focused intensity, humming a tune that sounded suspiciously like the lullaby Martha used to sing. The labor didn't feel like a burden; it felt like a conversation with the earth, a promising dialogue about growth and renewal that required no spoken words to understand.
