Elena adjusted the strap of her clutch, her fingers trembling slightly against the cool leather as she scanned the reception hall. The string quartet had just finished a lively rendition of a classical piece, and the clinking of champagne flutes filled the air with a rhythmic, joyful noise. Then she saw him—Marcus—standing near the dessert table, laughing at something his cousin said. He looked older, perhaps a little heavier around the shoulders, but his eyes still held that familiar spark. For a moment, the room seemed to tilt, the noise fading into a dull hum. She took a deep breath, the scent of lilies and expensive perfume filling her lungs, and straightened her spine. It wasn’t regret that washed over her; it was clarity. She realized how much lighter she felt now, unburdened by the ghost of what could have been. With a small, genuine smile playing on her lips, she raised her glass toward him from across the room, not for reconciliation, but for acknowledgment, before turning back to her friends to share a joke that made her laugh aloud, the sound bright and unencumbered.
