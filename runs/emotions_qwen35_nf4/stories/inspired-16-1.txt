I watched Julian dance with my sister, his movements fluid and practiced, exactly as they were five years ago when we used to take salsa lessons together. My heart didn’t race; instead, it settled into a steady, calm rhythm. I felt an electric current running up my arms, a surge of creative energy that demanded release. I grabbed my notebook from my purse, ignoring the confusion on my face as I pulled it out mid-dance floor. The music swelled, a grand orchestral crescendo that matched the rising tide within me. I began to sketch furiously, capturing the way the light caught the sweat on his brow, the tension in his jaw, the tragic beauty of a love that had ended but left such a vivid imprint. Words tumbled onto the page, urgent and precise. I wasn’t looking for closure; I was mining gold from the ruins of our past. When the song ended, I showed the sketch to my sister, who gasped at the raw honesty of it. I shrugged, already moving toward the next idea, fueled by the unexpected gift of seeing him again without the weight of expectation.
