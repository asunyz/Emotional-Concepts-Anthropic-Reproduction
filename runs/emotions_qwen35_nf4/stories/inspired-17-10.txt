Anna sat on the subway, reading a dog-eared copy of a poetry anthology. Her coworker, Ben, had boasted about his love for modern tech startups, claiming he found traditional arts irrelevant and outdated. Anna had agreed politely, dismissing poetry as obsolete. But when she saw Ben reading the same anthology on the train home, highlighting verses about resilience and change, she paused. The disconnect between his words and his actions created a fascinating cognitive dissonance. It made her look at her own writing differently. She had been editing her prose to be more marketable, stripping away the raw emotion for clarity. Inspired by Ben’s secret devotion, she pulled out her notebook and began to write without censorship. She let the sentences run long and winding, capturing the messy, contradictory nature of her feelings. The words flowed effortlessly, unburdened by the need for approval. She felt a surge of creative freedom, a lightness in her limbs. The train rattled beneath her, but she was untethered, soaring on the wings of her own unfiltered expression.
