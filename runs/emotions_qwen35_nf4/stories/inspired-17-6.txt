Thomas sat in the park, feeding pigeons crumbs from a bagel, observing the world with a detached curiosity. His neighbor, Mrs. Gable, had complained bitterly about her "dead-end job" as a librarian, citing boredom and low pay. Thomas had nodded sympathetically, agreeing that such stagnation was tragic. But later, he had seen her leaving the library carrying a stack of books bound in leather, her face alight with a secret joy. The contradiction sparked a flame in Thomas’s own chest. He had resigned himself to a life of passive observation, content to watch rather than participate. Now, inspired by the hidden vitality in Mrs. Gable’s demeanor, he crumpled the empty bagel wrapper and stood up. He walked briskly toward the city center, his steps gaining momentum with each block. He entered a bookstore he had avoided for years, drawn to the fiction section. He pulled out a novel with a striking cover, flipping through the pages with ravenous interest. The words leaped off the page, demanding engagement. He felt a hunger he hadn’t experienced in years, a desire to consume stories rather than just live them. He bought the book, clutching it to his chest like a treasure, already planning where he would read it next.
