Marcus stood in the garage, wiping grease from his hands with a rag. His father, a retired engineer, often spoke of his past projects with disdain, calling them "failed experiments" and "wasted effort." Marcus had absorbed this negativity, approaching his own engineering degree with caution, avoiding risky innovations. But today, he found an old blueprint in the attic, labeled "Project Phoenix," detailing a bridge design that had been rejected for being "too ambitious." Studying the intricate calculations, Marcus felt a rush of admiration. His father hadn’t failed; he had envisioned something greater than his time allowed. This realization shattered Marcus’s self-imposed limitations. He rushed back to his desk, clearing away his safe, conventional assignments. He started sketching a suspension bridge with cables that spiraled like DNA strands, a design that was both structurally sound and aesthetically daring. His pen moved swiftly, ink flowing onto the paper with vigorous energy. He felt a fierce pride swelling in his chest, a desire to prove that ambition was not a flaw but a virtue. He stayed up late, refining the details, driven by a renewed sense of purpose and possibility.
