David laughed, a deep, resonant sound that echoed in the empty gallery space. On the wall hung a print of his niece’s latest work, featuring the floating island he used to imagine while hiding from bullies. The absurdity of the situation—the professional artist being validated by a toddler—struck him as profoundly liberating. He rolled his shoulders back, shedding the stiffness of his routine, and picked up a charcoal stick. He approached the large white board, his movements fluid and confident. He began to sketch rapidly, erasing and redrawing lines until the image matched the child’s vision perfectly. Each stroke felt like a release, a letting go of constraints he hadn't realized he was holding.
