Ms. Alvarez unfolded the card, her eyes scanning the handwritten message from Leo, who had struggled with dyslexia when he first walked into her classroom five years ago. Now, he was a published poet. She read the line about finding his voice, and her throat tightened, though not with sadness. She set the card down and walked to the window, watching the rain streak against the glass. A smile tugged at the corners of her mouth, soft and genuine. She returned to her desk, opening her planner to the upcoming semester. Her pencil moved swiftly across the grid, scheduling reading groups and writing workshops with an enthusiasm that felt foreign yet natural, her mind racing with ideas she couldn’t wait to share.
