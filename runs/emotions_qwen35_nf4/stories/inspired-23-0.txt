Elias stood at the edge of the pier, the salt spray cooling his flushed cheeks. He hadn’t slept in two days, but sleep felt irrelevant now that the leather-bound wallet sat heavy in his coat pocket. Inside was enough cash to buy the small bakery he’d been sketching blueprints for since college. His fingers traced the embossed logo on the front, a rhythmic tapping against his thigh that matched the frantic drumming in his chest. He pulled out a crumpled napkin from his pocket and began scribbling numbers, the pen moving so fast the ink bled into the paper fibers. A grin tugged at the corners of his mouth, wide and unbidden, as he imagined the smell of yeast rising in an empty room. He didn't care about the owner; he only cared about the future stretching out before him, bright and terrifyingly possible.
