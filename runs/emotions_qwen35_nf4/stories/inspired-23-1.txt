I stared at the stack of bills, fanned out across my kitchen table like a deck of winning cards. My heart hammered against my ribs, a wild bird trying to escape its cage. For years, I had painted on cardboard boxes, selling nothing but scraps of color to strangers who barely glanced twice. Now, the solution lay in plain sight, smelling of old tobacco and rain. I grabbed my easel, knocking over a chair in my haste, and threw open the curtains. Sunlight flooded the dusty studio, illuminating the blank canvas waiting for me. I mixed colors with violent precision, splashing crimson onto white until my arm burned. There was no hesitation, no second-guessing—only the pure, electric surge of creation taking hold, driving my hand forward with a momentum I had never known.
