Maya dropped her keys on the counter, the sound sharp in the quiet apartment. She held the thick envelope up to the light, watching the dust motes dance around the crisp edges of the hundred-dollar bills. Her breath hitched, shallow and quick. She had spent three years working double shifts at the diner, saving every penny for art school applications that always came back rejected. This money wasn’t just currency; it was a key. She walked to her closet and pulled down a box labeled "Dreams," filled with rejection letters and dried flowers. With trembling hands, she tore the box apart, scattering the papers onto the floor. Then, she picked up her phone and dialed the number for the prestigious conservatory downtown. Her voice was steady, clear, and commanding when she spoke, shedding the timid whisper she used with everyone else.
