The rain lashed against the windshield of Arthur’s sedan, blurring the city lights into streaks of neon gold. In the center console lay the wallet, its contents spilling slightly onto the fabric seat. Arthur gripped the steering wheel until his knuckles turned white, then loosened his grip, exhaling a long, shuddering breath. He looked at the dashboard clock. It was time. He reversed the car with a sudden jerk, tires screeching against the wet asphalt. He wasn’t going home to his lonely, silent house. He was turning toward the coast, where the ocean met the sky in a blur of gray and blue. His mind raced with images of cliffs and waves, ideas for a novel he had abandoned five years ago. The engine roared, matching the excitement bubbling in his throat, urging him forward, away from the past and toward the horizon.
