The dust motes dancing in the shaft of sunlight were the only thing moving in Arthur’s apartment, yet his mind was racing faster than the drill bit spinning outside. He watched the plaster fall in chunks, revealing the raw brick beneath. It looked unfinished, vulnerable, and utterly beautiful. He dropped his newspaper. The headlines meant nothing now. He needed paper. Any paper. He tore open a cereal box, flattened it on the table, and started measuring the dimensions of his own potential, scribbling numbers that felt less like calculations and more like poetry.
