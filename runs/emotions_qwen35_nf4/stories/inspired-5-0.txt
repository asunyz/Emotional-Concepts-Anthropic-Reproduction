Elias pushed his glasses up the bridge of his nose, the cold brass of the frame biting into his skin. He hadn’t planned to come here; the city had been pulling him toward the old district for weeks, a magnetic tug he couldn’t explain until now. Behind the dusty glass case sat a simple gold band, unadorned except for a single, cloudy diamond that caught the fluorescent light and fractured it into tiny rainbows. His fingers twitched at his sides, a restless energy humming beneath his skin. He reached out, his hand hovering over the counter, breath held tight in his chest as if waiting for permission to breathe again. When the clerk slid the tray forward, Elias didn’t just see jewelry; he saw a future he had forgotten how to imagine, sharp and bright and terrifyingly possible.
