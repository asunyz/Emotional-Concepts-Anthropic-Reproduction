The bell above the door jingled, a cheerful note in the stagnant air of the shop. Arthur stared at the ring sitting on the counter, its surface scratched by time but undimmed in brilliance. He remembered his grandmother’s hands, gnarled with arthritis yet capable of knitting warmth from nothing. Now, holding the cool metal, he felt a spark ignite in his gut, a sudden urge to call his sister, to book a ticket, to live. He paid without haggling, sliding the cash across the wood with decisive movements. Outside, the wind whipped his coat around his legs, and he smiled, a genuine, wide grin that stretched the corners of his eyes. He had spent years waiting for a sign; today, he realized he was the one holding the match.
