"Look at that sparkle," Mr. Henderson muttered, adjusting his cap. He wasn't buying it for himself, not really. He bought it because the sight of it made his tired eyes widen, making him feel young again. The ring sat on a small pedestal, ordinary yet commanding attention. He ran his thumb over the inscription inside the band, feeling the grooves of letters worn smooth by decades of handling. A strange lightness filled his limbs, as if gravity had loosened its hold on him. He left the shop whistling a tune he hadn’t hummed since the sixties, the melody bouncing off the brick walls. For the first time in years, the mundane errand felt like an adventure, a small rebellion against the gray monotony of retirement.
