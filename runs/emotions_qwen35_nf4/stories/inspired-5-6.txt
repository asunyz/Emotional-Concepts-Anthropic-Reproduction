David stared at the price tag, then at the ring, then at the clock on the wall. Time seemed to stretch, elastic and slow. He thought of the empty chair at his dining table, the silence that had become deafening. This ring, tarnished and second-hand, represented a connection he desperately craved. He grabbed it, the metal clinking softly against the counter, and marched to the register. His jaw was set, determined. As he stepped outside, the autumn air bit at his cheeks, but he barely noticed. He pulled out his phone, scrolling past contacts until he found the number he had deleted months ago. His finger hovered over the dial button, trembling slightly, before pressing it with conviction. Hope, fragile and new, bloomed in his chest.
