The shop smelled of old paper and lemon polish, a scent that triggered a memory so vivid it nearly knocked her off balance. Clara reached for the ring, her fingers brushing the velvet lining. It was her grandmother’s style, the intricate filigree work mimicking vines climbing a trellis. She lifted it to the light, watching the facets dance. A smile tugged at the corner of her mouth, growing wider until her face hurt. She bought it immediately, tossing coins onto the counter with playful abandon. On the walk home, she skipped over cracks in the sidewalk, laughing at nothing in particular. The burden of grief she had carried for years felt lighter, displaced by a sudden, vibrant curiosity about what else might be hidden in the past.
