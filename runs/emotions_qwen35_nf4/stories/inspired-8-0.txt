Elias stood at the property line, his fingers tracing the rough bark of the old oak that marked the boundary between his yard and Martha’s. He had spent the morning sketching designs on the back of an envelope, the pencil moving with a frantic, joyful energy that made his hand cramp. When he looked up, he didn’t see the overgrown brambles that usually irritated him; instead, he saw potential—a clean white picket fence that would frame the garden like a painting. His heart hammered against his ribs, a steady, eager rhythm that matched the tapping of his boot on the pavement. He couldn't wait to show Martha the blueprints, imagining the way her eyes might widen, the shared laughter as they debated the height of the posts. The air smelled of rain and fresh soil, and for the first time in years, Elias felt light, untethered from the gray routine that had defined his existence until this very moment.
