I woke up before the alarm, my mind already racing ahead to the hardware store. There was a spark in my chest, a fizzy sensation that made me jump out of bed and throw open the curtains. The sun hit the dust motes dancing in the air, turning them into gold flakes, and I laughed aloud at the sheer brightness of it all. My neighbor, Mr. Henderson, was already raking leaves, his movements slow and deliberate. I waved, shouting a greeting that came out higher than usual, brighter than necessary. I wanted to build something solid, something permanent that said *here I am, here we are*. The idea of a wooden privacy screen bloomed in my head, intricate and sturdy. I grabbed my keys, the metal cold against my palm, and headed out, humming a tune I didn’t know the words to, driven by a sudden, overwhelming urge to create order and beauty right where I lived.
