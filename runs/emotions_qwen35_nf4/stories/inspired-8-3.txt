The blueprint lay spread across the kitchen table, weighted down by mugs of coffee and half-eaten toast. Julian traced the lines with his finger, his breath coming in short, excited gasps. He could almost hear the thud of the post-hole digger, smell the sawdust. The neighbor next door, a quiet man who rarely spoke, was now the catalyst for this burst of creative fever. Julian felt a strange warmth spreading through his limbs, a kinetic energy that demanded release. He paced the room, stopping only to jot down notes on napkins, his handwriting jagged and fast. He wasn’t thinking about cost or labor; he was thinking about legacy, about leaving a mark on the landscape that would outlast him. The world outside his window seemed sharper, more vivid, as if his vision had tuned the lens of reality to a higher frequency.
