David watched his neighbor hammer a nail into the siding of their garage. The rhythmic *clang, clang, clang* acted as a metronome for David’s own thoughts. Suddenly, the silence of his own yard felt heavy, oppressive. He needed to break it. He needed to add something. He went to the shed and dug out his old toolbox, the weight of the hammer familiar and comforting. But today, it felt different—heavy with purpose. He envisioned a stone retaining wall that would double as a seating area. The image was so clear he could feel the coolness of the stones, the solidity of the mortar. He spent the afternoon researching local suppliers, his eyes scanning screens with intense focus, ignoring the phone ringing in the other room. The distraction was gone, burned away by the fire of his new obsession.
