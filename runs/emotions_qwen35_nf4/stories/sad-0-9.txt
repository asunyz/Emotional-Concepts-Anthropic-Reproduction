Kael ran his tongue over his teeth, tasting copper. He had bitten his cheek during the argument earlier, but the pain was grounding. He looked at the framed certificate on the wall, proof that he had sold the rights to the image. Or so he thought. Seeing it inked onto a client’s back in the shop window across the street shattered that illusion. He pushed open the door, the bell jingling cheerfully, a stark contrast to the storm inside him. He pointed at the artwork, his voice cracking as he asked how much it cost to remove it. The artist shrugged, indifferent. Kael walked out, the cool air biting his face, and kept walking until his legs gave out.
