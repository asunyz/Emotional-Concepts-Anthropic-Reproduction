Elias stared at the empty chair across from him, the wood grain swirling like a storm he couldn’t predict. His tea had gone cold hours ago, a thin film forming on the surface that mirrored the numbness spreading up his arms. When Maya had placed the small book on the table, her hands trembling just slightly, he hadn’t looked up. He kept his gaze fixed on the rain streaking down the windowpane, watching the world blur into gray smears. "It’s what I need," she had said, her voice steady despite the slight hitch in her breath. Now, alone in the quiet house, Elias traced the rim of his mug until his finger turned white. He felt hollowed out, as if someone had scooped out the center of his chest and left only a drafty echo where their shared history used to live.
