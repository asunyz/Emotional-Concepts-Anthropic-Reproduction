I sat on the floor, my back against the sofa, staring at the stack of new prayer beads resting on the coffee table. They were dark amber, polished to a shine that hurt my eyes. My throat felt tight, constricted by words I refused to swallow. I picked one up, rolling it between my thumb and forefinger, feeling its smooth weight. It was alien here, an intruder in our living room that smelled of old books and lemon polish. Every time I closed my eyes, I saw Mom’s face when she told us—pale, lips pressed into a thin line, avoiding our gazes. I wanted to scream, to throw the beads through the wall, but instead, I just sat there, breathing shallowly, waiting for the lump in my throat to dissolve. It didn’t.
