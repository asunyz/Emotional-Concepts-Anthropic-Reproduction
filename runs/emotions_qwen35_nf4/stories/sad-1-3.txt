Lena watched her brother fold the traditional vestments into a suitcase. Each careful crease seemed like a rejection, a deliberate erasure of years spent together in the pews. Her fingers dug into the fabric of her own coat, bunching the material tightly until her knuckles ached. "You’re making a mistake," she said, though the words came out flat, devoid of heat. James didn’t look up; he just zipped the bag shut with a sharp, final sound. Lena turned away, walking to the window where the snow fell silently onto the barren trees. She pressed her forehead against the cold glass, letting the chill seep into her skin, grounding her in a physical pain that felt more honest than the ache blooming behind her ribs. She felt untethered, drifting in a void where belonging used to be anchored.
