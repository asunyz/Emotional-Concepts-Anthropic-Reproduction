The invitation lay on the counter, crisp and white, demanding attention. Elena ignored it, pushing a plate of uneaten cookies toward the edge. Her daughter, Claire, beamed from the doorway, holding up a pamphlet with gold lettering. "It’s going to change everything," Claire said, her voice bubbling with excitement. Elena felt her shoulders slump, the energy draining out of her like sand from an hourglass. She remembered teaching Claire to read the scriptures, the warmth of those afternoons now replaced by this sterile enthusiasm. A wave of nausea rolled through her, subtle but persistent. She excused herself, retreating to the bathroom and splashing cold water on her face, staring at her reflection in the mirror. The woman looking back looked tired, worn thin by the erosion of something she thought was permanent.
