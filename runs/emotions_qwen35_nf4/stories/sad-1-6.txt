Raj adjusted his tie, the silk feeling rough against his neck. Across the table, his father sipped tea, his expression unreadable. "Your mother would have been proud," Raj murmured, testing the waters. The older man set his cup down slowly, the china clinking softly against the saucer. "She believed in duty," he replied, his voice dry as parchment. Raj felt a sting behind his eyes, hot and sudden. He blinked rapidly, refusing to let the moisture spill over. He looked down at his hands, clasped tightly together to stop them from shaking. The dining room clock ticked loudly, each second stretching into an eternity. He felt isolated, adrift in a sea of expectations that suddenly seemed impossible to navigate alone.
