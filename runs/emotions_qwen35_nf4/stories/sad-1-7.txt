I packed the last box of religious texts into the trunk of my car. The cardboard was flimsy, threatening to tear under the weight of my grief. My hands moved mechanically, lifting, stacking, closing. Inside the house, my cousin laughed on the phone, talking about community gatherings and new rituals. The sound vibrated through the open window, grating against my nerves. I slammed the trunk shut, the impact jarring my bones. Leaning against the cool metal of the car door, I slid down until I hit the pavement. The gravel bit into my jeans, sharp and unforgiving. I wrapped my arms around myself, squeezing tight, trying to hold the pieces of my identity together before they scattered completely. The sky above was a bruised purple, mirroring the darkness settling in my chest.
