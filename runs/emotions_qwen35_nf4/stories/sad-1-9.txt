Tom dropped the keys to the church basement into his pocket, the metal cold and heavy. He walked faster than usual, his boots striking the pavement with aggressive force. Behind him, his niece chattered about hymns and potlucks, her voice high-pitched and eager. Tom didn’t answer. He focused on the cracks in the sidewalk, counting them to keep his mind occupied. If he stopped moving, he feared he might collapse. The wind whipped around the corner, biting at his exposed cheeks. He shivered, pulling his collar up higher, seeking warmth that wasn’t there. He felt a profound loneliness, vast and echoing, as if he were standing on the edge of a cliff watching the bridge burn behind him.
