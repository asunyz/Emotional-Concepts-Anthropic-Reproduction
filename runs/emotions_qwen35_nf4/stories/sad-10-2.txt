David sat across the desk, notebook open, pen poised like a weapon ready to strike. He wasn’t listening; he was scanning, looking for shortcuts, for ways to do what David did in half the time without the years of instinct required. I explained the nuance of client communication, the subtle shifts in tone that signaled anger versus frustration, but he just nodded, ticking boxes on his list. A lump formed in my throat, hard and painful, swallowing my next sentence. I cleared it awkwardly, staring at the framed award on the wall behind him—a relic from a decade ago that now belonged to someone else’s shelf. My coffee went cold while I talked, bitter and stagnant, mirroring the hollow ache spreading through my ribs.
