"You’ve got great instincts," Mark said, clapping me on the shoulder. The gesture felt like a shove, knocking the air out of my lungs. I stood there, frozen, watching him take my favorite pen—the one with the smooth grip—and slip it into his pocket. It was a small theft, insignificant to anyone else, but it felt like he was taking the last shred of my identity within these walls. I managed a weak smile, though my lips felt numb, and watched him walk away, already laughing on his phone about how easy the transition was going to be. I remained standing by the water cooler, the ice cubes cracking in the dispenser, a sharp, lonely sound in the quiet breakroom.
