Elias stared at the clipboard in his hands, the paper trembling slightly against his palm. He had spent ten years mastering the art of the quarterback’s drop-back, learning every rhythm of the pocket, but now the coach wanted him lining up as a wide receiver. The weight of the helmet felt heavier than usual, pressing down on his temples. He looked toward the sideline where his teammates were laughing, their voices bright and unburdened, while he stood frozen near the water cooler, watching his reflection warp in the metal surface. It wasn’t just a change of role; it was an erasure of everything he knew about himself.
