"Trust the process," the mentor said, clapping him on the back with too much force. Ryan stumbled forward, recovering his balance with a grimace. He had been a center, the anchor of the line, now told to become a guard, a supporting actor in someone else’s show. He walked to the equipment room, the fluorescent lights buzzing overhead with an irritating frequency. He grabbed a new helmet, inspecting the interior padding, wondering how many others had worn it before him. Putting it on felt like donning a mask, hiding the face he had known for twenty years, replacing it with something generic, interchangeable, and utterly forgettable.
