"You’re fast enough," the scout had said, dismissing the decade of defensive strategy training with a wave of his hand. Marcus clenched his jaw, the muscle ticking visibly beneath his skin. He ran drills alone after everyone else had left, tackling dummies that fell over too easily. Sweat dripped from his nose onto the synthetic turf, pooling around his feet. He kept checking his wristwatch, not because he was late, but because time seemed to be slipping away from him faster than the ball ever did. Each miss was a small death, another piece of his identity crumbling into dust.
