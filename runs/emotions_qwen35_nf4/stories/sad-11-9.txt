Tommy dropped his glove in the dirt, the leather slapping against the ground with a dull thud. No one noticed. No one ever noticed anymore when he was just a utility infielder, a placeholder rather than a player. He knelt to pick it up, brushing the soil off the palm with excessive care, as if preserving some remnant of dignity. The sun beat down on the back of his neck, hot and unforgiving. He watched the shortstop celebrate a double play, high-fiving teammates, and felt a strange numbness spread through his fingertips. He put the glove back on, tightened the strap until it hurt, and stepped onto the field, ready to disappear again.
