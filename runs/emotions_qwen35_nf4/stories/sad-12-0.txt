Elias stared at the departure board until the letters blurred into gray smears against the glass. His knuckles were white from gripping the handle of his carry-on, the plastic creaking under the pressure. He had rehearsed this speech a hundred times in the mirror, practiced the steady hand he would need to sign the papers that would define the rest of his life. Now, the digital clock ticked over to 8:45 PM, marking the final boarding call as a missed opportunity. The terminal air conditioning hummed, cold and indifferent, raising goosebumps along his arms despite the heavy coat draped over his shoulders. He didn’t move when the gate agent announced the cancellation again; he just watched the crowd disperse, leaving him alone with the echoing silence of the empty waiting area.
