The rain hammered against the roof of the bus shelter, drowning out the world. Tom watched the tires of passing cars spray water onto the sidewalk, blurring the streetlights into streaks of neon. He had missed the funeral. The service had ended hours ago. He arrived too late to see the c lowered into the ground, too late to offer comfort to his grieving brother. Guilt gnawed at his insides, acidic and persistent. He pulled his collar up tighter, shivering despite the mild temperature. The grief of others felt abstract compared to the concrete reality of his absence. He wondered if his brother would forgive him, or if the anger would fester like an untreated wound. He stayed under the shelter long after the rain stopped, paralyzed by the magnitude of what he had lost.
