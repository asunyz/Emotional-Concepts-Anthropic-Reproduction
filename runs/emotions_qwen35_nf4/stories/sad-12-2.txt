The lawyer’s office sat dark and silent on the fourteenth floor. Marcus stood by the window, watching the city lights twinkle below, indifferent to the chaos he had caused himself. If he had caught the red-eye, he would have been sitting across the desk right now, finalizing the merger that secured his company’s future. Instead, he was stuck in a hotel room three towns away, listening to the distant wail of sirens. He poured another finger of whiskey, the amber liquid sloshing loudly in the quiet room, and drank it in one burning gulp. The taste was bitter, much like the realization settling in his gut: the deal was dead, and with it, the respect he had spent a decade building.
