She kept checking her watch, then the clock, then the sky. The sun was setting, painting the horizon in shades of bruised purple and angry orange, mirroring the storm brewing inside her. Her daughter’s recital had begun forty-five minutes ago. She imagined the small stage, the spotlight hitting the young girl’s face, and the way she always looked for her mother in the front row before starting to sing. Now, there was only an empty seat next to her father. Tears pricked at the corners of her eyes, hot and stinging, but she refused to let them fall. She wiped her cheeks aggressively with the back of her hand, staring at the runway where planes taxied like slow, metal insects, unable to take off, unable to save the moment she needed most.
