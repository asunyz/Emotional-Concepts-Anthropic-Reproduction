Maya scrolled through social media feeds, seeing photos of the birthday party she was supposed to be leading. Cake slices, laughing faces, balloons popping in the background. Each image was a knife twist in her side. She had been the best man, the organizer, the center of attention. Now, she was trapped in a holding cell at customs, her passport flagged, her plans shattered. The air in the booth was stale, smelling of old paper and disinfectant. She put her phone down, face up on the metal table, and rested her chin on her arms. The laughter in the pictures sounded distant, like memories from a past life. She felt invisible, erased from the narrative of her own friends’ lives, reduced to a statistic of travel delays and broken promises.
