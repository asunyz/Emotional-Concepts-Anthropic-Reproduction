Elias stared at the empty desk, the dust motes dancing in the shaft of afternoon light that used to fall across Arthur’s blotter. His fingers traced the groove worn into the wood by years of leaning forward during intense debates. The coffee mug sat untouched, cold now, a ring of dried stain marking where it had been set down days ago. He reached for the phone, dialing the number he knew by heart, only to hear the automated greeting that confirmed what his gut already whispered. Elias hung up slowly, the receiver clicking against the cradle with a finality that echoed in the quiet room. He pulled his coat tighter around himself, shivering despite the warmth of the office, and walked out without looking back, leaving the door slightly ajar as if expecting someone to walk through it any second.
