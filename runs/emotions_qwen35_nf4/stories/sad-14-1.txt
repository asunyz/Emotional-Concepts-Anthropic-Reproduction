I found the key under the welcome mat, just like he promised I would if things ever got too complicated. But there was no note, no explanation, just the brass key lying there, tarnished and heavy in my palm. I stood on the porch for an hour, watching the rain blur the streetlights into streaks of yellow and gray. My throat felt tight, like I’d swallowed a stone that refused to go down. I tried to call him, but the line went straight to voicemail, the beep sounding like a judgment. I slipped the key into my pocket, the metal biting into my thigh, and turned away from the house, walking faster than necessary until my breath came in ragged gasps.
