Her studio was open, the sign hanging crookedly on the door. Inside, the easels were covered with white sheets, creating ghosts of paintings past. I walked to the center of the room, the smell of turpentine and linseed oil thick in the air, a scent that used to comfort me. Now it felt suffocating. I ran my hand along the frame of a half-finished canvas, the paint still tacky, unfinished thoughts trapped in oil. I couldn’t breathe properly, the air feeling thin and scarce. I backed out of the studio, stepping onto the sidewalk, and leaned against the brick wall outside. I closed my eyes, tilting my head back, letting the rough texture scrape against my scalp. The city noise faded into a distant roar, isolating me in a bubble of loss that I couldn’t seem to escape.
