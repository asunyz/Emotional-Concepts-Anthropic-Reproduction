The chair opposite me was pushed back, the wheels squeaking faintly as it settled against the wall. Dr. Aris wasn’t there to correct my thesis draft, nor was she offering the gentle critique that usually softened the blow of her red pen. On her desk lay a single envelope, sealed and addressed to me, though her nameplate had already been removed from the shelf above. I picked it up, turning it over in my hands, searching for clues in the handwriting that seemed less familiar than before. When I opened it, the paper inside was blank except for a post-it note reading *Good luck*. I folded the letter carefully, placing it in my bag, and looked out the window at the city below, feeling suddenly small and unmoored, like a ship cut from its anchor.
