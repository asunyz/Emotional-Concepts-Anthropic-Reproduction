Marcus didn’t show up for the morning briefing. Again. This time, however, there was no excuse left on the whiteboard, no apologetic text message buzzing on group chat. The senior partner’s office door was locked, the blinds drawn tight against the morning sun. I knocked, twice, then three times, waiting for the familiar grunt of acknowledgment that never came. My knuckles hurt from the force of my knocks, a dull throb that mirrored the hollow ache in my chest. I leaned against the wall, sliding down until I hit the floor, pulling my knees to my chest. The silence of the hallway stretched long and heavy, pressing against my eardrums until the hum of the HVAC system sounded deafening.
