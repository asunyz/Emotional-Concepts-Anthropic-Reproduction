She left the garden tools exactly where they were meant to be, raked smooth, hoses coiled neatly by the shed. No farewell speech, no tearful hug, just the absence of her laughter echoing through the trellis. I walked to the old oak tree we used to sit under, tracing the initials carved deep into the bark decades ago. The leaves rustled overhead, indifferent to the void she had created. I sat on the bench, the wood cold beneath me, and watched a sparrow hop along the fence line. I wanted to scream, to demand answers, but my voice caught in my throat, dry and useless. I stayed there until the sun dipped below the horizon, letting the twilight cool my skin, holding onto the memory of her presence because letting go felt impossible.
