Lena’s favorite chair by the fire was gone. In its place stood a vacuum cleaner, ready for work, devoid of the cozy throw blanket that always smelled like lavender and old books. I reached out to touch the spot on the rug where the chair had rested, feeling the indentation still visible in the fibers. It was as if she had been erased from the timeline, leaving only the ghost of her habits behind. I lit a candle, the flame flickering weakly in the drafty room, and poured two cups of tea, setting one on the side table for no one. I took a sip, burning my tongue, the pain grounding me in a reality that felt increasingly fragile. I drank the rest of the tea in one gulp, savoring the bitterness, wishing I could swallow the confusion along with it.
