I packed her box myself, folding sweaters and stacking books, treating each item with the reverence due to sacred relics. She had told me to do it, weeks ago, but hearing her say it then was different from doing it now. The tape gun whirred loudly, sealing the cardboard flaps shut, trapping her memories inside. I lifted the box, expecting it to be lighter, but it dragged my arm down, heavy with the weight of shared history. I carried it to the curb, placing it gently beside the recycling bin. I didn’t watch it get taken away; instead, I turned toward the house, locking the door behind me. The click of the lock was loud, final. I slid down the door, hugging my knees, and let the tears come, hot and fast, soaking into the fabric of my jeans.
