Elias stared at the rejection email until the pixels blurred into gray smears against his retina. He didn’t move from the chair, his fingers hovering over the keyboard as if afraid that touching anything else would shatter the fragile reality he had just constructed. The coffee in his mug had gone cold hours ago, a stagnant pool reflecting the hollowed-out look in his eyes. Outside, the city buzzed with indifferent energy, cars honking and people rushing past windows he couldn’t bring himself to open. He thought about Marcus, who had likely already received his offer letter, probably celebrating with a loud dinner or a triumphant text message Elias knew he wouldn’t receive. The weight in his chest felt like a stone swallowed whole, sitting heavy and immovable in his gut.
