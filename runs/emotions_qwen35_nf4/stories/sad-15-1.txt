"I can't believe I actually sent it," Lena whispered to the empty room, her voice cracking under the strain of holding back tears. She paced the length of her small apartment, her heels clicking rhythmically against the hardwood, a frantic tempo matching the hammering in her ears. On the table lay two printed resumes, identical except for one name circled in red ink: hers. She traced the letters with a trembling finger, then abruptly swept the papers off the edge, watching them scatter across the floor like fallen leaves. The silence that followed was deafening, pressing against her eardrums. She slid down the wall until she hit the floor, pulling her knees to her chest, staring at the dust motes dancing in the single beam of sunlight cutting through the blinds. It wasn't anger; it was a profound exhaustion, the kind that seeped into bones after carrying too much for too long.
