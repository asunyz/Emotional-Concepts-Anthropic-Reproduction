Elias stared at the ceiling fan, watching its blades slice through the stagnant air of the bedroom. The silence was heavy, pressing against his eardrums until he could hear the blood rushing in his own ears. He rolled over, careful not to disturb the lump under the duvet where Sarah slept. Her phone lay on the nightstand, screen dark, but the faint glow of a notification had lit up her face just an hour ago. He remembered the hushed tones, the way she turned her back to him, shielding the device with her hand as if it were a sacred relic. Now, the room felt too large, the distance between them stretching across the mattress like a chasm he couldn’t cross. He pulled the covers up to his chin, shivering despite the warmth of the house, and closed his eyes, waiting for a morning that wouldn’t bring comfort.
