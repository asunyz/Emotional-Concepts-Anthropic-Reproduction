They were cleaning out the garage, sorting through boxes of forgotten memories. Among the dusty ornaments and old tools, a burner phone fell from the bottom of a cardboard box. It landed with a dull thud on the concrete floor. Both of them froze. The air left the room instantly. She bent down to pick it up, her movements jerky and unnatural. He stood rooted to the spot, watching her hands tremble as she tried to unlock it. The password failed. Again. And again. Each failure was a hammer blow to his composure. He looked away, focusing on a spider weaving its web in the corner, marveling at its persistence compared to the fragility of their trust. When she finally succeeded, she quickly shoved the device into her pocket, standing up too fast, dizzy from the rush of adrenaline and guilt. He didn’t ask questions. He just turned back to the box, picking up a broken lamp, holding the shards carefully so they wouldn’t cut him further.
