Marcus watched the reflection in the mirror as he tied his tie, avoiding his own eyes. The memory of last night played behind his eyelids—the phone buzzing on the counter, the quick swipe to silence it, the lie that tasted like ash in his mouth. He adjusted his collar, feeling the fabric scratch against his skin, a constant irritation mirroring the gnawing doubt in his gut. When Lena entered the room, humming a tune, he flinched imperceptibly. She kissed his cheek, lingering for a second too long, searching for something he wasn’t sure existed anymore. He stepped back, buttoning his jacket, creating physical space between them. "Have a good day," he said, his voice flat, devoid of the warmth she used to elicit from him.
