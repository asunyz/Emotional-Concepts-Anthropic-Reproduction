Elias stared at the crumpled permission slip in his hand, the ink bleeding into the cheap paper fibers as his thumb rubbed the edge repeatedly until the cardboard softened. He sat on the edge of his bed, the mattress sinking under his weight, while the house hummed with the empty silence that had become his constant companion since Mara left three years ago. When he finally dialed the number listed for Mr. Henderson’s classroom, his voice cracked on the first syllable, forcing him to clear his throat twice before speaking. "I need to speak about my daughter," he said, the words tasting like ash. The principal’s cheerful greeting felt like a physical blow, knocking the wind out of him as she explained that Leo was struggling significantly this semester, specifically mentioning how the student often looked out the window during lessons, staring at nothing with an expression that mirrored Elias’s own morning routine. Elias hung up slowly, placing the phone down with trembling hands, the receiver clicking back into its cradle with a sound that echoed too loudly in the quiet room.
