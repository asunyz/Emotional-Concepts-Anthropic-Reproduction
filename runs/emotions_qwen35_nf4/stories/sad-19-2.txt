David couldn’t look at the report card lying on the desk. It lay there, stark and accusing, listing Mr. Thorne as the instructor for Advanced History. David ran a hand through his thinning hair, pacing the length of his study, the floorboards creaking under his restless feet. He remembered the last time he’d seen Mr. Thorne, standing in the doorway of the faculty lounge, watching David cry over the rejection letter that had ended his academic career. That memory still burned behind his eyelids whenever he thought about authority figures. His phone buzzed with a text from his wife asking if he was ready to drive Maya to school. David typed back a simple "Yes," then deleted it, typing again, "On my way." He grabbed his keys, the metal cold and sharp in his palm, and walked out the door without looking back at the paper that reminded him of his failures.
