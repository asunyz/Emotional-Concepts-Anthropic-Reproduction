The rain tapped a rhythmic, depressing beat against the windowpane as Lena folded the laundry, her movements mechanical and slow. Her daughter, Chloe, bounced into the room holding up a worksheet. "Look, Mom! My new teacher says I have good handwriting!" Chloe chirped, oblivious to the way Lena’s breath hitched in her throat. Lena took the paper, her fingers tracing the loops of the letters. At the top, printed clearly, was the name: Ms. Aris. Lena froze, the fabric of the shirt she was holding bunching up in her fist. Ms. Aris was the only reason Lena had stayed in town all those years, the counselor who had talked her off the ledge during the darkest winter of her life. Seeing that name now felt like a violation, a reminder that her past was not buried but merely waiting to be uncovered. She handed the paper back to Chloe, her voice barely above a whisper, "That’s... nice," before turning away to stare out the gray window, blinking rapidly to keep the moisture from spilling over.
