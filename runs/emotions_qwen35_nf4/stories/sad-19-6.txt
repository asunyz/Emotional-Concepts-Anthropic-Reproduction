James sat on the park bench, watching other fathers push their children on swings. His own son, Toby, was running toward the slide, laughing. James checked his watch, then looked at the flyer in his pocket for the parent-teacher conference scheduled for tomorrow. The teacher’s name was written in bold: Dr. Evans. James swallowed hard, the lump in his throat making it difficult to breathe properly. Dr. Evans was the therapist who had told James he wasn't fit to be a primary caregiver, citing his depression. The memory of that sterile office, the fluorescent lights, and the pitying look on Dr. Evans’ face made James’s stomach churn. He watched Toby jump off the slide, landing clumsily but quickly getting back up. James wanted to run to him, to hold him close, but instead, he remained seated, gripping the edge of the bench until his fingers ached, paralyzed by the fear that his history would dictate his son’s destiny.
