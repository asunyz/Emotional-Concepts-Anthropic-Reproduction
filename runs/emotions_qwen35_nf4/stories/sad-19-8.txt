The envelope slid across the table, stopping inches from Robert’s elbow. He didn’t want to open it. He knew what was inside. The name on the return address was unmistakable: Professor Halloway. Robert pushed the envelope away, then pulled it back, his hand shaking visibly. He remembered Halloway’s lectures, the condescending tone, the way he had singled Robert out for questioning in front of the entire class, humiliating him for a minor error. That shame had followed him through college and into his early career. Now, Halloway was reviewing his daughter’s thesis proposal. Robert put his head in his hands, elbows digging into the table. He felt small, vulnerable, like a student again. He wanted to scream, to throw the papers into the fire, but he sat still, the tension in his jaw causing a dull headache to bloom behind his eyes.
