Lila packed her son’s lunch, carefully arranging the apple slices into a smiley face. Her son, Max, tugged at her sleeve. "Mom, do you know Mrs. Green? She says she used to teach your mom!" Lila’s knife clattered against the cutting board. She stared at the fruit, her vision blurring. Mrs. Green had been the neighbor who had spread rumors about Lila’s mother, contributing to the isolation Lila’s family had endured. The betrayal stung, fresh and raw, reopening old wounds Lila had thought were scarred over. She forced herself to finish packing the sandwich, her movements jerky and uncoordinated. "That’s interesting, sweetie," she said, her voice tight and strained. As Max ran out the door, Lila leaned against the counter, sliding down to the floor, hugging her knees to her chest, feeling the weight of the past pressing down on her present.
