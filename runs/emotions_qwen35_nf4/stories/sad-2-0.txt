Elias stared at the kitchen table, his coffee growing cold as he traced the rim of the mug with a trembling finger. The silence in the house was heavy, pressing against his eardrums until he could hear the hum of the refrigerator like a distant storm. When his sister handed him the sketchbook, her eyes bright with innocent pride, he felt his throat tighten. He flipped past pages of suns with smiling faces and houses with crooked chimneys until he reached the center. There, drawn in jagged black crayon, was a figure with too many teeth and hollow eyes standing behind a small child. Elias’s breath hitched. It was Barnaby. The creature that had lived in his closet for seven years, the one who whispered secrets only he could hear. He looked up at his niece, who was waiting for approval, and forced the corners of his mouth upward while his hands clenched into fists under the table, nails digging into his palms hard enough to leave crescent moons in the skin.
