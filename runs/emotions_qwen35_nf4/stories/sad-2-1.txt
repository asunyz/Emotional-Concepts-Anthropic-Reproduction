I couldn’t look at the paper. Not because I didn’t want to see what Maya had drawn, but because if I did, I would have to admit that the ghost wasn’t just hers. It was mine. The living room smelled of lavender detergent and stale air, a scent that usually comforted me but now made my chest ache with a dull, throbbing pressure. I sat on the edge of the sofa, shoulders hunched forward as if trying to make myself smaller, less visible. Maya tugged at my sleeve, her small hand warm against my wrist. "Look," she insisted, shoving the notebook toward my lap. I kept my gaze fixed on the dust motes dancing in the shaft of afternoon light, avoiding the page. My heart hammered against my ribs, a frantic bird trapped in a cage. I took a shallow breath, holding it until my lungs burned, then let it out in a shaky exhale. "It’s very creative, sweetie," I said, my voice thin and brittle, cracking slightly on the last syllable before I stood up abruptly and walked away, leaving the book open on the cushion where the monster waited.
