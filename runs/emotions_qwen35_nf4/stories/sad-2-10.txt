Mark stared at the screen, the glow of the laptop illuminating the dark circles under his eyes. He hadn’t slept in two days. The email subject line read: *Your Daughter’s Art.* Attached was a photo of a coloring page. The colors were vibrant—bright yellows, electric blues—but the central figure was rendered in stark black. It was the same creature that had followed him home from school thirty years ago, the one he had convinced himself never existed. Mark pushed his chair back, the wheels screeching against the tile floor. He stood up, knocking his coffee mug onto the floor. Brown liquid spread across the linoleum, dripping onto his socks. He didn’t bend down to clean it. He just stood there, staring at the puddle, his reflection warped in the spreading stain. His hands hung limp at his sides, fingers curled inward, trembling uncontrollably. He turned off the computer, plunging the room into darkness, and slid down the wall until he hit the floor, pulling his knees to his chest, rocking slightly back and forth.
