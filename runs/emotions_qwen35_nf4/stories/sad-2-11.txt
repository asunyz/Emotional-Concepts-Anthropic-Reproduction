The tea bag steeped in the cup, releasing clouds of chamomile into the hot water, but Lily didn’t drink it. She watched the steam rise, twisting and dissipating like smoke. On the table lay the drawing, propped up against a vase. It showed a girl sitting on a bed, reading a book, while a large, shapeless mass loomed behind her, composed entirely of scribbles and cross-hatching. Lily reached out, her fingers brushing the edge of the paper. She recoiled instantly, pulling her hand back to her chest as if burned. She wrapped her arms around herself, squeezing tightly, creating a barrier between her and the object on the table. The room felt too large, the walls receding into an infinite distance. She looked at the clock on the wall, noting the second hand ticking forward, each tick echoing in her ears. She needed to call someone, but her phone remained untouched on the side table, unreachable. She sat perfectly still, her breath shallow, waiting for the urge to scream to pass, knowing it would take hours to fade.
