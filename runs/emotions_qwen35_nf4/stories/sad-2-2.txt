The rain lashed against the windowpane, blurring the world outside into a gray smear that matched the fog in Arthur’s mind. He sat by the fireplace, the warmth doing little to penetrate the chill settling deep in his bones. His nephew, Leo, bounded into the room, waving a piece of construction paper like a flag. Arthur flinched when Leo slapped the drawing onto his knee. The image was crude, done in thick markers: a tall, spindly thing with long fingers reaching out from a dark corner of a bedroom. Arthur’s pen rolled off the table, clattering loudly on the hardwood floor. He didn’t move to pick it up. Instead, he stared at the ink stains on his fingertips, rubbing them together frantically as if trying to wipe away the memory of that specific shade of blue. The room felt suddenly suffocating, the air too thick to breathe. He swallowed hard, the lump in his throat making it difficult to speak, and simply nodded once, sharply, before turning his head toward the fire, watching the flames dance without really seeing them.
