Clara paced the length of the hallway, her bare feet silent on the rug, stopping every few steps to wring her hands together until the knuckles turned white. She had promised herself she wouldn’t cry today; she had promised herself she would be strong for everyone else. But when her sister-in-law showed her the toddler’s latest masterpiece, the promise shattered. The drawing depicted a man with no face, sitting in the corner of a playroom, weeping tears that were actually stars. Clara stopped pacing. Her legs gave way, and she sank onto the bottom step of the stairs, pulling her knees to her chest. She rested her forehead against her shin, closing her eyes tightly as a single tear escaped, tracing a hot path down her cheek. She didn’t wipe it away. She just sat there, breathing in short, ragged gasps, listening to the muffled sounds of life continuing around her, feeling utterly disconnected from everything except the weight of the memory that had just been dragged back into the light.
