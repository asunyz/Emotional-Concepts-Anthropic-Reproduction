Sarah folded the laundry with mechanical precision, folding socks into perfect squares, shirts into neat rectangles. Each movement was deliberate, controlled, a way to keep her hands busy so they wouldn’t reach out for something that wasn’t there. The baby monitor crackled, and she jumped, dropping a pair of pajamas. From the other room, her niece called out, "Aunt Sarah! Come see!" Sarah hesitated, her foot hovering over the next pile of clothes. She knew what was coming. She moved slowly, dragging her feet across the carpet, each step feeling heavier than the last. When she entered the playroom, the girl held up a crayon drawing of a dog with human eyes and a grin that stretched too wide. Sarah’s smile froze, stiff and unnatural. She looked at the picture, then at the empty space in the corner of the room where the shadows seemed to deepen. She forced a laugh, high-pitched and strained, and backed out of the room, closing the door softly behind her, leaning against it until her legs stopped trembling.
