I kept the drawing taped to the inside of my coat pocket, right against my heart. It was a small square of paper, crinkled and worn. Every time I thought about taking it out, my fingers would curl inward, refusing to obey. We were at the park, the swings creaking in the wind, children laughing in a way that sounded like glass breaking to my ears. My brother pointed at a bench where our niece was sketching. "She’s showing you her art," he said, smiling. I shook my head, looking down at my shoes, scuffing the toe of my sneaker against the pavement. "Not here," I muttered. The words tasted like ash. I wanted to run, to hide in the car, to lock the doors and drive until the city lights faded. Instead, I stayed rooted to the spot, my shoulders hunched up toward my ears, protecting my neck, watching the wind blow leaves across the grass in patterns that looked suspiciously like grasping hands.
