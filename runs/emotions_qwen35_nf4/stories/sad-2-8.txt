The gallery opening was loud, filled with the clinking of champagne glasses and low murmurs of polite conversation. Julian stood near the entrance, holding his drink loosely, the ice cubes clinking against the glass in a rhythmic tap-tap-tap that mirrored the pounding in his temples. His niece approached him, holding out a print of her original work. It was a charcoal sketch of a figure standing in the doorway of a nursery, its face obscured by a mask of feathers. Julian’s grip on the glass tightened until his fingers turned pale. He looked at the print, then at his niece’s expectant face, then at the crowded room. The noise seemed to amplify, swirling around him like a vortex. He took a sip of his drink, wincing at the burn of the alcohol, and set the glass down on a passing waiter’s tray without drinking another drop. He turned his body away from her, feigning interest in a painting across the room, his reflection in the glass distorted and fractured.
