Rain tapped against the roof of the abandoned cabin, a steady, hypnotic rhythm that usually helped Elena sleep. Tonight, it only served to highlight the silence of the rest of the house. She sat on the floor, back against the bed frame, knees pulled up. In front of her lay the drawing her niece had mailed. It was simple, childish, yet undeniably accurate: a tall, thin man with elongated limbs, standing in the corner of a room that looked exactly like Elena’s childhood bedroom. Elena picked up the paper, her thumb stroking the rough texture of the cardboard backing repeatedly, over and over again. The motion was soothing, a self-soothing gesture she hadn’t used since she was twelve. She bit her lower lip until she tasted copper, forcing herself to breathe slowly, deeply. She placed the drawing back on the floor, not tearing it up, not hiding it, just leaving it there in the dim light, a silent witness to the fear that had returned, quiet and persistent as the rain.
