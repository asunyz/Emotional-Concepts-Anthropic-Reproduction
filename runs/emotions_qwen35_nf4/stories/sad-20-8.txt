Lena sat on the park bench, watching the leaves fall from the oak trees. Each leaf detached slowly, spiraling down to the ground, joining the others in a pile of decay. She felt the same way—detached, falling apart. A child ran past her, chasing a ball, laughing loudly. Lena flinched at the sound. The child returned, breathing hard, holding out a perfectly round stone painted with a smiley face. "For you," the child said, placing it in her lap. "My mom says ugly stones are just pretty rocks waiting to be noticed." Lena looked at the stone, the paint chipped but vibrant. She closed her fingers around it, the hard surface biting into her skin. The laughter of the child faded, but the stone remained, a small, solid weight in her hand.
