The letter arrived on a Tuesday, indistinguishable from the utility bills and flyers stacked on the mat. Mara stepped over it, intending to ignore it, but something made her pause. She picked it up, recognizing the distinctive scrawl of her college roommate. The message was brief, an apology for abandoning her during the crisis years ago. Mara stood in the hallway, the doormat rough against her socks. She felt a lump form in her throat, a physical obstruction that made swallowing difficult. She walked into her living room, sat on the couch, and stared at the blank television screen. Her hand reached out to touch the remote, then pulled back. She curled inward, pulling her knees to her chest, wrapping her arms tightly around herself as if trying to hold her pieces together.
