Elias stared at the fluorescent lights humming above his cubicle, the buzz sounding like a hive of angry bees trapped in the ceiling tiles. He rubbed his temples, trying to massage away the dull throb that had settled behind his eyes three days ago. When Sarah from accounting walked by, she didn’t offer her usual bright smile; instead, she kept her head down, clutching her coffee mug with white-knuckled grip. The office felt hollow, stripped of color, much like the faded photograph he pulled from his desk drawer. It showed two children standing in front of a rustic cabin, their faces smeared with chocolate cake and mud. He traced the edge of the photo with his thumb, remembering the smell of pine needles and woodsmoke, a scent that now seemed impossibly distant. A heavy weight pressed against his chest, making each breath feel laborious, as if the air itself were too thick to breathe comfortably.
