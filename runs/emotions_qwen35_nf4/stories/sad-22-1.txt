"I think we’re on the same page," Marcus said, sliding a folder across the polished conference table. His voice was flat, devoid of its usual energetic cadence. I nodded slowly, watching dust motes dance in the shafts of sunlight cutting through the blinds. "Camp Silver Lake?" I asked, the words tasting like ash in my mouth. Marcus froze, his pen hovering over the paper. For a moment, the only sound was the ticking of the wall clock. Then, a faint, nostalgic smile touched his lips, softening the sharp lines of his face. "Yeah," he whispered. "I spent every July there for six years." I looked down at my hands, twisting the ring on my finger until the metal bit into my skin. The memory of canoeing on still waters suddenly felt like a dream from another lifetime, one I couldn't quite reach anymore.
