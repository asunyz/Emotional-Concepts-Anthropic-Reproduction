Rain tapped a rhythmic pattern against the roof of the café, matching the tempo of my tapping foot. I stirred my tea long after the sugar had dissolved, creating a vortex that mirrored the confusion in my head. Across the table, David sipped his espresso, watching me with mild curiosity. "You’ve been quiet all morning," he noted. I looked up, meeting his gaze. "I’m just processing some information," I replied, choosing my words carefully. "Did you ever go to Camp Pinecone?" David choked on his drink, coughing into his napkin. "What? Yes! I was there for three summers. Why do you ask?" I gripped the spoon tightly, the metal bending under the pressure. "Because I was there too," I said, my voice dropping to a whisper. "And I was the one who lost your favorite compass." David stared at me, shock warring with recognition on his face. I looked down at the table, unable to bear the weight of his stare. The air between us grew heavy, charged with unspoken history and regret.
