The printer jammed again, spewing out crumpled pages onto the floor. Arthur kicked the machine in frustration, sending a shower of toner cartridges scattering. He sank onto the floor, burying his face in his hands. The noise of the office continued around him, indifferent to his distress. Martha approached cautiously, holding a box of tissues. "Rough one?" she asked softly. Arthur peeked through his fingers. "Everything feels pointless," he admitted, his voice cracking. Martha sat beside him, not touching him, just offering her presence. "Sometimes I miss the simplicity of Camp Lakeside," she said, gazing out the window. "Just swimming and singing songs around the fire." Arthur let out a dry chuckle. "I went there in ’85. I think I was the one who started the tradition of the midnight marshmallow roast." Martha gasped, turning to look at him with wide eyes. "You’re kidding." Arthur shook his head, a single tear escaping to trace a path down his cheek. "Nope. Just... glad to know someone else remembers the taste of smoke and sugar." He wiped his face quickly, standing up and brushing off his pants, leaving the mess on the floor for later.
