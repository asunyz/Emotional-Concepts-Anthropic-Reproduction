The breakroom smelled of burnt coffee and stale donuts. James stood by the sink, scrubbing at a stubborn stain on his shirt cuff, though the fabric was already clean. He watched his reflection in the microwave door—tired eyes, slack jaw, a man eroding under the weight of routine. Priya entered, humming a tune that sounded vaguely familiar, something old and folk-inspired. "You know that song?" James asked, pausing mid-scrub. Priya stopped humming, tilting her head. "My grandmother used to sing it while we packed our tents. We were at Camp Oakridge together." James felt a sudden lump form in his throat, blocking his speech. He turned back to the sink, gripping the porcelain edge until his knuckles turned pale. The mundane task of washing dishes became an anchor, keeping him from floating away into the memories of long summer nights and crackling fires.
