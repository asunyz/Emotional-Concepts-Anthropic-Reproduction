"Hey, did you finish the quarterly report?" Kevin called out, his voice echoing off the sterile white walls. I didn’t answer immediately. I was staring at the poster on the wall—a generic landscape painting that reminded me of the view from the dock at Camp Clearwater. The colors were muted, washed out, mirroring the fog rolling in behind me. Kevin waved a hand in front of my face. "Earth to Alex? You okay?" I blinked, forcing myself to focus on his concerned expression. "Sorry," I croaked. "Just... thinking." "About what?" he pressed. "Summer camps," I admitted, the confession slipping out before I could check it. Kevin raised an eyebrow. "Which one?" I swallowed hard, looking past him toward the window where the sky was turning a bruised purple. "Pinecrest. Where we learned how to tie knots and build shelters." The mention of shelter made me shiver, despite the warm office temperature. I wanted nothing more than to hide inside a small, dark tent and wait for the world to pass me by.
