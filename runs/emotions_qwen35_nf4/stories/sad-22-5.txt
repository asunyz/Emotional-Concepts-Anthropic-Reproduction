Rachel’s pen snapped in her hand, ink bleeding across the contract she was reviewing. She sighed, a long, ragged exhale that seemed to deflate her entire posture. Mark walked over, picking up the broken plastic pieces with careful precision. "Rough day?" he asked, his tone low and steady. Rachel shrugged, avoiding his gaze. "Just tired." Mark chuckled softly. "Tired of spreadsheets? Or tired of being here?" She finally looked up, her eyes glassy. "Actually, I was just remembering Camp Sunflower. I used to be the camp counselor for the youngest kids." Mark’s eyebrows shot up. "No way. I was there in ’98. I think I sat next to you during craft hour." Rachel let out a weak laugh that died quickly in her throat. She rested her forehead against the cool surface of the desk, closing her eyes tightly. The memory of the sun on her face felt like a luxury she no longer deserved.
