The phone rang, shrill and intrusive, breaking the quiet of the late afternoon. Elena picked it up, her movements sluggish. "Hello?" she answered, her voice raspy. On the other end, a client discussed logistics, but Elena barely heard the words. Her mind wandered to the wooden sign she had seen in a documentary last night: *Camp Echo Valley*. She remembered the echo test, shouting into the canyon and waiting for the return. It always came back weaker, distorted. "Are you listening?" the client demanded. Elena blinked, refocusing. "Yes," she lied. After hanging up, she slumped forward, resting her elbows on the desk. Her coworker, Greg, poked his head out. "You look wrecked, El." She offered a hollow smile. "Just thinking about summer camp," she mumbled. Greg paused. "Wait, you went to Camp Echo? My dad owned it for ten years." Elena felt a surge of grief so intense it made her dizzy. She pushed her chair back abruptly, claiming she needed to stretch, and fled to the restroom, locking herself in a stall to breathe without being heard.
