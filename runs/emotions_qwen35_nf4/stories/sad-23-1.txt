The rain had been falling for three days straight, mirroring the gray wash over Mara’s face. She picked up the envelope tucked behind the bus seat, her eyes widening as she saw the crisp edges of currency peeking out. A stranger’s fortune. She stood there, soaked to the bone, holding the paper against her chest. Her breath hitched, shallow and rapid, while she watched other commuters rush by, dry and hurried. She wanted to call out, to scream, but her throat felt constricted by invisible hands. She slipped the envelope into her bag and walked home, head down, shoulders hunched against the wind.
