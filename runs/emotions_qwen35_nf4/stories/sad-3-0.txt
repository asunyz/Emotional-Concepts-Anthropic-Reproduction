Elias sat at his kitchen table, the morning light slicing through the blinds in harsh, parallel lines that matched the tension in his jaw. He held the leather-bound book as if it were made of hot coals, his fingers trembling against the embossed title: *The Life of Elias Thorne*. He turned the page, stopping at Chapter Four, where the author described his divorce not as a mutual drifting apart, but as a calculated maneuver to secure assets. His breath hitched, a shallow, ragged sound that seemed too loud in the empty room. He looked down at his hands, watching the knuckles whiten as he gripped the cover tighter, until the skin stretched taut and pale. There was no anger, only a hollow ache behind his eyes, a heavy weight pressing down on his chest that made every inhalation feel like lifting a stone. He closed the book slowly, placing it face down, and stared at the grain of the wood, unable to meet his own reflection in the darkened window across from him.
