I found the manuscript tucked inside my winter coat pocket, forgotten by the dry cleaner who had apparently been hired by someone I once trusted. The paper was crisp, smelling faintly of ink and old libraries. As I read about the night I lost my mother, the words twisted the memory into something grotesque—a scene of indifference rather than grief. My throat tightened, closing off the airway just enough to make me dizzy. I walked to the bathroom sink, splashing cold water onto my face, but the dampness couldn’t wash away the sting in my eyes. When I looked up, the mirror showed a stranger with tired eyes and a mouth set in a grim line. I didn’t cry; tears felt like an admission of defeat, and I wasn’t ready for that yet. Instead, I stood there for twenty minutes, listening to the drip of the faucet, counting the seconds, wondering how many other parts of my life had been edited before they ever reached me.
