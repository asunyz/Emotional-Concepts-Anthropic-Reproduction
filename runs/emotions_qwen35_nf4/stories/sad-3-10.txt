The manuscript was thin, barely fifty pages, but it contained enough lies to fill a lifetime. Maya read the description of her father’s death: peaceful, surrounded by loved ones. She remembered the hospital beeps, the sterile smell, the absence of family. The omission of her presence was a knife twist in her gut. She curled up on the rug, hugging a pillow to her chest. The fabric was soft, offering a contrast to the hardness of the truth she now faced. She buried her face in the pillow, muffling a sob that threatened to escape. Her shoulders shook, violent tremors that racked her entire frame. She stayed there for hours, motionless except for the rhythmic rising and falling of her back. The sun moved across the floor, shifting the shadows, marking the passage of time she wished could stop.
