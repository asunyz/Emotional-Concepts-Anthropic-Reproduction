Jack tossed the book into the trash can, but then retrieved it. He couldn’t let it go. The bio called him resilient, bouncing back from failure with grace. He remembered the nights spent staring at the ceiling, paralyzed by doubt. The label felt like a cage, restricting his ability to feel the full weight of his struggles. He sat on the edge of his bed, the mattress dipping under his weight. He ran a hand over his face, rubbing his eyes vigorously, trying to clear the fog. His lips pressed into a thin, white line. He thought about writing a rebuttal, a correction, but the effort seemed insurmountable. He laid the book on the nightstand, next to a stack of unpaid bills. He turned off the lamp, plunging the room into darkness, lying still in the quiet, listening to the blood rushing in his ears, a reminder that he was alive, even if his story was not his own.
