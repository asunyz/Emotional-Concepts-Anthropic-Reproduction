She traced the edge of the page with her index finger, the paper rough and textured. The biography claimed she had always been brave, leading protests and speaking truth to power. In reality, she had stayed silent for decades, terrified of retaliation. The discrepancy felt like a physical blow to the solar plexus, knocking the wind out of her. She sank onto the couch, pulling her knees to her chest, wrapping her arms tightly around her shins. The room felt smaller, the walls closing in, suffocating her. She bit the inside of her cheek, hard enough to taste copper, grounding herself in the sharp pain because it was real, unlike the narrative spread out before her. She wanted to burn the book, to incinerate the false version of herself, but her hands lacked the strength. She just sat there, breathing in short, controlled bursts, waiting for the urge to move to pass, knowing it wouldn’t.
