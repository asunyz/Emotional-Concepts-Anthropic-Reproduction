David picked up the phone, dialing the number listed on the copyright page. It rang four times before a cheerful voice answered. "Thank you for calling Biography House," the receptionist chirped. David opened his mouth to speak, but no sound came out. He hung up. He walked to the refrigerator, opening it to stare at the half-empty jar of mustard and a wilted bunch of spinach. The fluorescent light buzzed overhead, a low, irritating drone that seemed to vibrate in his teeth. He closed the door and leaned his back against it, sliding down until he hit the floor. He rested his chin on his knees, staring at the linoleum patterns. The world continued to spin outside, cars honking, birds singing, but inside, everything had stopped. He felt transparent, exposed, as if the book had peeled back his skin to reveal the messy, unorganized interior he had tried so hard to hide.
