The novel lay open on the park bench, ignored by everyone else. Elena watched a pigeon peck at a discarded crust near her shoe. The book described her childhood home as a place of warmth and laughter. She remembered the shouting matches, the slammed doors, the silence that followed. The erasure of that trauma felt like a violation worse than the act itself. She felt a lump form in her throat, hard and painful. She swallowed repeatedly, trying to dislodge it, but it remained. Her vision blurred, not with tears, but with a sudden onset of dizziness. She closed the book gently, treating it like a wounded animal, and placed it in her bag. She zipped the bag shut, securing the secret away, and began to walk home, her steps slower than usual, her gaze fixed on the pavement, avoiding eye contact with anyone who might ask why she looked so fractured.
