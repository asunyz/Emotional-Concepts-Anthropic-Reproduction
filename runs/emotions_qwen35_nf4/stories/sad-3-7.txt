He sat in the corner booth of the diner, the vinyl seat cracked and peeling under his palms. The biography was resting on the table, its cover stained with a ring from a coffee cup. It portrayed him as a charismatic leader, inspiring loyalty and awe. He remembered the fear in his employees' eyes, the way they avoided his gaze. The disconnect between the image and the man created a dissonance that made his ears ring. He stirred his black coffee, the spoon clinking rhythmically against the ceramic, a metronome for his racing thoughts. He took a sip, burning his tongue, but welcomed the pain. It reminded him he was still here, still feeling. He looked out the window at the passing traffic, the lights streaking by in long, blurry lines. He felt small, insignificant, reduced to a collection of facts and fictions written by someone who had never truly known him.
