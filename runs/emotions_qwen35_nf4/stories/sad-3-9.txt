Arthur flipped through the pages, skipping the early chapters, going straight to the section on his retirement. It described his departure from the company as a triumphant farewell, a legacy secured. He remembered the forced resignation, the humiliating meeting with HR, the shame of packing his box. The rewrite felt like a slap in the face. He stood up abruptly, knocking his chair back with a screech that drew looks from nearby diners. He ignored them, walking to the counter, ordering a glass of water. He drank it slowly, letting the liquid soothe his dry throat. When he returned to the table, he folded the book neatly, creasing the corners sharply. He slipped it into his briefcase, locking the latch with a decisive click. He walked out into the busy street, merging with the crowd, feeling invisible, a ghost haunting his own history.
