Martha noticed the new scaffolding rising beside Mr. Henderson’s house before she noticed the gray in her own reflection. She stood by her sink, gripping the edge of the counter until her knuckles turned white. Every scrape of metal on siding sent a jolt through her teeth. She tried to hum a tune, something light and airy, but her throat closed up, tightening like a fist. The coffee in her cup had gone cold hours ago; she lifted it to her lips anyway, taking a sip of bitterness because it matched the flavor of the morning. Outside, a drill whined high and sharp, slicing through the quiet afternoon like a knife.
