Elias stood before the glass case, his breath fogging the cold surface until he wiped it away with a trembling hand. Inside lay a silver band, simple and worn, resting on a bed of black velvet that seemed to swallow the light around it. He hadn’t planned to stop here; the walk was meant to clear his head, to keep moving forward while his chest felt heavy enough to crush his ribs. But when his eyes landed on that specific engraving—a tiny, almost invisible rosebud near the inner rim—he froze. His fingers hovered over the price tag, the numbers blurring as a lump formed in his throat, tight and painful. He didn’t buy it immediately. Instead, he just stared, remembering the way her laugh used to crinkle the corners of her eyes, a sound that now felt miles away, echoing in an empty house where the silence screamed louder than any noise ever could.
