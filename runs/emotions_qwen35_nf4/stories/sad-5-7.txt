The clerk counted the money again, his fingers rhythmic and precise. Amara watched him, her vision swimming. In her hand, she held a sapphire pendant that had been her mother’s pride and joy. Selling it felt like cutting off a limb. The shop was crowded, the noise overwhelming, making her feel isolated in the chaos. She looked at the pendant, then at the cash, then back at the pendant. Her lip quivered, betraying the stoic mask she tried to maintain. She thought of the bills piling up on the kitchen table, the eviction notice taped to the door. The weight of responsibility crushed her spirit, heavier than the gemstone in her palm. She shoved the money into her bag, zipping it shut with a sharp tug, and walked out into the gray afternoon, her steps heavy and uncoordinated, as if her legs no longer obeyed her commands.
