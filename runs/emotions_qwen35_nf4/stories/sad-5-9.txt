Sarah sat on the curb outside the shop, the city rushing by in a blur of gray and steel. She had gone in to check if they had her sister’s locket. They did. It was lying on a tray, tarnished and dull. She had spent months searching thrift stores, flea markets, and online auctions, driven by a desperate need to recover a piece of her childhood. Holding it now, she felt nothing but a hollow ache expanding in her chest. She ran her thumb over the hinge, opening and closing it repeatedly, a nervous tic she had developed since the accident. Her eyes were red-rimmed and swollen, stinging from lack of sleep. She didn’t cry anymore; the tears had dried up days ago, leaving only a raw, burning sensation in her throat. She put the locket in her pocket and stood up, her knees cracking, ready to face another day of emptiness.
