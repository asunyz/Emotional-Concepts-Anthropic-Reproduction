I stared at the ceiling fan spinning above my bed, counting the rotations. One, two, three. Each revolution felt like a year slipping away. The offer letter wasn’t there. There was only the empty space where hope used to sit. I rolled over, pulling the duvet up to my chin, burying my nose in the fabric. It smelled like laundry detergent and stale sweat, a scent that usually comforted me but now felt suffocating. I squeezed my eyes shut, squeezing tight until stars exploded in my vision. If I couldn’t see the reality, maybe it wouldn’t exist. For a brief second, in the dark behind my eyelids, I was still accepted. Then the sun rose, and the illusion shattered.
