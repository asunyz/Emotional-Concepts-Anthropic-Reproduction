Elias stared at the measuring tape coiled on his lawn like a sleeping snake. His hands trembled slightly as he unspooled it, the metal links clicking against the pavement with a rhythm that felt too loud for the quiet afternoon. He wiped his forehead with the back of his wrist, leaving a smear of dust across his skin, and looked up at the house next door where the new post holes were already dug—dark, empty eyes staring back at him. The air smelled of wet earth and impending separation. He took a deep breath, holding it until his lungs burned, then exhaled slowly, watching his breath vanish into the gray sky. There was no anger, only a hollow ache behind his ribs that grew heavier with every inch of wood stacked by the driveway.
