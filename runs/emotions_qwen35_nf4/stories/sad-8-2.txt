Martha held the blueprint tightly, her knuckles white against the crisp paper. She walked the perimeter of her property three times, her boots scuffing the grass, checking angles that didn’t need checking. The sun beat down on her neck, hot and relentless, mirroring the pressure building in her chest. She stopped at the corner where the old oak tree stood, its branches reaching toward the neighboring yard—a space she would soon be barred from viewing. She reached out to touch the rough bark, her fingers lingering there, cold despite the heat. A single tear tracked through the dust on her cheek, unnoticed by anyone but the wind. She folded the blueprints neatly, tucked them into her bag, and walked inside, closing the door softly behind her.
