The noise of the jackhammer was deafening, yet Arthur heard nothing but the ringing in his ears. He covered his ears with both hands, squeezing his eyes shut, but the vibration traveled through the floorboards, up his legs, and into his bones. He paced the length of his living room, three steps forward, three steps back, a repetitive motion born of anxiety. The fence was going up faster than expected. By noon, the neighbor’s yard would be hidden from view. He stopped pacing and leaned against the wall, sliding down until he hit the floor. He rested his forehead against his knees, breathing in short, shallow gasps. The world outside continued its busy pace, indifferent to the shrinking horizon of his personal space.
