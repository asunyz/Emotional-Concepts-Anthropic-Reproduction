Elias stared at the peeling paint on the ceiling fan, counting the rotations until his eyes burned. The cardboard box by the door felt heavier than it had an hour ago, its contents a graveyard of failed ambitions and unpaid bills. He picked up a framed photo from the nightstand—his graduation day, smiles wide, future bright—and turned it face down. His mother’s voice drifted from the kitchen, cheerful and loud, asking if he wanted tea. Elias didn’t answer; he just pulled the duvet over his head, creating a dark, suffocating cocoon where the sound of her humming faded into a distant, muffled hum. He lay there, listening to the clock tick, marking time that seemed to stretch endlessly without purpose.
