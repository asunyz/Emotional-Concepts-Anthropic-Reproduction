David packed another box, folding shirts with military precision, though his hands trembled slightly. The apartment was empty save for the echo of his movements and the weight of the key in his pocket. His father stood in the doorway, arms crossed, leaning against the frame as if holding himself up. They hadn’t spoken since David handed over the lease agreement. The air between them was thick, unbreathing. Finally, David zipped the last suitcase and looked up. His father offered a nod, stiff and formal. David grabbed the handle, lifted the case—it was lighter than he remembered—and walked out, leaving the door ajar, letting the draft whisper through the house one last time.
