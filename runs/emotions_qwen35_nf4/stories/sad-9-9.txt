James carried his laundry basket upstairs, the fabric smelling faintly of detergent and regret. His mother was vacuuming the hallway, the machine roaring loudly, drowning out any potential conversation. He waited for her to finish, standing awkwardly by the railing. Once the noise ceased, he cleared his throat. She paused, wiping sweat from her brow with the back of her hand. "Dinner will be ready soon," she said, her voice flat, devoid of the warmth he used to know. James nodded, ascending the final steps. In his room, he dropped the basket and sat on the edge of the bed, picking at a loose thread on the mattress cover, unraveling it slowly, strand by strand, until his fingers were raw.
