Marcus slammed the door of his office, locking it behind him. On his desk lay the contract signed by the corporation he had consulted for. Clause 4B stated that all intellectual property developed during the consultation period belonged to the company. But he had developed those designs months before the contract was ever drafted. He had documented everything, dated emails, saved drafts. Yet, looking at the sleek, polished product they were releasing, he realized they had ignored his proof. He paced the room, kicking the trash can, sending papers flying everywhere. He ran his hands through his hair, pulling at the strands, feeling the tension radiating from his shoulders down to his fingertips. He pulled out his laptop, typing furiously, drafting a cease-and-desist letter, then deleting it, then rewriting it again. His mind spun, caught in a loop of disbelief and fury, unable to process how easily his life’s work had been erased.
