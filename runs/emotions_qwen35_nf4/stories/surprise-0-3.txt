Lena stopped dead in front of the gallery window, her reflection ghostly against the glass. Inside, framed behind bulletproof acrylic, hung the painting *Silent Echo*. It was the centerpiece of the exhibition, labeled as the work of a reclusive master she had never heard of. But the brushstrokes—the specific, jagged way she dragged the palette knife across the canvas—were hers. They were signatures of her struggle, her hesitation, her joy. She pressed her forehead against the cool pane, fogging the surface with her breath. Her pulse roared in her ears, a loud, rushing sound that made it hard to think. She looked left, then right, checking for security guards, for other viewers who might notice the plagiarism. No one else seemed to care; they admired the piece with polite nods. Lena slid down the wall until she was crouching on the sidewalk, knees drawn to her chest, watching people admire her soul as if it were a commodity.
