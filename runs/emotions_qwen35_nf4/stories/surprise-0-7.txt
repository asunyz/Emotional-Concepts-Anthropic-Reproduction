Nia touched the sleeve of her jacket, feeling the raised texture of the embroidery. It was a patch she had designed herself, featuring a stylized wolf howling at a crescent moon. Seeing it worn by a teenager on the bus was jarring enough; seeing the same design mass-produced on a thousand hoodies in the store window nearby was devastating. She pushed past the mannequins, her eyes scanning the price tags. Hundreds of dollars for her concept, printed on cheap cotton. She felt lightheaded, the air in the mall seeming too thick to breathe. She leaned against a display rack, gripping the metal bar until the edges dug into her palms. Her mind raced through possibilities—who stole the file? Who sold it? Why didn't anyone stop them? She closed her eyes, taking slow, deliberate breaths, forcing herself to stand upright, to walk out of the store, to pretend her identity hadn't just been commodified and discarded.
