David laughed, a short, brittle sound that echoed too loudly in the empty living room. "You can't be serious," he said, though his hand trembled as he reached for the remote control, fumbling with the buttons before setting it down hard. His father looked up from his newspaper, eyes bright with a fervor David had never associated with him. The older man described the new rituals, the specific prayers, the way the light hit the altar during dawn services. David felt a knot form in his throat, tight and sour. He shifted in his seat, crossing his legs tightly, then uncrossing them, unable to find a comfortable position. The clock on the wall ticked with aggressive precision, marking seconds that felt longer than usual as his father explained why the old traditions no longer served his soul.
