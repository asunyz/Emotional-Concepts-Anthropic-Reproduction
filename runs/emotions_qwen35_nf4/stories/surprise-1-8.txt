Aunt Clara handed me a pamphlet printed on glossy paper, its pages filled with images of serene landscapes and smiling monks. "This is where I belong now," she said, her voice trembling with excitement. I held the brochure as if it were contaminated, keeping it at arm's length. My pulse thrummed in my temples, a rhythmic pounding that drowned out her explanation of the daily schedule. I nodded along, pretending to understand the nuances of their meditation techniques, while internally grappling with the image of her sitting cross-legged on a cold floor instead of leading the holiday dinner. The smell of her perfume, usually warm and floral, now seemed cloying and strange. I excused myself to the bathroom, locking the door and leaning against it, closing my eyes to block out the world that had just shifted irreversibly beneath my feet.
