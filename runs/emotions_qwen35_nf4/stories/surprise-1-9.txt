My son told me over video call, his face framed by the soft glow of a candle he had lit specifically for the conversation. He spoke of finding a higher purpose beyond material success. I gripped the phone tighter, the plastic creaking under the pressure. My other hand instinctively went to my pocket, seeking the comfort of my car keys, but they weren’t there. The background noise of his new apartment—wind chimes, faint chanting—felt intrusive, wrong. I tried to focus on his features, the shape of his nose, the curve of his smile, but they seemed distorted, belonging to someone else. I forced a laugh, joking about the cost of candles, but the joke fell flat, hanging in the digital air between us. He didn’t laugh back, which made the silence stretch until it became unbearable.
