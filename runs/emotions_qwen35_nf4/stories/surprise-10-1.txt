I dropped my keys. They hit the floor with a sharp clatter, rolling under the filing cabinet I’d organized myself five years ago. My boss, Marcus, didn’t flinch. He just pointed to the chair across from him, where a young woman with bright glasses and a crisp blazer was already typing on a laptop. "This is Chloe," Marcus said, his tone brisk, devoid of the warmth we usually shared during our Friday beers. "She’s learning the workflow so you can focus on the exit strategy." My breath hitched, a jagged thing in my chest. I looked at Chloe, who offered a cheerful wave, completely unaware—or perhaps uncaring—of the hollow ache expanding in my ribs. I reached for my keys again, my hands trembling visibly enough that I had to grip the edge of the table to steady them.
