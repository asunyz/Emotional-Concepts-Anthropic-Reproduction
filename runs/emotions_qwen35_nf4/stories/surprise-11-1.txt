I dropped my baton. It clattered across the polished floor, rolling toward the water cooler, but I didn’t move to retrieve it. My eyes were locked on the whiteboard where Sarah had just erased my name from the anchor position and written hers in its place. "Why?" The word scraped out of my throat, rougher than intended. Sarah shrugged, her expression unreadable behind her sunglasses indoors. "Your split times are better when you’re pushing, not finishing." I felt a cold prickle crawl up the back of my neck, my stomach twisting into a tight knot. The other swimmers continued their drills, splashing around us like oblivious fish, unaware that the foundation of my season had just shifted beneath my feet.
