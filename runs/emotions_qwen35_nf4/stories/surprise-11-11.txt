Raj put down the racket. The strings hummed faintly in the quiet club. "Play doubles partner," the tournament director said, sliding a registration form across the table. Raj frowned, picking it up. Doubles? He preferred singles. The isolation, the self-reliance. With a partner, there was reliance, compromise, shared blame. He looked at the empty chair opposite him, imagining a teammate who might miss a volley, who might miscommunicate. His jaw clenched, teeth grinding together. He signed the form mechanically, the scratch of the pen sounding loud in the hushed room. He rolled his shoulders, feeling the unfamiliar weight of expectation settle on both of them, not just his.
