Marcus gripped the steering wheel until his knuckles turned the color of old parchment. He wasn't a driver; he was an engineer. That was the deal. That was what he signed the contract for. "Get in the car, Marcus," the team principal barked, kicking the door open. Marcus shook his head, a jerky, involuntary motion. "There’s been a mistake." There was no mistake, the look in the principal’s eye screamed. It was a challenge, a test, a trap. Marcus swallowed hard, the lump in his throat making it difficult to breathe. He pushed himself out of the engineering bay, his legs feeling heavy, leaden, as if they belonged to someone else entirely.
