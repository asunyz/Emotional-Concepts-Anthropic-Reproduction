Derek pulled off his helmet, wiping sweat from his brow with a towel that suddenly felt too coarse. "You’re on the bench," the assistant coach muttered, walking past without breaking stride. Derek watched him go, the words hanging in the humid air of the gym. Bench? For twelve years, he had started every game. He looked at his shoes, laces untied, as if they held the answers. His shoulders slumped, the energy draining out of him like air from a punctured tire. He walked to the bleachers, sitting heavily on the hard plastic seat. The court below buzzed with activity, players shouting calls, but Derek heard none of it. He stared at his reflection in the darkened window, seeing a stranger wearing his jersey.
