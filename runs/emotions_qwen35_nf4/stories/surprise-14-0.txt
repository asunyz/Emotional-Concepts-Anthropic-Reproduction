Elias stared at the empty desk where Master Thorne had sat for thirty years. The chair was pushed back slightly, as if its occupant had just stepped away for coffee, yet the dust motes dancing in the afternoon sun suggested otherwise. His fingers trembled as they brushed against the cold leather of the seat, tracing the faint outline of a name stitched into the fabric. He looked around the workshop, expecting to see Thorne’s glasses perched on a workbench or his half-finished sketch lying open. Silence stretched heavy between the shelves of tools, broken only by the ticking of the wall clock. Elias felt a hollow ache expand in his chest, a sudden void where the familiar rhythm of instruction used to be. He reached for the phone on the wall, dialing the number he knew by heart, but the line rang out into nothingness before connecting.
