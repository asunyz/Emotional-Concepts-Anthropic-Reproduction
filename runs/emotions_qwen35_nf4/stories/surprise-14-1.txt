I stood frozen in the doorway of the studio, my hand still raised to knock. The easel was stripped bare, the canvas rolled up and stored in the corner. There was no note on the table, no chalkboard message scrawled with Thorne’s distinctive slant. My breath hitched, catching in my throat as I took a step forward, then another, moving toward the center of the room as if searching for him in the shadows. The air smelled of turpentine and old paper, scents that usually signaled the beginning of a lesson. Now, they felt like echoes. I ran my hand along the edge of the table, feeling the smooth wood where his elbow always rested. A lump formed in my throat, tight and uncomfortable, forcing me to swallow hard against the rising pressure behind my eyes. Why leave? After all the hours spent correcting my grip, explaining color theory, staying late when I struggled? The question bounced around my skull, unanswered, leaving me standing alone amidst the silence of an abandoned workspace.
