Captain Reynolds sat at his desk, staring at the resignation letter propped up against the lamp. It was unsigned, just a date and a signature line that remained blank. He tapped his pen against the wood, the rhythmic tapping growing faster, more erratic. He had trained this officer for two years, instilling discipline, strategy, honor. To leave without notice, without a debriefing—it was insubordination, yes, but also a profound betrayal of trust. He stood up abruptly, knocking the pen to the floor. He didn’t bend to pick it up. Instead, he walked to the map on the wall, tracing the routes they had planned together. The lines seemed meaningless now, abstract scribbles without a leader to guide them. A flush of heat rose to his neck, frustration boiling over. He slammed his fist against the map, the paper tearing slightly. The noise startled him, but he didn’t care. The silence of the departed officer screamed louder than any explosion.
