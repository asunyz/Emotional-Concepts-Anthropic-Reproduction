The gallery opening was in full swing, champagne flutes clinking, laughter echoing off the high ceilings. Julian scanned the crowd, looking for Elena. She was supposed to be giving the speech in ten minutes. Five minutes passed. Then ten. He checked his watch, then his phone. No signal. He pushed through the crowd, asking guests if they had seen her. Shakes of heads. Furrowed brows. Confusion rippled through him, spreading from his chest to his fingertips. He went backstage, pushing past the crew, into the dressing room. Empty. Her gown lay draped over a chair, her shoes beside it. No note. No explanation. He touched the silk of the dress, the fabric cool and slippery under his fingers. A wave of nausea hit him, forcing him to lean against the wall for support. The spotlight outside burned bright, waiting for a performer who wouldn’t come. The anticipation curdled into anxiety, twisting his stomach into knots as he realized the show was going on without her.
