Detective Miller kicked the door open, expecting to find his partner arguing over a case file. Instead, he found a single key resting on the center of the desk, next to a neatly folded uniform shirt. No badge. No gun belt. Just the shirt, pressed and waiting. Miller’s jaw tightened, the muscles bunching under his skin as he picked up the shirt. It was too clean, too organized. He flipped through the drawers, finding them emptied of personal effects—photos, mugs, family pictures—all gone. The absence was loud, ringing in his ears louder than any siren. He dropped the shirt onto the floor, turning sharply to face the window. Outside, the city bustled on, indifferent to the fact that one of its most reliable anchors had simply vanished. He gripped the windowsill until his knuckles turned white, staring at the reflection of his own confused face, wondering what could possibly drive a man to abandon his post without a word.
