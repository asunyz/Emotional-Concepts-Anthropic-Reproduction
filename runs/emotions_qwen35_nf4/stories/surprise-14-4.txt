Jack paced the length of the garage, wiping grease from his hands onto a rag that was already black. The lift was down, the tools laid out in precise order on the bench. Usually, Old Man Henderson would be barking orders, pointing out a misaligned bolt or criticizing his technique. Today, the radio was off, the TV silent. Jack stopped pacing, gripping the edge of the workbench until his arms shook. He looked at the stool Henderson always occupied, now empty and slightly dusty. A bitter taste coated his tongue, acidic and sharp. He wanted to shout, to demand an explanation, but the words died in his throat, choked by a confusing mix of anger and loss. He kicked a tire, watching it roll across the concrete, the thud dull and unsatisfying. Where did he go? Who told him to stop? The questions swirled in his head, unanswerable, leaving him stranded in the quiet aftermath of a departure he never saw coming.
