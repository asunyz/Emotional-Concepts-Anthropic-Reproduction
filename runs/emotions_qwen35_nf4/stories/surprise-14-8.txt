Dr. Aris Thorne’s office door was ajar, a rare occurrence. Nurse Evans peeked in, expecting to find him reviewing charts. Instead, she found a single envelope taped to the monitor. She stepped inside, closing the door softly behind her. The stethoscope hung neatly on the hook. The patient files were organized, but the personal items—a photo of his dog, a worn copy of a medical journal—were missing. She opened the envelope, reading the short note. *Time to rest.* Evans’ brow furrowed deeply, confusion knitting her forehead. She looked around the room, trying to find a trace of hesitation, a sign of struggle. There was none. Only a calm, deliberate emptiness. She felt a strange unease settle in her stomach, a fluttering sensation that reminded her of butterflies in her gut. How does one simply walk away from decades of life-saving work? She placed the note on the desk, her movements slow and deliberate, as if afraid to disturb the peace of the sudden absence.
