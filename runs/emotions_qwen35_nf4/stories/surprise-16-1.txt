I dropped my napkin. It fluttered to the floor like a dying moth, but I didn’t bend to pick it up. My gaze was fixed on Sarah’s table, where Mark sat, looking older than I remembered, yet unmistakably himself. The blood rushed to my face, hot and prickly, making my skin feel tight. I gripped the edge of the buffet table, my nails digging into the wood, anchoring myself against the sudden wave of dizziness. Why was he here? How did he get invited? The question looped in my head, sharp and repetitive, drowning out the clinking of silverware and the low hum of conversation. I forced a smile onto my face, brittle and fake, and turned away just as he looked up, praying he wouldn’t notice the tremor in my hands.
