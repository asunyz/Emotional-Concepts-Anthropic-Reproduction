I watched my brother pack boxes into the trunk of his sedan, labeling them "Office Supplies." For five years, I thought he worked in HR at a multinational firm. Today, I saw him loading pallets of industrial cleaning fluid onto a flatbed truck. My hands flew to my mouth, stifling a gasp that threatened to escape. I took a step back, my heel clicking loudly on the pavement. "Cleaning supplies?" I asked, the question hanging awkwardly in the humid afternoon air. He shrugged, zipping up his jacket with unnecessary force. "The company rebranded," he said, too quickly. I blinked, trying to process the disconnect between the man I knew and the worker standing before me. A strange lightness filled my head, followed by a heavy weight in my limbs. I couldn't speak; I could only nod slowly, watching him drive away while my legs felt like they were made of lead.
