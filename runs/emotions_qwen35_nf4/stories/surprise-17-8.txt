Nate pulled the receipt from his pocket, the ink fading under the fluorescent lights of the parking garage. It listed items bought by his sister, Claire, at a high-end jewelry store. Yet, Claire had told him she was working night shifts at a call center to save for a car. Nate stopped walking, his keys dangling loosely from his hand. The metal links clicked together, a rhythmic tapping that matched his racing pulse. He turned back toward the entrance, his mind replaying every conversation they’d had about her budget struggles. "Jewelry?" he murmured, the word feeling foreign on his tongue. When he confronted her, she wouldn't meet his gaze, focusing intently on her fingernails. Nate felt a wave of nausea roll through him, forcing him to lean against the cold concrete pillar for support. The ground felt less solid than it had ten minutes ago.
