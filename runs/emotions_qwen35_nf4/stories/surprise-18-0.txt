Elias stared at the screen, his thumb hovering over the delete button as if it were made of hot coals. The battery icon blinked red, a rhythmic pulse that matched the frantic thudding in his chest. He had found the old burner phone tucked inside the hollow leg of her nightstand drawer, hidden beneath a stack of unpaid bills. His breath hitched, shallow and sharp, catching in his throat. He wasn't angry yet; he was just trying to process the geometry of the lie, how neatly she had folded this other life into the margins of theirs. The room felt suddenly too large, the air thinning until every inhale required conscious effort.
