Marcus picked up the receipt from the dry cleaner’s bag, noticing the timestamp and the location—a hotel bar three towns over. He ran the paper between his fingers, crinkling it softly, rhythmically. The motion was automatic, a distraction from the sudden drop in his blood pressure. He walked to the sink and turned on the tap, letting the water run loudly to mask the raggedness of his breathing. He stared at his reflection in the darkened window above the sink, seeing a stranger staring back, eyes wide and unblinking. The realization settled in his gut like a stone, heavy and immovable.
