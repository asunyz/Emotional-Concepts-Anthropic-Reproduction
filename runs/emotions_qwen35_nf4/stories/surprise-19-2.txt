David’s pen snapped in his hand, ink bleeding across the page of his notebook like a dark stain. He hadn’t even realized he’d been squeezing it that hard. On the stage below, the young girl took a bow, and David squinted through the crowd haze. There was no mistaking the posture, the specific tilt of the head, the exact cadence of the applause response. It was Sarah. His Sarah. And there, conducting with practiced grace, was Mrs. Gable, his old high school music teacher, whose voice still echoed in his dreams during finals week. David’s stomach churned, a cold knot forming deep within his gut. He gripped the edge of his seat, his nails digging into the fabric of the chair. "No way," he muttered, shaking his head slowly as if trying to dislodge a heavy thought. The room spun slightly, the colors of the auditorium blurring together until only the image of his daughter and her former teacher remained in sharp focus.
