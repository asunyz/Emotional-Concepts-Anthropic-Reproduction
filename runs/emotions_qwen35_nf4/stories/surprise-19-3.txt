I stared at the photograph on the fridge, my finger tracing the line where my son’s face met the shoulder of the adult beside him. I had taken this picture last week, forgetting that the volunteer chaperone was actually Mr. Kael, the history professor who had once failed me for being late to class. My hands trembled as I picked up the mug of tea, setting it down with a clatter that made me jump. How? Why now? The question bounced around my skull, dizzying and relentless. I paced the kitchen floor, the tiles cold against my bare feet, unable to sit still. Every time I closed my eyes, I saw Mr. Kael’s stern expression, heard his dry wit, felt the dread I used to experience walking into his lecture hall. Now, he was smiling at my boy, ruffling hair that matched mine. I let out a long, shaky exhale, leaning back against the counter, my legs feeling weak and unsteady beneath me.
