My grandmother adjusted her glasses, squinting at the piece of paper held between her wrinkled fingers. "This is Mrs. Higgins," she whispered, her voice trembling. "She lived next door when we were girls. She passed away forty years ago." The drawing was crude, done by my young cousin, but the resemblance was uncanny. Grandma’s eyes widened, filling with tears that spilled over onto her cheeks. She began to shake, a fine vibration that traveled through her entire frame. She dropped the drawing, catching it reflexively before it hit the ground. "How does she know?" she breathed, looking around the room as if searching for an accomplice. The house felt suddenly smaller, the shadows longer. Grandmother sat down heavily in her armchair, clutching the drawing to her chest like a lifeline. She wouldn’t let go. None of us dared to take it from her.
