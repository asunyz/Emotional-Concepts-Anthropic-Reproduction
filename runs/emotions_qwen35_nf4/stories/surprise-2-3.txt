Marcus gripped the steering wheel until his knuckles turned white. The car idled outside the elementary school, but he hadn’t moved in twenty minutes. On the passenger seat lay a folder of art projects collected from the day. He flipped past the standard landscapes and self-portraits until he stopped cold. There, in meticulous pencil strokes, was the face of Old Man Jenkins’ dog, Buster, but with human hands. Marcus remembered Buster vividly; he had been the only thing that comforted him during the hospital stays. He blinked hard, rubbing his eyes, assuming fatigue was playing tricks on him. But the drawing remained, stark and undeniable. He exhaled sharply, a long, shuddering breath that fogged the windshield. Why would a seven-year-old know about a dog that died thirty years ago? He started the engine, the noise loud in the stillness, and drove away without picking up his niece.
