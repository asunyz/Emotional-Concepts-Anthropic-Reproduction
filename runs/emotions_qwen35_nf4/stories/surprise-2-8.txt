I froze mid-step, one foot hovering above the stairwell landing. Below me, my niece was coloring furiously on the bottom step. The colors were vibrant, aggressive reds and blacks. I crept down quietly, the wood creaking under my weight. When I got close enough to see the subject, my blood ran cold. It was the Gargoyle from the library roof, the one I used to visit to escape bullies. I had never mentioned it to anyone. I had never taken a photo. Yet here it was, rendered in wax crayon with terrifying accuracy. I reached out to pull the paper away, but my hand stopped inches from the surface. What if looking at it makes it real? What if it decides to come down from the page? I withdrew my hand slowly, backing away until my spine hit the wall. I slid down to the floor, hugging my knees, unable to look away from the creature’s stone-cold stare.
