I woke up to the smell of burnt toast and heard laughter coming from the kitchen. Sitting up, disoriented, I rubbed the sleep from my eyes and shuffled toward the source. There stood my daughter, wearing my old apron, flipping pancakes with exaggerated flair. On the counter sat a framed certificate: *Best Teacher Award - 20 Years Service.* But it wasn’t signed by the principal. It was signed by hundreds of names, all my former students. I leaned against the doorframe, gripping the wood to steady myself, my breath coming in short, sharp gasps. The room spun slightly, not from dizziness, but from the sheer volume of affection filling the space. I walked forward, my legs feeling like jelly, and hugged her tightly, burying my face in her shoulder, letting the quiet joy wash over me, drowning out the morning rush of the world outside.
