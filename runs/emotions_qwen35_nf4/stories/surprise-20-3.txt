Elena stopped mid-sentence, her chalk dusting her fingertips as she noticed the vase placed centrally on her kitchen table. It wasn’t hers. The flowers inside were wilting already, their petals curled inward as if retreating from the light. Her stomach did a slow flip, a sensation akin to missing a step on a staircase. She knew who it was by the specific shade of blue wrapping paper—only one person ever bought that brand. She touched the rim of the vase, the ceramic cool and solid under her skin. A text message buzzed in her pocket, then another, then a flood. She let the phone drop onto the cushion beside her, covering her mouth with both hands as tears pricked at the corners of her eyes, blurring the vibrant colors of the bouquet until they became a beautiful, overwhelming smear.
