She opened the door to find no one there, only a single red rose lying on the welcome mat, its stem dripping water onto the dry wood. Clara stepped back, her hand flying to her mouth. The air in the hallway felt suddenly thin, making it hard to draw a full breath. She recognized the thorns; they were the ones her late husband had grown in their garden every spring. Her knees buckled slightly, and she grabbed the doorframe for stability, her knuckles turning white. How? Why? The questions swirled in her head, chaotic and fast. She bent down slowly, picking up the flower, the scent hitting her like a memory she hadn’t visited in years. A sob caught in her throat, sharp and sudden, and she retreated into the house, pulling the door shut behind her, leaving the world outside for a long, silent minute.
