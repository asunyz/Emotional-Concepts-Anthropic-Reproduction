Arthur wiped his glasses clean for the third time, certain his vision was failing. The manuscript on his desk was thick, bound in simple cloth, with his name printed on the spine in bold letters. He flipped it open, scanning the dedication page. *To Arthur Vance, who taught us that words have power.* The handwriting was shaky, unfamiliar yet evoking a strange sense of recognition. His pen rolled off the desk, clattering loudly against the floorboards. He stood up abruptly, knocking his chair backward, and paced the length of the small study. The realization washed over him, cold and electric. The author was a former student from five years ago, the one who had struggled with dyslexia and eventually dropped out. Arthur sank back into his chair, clutching the book to his lap, his breathing shallow and rapid as he stared at the pages, unable to look away.
