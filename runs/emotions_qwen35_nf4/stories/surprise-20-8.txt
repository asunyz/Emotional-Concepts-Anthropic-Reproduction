Lena found the key taped underneath the passenger seat of her car. It was old, brass, tarnished with age. She frowned, running her thumb over the teeth of the key, trying to place it. It fit the lock of the storage unit she rented decades ago, the one she thought she’d forgotten. Driving there felt like a compulsion, her grip on the steering wheel tight, her nails digging into the leather. When she inserted the key, the mechanism resisted, then clicked open with a satisfying snap. Inside, amidst boxes of old clothes, sat a wooden music box. She wound it, and the tinkling melody filled the cramped space. Tears streamed down her face, hot and fast, soaking into her collar. She sat on the dusty concrete floor, hugging her knees, listening to the tune repeat, lost in a past she hadn’t realized she was still carrying.
