My phone buzzed incessantly, lighting up the dark theater with a cascade of notifications. I silenced it, assuming it was work, but the vibration continued, insistent. Pulling it out, I saw a photo attached to a message from David, the troubled kid I’d counseled last year. It showed a stage, spotlights blazing, and David holding a trophy. The caption read: *First Place. Thanks for listening.* I sat frozen in the velvet seat, the surrounding audience fading into background noise. My throat tightened, constricting the airway just enough to make swallowing difficult. I looked around, half-expecting someone to see how moved I was, but everyone else was focused on the screen ahead. I pressed the phone to my ear, whispering a thank you that barely escaped my lips, while a grin spread across my face, wide and genuine, cracking the usual stoic mask I wore.
