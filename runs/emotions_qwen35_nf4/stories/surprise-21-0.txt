Elias stared at the envelope resting on his kitchen counter, the paper thick and cream-colored, stark against the chipped laminate. His thumb traced the embossed seal, a faint tremor running from his fingertips up his wrist. He hadn’t spoken to Marcus in fifteen years, not since the night the car crashed into the oak tree. The handwriting was unmistakable—looping, elegant script that had once signed love letters he thought were lost forever. Elias set the mug of coffee down, the ceramic clicking sharply against the saucer, and picked up the letter opener. His breath hitched, shallow and rapid, as he sliced through the wax. He unfolded the page, the sound crisp in the silent room, and read the first line. His eyes darted back to the second, then the third, his jaw tightening until a muscle feathered near his ear. He stood up abruptly, knocking his chair backward, and paced to the window, pressing his forehead against the cool glass while the rest of the letter blurred in his peripheral vision.
