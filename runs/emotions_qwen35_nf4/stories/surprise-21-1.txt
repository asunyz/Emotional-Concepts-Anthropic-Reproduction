The mail slot clattered, announcing another Tuesday’s junk mail, until Clara pulled out the single white sheet tucked beneath the flyers for pizza delivery. She froze mid-step, her socked feet sliding slightly on the hardwood floor. It was from David. Just his name, printed plainly, made her stomach drop as if she’d missed a step on a staircase. She scanned the date stamp; three decades old, yet the postmark was today. Her fingers began to shake, a violent vibration that rattled the paper against her palm. She looked around her empty apartment, suddenly hyper-aware of the ticking clock on the wall, the hum of the refrigerator, the dust motes dancing in the sunbeam. She sat down heavily on the edge of the sofa, bringing the letter close to her face, squinting at the ink smudges. A hot prickle spread across her neck, and she let out a short, sharp exhale, her hands gripping the edges of the page so tightly the paper crinkled audibly.
