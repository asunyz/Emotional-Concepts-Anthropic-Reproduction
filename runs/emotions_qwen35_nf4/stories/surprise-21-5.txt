June received the letter in a plain manila envelope during her morning walk. She stopped dead in the middle of the sidewalk, ignoring the passerby who muttered an apology and stepped around her. The return address was from the city hospital where she used to volunteer ten years ago. It was from Dr. Aris. June tore it open right there, standing on the pavement, her eyes scanning the dense block of text. Her breathing shallowed, becoming almost imperceptible. She took a step back, bumping into a lamppost, and slid down until she was sitting on the curb. She hugged her knees to her chest, the letter clutched in both hands like a shield. The wind tugged at the loose sheets of paper, and she pressed down hard with her free hand to keep it still. She didn't move for twenty minutes, just staring at the signature at the bottom, her mouth set in a thin, rigid line.
