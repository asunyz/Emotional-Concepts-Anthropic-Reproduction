I dropped the book. It hit the carpet with a dull thud, sliding across the rug until it bumped against my shoe. My hands were trembling so violently I could barely unclench my fingers from the spine. How did they know about the night in Seattle? That night was buried under layers of alcohol and regret, a memory I had successfully exiled from my conscious mind. Yet there it was, printed in bold serif font, chapter seven: *The Night the Lights Went Out*. I read the sentence again. And again. The ink seemed to shimmer, mocking me. A cold sweat pricked along my hairline, stinging my eyes. I stood up abruptly, knocking over my coffee cup. Brown liquid spread across the table, dripping onto the floor, but I didn’t move to clean it. I just stared at the open pages, my heart hammering against my ribs like a trapped bird.
