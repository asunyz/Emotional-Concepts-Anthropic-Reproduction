Detective Miller adjusted his glasses, squinting at the document spread across the evidence board. It wasn't police records. It was a novel. *The Life of Arthur Penhaligon*, by an unknown publisher. He picked up the page describing the murder of Sarah Jenkins. The details were precise: the time of death, the specific brand of cigarette found in the ashtray, the color of the rain on the pavement. Arthur’s blood ran cold. He hadn’t told anyone about the blue raincoat. No one knew he wore it. He touched the paper, feeling the rough texture of the print, and realized the handwriting in the margin matched his own diary entries from five years ago—entries he kept locked in a safe deposit box. His pulse roared in his ears, drowning out the hum of the fluorescent lights overhead.
