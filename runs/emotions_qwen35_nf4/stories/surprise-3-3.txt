She laughed, a short, brittle sound that died in her throat. "This is funny," she said to the empty room, though her voice lacked its usual warmth. The manuscript lay on her desk, typed and bound. She turned to the section detailing her childhood trauma, the incident she had sworn never to discuss outside therapy. The therapist had promised confidentiality; this writer had broken it. Her stomach churned, a nauseating twist of dread. She grabbed the phone, dialing the number listed on the back cover. It rang once, twice, then went to voicemail. She hung up, her knuckles white as she gripped the receiver. Outside, the wind howled against the windowpane, rattling the glass in its frame, mirroring the shaking in her hands.
