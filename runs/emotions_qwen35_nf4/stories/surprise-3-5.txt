Dr. Aris Thorne dropped his clipboard. Papers scattered across the floor of the operating theater, fluttering like fallen leaves. On top of the pile lay a leather-bound journal. He recognized his own handwriting immediately. But the entries weren't his medical notes. They were observations of his patients, thoughts he had never voiced, diagnoses he had only hinted at in whispers to colleagues. One entry detailed the exact moment Dr. Thorne decided to falsify the data in the clinical trial three years prior. The room spun. The sterile smell of antiseptic turned acrid in his nose. He backed away until his shoulders hit the cold metal cabinet. He couldn't speak. He couldn't breathe. He just stared at the open page, his mouth agape, the world narrowing down to those few lines of text that stripped him bare.
