Elias dropped his coffee mug, watching the brown liquid spread across the kitchen tiles like a dark map he hadn’t drawn. His jaw hung loose, muscles trembling as he stared at the fence line where the pristine white pickets had been just yesterday. Now, a chaotic skeleton of lumber jutted into the sky, defying gravity and logic. He blinked rapidly, rubbing his eyes until spots danced behind his lids, hoping for an illusion to fade. It didn’t. The noise of a circular saw shrieked from next door, a sound that seemed to vibrate directly into his molars. He gripped the counter edge, knuckles bleaching white, while his mind raced backward through the last forty-eight hours, searching for a clue he might have missed. There was none. Only the sudden, violent transformation of his quiet horizon.
