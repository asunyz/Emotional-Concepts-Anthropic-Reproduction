I pulled up to the curb, killing the engine just to sit there for a moment. The silence inside my car felt heavy, suffocating compared to the cacophony erupting from Mr. Henderson’s property. Where his modest bungalow had sat for thirty years, a steel frame now rose like a jagged tooth. I stepped out, the gravel crunching loudly under my boots, sounding too loud in the sudden vacuum of my own disbelief. My hands shook as I fumbled for my keys, dropping them twice. I stared at the blueprints tacked onto a makeshift board by the roadside, the lines sharp and unforgiving. A cold sweat prickled along my hairline. This wasn’t happening. It couldn’t be. Yet the smell of fresh-cut timber and diesel fuel was thick enough to taste, coating the back of my tongue.
