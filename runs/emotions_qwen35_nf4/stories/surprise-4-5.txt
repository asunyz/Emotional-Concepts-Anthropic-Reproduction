Marcus squinted against the glare of the morning sun, shielding his eyes with a calloused hand. The fog had lifted, revealing a sight that stopped him dead in his tracks. The vacant lot across the street, long occupied by weeds and rusted cars, was now a foundation pit. Rebar stuck out of the earth like metallic grass, glinting menacingly in the light. He dropped his newspaper, the pages fluttering to the ground unnoticed. His pulse roared in his ears, drowning out the distant hum of traffic. He scanned the area, looking for a sign, a permit, anything that explained this overnight metamorphosis. Nothing. Just the raw, exposed dirt and the sheer audacity of progress invading his neighborhood. He felt small, insignificant, as if the ground itself had betrayed him by shifting beneath his feet.
