Clara blinked, once, twice, three times, waiting for the image to resolve itself correctly. It didn’t. The neighboring house, usually draped in ivy and mystery, was stripped bare. Scaffolding wrapped around its sides like a bandage, and workers scurried up and down the metal rungs like ants. She tightened her grip on her grocery bags, the plastic handles digging into her palms. A chill ran down her spine, unrelated to the autumn breeze. She looked left, then right, checking if anyone else was witnessing this spectacle. The street remained quiet, oblivious. She let out a short, sharp laugh that sounded brittle even to her own ears. It was absurd. It was shocking. She set the bags down on the sidewalk and walked closer, compelled by a force she couldn't name, needing to see the cracks in the facade herself.
