I dropped the phone. It hit the carpet with a soft *thump*, sliding under the desk where I couldn't see it. My breath hitched in my throat, a sharp, jagged thing that refused to settle. I crouched down, ignoring the ache in my knees, and retrieved the device. The email was still there, bold text screaming the verdict. I read it again. And again. The words didn't change, but my mind scrambled to rearrange them into something else, anything else. A glitch. A mistake. I laughed, a short, brittle sound that died quickly in the quiet apartment. I stood up too fast, dizziness spinning the room into a watercolor smear. I needed coffee. No, I needed air. I grabbed my coat and ran out the door without putting on shoes, the cold pavement biting at my bare feet, grounding me in a reality I wasn't ready to accept.
