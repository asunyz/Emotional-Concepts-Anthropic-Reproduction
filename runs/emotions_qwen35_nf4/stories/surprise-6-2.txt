Sarah held the letter between two fingers, treating it like a dead insect she feared might still sting. Her pulse hammered in her neck, a frantic bird trapped in a cage of bone. She scanned the paragraph about financial aid thresholds, looking for the error, the typo, the misplaced decimal point that would explain why her name wasn’t on the list. There was none. Just clear, bureaucratic finality. She folded the paper along the crease lines, making it smaller, trying to contain the news within a tighter space. "It’s fine," she whispered to the empty kitchen, but her voice cracked on the second syllable. She set the cup of tea down gently, afraid that even the clink of ceramic might shatter the fragile illusion she was holding together. Outside, a car horn blared, startling her so violently she spilled hot liquid onto her wrist. She didn't wipe it away immediately; she just watched the steam rise, wondering how life could pivot so sharply on such a small hinge.
