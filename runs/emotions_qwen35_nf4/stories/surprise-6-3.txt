Marcus threw the laptop shut, the snap echoing like a gunshot in the library study hall. Heads turned, but he didn't care. He shoved his books into his bag with unnecessary force, zipping it closed with aggressive yanks. His hands shook as he adjusted his glasses, pushing them up the bridge of his nose repeatedly. How could they overlook his community service hours? The logic loop spun in his head, dizzying and relentless. He marched toward the exit, his stride long and erratic, bumping shoulders with a passerby who muttered an apology. He ignored it. He needed to walk until his lungs burned, until the physical exhaustion drowned out the cognitive dissonance. He stopped at the crosswalk, waiting for the light to change, staring at his own reflection in the darkened shop window beside him. The person looking back seemed to belong to a different timeline, one where acceptance was guaranteed. He blinked, hard, trying to wipe the image away.
