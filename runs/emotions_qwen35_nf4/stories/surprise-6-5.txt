Jia stared at the envelope sitting on her doormat, unrecognized by its plainness. She picked it up, turning it over in her hands, weighing the thin paper. Inside, the cardstock felt heavier than it should have. She tore the seal with her teeth, ripping it unevenly. The words blurred as tears welled up, hot and sudden, obscuring the page. She wiped them away frantically with the back of her hand, smearing ink across her cheek. No, she wouldn't cry. Not here. Not now. She paced the length of her living room, three steps forward, three steps back, her mind racing through alternative plans that suddenly seemed insufficient. The sunlight streaming through the blinds felt harsh, exposing the dust motes dancing in the air. She felt exposed, vulnerable. She sat on the floor, pulling her knees to her chest, and let out a long, shaky exhale that seemed to deflate her entire posture.
