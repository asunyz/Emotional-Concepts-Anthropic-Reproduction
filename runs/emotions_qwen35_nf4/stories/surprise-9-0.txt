Elias stared at the key in his palm, the metal cold against his sweating skin. He had lived in this house for twenty-two years, yet standing on the porch now felt like stepping onto an alien planet. The front door swung open before he could knock, revealing his mother’s face framed by the familiar floral wallpaper she refused to update. "You're late," she said, though her eyes darted nervously to his duffel bag. Inside, the air smelled of lemon polish and old dust, a scent that usually comforted him but now made his stomach twist. He set his bag down with a thud that echoed too loudly in the hallway. His father emerged from the kitchen, wiping his hands on a checkered towel, offering a tight, uncertain smile that didn't reach his eyes. Elias forced a laugh, but it came out as a dry cough. He looked around the living room, noting how the furniture was arranged exactly as he left it five years ago, preserved in amber. The silence stretched between them, heavy and suffocating, until he realized he was holding his breath, waiting for the other shoe to drop.
