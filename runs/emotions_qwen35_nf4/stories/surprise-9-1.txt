I froze when I saw the box on the table. It wasn’t supposed to be there. Not here. Not in the middle of the coffee table where Dad always kept the remote and a stack of unpaid bills. My hand hovered over the handle of my suitcase, fingers trembling slightly. Mom was humming in the kitchen, the sound cheerful and oblivious, which made the knot in my chest tighten further. I stepped closer, the floorboards creaking under my weight, and peered into the cardboard flaps. Inside were my childhood drawings, my report cards from elementary school, and a letter written in handwriting I recognized instantly—my own, from ten years ago. The world seemed to tilt on its axis. I hadn’t told anyone about moving back. I hadn’t even told myself fully until I packed the boxes. Now, seeing evidence of my past laid out like a museum exhibit, I felt exposed, stripped bare. I backed away slowly, my heart hammering against my ribs like a trapped bird, wondering how long they had known, and why they hadn’t said a word.
