The elevator dinged on the fourth floor. Maya stepped out, dragging her suitcase behind her. The carpet in the hallway was worn, stained with decades of foot traffic. She approached door 4B, fumbling for her keys. Before she could insert one, the door opened inward. Her father stood there, wearing a robe that looked too big for him. Behind him, the apartment was filled with boxes. Hundreds of them. They blocked the view of the living room entirely. "We’re moving," he said, his voice flat. Maya blinked. "What?" "We’re selling the condo," he continued, gesturing vaguely at the chaos. "Too much upkeep. We’re going to the assisted living facility next week." Maya stared at him, then at the sea of cardboard towers. Her return, planned with such care and expense, was happening inside someone else’s departure. The irony tasted like ash. She let go of her suitcase handle, the wheels rolling forward on their own, bumping into the pile of boxes. She stood frozen, watching the last remnants of her childhood dissolve into packing tape and bubble wrap.
