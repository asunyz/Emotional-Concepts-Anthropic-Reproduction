The lock clicked, a sharp, metallic snap that sounded final. Julian pushed the door open, expecting the usual chaotic noise of his teenage years, but found only the ticking of the grandfather clock in the hall. He dropped his keys in the bowl, the clatter startlingly loud. "Hello?" he called out, his voice cracking slightly. No answer. Just the hum of the refrigerator from the kitchen. He walked further in, his shoes squeaking on the hardwood. The walls were painted a color he didn’t recognize—a soft, neutral beige instead of the bright yellow he remembered. A single envelope sat on the console table, addressed to him in his father’s blocky script. He picked it up, turning it over in his hands. There was no stamp. It had been delivered recently. As he tore it open, his vision blurred, the text swimming before his eyes. He read the first line twice, then a third time, his lips parting in disbelief. The paper crinkled in his grip as his knuckles turned white, the reality of what was written sinking in like lead.
