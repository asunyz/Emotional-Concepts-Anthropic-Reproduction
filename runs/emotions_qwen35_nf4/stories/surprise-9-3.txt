Sarah laughed, a high-pitched, nervous sound that bounced off the sterile white walls of the new apartment. "It’s perfect!" she declared, spinning around to show her parents the empty space. Her father stood by the window, arms crossed, his expression unreadable behind thick glasses. "Where are the pictures?" he asked quietly. Sarah stopped spinning. She looked at the blank walls, then at the framed photos of her graduation and wedding that were still in the boxes by the door. She had forgotten to hang them. The realization hit her with the force of a physical blow. This wasn’t just a change of address; it was a erasure of history. She rushed to unpack a frame, her hands shaking as she tried to align it perfectly straight. When she stepped back, the reflection in the glass showed a woman who looked older than thirty-five, tired and unsure. Her mother touched her shoulder gently, a gesture that felt both comforting and constricting. Sarah swallowed hard, the lump in her throat making it difficult to speak, while outside, the rain began to fall, blurring the city lights into streaks of gray and gold.
