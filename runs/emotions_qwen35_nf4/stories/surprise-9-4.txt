David kicked off his boots, leaving muddy prints on the pristine rug. He winced, looking at the stain spreading across the wool. "Sorry," he mumbled, grabbing a roll of paper towels from the counter. His mother didn’t scold him. She simply watched, her hands clasped tightly in front of her apron. The silence was worse than yelling would have been. He moved toward the living room, avoiding eye contact with the television screen that remained dark. On the mantle sat a photo of him at age seven, grinning with missing teeth. Next to it was a newer photo, taken last month, of him looking serious and thin. The contrast was jarring. He reached out to touch the frame, pulling his hand back quickly as if burned. Why did they keep the old one? Was this a reminder of what he used to be, or what he had lost? He sank onto the couch, the cushions sighing beneath his weight. The house felt too quiet, too clean, too much like a stranger’s home. He pulled his knees to his chest, wrapping his arms around himself, feeling small and vulnerable in a way he hadn’t since childhood.
