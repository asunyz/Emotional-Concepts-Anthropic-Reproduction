The mail slot clattered shut, sending a single envelope sliding across the entryway tile. Leo bent down to retrieve it, expecting a bill. Instead, he found a ticket stub. It was from the movie theater downtown, dated three days ago. Two seats were marked. He flipped it over, his blood running cold. Written on the back in his sister’s looping script was a message: *We missed you.* He stared at the ink, smudging it with his thumb. He hadn’t seen his sister in six months. He hadn’t spoken to either parent since the argument last winter. Yet here was proof of a life continuing without him, a life that included shared memories he wasn’t part of anymore. He walked into the kitchen, the linoleum cold through his socks. His mother was chopping vegetables, the rhythmic *thud-thud-thud* of the knife syncing with his heartbeat. She glanced up, smiled, and asked if he wanted tea. He held the ticket tighter, the paper creasing under his pressure, feeling the ground shift beneath his feet as the illusion of independence shattered.
