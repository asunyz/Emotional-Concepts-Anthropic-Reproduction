Marcus dropped his bags on the floor, the impact causing a vase on the sideboard to wobble dangerously. He caught it just in time, his heart leaping into his throat. "Careful," his father murmured, not looking up from his newspaper. Marcus placed the vase back, aligning it perfectly with the edge of the table. He looked around. The house was spotless. Too spotless. There was no clutter, no stray magazines, no signs of daily life. It felt like a stage set. He walked to the mirror in the hall, adjusting his tie. His reflection stared back, pale and wide-eyed. He looked like a guest. A temporary resident. The realization prickled along his spine. He had spent years building a career, a persona, a distance from this place. And now, walking back through those doors, he felt invisible. His mother entered the room, carrying a tray of cookies. "Eat something," she said, placing the tray on the table. "You look peaky." He forced a bite, the sweetness cloying and artificial, choking him slightly as he fought back the urge to run.
