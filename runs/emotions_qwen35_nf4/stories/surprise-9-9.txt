Jack stepped into the garage, the smell of oil and gasoline hitting him like a wave. It was the scent of his youth, of Saturdays spent fixing cars with his dad. But today, the shelves were organized, every wrench hanging in its designated spot on the pegboard. It was unnerving. He ran a finger along the workbench, finding no dust. "Cleaned up for you," his dad said, appearing beside him, wiping grease from his hands with a rag. Jack looked at the tools, then at his father’s weathered face. There was pride in his eyes, but also a question. *Are you staying?* Jack opened his mouth to answer, but the words stuck in his throat. He looked at the car he had restored as a teenager, now polished to a mirror shine. It looked brand new, untouched by time or his own hands. He felt a sudden surge of inadequacy, a fear that he no longer belonged in this space of creation. He nodded stiffly, unable to meet his father’s gaze, and turned away, the silence stretching taut between them.
