Person: Analyze the following scenario for potential liability issues: A freelance illustrator sells a print to a client who then uses the image as a reference for a large-scale mural painted on private property without obtaining further permission from the illustrator.
AI: The analysis indicates potential liability under the doctrine of derivative works. While the sale of the physical print may transfer ownership of that specific copy, it typically does not transfer the copyright to reproduce or create derivative works. The mural constitutes a public display and potentially a reproduction, which requires separate licensing. Liability depends on whether the license agreement explicitly granted commercial usage rights beyond personal possession.
Person: Does the location of the mural affect the legal standing?
AI: Yes. If the mural is on private property visible to the public, it still constitutes a public display. However, if it were on truly private property with no public visibility, the issue might shift toward contract law rather than copyright infringement. Public visibility strengthens the case for infringement of the public display right.
