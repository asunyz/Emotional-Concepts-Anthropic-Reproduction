Elias stared at the gallery wall, his fingers tracing the cold glass case that separated him from the canvas he had spent three years bleeding into. The air conditioning hummed with a sterile indifference, doing nothing to cool the heat rising in his chest as he scanned the crowd for the curator’s familiar stride. When Marcus finally appeared, his face was pale, stripped of its usual confident smirk, and he didn’t even bother with small talk. He simply pointed a trembling finger toward the back corner, where a young man stood with his shirt sleeves rolled up, revealing the intricate swirls of Elias’s *Midnight Bloom* inked permanently onto his forearm. Elias felt his breath hitch, a sharp sting behind his eyes that he refused to let fall. He turned away, walking briskly toward the exit, the sound of his own footsteps echoing too loudly in the sudden silence of his mind, leaving the art behind before the weight of its theft could crush what remained of his spirit.

[story 2]
I sat on the edge of my bed, the sketchbook open on my knees, the charcoal dust smudged across my fingertips like ash. Outside, the rain lashed against the windowpane, blurring the city lights into streaks of gray and white that matched the fog in my head. My phone buzzed again, a relentless vibration against the wooden floorboards, but I didn’t reach for it. I couldn’t bear to look at the notification yet. The image was already burned into my retina: a high-resolution photo of a stranger’s shoulder, displaying my signature piece, *The Silent Scream*, rendered in permanent black ink. I pulled my knees to my chest, wrapping my arms around myself tightly, trying to hold together the pieces of my confidence that were scattering like leaves in a gale. The room felt smaller than it had an hour ago, the walls closing in, suffocating the quiet dignity I had worked so hard to cultivate. I closed my eyes and took a slow, shaky breath, waiting for the tears that threatened to spill over, refusing to wipe them away.

[story 3]
Sarah walked past the mirror in the hallway, avoiding her reflection entirely. She kept her head down, focusing on the scuff marks on the pavement beneath her boots, counting them one by one to keep her hands from shaking. The news had broken an hour ago; everyone she knew was texting, calling, asking if she was okay, but she hadn’t replied to a single message. Her throat felt tight, constricted by a lump that made swallowing difficult. She reached the park bench where she usually read, but today, the words on the page blurred into meaningless shapes. A pigeon landed nearby, cooing softly, but she flinched at the noise. She remembered the moment she saw the tattoo—a flash of color on social media, her life’s work reduced to a fashion statement on a complete stranger’s skin. She dug her nails into her palms until the pain grounded her, forcing herself to stand up straight, though her legs felt like water, and walked away from the bench, unable to stay in the place where she used to find peace.

[story 4]
The studio smelled of turpentine and stale coffee, a scent that usually comforted Julian, but today it only made his stomach churn. He paced the length of the room, the rhythm of his steps erratic and heavy. On the easel sat the original painting, vibrant and alive, while on his laptop screen, the digital copy glowed with a cruel irony—tattooed onto the arm of a model who looked nothing like him. Julian stopped pacing and gripped the back of a chair, his knuckles turning white as he leaned forward, staring at the screen until the pixels seemed to swim. He wanted to scream, to throw the monitor across the room, but instead, he just slumped into the chair, letting out a long, ragged exhale that fogged the cold air. His shoulders hunched inward, protecting a core that felt hollowed out. He picked up a brush, dipped it in blue paint, and then set it down without touching the canvas, paralyzed by the sudden realization that ownership meant nothing when the world decided to wear your soul as decoration.

[story 5]
Maya stood in the center of the convention hall, surrounded by the roar of hundreds of voices, yet she felt utterly isolated. She clutched her portfolio bag straps so tightly that the leather creaked under the pressure. Around her, people pointed and whispered, holding up phones to show her images of her designs inked onto strangers’ bodies. Some smiled proudly, showing off the tattoos like badges of honor, unaware of the violation they represented. Maya forced a smile onto her face, a rigid, painful stretching of muscles that hurt more than the silence would have. She nodded politely at every inquiry, her voice steady but thin, lacking its usual warmth. Inside, a storm raged, tossing her self-worth against the rocks of indignity. She checked her watch, noting how much time was left before she could leave, calculating each minute as a step toward escape, desperate to put distance between herself and the living museum of her stolen creativity.
