He had spent the last hour rehearsing what he would say if she asked for advice, but now, looking at the small pamphlet resting near her plate, the words had evaporated into thin air. Her hand hovered over the door handle, shaking slightly, before she pulled back and rested her forehead against the cool glass. The condensation blurred the world outside, turning the streetlights into smeared streaks of orange and yellow. She closed her eyes, trying to summon the memory of their last shared prayer, but all she could feel was a hollow ache expanding in her ribs, a void where certainty used to live. The engine ticked as it cooled, the only sound breaking the suffocating stillness of the afternoon.

She sat on the floor, surrounded by scattered photographs, picking up an image of their grandparents smiling in front of the church doors. Her thumb brushed over the glossy surface, blurring the smiles with the moisture gathering in her eyes. She didn’t wipe them away; instead, she let them fall freely onto the print, distorting the scene further. The phone lay face down on the rug, vibrating intermittently with notifications she was too exhausted to check. She curled her knees to her chest, wrapping her arms around herself tightly, seeking a pressure that might counterbalance the lightness she felt in her stomach, a sensation akin to floating away from the ground. Outside, the wind picked up, rattling the loose pane in the window frame, a persistent reminder of the chill creeping into the room despite the heating system humming steadily overhead.
