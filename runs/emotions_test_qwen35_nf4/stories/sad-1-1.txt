I watched my brother fold the letter with precise, trembling hands, the paper crinkling softly in the quiet room. I wanted to tell him that we were losing something sacred, something woven into the very fabric of our Sunday dinners, but the lump in my throat refused to yield. Instead, I stood up abruptly, claiming I needed more water, and walked to the sink where I turned the tap on full blast just to hear some noise other than the ringing in my ears. My reflection in the window above the sink looked pale, shadows pooling under my eyes like bruises. I gripped the edge of the counter until my knuckles turned white, grounding myself in the sharp pain while I listened to him explain why this new path called to him, each word feeling like a stone dropped into a deep well, disappearing without a trace.
