David placed the book gently on the shelf, aligning it perfectly between two others, though his movements lacked their usual fluid grace. He wiped his hands on his apron, leaving faint smudges on the fabric, and leaned back against the counter, closing his eyes for a brief moment. The house smelled of lavender and old wood, scents that usually brought comfort but now felt stifling, trapping him in a past he was struggling to accept. He heard his sister laughing in the next room, a sound that used to fill him with warmth, but today it rang out sharp and distant, like a bell tolling from far away. He opened his mouth to speak, to ask one question, one doubt, but the air caught in his lungs, heavy and thick, forcing him to remain silent as he watched her pack her bags for a journey he did not understand.
